Here is a detailed review of the Bali Zoning Map tool prototype, based on the provided brief.

This review analyzes the tool's potential as a client-facing instrument for Bali Zero, focusing on its effectiveness in converting user anxiety into trust and generating high-quality leads for due diligence services.

### Overall Assessment

The Bali Zoning Map is a brilliantly conceived tool that is far more than a simple prototype; it's a masterclass in product-led growth and trust-building. It correctly identifies the single greatest fear of its target audience—losing hundreds of thousands of dollars on an illegal property—and addresses it head-on with transparency and expertise. Its primary strength lies not in its technical implementation but in its strategic and psychological framing. It positions Bali Zero not as a seller of property, but as a protector of investment. While there are clear areas for improvement, particularly regarding the map's accuracy and the tool's overall interactivity, the foundational concept is exceptionally strong. It successfully shifts the conversation from "What can I buy?" to "What can I *safely* buy?"

---

### 1. UX/Product Hierarchy

**Score: 9/10**

The information hierarchy in the side panel is logical, persuasive, and demonstrates a deep understanding of the user's mindset.

1.  **Header & Badge:** Immediately orients the user with the most critical information: "Where am I, and what is the top-level risk?" This is perfect.
2.  **Hak Milik Scam Warning:** Placing this immediately after the header is a stroke of genius. It’s a bold, pattern-interrupting move that instantly builds credibility. Instead of hiding the industry's dirty secret, the tool puts it in the spotlight, framing itself as the user's advocate from the very first second. This placement is non-negotiable and should be preserved.
3.  **Ownership & PBG Risk:** The flow from the scam warning to the legitimate ownership options (`Hak Pakai`, HGB) and then to the building permit (PBG) risk is a natural progression. It follows the mental model of a potential buyer: "Okay, freehold is a trap. So how *can* I own? And once I own, can I build?"
4.  **Value, then CTA:** The tool provides significant value—risk assessment, action items, a case study—*before* presenting the `$850` Due Diligence CTA. This is a classic and effective strategy. The user understands the complexity and the stakes *before* being asked to pay, making the price feel justified.

The current order is nearly perfect. It tells a compelling story: "Here's the danger, here's the path to safety, here's what you need to check, and if you need an expert, we're here."

### 2. Trust Calibration

**Score: 8/10**

The tool overwhelmingly feels like a serious legal instrument, not a marketing gimmick. Several elements contribute to this:

*   **Integrity Signals:** The "Hak Milik scam warning" and, most importantly, the **Black Zone handling** for Tabanan are powerful signals of honesty. Refusing to offer a paid service for a prohibited zone proves the tool's primary purpose is protection, not just revenue. This "costly signal" is worth more than any marketing copy.
*   **Data-Driven Presentation:** The use of a percentage-based PBG risk meter, hard numbers in the stats section (`$10.4B at-risk`), and the citation of the official GISTARU source ground the tool in objectivity and fact.
*   **Professional Aesthetics:** The "Palette D" dark mode with its signal red and violet hues connotes a serious, premium service—more akin to a financial dashboard than a travel blog.

The primary element hurting trust is the **"hand-drawn" SVG map**. For a tool concerned with legal boundaries and precision, "hand-drawn" suggests "imprecise" and "artistic," which undermines the authoritative feel. This is the single biggest point of friction against achieving a perfect trust score.

### 3. Conversion Flow

**Score: 9/10**

The conversion flow is well-structured to maximize both high-intent conversions and lead capture.

The sequence is:
1.  **Value Demonstration:** Detailed risk analysis.
2.  **Primary CTA (`$850 DD Service`):** Presented at the moment of maximum perceived risk and need.
3.  **Social Proof (`Case Study`):** Immediately reinforces the value of the primary CTA.
4.  **Secondary/Soft CTA (`Free PDF Summary`):** Acts as a down-sell or exit-intent capture for users not ready to commit to the paid service.

This is the correct sequence. Placing the free lead magnet before the paid CTA would devalue the main offering. The current flow effectively segments users: those with high intent and budget are primed for the DD service, while those with lower intent or budget are still captured in the funnel via the free summary. It’s an efficient and user-respecting funnel.

### 4. Data Quality

**Score: 10/10**

The data, as described, appears to be of very high quality and realism.

*   **Location Selection:** The 10 demo locations are a perfect cross-section of the Bali market, covering everything from hyper-developed hotspots (Canggu) and luxury enclaves (Nusa Dua) to culturally sensitive areas (Ubud) and untouchable heritage sites (Jatiluwih).
*   **Zoning Realism:** The distribution of zone colors (mostly amber, few green) is an honest reflection of the complex reality of Bali development, where straightforward "green" zones are rare in desirable areas. Citing Ubud's `Subak` (rice paddy) issues as a "red" zone is a specific, accurate, and high-value warning.
*   **Legal Accuracy:** The clear distinction between `Hak Milik` (the "scam"), `Hak Pakai`, HGB, and Leasehold—and identifying the central role of a PMA company for HGB—demonstrates an expert, up-to-date understanding of Indonesian property law for foreigners.

The data isn't just "correct"; it's curated to teach the user and protect them from the most common and costly mistakes.

### 5. Missing Elements

**Score: 5/10**

While the existing elements are strong, several critical features are missing to make the tool "10x more useful" and transition it from a demo into an indispensable utility.

1.  **True Map Interactivity (GIS Backend):** The biggest missing piece. The user should be able to click *anywhere* on the map—or better yet, draw a polygon around a specific plot of land—and get its zoning data instantly. The current 10-pin system is a lookup table; a real GIS-powered map would be a true utility.
2.  **Market & Pricing Data:** The "where" and "what" are covered, but the "how much" is missing. Integrating a data layer with median land/villa prices for the selected zone and historical price appreciation trends would be a game-changer. It bridges the gap between legal diligence and financial diligence.
3.  **Infrastructure & Environmental Data:** Layers for flood risk, internet quality, road access, and planned government projects would provide a much more holistic view of a property's true value and livability.
4.  **Comparison Tool:** The ability to select two or three locations and view their data side-by-side would be an incredibly powerful feature for aiding user decision-making.

### 6. SVG Map Design

**Score: 3/10**

The "hand-drawn" map is the prototype's most significant conceptual flaw. While acceptable as a placeholder, it is fundamentally at odds with the tool's purpose. A tool designed to build trust around precision, legal accuracy, and data cannot present its foundational spatial information in a format that looks artistic and imprecise. The map's shapes must be realistic and derived from actual geographic data. The current approach severely undermines the "serious legal instrument" feel and should be replaced with a geographically accurate (even if simplified) SVG.

### 7. Black Zone Handling

**Score: 10/10**

The "no upsell" integrity approach for black zones is perfect. It is the single most powerful trust-building element in the entire tool. By refusing to monetize a user's interest in a prohibited area and instead offering an honest "DO NOT BUY" warning, Bali Zero makes an irrefutable statement: "We prioritize your financial safety over our revenue." This act of commercial integrity will generate more long-term brand loyalty and high-quality leads than any short-term fee ever could. It is the core of the tool's competitive edge and must be protected at all costs.

### 8. Mobile Experience

**Score: 9/10**

The described mobile approach—a single-column vertical stack with the map on top and the panel below—is the correct and most effective pattern. It’s intuitive, accessible, and prioritizes content logically for a smaller screen. The map provides initial context, and the natural vertical scrolling allows for uninterrupted reading of the detailed information. The detail about horizontal scrolling for the preset buttons is also a smart, space-saving implementation. This is a robust, no-frills solution that rightly prioritizes usability over flashy but complex mobile UI.

### 9. Indonesian Jargon Handling

**Score: 7/10**

The badge + tooltip + English translation approach is a good start, but likely insufficient for the gravity of the decisions being made. Jargon like `Hak Pakai`, `HGB`, and `PMA` are not just flavor text; they are legally distinct concepts with massive financial implications. While tooltips are useful, they are transient. For core concepts, the explanation should be more persistent. For example, within the "Foreign Ownership Status" table, brief, one-sentence explanations could be included as sub-headings under each ownership type. The goal should be to minimize the user's cognitive load and eliminate the need to hunt for critical definitions.

### 10. Competitive Edge

**Score: 8/10**

The tool's competitive edge is not in its breadth but in its depth and honesty. Compared to a typical property portal (e.g., Lamudi), this tool is:

*   **Vastly Better:** By focusing on **trust and user protection** rather than inventory and sales, it carves out a unique and valuable niche. Its "problem-first" approach and radical honesty (scam warnings, black zones) differentiate it from every agent and portal whose primary goal is to make a commission. It is an expert advisor, not a catalogue.
*   **Temporarily Worse:** Its primary weakness is its current lack of scale. With only 10 demo points and a non-interactive map, it is not yet a universal utility. A user cannot research a specific property they found elsewhere.

Strategically, its edge is its power as a lead-generation magnet for high-quality, educated clients who understand the risks and value expert help. It doesn't compete with property portals; it sits on top of them as an essential validation layer.
