# KBLI 2025: What Changed and Why It Matters

## A Regulatory Briefing for Foreign Business Owners Operating in Indonesia

---

## Executive Summary

On February 12, 2025, Indonesia's national statistics agency (Badan Pusat Statistik, BPS) published Peraturan BPS Nomor 7 Tahun 2025, formally replacing the KBLI 2020 business classification system with KBLI 2025. Every company operating in Indonesia — domestic or foreign-owned — must align its business licenses to the new classification framework by **June 18, 2026**. Failure to migrate carries consequences ranging from inability to renew licenses to suspension of operations through the OSS (Online Single Submission) system.

This document provides a comprehensive analysis of what changed, which businesses are most affected, and the operational steps required for compliance.

---

## 1. What Is KBLI and Why Does It Matter?

KBLI (Klasifikasi Baku Lapangan Usaha Indonesia) is Indonesia's official standard for classifying business activities. It determines:

- **What a company is legally permitted to do.** Every business license (NIB, Sertifikat Standar, Izin Usaha) is tied to specific KBLI codes. Operating outside your registered codes constitutes a licensing violation.
- **Tax classification.** The Directorate General of Taxes maps KBLI codes to KLU (Klasifikasi Lapangan Usaha) codes for fiscal profiling, audit selection, and reporting obligations.
- **Foreign ownership eligibility.** Indonesia's Investment Positive List (Daftar Positif Investasi, currently governed by PP 5/2021 as amended by PP 49/2023) determines whether foreign entities may hold equity in a given sector — and to what percentage — based on KBLI codes.
- **Risk classification and licensing requirements.** Under the Job Creation Law framework (PP 5/2021 and PP 6/2021), each KBLI code carries a risk level (Rendah, Menengah Rendah, Menengah Tinggi, Tinggi) that determines the licensing pathway — from automatic NIB issuance for low-risk activities to full environmental impact assessment (AMDAL) for high-risk sectors.

In practical terms, KBLI codes are not an administrative formality. They are the legal backbone of every business license issued through OSS.

| New codes introduced         | —         | 234       | Covering digital economy, green economy, creative industry |

The net reduction in total codes reflects a deliberate consolidation strategy. BPS eliminated redundant subcategories — particularly in wholesale and retail trade — while adding granularity in sectors that barely existed when KBLI 2020 was drafted: digital platforms, renewable energy, carbon trading, and gig-economy services.

## 3. What Changed: Key Differences Between KBLI 2020 and KBLI 2025

### 3.1 Digital Economy Recognition

KBLI 2020 was drafted in a pre-pandemic regulatory mindset. Digital businesses were shoehorned into legacy categories designed for physical operations. KBLI 2025 corrects this with explicit classifications:

- **Code 63122 — Web Portals and Digital Platforms.** Now formally covers SaaS businesses, e-commerce marketplaces, and digital platform operators. Under KBLI 2020, these were often registered under generic IT codes, creating ambiguity during tax audits.
- **Code 62019 — Other Software Development Activities.** Expanded scope covering custom software, mobile applications, and AI-driven development. Foreign ownership: 100% permitted (TERBUKA).
- **Code 62201-62209 — IT Consulting.** Clearer delineation between IT consulting subtypes, with 100% foreign ownership confirmed across all subcategories.

### 3.2 Consolidation of Retail and Trade Codes

The wholesale (Section 46) and retail (Section 47) sectors saw the most aggressive consolidation. KBLI 2020 had an excessively granular breakdown — separate codes for two-wheeled vehicle parts versus four-wheeled vehicle parts, for example. KBLI 2025 merges these:

- **Code 47192 — General Retail Trade (Non-Food).** Covers mixed retail operations excluding food, beverage, and tobacco. Low risk (Rendah), NIB-only licensing. Previously split across multiple subcategories.
- Multiple vehicle parts retail codes (47821, 47822, 47831, 47832) consolidated into **Code 47820**.

### 3.3 Food and Beverage Sector Restructuring

The F&B sector received significant attention given Indonesia's booming restaurant and cloud kitchen economy:

- **Code 56101 — Food Service in Permanent Buildings (Restaurants).** This is the primary restaurant code covering sit-down restaurants, warungs, fast food, food courts, and hotel restaurants. Risk classification: Menengah Rendah. Requires NIB and Sertifikat Standar. Foreign ownership: 100% permitted with IDR 10 billion minimum investment per PP 5/2021.

The distinction between permanent-structure food service (56101) and mobile food service has been sharpened, reducing the classification disputes that previously triggered licensing audits.

### 3.4 Foreign Ownership Landscape

Of the 1,563 codes in KBLI 2025:

- **1,512 codes (96.7%)** are classified as TERBUKA (open) — permitting up to 100% foreign ownership through a PT PMA structure.
- **12 codes** are TERBATAS (restricted) — foreign ownership permitted but capped below 100%, often at 49% or 67%.
- **39 codes** are TERTUTUP (closed) — no foreign ownership permitted.

This represents an increasingly open investment environment compared to the KBLI 2020 era, when the negative list imposed restrictions on a substantially larger number of sectors.

### 3.5 Green Economy and Sustainability Codes

KBLI 2025 introduces or reclassifies codes for:

- Renewable energy generation and distribution
- Carbon credit trading and verification
- Waste management and circular economy activities
- Environmental consulting and impact assessment

These reflect Indonesia's commitments under its Enhanced Nationally Determined Contribution (ENDC) and the JETP (Just Energy Transition Partnership) framework.

## 4. The Migration Deadline: June 18, 2026

### 4.1 What the Deadline Means

Peraturan BPS No. 7/2025 establishes a **transitional period of approximately 16 months** from publication (February 2025) to mandatory compliance (June 18, 2026). During this transition:

- Companies can continue operating under KBLI 2020 codes.
- The OSS system will accept both KBLI 2020 and KBLI 2025 codes for new applications (subject to system updates — as of March 2026, OSS has not yet fully integrated KBLI 2025).
- After June 18, 2026, only KBLI 2025 codes will be recognized.

### 4.2 What Happens If You Miss the Deadline

Non-compliance carries escalating consequences:

1. **License renewal blocked.** OSS will not process renewals referencing deprecated KBLI 2020 codes.
2. **New activity registration impossible.** Expanding into a new line of business requires KBLI 2025 codes.
3. **Tax filing misalignment.** The Directorate General of Taxes is migrating KLU codes to align with KBLI 2025. Mismatched codes between your NIB and your tax profile trigger audit flags.
4. **Investment approval delays.** BKPM (Indonesia Investment Coordinating Board) will process new investment applications exclusively under KBLI 2025.
5. **Operational suspension risk.** In extreme cases — particularly for regulated sectors (financial services, healthcare, education) — operating under revoked codes could result in license suspension.

### 4.3 Current OSS Status (March 2026)

A critical operational reality: as of March 2026, the OSS system has not yet been fully updated to reflect KBLI 2025. This creates a transitional gap where:

- Companies that want to proactively migrate may encounter system limitations.
- Some new KBLI 2025 codes cannot yet be selected in OSS.
- BPS and BKPM have indicated full OSS integration will occur before the June 2026 deadline, but no specific date has been announced.

This means companies should prepare their migration audit now but may need to execute the actual code change in the OSS system closer to the deadline.

## 5. Who Is Most Affected?

### 5.1 PT PMA Companies (Foreign-Owned)

Foreign-owned companies face the highest stakes because KBLI codes directly determine:

- Whether they can continue operating under their current structure
- Foreign ownership percentage limits
- Minimum capital requirements (IDR 10 billion per PP 5/2021, approximately USD 625,000)
- Whether additional technical permits are required

**Example — Management Consulting (Code 70209):** A PT PMA registered under KBLI 2020 management consulting codes that map directly to 70209 faces minimal disruption — this is a 1:1 match with unchanged scope. Foreign ownership remains 100% permitted, risk classification is Rendah (low), and only a NIB is required.

**Example — Restaurant Operations (Code 56101):** A PT PMA operating a restaurant needs to verify that its current KBLI registration maps correctly to 56101. If the company was registered under a subcategory that has been merged, the code must be updated. The risk classification (Menengah Rendah) also requires confirming that the Sertifikat Standar is current.

### 5.2 Multi-Activity Companies

Companies registered with multiple KBLI codes face the most complex migration. Each code must be individually verified:

- Does it still exist in KBLI 2025?
- Has it been merged into a different code?
- Has the risk classification changed (potentially requiring additional permits)?
- Has the foreign ownership status changed?

### 5.3 Companies in Renumbered Sectors

The 266 renumbered codes are particularly problematic. The underlying business activity is identical, but the code itself has changed. Administrative systems (tax, banking, licensing) that reference the old code will progressively fail to match.

## 6. The Three-Step Migration Process

### Step 1: KBLI Audit

Map every KBLI code currently listed on your NIB to its KBLI 2025 equivalent:

- **Direct match (928 codes):** No action required beyond confirming the code in the OSS system.
- **Renumbered (266 codes):** Update the code number; scope is unchanged.
- **Merged/aggregated (194 codes):** Verify that the new consolidated code covers all your previous activities.
- **Deprecated with no match:** Requires evaluation — the business activity may need to be registered under a different classification.

### Step 2: Impact Assessment

For each changed code, evaluate:

- Has the risk classification changed? (e.g., from Rendah to Menengah Rendah — requiring additional documentation)
- Has the foreign ownership percentage changed?
- Are new technical permits or certifications required?
- Does the KLU mapping change affect your tax reporting obligations?

### Step 3: OSS Update and License Alignment

Once the OSS system supports KBLI 2025 codes:

- Submit the updated KBLI codes through OSS.
- Obtain updated NIB reflecting the new codes.
- Ensure the Sertifikat Standar or Izin Usaha matches the new risk classification.
- Notify your tax consultant to update the KLU mapping with the tax office.
- Update company documents (akta, SK Kemenkumham) if KBLI codes are referenced.

## 7. Regulatory Context

### 7.1 Governing Regulations

- **Peraturan BPS No. 7 Tahun 2025** — The primary regulation establishing KBLI 2025.
- **PP 5/2021 (Government Regulation on Risk-Based Business Licensing)** — Defines risk categories and licensing requirements tied to KBLI codes.
- **PP 6/2021 (Government Regulation on Business Licensing in Regions)** — Regional implementation of KBLI-based licensing.
- **PP 28/2024** — Updates to licensing metadata, including business scale parameters and permit requirements per KBLI code.
- **PP 49/2023** — Amendment to the Investment Positive List affecting foreign ownership percentages.
- **Keputusan Menteri M.IP-08.GR.01.01/2025** — Updated immigration categories aligned with business license types.

### 7.2 The Job Creation Law Framework

KBLI 2025 operates within the Job Creation Law (Undang-Undang Cipta Kerja) framework, which fundamentally restructured Indonesia's licensing regime in 2020. The risk-based approach (risiko rendah, menengah rendah, menengah tinggi, tinggi) determines the licensing pathway:

| Risk Level                    | License Required                     | Processing                   |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Rendah (Low)                  | NIB only                             | Automatic                    |
| Menengah Rendah (Medium-Low)  | NIB + Sertifikat Standar             | Automatic (self-declaration) |
| Menengah Tinggi (Medium-High) | NIB + Sertifikat Standar             | Verified (7-day review)      |
| Tinggi (High)                 | NIB + Izin Usaha + Technical Permits | Full review + AMDAL          |

KBLI 2025 reassigns risk levels for some activities, which means the licensing pathway may change even when the underlying business activity is identical.

## 8. Common Misconceptions

**"My business hasn't changed, so I don't need to do anything."**
Incorrect. Even if your business activities are identical, the classification code itself may have changed. OSS and tax systems will progressively reject deprecated codes.

**"This is just a renumbering exercise."**
Partially true for the 928 direct-match codes, but 460 codes (30% of the total) involve mergers, renumbering, or new classifications that may affect licensing requirements.

**"The deadline will be extended."**
Possible but not guaranteed. Indonesia has a history of extending regulatory deadlines, but companies that wait for an extension risk being unprepared if it does not materialize.

**"My accountant will handle it."**
KBLI migration is not a tax filing exercise. It requires cross-functional coordination between corporate legal (akta amendments), licensing (OSS updates), tax (KLU alignment), and potentially immigration (if visa type is tied to specific business activities).

## 9. Sector Spotlight: High-Impact Changes

### Technology Sector

The IT and digital economy sector benefits most from KBLI 2025. Previously ambiguous classifications now have dedicated codes with confirmed 100% foreign ownership. Companies operating SaaS platforms, AI services, or digital marketplaces should proactively adopt the new codes — they provide clearer regulatory standing than the legacy classifications.

### Food and Beverage

The consolidation of F&B codes simplifies compliance for multi-format operators (e.g., a company running both a restaurant and a catering service). However, the Menengah Rendah risk classification for restaurant operations (56101) means Sertifikat Standar compliance must be maintained — a self-declaration of adherence to food safety standards.

### Retail and E-Commerce

Online retail (47911 — retail via internet) is now explicitly classified with 100% foreign ownership permitted. Brick-and-mortar retailers registered under codes that have been consolidated need to verify their updated classification.

### Real Estate

Real estate agencies (68201) and property management retain 100% foreign ownership status. However, the perennial restriction on land ownership by foreigners remains unchanged — PT PMA structures provide access to HGB (Hak Guna Bangunan, building rights) but not Hak Milik (freehold).
