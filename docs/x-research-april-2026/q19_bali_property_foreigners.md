Limited to one property per person in most cases; minimum property value threshold applies (~IDR 2–5 billion / ~$125K–$315K in Bali, varying by regency).  \nMaximum duration with extensions: 80 years total (initial 30 years + 20-year extension + 30-year renewal). Not automatic.  \nInheritance: Yes—the registered title can transfer to heirs under Indonesian succession rules. HGB (Hak Guna Bangunan) via PT PMA\nThis is the strongest commercial/investment option. A foreign-owned PT PMA (limited liability company) holds the HGB title.  \nProcess to obtain: (1) Incorporate PT PMA via OSS system (requires min. 2 shareholders, at least one resident director, appropriate KBLI codes for property/rental); (2) Minimum paid-up capital IDR 2.5 billion (~$150K) per BKPM 5/2025, with broader investment plan requirements; setup costs $3K–8K + 4–8 weeks; (3) Acquire land/building and convert to HGB in the company’s name (BPN process). Requires separate PBG building permit and tourism licenses (e.g., for rentals). Ideal for short-term rental businesses.  \nMortgage possibilities: Yes—HGB title is registered and mortgageable through the PT PMA (banks accept it as collateral).\n3. Leasehold (Hak Sewa)\nPurely contractual right; most accessible and cheapest entry for individuals. No residency or company required.  \nTypical terms in Bali: Initial 25–30 years, with negotiated extensions (common packages aim for 50–80 years total via addendums like 25+25 or 30+20+30). No statutory maximum. \npropertia.com\n  \nExtension clauses: Must be explicitly written into the original contract (pricing formula, timelines, binding on successors). Not automatic—renegotiated at expiry based on land value (often via independent appraisers).  \nRisks if landowner dies: Moderate. Indonesian inheritance law generally binds heirs to valid contracts, but disputes arise if the deed lacks clear succession language or if heirs challenge it. Far riskier than registered titles.  \nNotarization requirements: Strongly recommended (notarial deed via PPAT/PPJB → AJB). Not registered as a BPN title (land stays in owner’s Hak Milik), but a notarized lease provides stronger civil enforceability. Include full transfer/sale rights if desired.\n4.
