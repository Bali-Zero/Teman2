"Thought for 52s\nAIRE (Anagrafe degli Italiani Residenti all'Estero) is the official registry of Italian citizens living abroad for more than 12 months. Registration is both a right and a legal obligation for Italian citizens who transfer their residence abroad. It results in automatic cancellation from the Italian resident population registry (APR – Anagrafe della Popolazione Residente) of your last Italian municipality. \nesteri.it\nWhich Consulate Handles Bali?\nBali falls under the jurisdiction of the Italian Embassy in Jakarta (Ambasciata d’Italia a Jakarta – Ufficio Consolare). The Honorary Consulate in Denpasar (Bali) is limited/vacant and does not handle AIRE registrations or full consular services. All AIRE applications for Indonesia (including Bali) must be submitted to Jakarta. \nambjakarta.esteri.it +1\nContact: consolare.jakarta@esteri.it (appointments by email only for consular matters).\nComplete Process and Timeline\nDeadline: You must register within 90 days of establishing residence abroad (date of arrival or transfer for stays >12 months). You can pre-register before leaving Italy. \nambjakarta.esteri.it\nPreferred method – Online via FAST-IT portal (recommended):\nRegister or log in at serviziconsolarionline.esteri.it.\nIf you have an existing account from another consulate, use “Cambio Sede” to switch to “Indonesia > Jakarta”.\nFill the AIRE registration form (one per adult family member), attach required documents, and submit.\nThe Embassy reviews, transmits to your Italian Comune, which updates the registry. \nambjakarta.esteri.it\nAlternative – Postal application (if no scanner/printer or technical issues): Send by registered post to:\nAmbasciata d’Italia – Ufficio Consolare, Jl. Status “TRASMESSA AL COMUNE” means it has been forwarded; final confirmation comes from the Comune.\nThe service is free. \nambjakarta.esteri.it\nImportant: You must also notify your Italian Comune of the move abroad if you haven’t already.\nRequired Documents (Jakarta-specific)\nFor each Italian citizen and cohabiting family members (including non-Italians):\nSigned AIRE registration form (generated in FAST-IT, printed, signed by hand).\nCopy of valid Italian passport or Carta d’Identità for Italians; for non-Italian spouse/children: copy of their national passport + proof of relationship.\nProof of stable and legal residence in the consular district (Jakarta covers all Indonesia, including Bali):\n– Copy of KITAS (or other valid Indonesian residence permit).\n– Rental/work contract.\n– Utility bills (electricity, water, gas, phone) in your name showing the Bali address. \nambjakarta.esteri.it\nSpecial cases (minors with one parent, lost documents, etc.) have additional requirements – see the Jakarta embassy site or FAST-IT instructions.\nExact Legal References\nLegge 27 ottobre 1988, n. 470: Establishes the AIRE and the obligation to register (Art. 6–9). \nesteri.it\nDPR 6 settembre 1989, n. 323: Implementing regulation detailing procedures, forms, and transmission between consulates and Italian municipalities. \nnormattiva.it\nFailure to register triggers administrative fines (introduced by Legge 30 dicembre 2023, n. 213): €200–€1,000 per year of non-compliance, up to a maximum of 5 years. The competent authority is your last Italian Comune. \nesteri.it\nTax Consequences of Registering vs. NOT Registering AIRE\nRegistering:\nCancels you from the Italian APR → removes the automatic presumption of Italian tax residency under Art. 2 TUIR (for >183 days in the registry).\nProvides strong formal evidence of non-residency.\nHowever, AIRE is necessary but NOT sufficient. The Agenzia delle Entrate can still challenge and consider you fiscally resident in Italy if you maintain your “center of vital interests” (personal/family ties) or habitual abode in Italy (domicile/residenza factual criteria). Italian courts have repeatedly ruled that AIRE alone does not automatically prove non-residency. \nstudiocavallari.it\nNOT registering:\nYou remain listed in the Italian APR → absolute presumption of tax residency in Italy (worldwide income taxed in Italy, regardless of where you actually live).\nExtremely difficult to rebut without AIRE + strong proof of foreign residency. You risk double taxation and penalties. \nfiscoetasse.com\nInteraction with Italy-Indonesia Double Tax Agreement (DTA) (signed Jakarta 18 Feb 1990, in force since 2 Sept 1995):\nThe DTA (Art. 4) uses standard tie-breaker rules if both countries claim residency: (1) permanent home available; (2) center of vital interests (personal and economic relations); (3) habitual abode; (4) nationality; (5) mutual agreement between authorities.\nAIRE status helps significantly as evidence of Indonesian residency when applying the DTA tie-breakers. Without AIRE, the Agenzia delle Entrate may still treat you as Italian resident and use the DTA only to grant relief from double taxation (credit for Indonesian taxes paid), but you start from a disadvantaged position. \nfiscomania.com\nPractical advice: Combine AIRE with factual changes (sell/rent Italian property, move family, close Italian bank accounts, etc.) and possibly obtain an Indonesian tax residency certificate.\nImpact on Italian Healthcare (SSN)\nRegistration in AIRE generally causes loss of ordinary SSN coverage (no GP, no free Italian NHS services). You rely on Indonesian healthcare + private international insurance.\nTemporary stays in Italy: only urgent/emergency care is covered; elective care requires payment.\nRecent development (2025–2026): A bill (approved by Camera, under Senate review) allows AIRE-registered Italians in extra-EU/non-EFTA countries (like Indonesia) to access SSN in Italy by paying an annual voluntary contribution of €2,000 (non-fractionable; minors exempt if a parent pays). Contact your INPS office with your AIRE certificate for specific cases (e.g., totalization agreements – Italy-Indonesia has limited social security coordination compared to EU countries). \ninps.it\nImpact on Voting Rights\nAIRE registration is mandatory to be included in the electoral lists abroad and to vote by post at the consulate (Law 459/2001).\nWithout AIRE you cannot vote abroad.
