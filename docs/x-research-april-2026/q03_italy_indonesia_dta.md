"Thought for 1m 15s\nItaly-Indonesia Double Taxation Agreement (DTA): The full official title is the Agreement between the Government of the Republic of Indonesia and the Government of the Italian Republic for the Avoidance of Double Taxation with Respect to Taxes on Income and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion. It was signed in Jakarta on 18 February 1990 (in Italian, Indonesian, and English, with English prevailing in case of doubt). It entered into force on 2 September 1995 following ratification (Italy via Law No. 707 of 14 December 1994) and exchange of instruments. The current text incorporates modifications from the Multilateral Convention to Implement Tax Treaty Related Measures to Prevent Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (MLI). \npajak.go.id +1\nThe DTA applies to residents of Italy or Indonesia (Art. 1) and covers income taxes in both countries (Art. 2). It follows the OECD model with some deviations. Beneficial ownership is required for reduced withholding rates on passive income. The MLI has introduced (or reinforced) anti-abuse rules such as the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) across the treaty.\nKey Articles: Withholding Taxes and Permanent Establishment\nArticle 5 – Permanent Establishment (PE)\nA PE is a fixed place of business through which the business of an enterprise is wholly or partly carried on (para. 1). It explicitly includes: place of management, branch, office, factory, workshop, mine/quarry/extraction site, building/construction/assembly/installation project or supervisory activities (>6 months), and furnishing of services (including consultancy) by employees/personnel (>3 months aggregate in any 12-month period for the same or connected project) (para. 2).\nExclusions (para. 3): storage/display of goods, stock for storage/processing by another, purchasing/collecting information, advertising/scientific research (preparatory/auxiliary).\nAgency PE (para. 4): dependent agent who habitually concludes contracts or maintains/delivers stock (unless independent broker/commission agent acting in ordinary course; para. 6). Insurance PE via premium collection/ risk assumption (para. 5).\nControl of one company by another does not create a PE (para. 7). Protocol clarifications exist on storage depots and expenses.\nRelevance: An Italian resident (individual or company) owning shares in a PT PMA (Indonesian subsidiary) does not automatically create a PE in Indonesia. The PT PMA is a separate legal entity; mere ownership or control does not trigger PE. \npajak.go.id\nArticle 10 – Dividends\nDividends paid by a resident company of one state to a resident of the other may be taxed in the recipient’s state (para. 1).\nSource-state tax is limited (if beneficial owner):  \n10% of gross amount if the beneficial owner is a company (other than a partnership) holding directly at least 25% of the paying company’s capital.  \n15% in all other cases (including individuals).\nThe competent authorities settle application. Source state cannot tax undistributed profits or dividends paid to non-residents except in specific cases (para. 5).\nAdditional branch profits tax (up to 12% on after-tax PE profits) is allowed in some cases (para. 6), with carve-outs for old oil/gas/mining contracts (para. 7). \npajak.go.id\nArticle 11 – Interest\nInterest arising in one state and paid to a resident of the other may be taxed in the recipient’s state (para. 1). Source-state tax is limited to 10% of gross amount if beneficial owner (para. 2).\nExemptions (para. 3): interest paid to/paid by governments, local authorities, or certain wholly-owned agencies/instrumentalities (including on inter-government loans).\nDefinition (para. 4): government securities, bonds, debentures, debt-claims, etc. (including assimilated income). PE/fixed-base connection overrides (para. 5). Sourcing rules (paras. 6–7). Special relationship adjustment for excessive amounts (para. 8). \npajak.go.id\nArticle 12 – Royalties\nRoyalties arising in one state and paid to a resident of the other may be taxed in the recipient’s state (para. 1). Source-state tax limited to:  \n10% for industrial, commercial, or scientific equipment or information concerning industrial/commercial/scientific experience (know-how);  \n15% in all other cases (e.g., copyrights, patents, trademarks, films, etc.).\nBeneficial owner required; competent authorities settle application (para. 2).\nDefinition (para. 3) is broad.
