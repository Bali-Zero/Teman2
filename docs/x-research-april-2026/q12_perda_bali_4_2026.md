"Thought for 53s\nPerda Bali No. 4 Tahun 2026 (full title: Peraturan Daerah Provinsi Bali Nomor 4 Tahun 2026 tentang Pengendalian Alih Fungsi Lahan Produktif dan Larangan Alih Kepemilikan Lahan secara Nominee) was signed by Governor I Wayan Koster around 24 February 2026 and is now in force. It strengthens protection of productive agricultural land (especially LP2B — Lahan Pertanian Pangan Berkelanjutan, sustainable food-crop farmland including rice fields/sawah) against conversion to non-agricultural uses while also banning nominee (pinjam nama) ownership structures. It builds on national laws like UU No. 41/2009 (LP2B protection), PP No. 1/2011, Perpres No. 59/2019 (and updates), and spatial planning rules, plus prior Governor Instruction No. 5/2025 that imposed an absolute ban on conversions. \nindonesiaexpose.co.id +1\nIt includes a “freezing” (pembekuan) of all new conversion permits until ~87% of remaining sawah is permanently designated as LP2B. The goal is food sovereignty, Subak system preservation (UNESCO cultural heritage), ecological balance, and curbing tourism-driven speculation.\nHere is the point-by-point analysis based on official summaries, government statements, and reliable reporting as of April 2026:\nWhat exactly does this regulation criminalize?\nIt criminalizes (or triggers sanctions for) the conversion/alih fungsi of designated LP2B rice fields/sawah (and other productive ag land) to non-agricultural uses such as villas, hotels, commercial buildings, housing, or tourism infrastructure without proper approval. It also explicitly prohibits nominee ownership schemes (using Indonesian proxies to hold land for foreigners or others), which have historically enabled such developments on protected land. Legal conversions are effectively frozen; only very narrow exceptions (e.g., genuine public interest) may apply, subject to strict provincial oversight. \nindonesiaexpose.co.id\nWhat are the exact penalties?  \nCriminal (pidana): Primarily via national laws referenced by the Perda — UU 41/2009 Pasal 72–74: up to 5 years imprisonment and/or fine up to Rp1 billion for illegal LP2B conversion. UU 26/2007 (spatial planning): up to 3 years jail and/or Rp500 million fine. Additional KUHP charges possible for document fraud or abuse of authority.  \nAdministrative & fiscal sanctions (directly from Perda and related rules): written warnings, temporary/permanent stop-work orders, site closure, permit revocation/cancellation, administrative fines, loss of fiscal incentives (e.g., tax breaks), and mandatory demolition + land restoration to original agricultural use.  \nASN/officials involved face severe disciplinary action or prosecution.\nNo explicit “asset forfeiture” beyond demolition and permit revocation, but restored land effectively returns to public/agricultural control. \nindonesiaexpose.co.id +1\nWhich specific zones in Bali are affected?\nAll designated LP2B areas province-wide (including Luas Baku Sawah/LBS). Priority/high-scrutiny zones are the core rice-producing and Subak heartlands: Tabanan, Gianyar, Badung (especially Mengwi, Petang), Karangasem, Jembrana, and Bangli, plus parts of others. These align with provincial RTRW and kabupaten-level Perbup designations (e.g., Perbup Gianyar, Badung, Tabanan, Karangasem). Subak irrigation systems (UNESCO-listed) receive extra cultural/heritage protection and are almost always classified as LP2B. Non-LP2B land or already-converted/zoned tourism areas are generally unaffected. \ntarubali.baliprov.go.id\nHow does this impact existing villa developments? Grandfathering clause?\nNo broad grandfathering clause for illegal or non-compliant developments. Pre-existing legal (properly permitted, non-LP2B) structures are generally safe but may face audits. Illegal conversions on LP2B (even if built before the Perda) are at risk of stop-work orders, sealing, permit revocation, fines, demolition, and criminal probes. Recent examples (e.g., Jungle Padel in Badung’s Munggu and Subak Uma Canggu projects) show sealing attempts, though enforcement has been inconsistent (“tegas di atas kertas, tumpul di lapangan”). New or ongoing projects on LP2B face immediate freezing and heightened risk. \npawisikbali.com\nImpact on foreign investors looking at land in these areas.\nSevere negative impact. Nominee arrangements are now explicitly banned and prosecutable (both the foreign beneficiary and Indonesian nominees/facilitators). LP2B land cannot be converted for villa/hotel use, so development value is effectively zeroed out for non-agricultural purposes. Foreigners (via PMA companies) face stricter due diligence, potential permit denials, and criminal exposure. Many investors are shifting away from sawah-adjacent or “green” plots toward already-zoned commercial/tourism land.\nHow should property investment strategy change?  \nAvoid LP2B/sawah land entirely for any non-agricultural development (villas, hotels, etc.).  \nPrioritize land already zoned for tourism/commercial use in RDTR/KKPR (verify via official maps and provincial/kabupaten spatial plans).  \nConduct rigorous due diligence: confirm LP2B status (via provincial LP2B information system or Dinas Pertanian), Subak classification, and KKPR/RDTR compliance.  \nConsider long-term agricultural leases, eco-agritourism on permitted land, or non-LP2B areas.  \nFactor in incentives for keeping land as farmland (PBB tax reductions up to 100%, subsidies).  \nExpect slower permitting and higher compliance costs; focus on sustainable, low-impact projects. Overall, the regulation shifts Bali property investment toward regulated, non-agricultural zones and reduces speculative “sawah-to-villa” plays. \ninstagram.com\nAny enforcement cases so far?\nThe Perda is very recent (Feb 2026), so major criminal prosecutions are limited. Enforcement has focused on administrative actions: sealing of non-compliant projects (e.g., Jungle Padel in Badung and Subak Uma Canggu cases, which were sealed by DPRD Pansus TRAP but reportedly resumed in some instances). Broader “freezing” of permits and increased monitoring are in effect. Government rhetoric is strong (“pidana menanti pelanggar”), with readiness to pursue criminal cases, especially involving nominee schemes or large investors. Inconsistent field enforcement remains a noted criticism. \npawisikbali.com\nRelationship with KKPR/RDTR zoning.\nThe Perda directly reinforces and is integrated with RDTR (detailed spatial plans) and KKPR (Kesesuaian Kegiatan Pemanfaatan Ruang permits under Cipta Kerja framework). LP2B land cannot be rezoned or receive KKPR approval for non-agricultural use. Any proposed development must align with provincial/kabupaten RTRW/RDTR; the Perda adds a higher layer of prohibition and sanctions, including freezing conversions. It prevents local governments from approving changes that contradict LP2B designations.\nWhat about traditional Subak irrigation land?\nSubak systems (traditional cooperative rice irrigation, UNESCO World Heritage) are explicitly prioritized for protection. Most Subak land falls under LP2B or KP2B (kawasan) designations. The Perda treats conversion of Subak land as especially egregious because it threatens not just food production but cultural heritage and ecological balance. Additional cultural/heritage laws and provincial Subak revitalization programs reinforce this.\nPenalties for buyers vs sellers vs developers.\nThe Perda and referenced laws apply across the value chain without major differentiation, but practical focus is:  \nDevelopers/builders: Primary targets for conversion violations (stop-work, demolition, criminal charges).  \nBuyers/foreign beneficiaries (nominee users): Exposed to nominee ban penalties, permit revocation, and potential criminal liability (especially if they benefit from or direct the scheme).  \nSellers/Indonesian nominees/facilitators: Liable if they knowingly participate (administrative fines, criminal as accessories, loss of incentives).  \nOfficials/ASN: Disciplinary or criminal sanctions for facilitating violations.\nAll parties can face administrative sanctions; criminal liability often follows clear illegal conversion or fraud. \nlingkarinews.id\nBottom line: This is one of Bali’s strongest-ever tools to protect farmland and Subak heritage amid tourism pressure. It significantly raises risks and costs for sawah-based villa/hotel projects, especially those relying on nominee structures. Investors and developers should treat LP2B-designated land as effectively off-limits for non-agricultural development going forward.
