# Indonesia Visa Procedures: Apply, Renew, Convert, Exit (2026)

Most immigration problems in Indonesia are not caused by choosing the wrong visa type — they are caused by following the wrong procedure for the right visa. There is a meaningful difference between a fresh application, a renewal, a conversion, and an exit procedure, and using one when you needed another can reset your timeline entirely, void a permit already in progress, or trigger an overstay record that follows you on future applications.

This hub covers the four core procedures in Indonesia's immigration system. **Fresh Application** is for those entering on a new permit — either from abroad or through an in-country first-time application. **Renewal** extends a permit that is approaching its expiry date. **Conversion** changes the permit type while you are already inside Indonesia, typically from a short-stay visa such as C1 to a long-stay permit such as E23 or E28A. **Exit Procedure** covers voluntary departure, border runs, and Emergency Passage Orders — situations where leaving the country is part of the immigration strategy rather than the end of it.

The procedures are sequential within each path but do not cross over. You cannot renew a permit that has already expired — that becomes a fresh application. You cannot convert a permit that was not issued with conversion eligibility noted in its conditions. Understanding which gate you are standing at before you walk through it saves weeks and, not infrequently, hundreds of dollars in consultant fees and government penalties.

## Fresh Application {#fresh-application}

A fresh application is required when you have no current Indonesian permit and are either applying from your home country through a KBRI or KJRI, or entering Indonesia on a visa-on-arrival and initiating a long-stay permit from day one. The documents required vary significantly by permit type — an E23 Working KITAS requires RPTKA approval before the personal application can even open, while an E28A Investor KITAS requires the PT PMA deed and capital evidence. The most common mistake at this stage is arriving in Indonesia before the sponsor-side documents are ready, which wastes visa-free days and forces either an extension or an early exit. Start your sponsor paperwork at least 60 days before your intended entry date.

## Renewal {#renewal}

Renewal extends a permit that is still valid but approaching its expiry — the window to begin renewal is typically 30 to 60 days before the permit expires, depending on the permit type. The application is submitted through the same immigration office that issued the original permit, and the renewal does not change your permit category or your sponsor. Documents needed generally mirror the original application, updated to reflect the current period: new sponsor letter, updated financial statements for Retirement KITAS holders, or a fresh RPTKA extension confirmation for E23 holders. The critical mistake is waiting until the permit has expired — at that point, renewal is no longer available and a fresh application must be made, often requiring you to leave the country first.

## Conversion {#conversion}

Conversion changes your permit type while you remain inside Indonesia. The most common scenario is converting a C1 tourist visa or a Business Visit Visa to a long-stay permit such as an E23 or E28A. Not every permit is eligible for conversion — the original visa must carry a conversion notation in its issuance conditions, and certain visa categories explicitly prohibit in-country conversion. If conversion is permitted, it must begin before the current permit expires; there is no grace period. The timeline runs 30 to 45 days, during which your original permit remains valid. You should not leave Indonesia during an active conversion process unless your consultant has confirmed your re-entry will not void the application.

## Exit Procedure & Border Run {#exit-procedure--border-run}

An exit procedure is sometimes the right immigration move — not a workaround, but a deliberate strategic step. Border runs are used when a short-stay permit cannot be converted in-country and must be reset from zero: you exit, reapply at a KBRI or on arrival, and re-enter on a clean permit. Voluntary departure may be chosen when a permit situation has become complicated and a clean restart is preferable to contesting a status issue. Emergency Passage Orders (EPO) apply when deportation is imminent but the individual cooperates proactively with immigration authorities. The key misunderstanding here is treating a border run as a universal reset — it works for some permit categories and does nothing for others, and it does not clear a genuine overstay record.
