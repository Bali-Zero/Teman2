**Joint tax-immigration task force sealed 12 villas targeting nominee structures**

---

## The Facts

A joint task force comprising tax authorities and immigration officials has launched coordinated raids against PMA (foreign investment) companies suspected of using nominee arrangements for residential property ownership. The operation resulted in 12 villas being sealed in Pererenan, Badung Regency. The enforcement specifically targets companies allegedly using local Indonesian nominees to circumvent foreign property ownership restrictions while maintaining de facto control through undisclosed agreements. Social media reports from affected property owners indicate legal representatives are advising clients to delete communication records, suggesting extensive documentation scrutiny. The task force is focusing on residential freehold properties rather than commercial assets, distinguishing between legitimate business operations and circumvention schemes. This represents an escalation from sporadic enforcement to systematic audits combining tax compliance verification with immigration status checks.

---

## Bali Zero Take

### The Hidden Insight

This isn't random enforcement - it's a coordinated strategy targeting the intersection of tax compliance and immigration violations, suggesting authorities now have sophisticated methods to identify nominee structures.

### Our Analysis

The joint task force approach indicates authorities are cross-referencing property ownership patterns with visa status and tax filings to identify inconsistencies. PMAs holding residential property while directors hold tourist or social visas create obvious red flags. The focus on Pererenan, a high-density expat area, suggests this is a pilot program likely to expand to Canggu and Seminyak.

### Our Advice

If you're in a nominee structure, immediately audit your documentation for consistency between property ownership, visa status, and tax filings. Consider legitimate restructuring options before enforcement reaches your area.
