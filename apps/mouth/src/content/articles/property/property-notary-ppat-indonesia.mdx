## What Is a PPAT and Why Do You Need One?

If you are buying or selling property in Indonesia, one official stands between you and a legally recognized transfer: the **PPAT** (Pejabat Pembuat Akta Tanah), or Land Deed Official. Without a PPAT executing your transfer deed, ownership cannot change hands in the eyes of the law.

Indonesia's property system requires specific officials for specific tasks. A common source of confusion for foreign investors is the difference between a **notaris** (notary) and a **PPAT**. Many practitioners hold both titles, but their legal functions are entirely separate. Understanding who does what -- and when -- saves you money, time, and potential legal headaches.

This guide covers everything you need to know about PPAT and notary roles in Indonesian property transactions as of 2026, including the AJB process, fee structures, BPN registration, and practical tips for choosing the right professional in Bali.

## PPAT vs Notary: Understanding the Difference

### What a PPAT Does

A PPAT (Pejabat Pembuat Akta Tanah) is a government-appointed official authorized to create and execute **land deeds** (akta tanah). Their authority is limited to land and property matters:

- **AJB (Akta Jual Beli)** -- Sale and purchase deed for property transfers
- **APHT (Akta Pemberian Hak Tanggungan)** -- Mortgage/security deed
- **Akta Tukar Menukar** -- Exchange deed
- **Akta Hibah** -- Gift/grant deed
- **Akta Pembagian Hak Bersama** -- Joint rights division deed
- **Akta Pemasukan ke Perseroan** -- Deed of capital contribution (property into a company)

A PPAT is appointed by the Minister of Agrarian Affairs and Spatial Planning and must operate within a designated **jurisdictional area** (wilayah kerja), typically a kabupaten/kota (regency/city).

### What a Notary Does

A notaris (notary public) is authorized to create **authentic deeds** for general civil and commercial matters:

- Company establishment deeds (akta pendirian perusahaan)
- Powers of attorney (surat kuasa)
- Lease agreements (perjanjian sewa)
- Prenuptial agreements
- Wills and inheritance documents
- Loan agreements
- Sale and purchase binding agreements (PPJB)

### The Key Distinction

| Aspect                   | PPAT                               | Notary                             |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Appointing authority** | Minister of Agrarian Affairs       | Minister of Law and Human Rights   |
| **Scope**                | Land and property deeds only       | General civil and commercial deeds |
| **Jurisdiction**         | Limited to specific kabupaten/kota | Provincial-level jurisdiction      |
| **Property transfer**    | Can execute AJB (required)         | Cannot execute AJB                 |
| **Company deeds**        | Cannot create company deeds        | Authorized for company deeds       |
| **Governing law**        | PP 24/1997, PP 37/1998             | UU 2/2014 (Notary Law)             |
| **Regulatory body**      | BPN (Badan Pertanahan Nasional)    | Kemenkumham                        |

**Critical point:** Only a PPAT can execute the AJB that transfers property ownership at BPN. A notary alone cannot do this. However, a notary can create the preliminary binding agreement (PPJB) that precedes the AJB.

### Dual-License Practitioners (Notaris/PPAT)

In practice, most property transactions in Bali are handled by professionals who hold **both** the notaris and PPAT licenses. This is convenient because a typical property purchase involves:

1. **Notary function:** Drafting the PPJB (binding sale agreement) and any powers of attorney
2. **PPAT function:** Executing the AJB (deed of sale) and submitting to BPN

Using a single Notaris/PPAT office streamlines the process. However, you should understand which hat they are wearing at each stage, because the fee structures and legal responsibilities differ.

## The AJB Process: Step-by-Step Property Transfer

The **AJB (Akta Jual Beli)** is the definitive legal document that transfers property ownership in Indonesia. Here is the complete process:

### Step 1: Pre-AJB Due Diligence

Before the AJB can be executed, the PPAT must verify:

- **Certificate authenticity** -- Check the original certificate (SHM, SHGB, or SHP) against BPN records
- **No encumbrances** -- Verify no mortgages, liens, court orders, or disputes exist
- **Tax compliance** -- Confirm PBB (property tax) is paid up to date
- **Identity verification** -- Verify buyer and seller identities
- **Zoning compliance** -- Ensure the property use matches the designated zone
- **PPJB review** -- Review any existing binding agreement terms

**Cost for certificate check (pengecekan sertifikat) at BPN:** IDR 50,000 per certificate (government fee)

### Step 2: Tax Payments

Both buyer and seller must pay their respective taxes before AJB execution:

| Tax                                 | Who Pays              | Rate                      | Example (IDR 5B property) |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **BPHTB** (Buyer's transfer tax)    | Buyer                 | 5% of (NJOP - NPOPTKP)    | ~IDR 225,000,000          |
| **PPh Final** (Seller's income tax) | Seller                | 2.5% of transaction value | IDR 125,000,000           |
| **PBB** (Annual property tax)       | Seller (current year) | 0.1-0.3% of NJOP          | Varies                    |

**NPOPTKP** (tax-free threshold) varies by region. In Bali, it is typically IDR 80,000,000 for sale/purchase transactions.

**Example calculation for a property with NJOP of IDR 5 billion:**

- BPHTB = 5% x (IDR 5,000,000,000 - IDR 80,000,000) = IDR 246,000,000
- PPh Final = 2.5% x IDR 5,000,000,000 = IDR 125,000,000

### Step 3: AJB Signing

The AJB signing occurs at the PPAT's office with all parties present:

- **Seller** (or authorized representative with notarial power of attorney)
- **Buyer** (or authorized representative)
- **Two witnesses** (typically PPAT office staff)
- **The PPAT** who reads and explains the deed

The AJB contains:

- Full property description (location, boundaries, certificate number)
- Transaction price
- Payment confirmation
- Seller's declarations (no disputes, valid ownership)
- Buyer's acceptance
- Tax payment proof references

### Step 4: BPN Registration (Balik Nama)

After AJB signing, the PPAT submits documents to BPN for **balik nama** (name transfer) on the certificate:

**Timeline:** 5-14 working days (varies by BPN office workload)

**Documents submitted by PPAT to BPN:**

- Original AJB (one copy)
- Original property certificate
- Buyer and seller identity documents
- BPHTB payment proof (SSB)
- PPh payment proof (SSP)
- PBB payment proof (SPPT and STTS)
- Application form

**BPN registration fee:** Calculated based on property value, typically IDR 50,000-300,000

### Step 5: Certificate Collection

Once BPN completes the transfer, the certificate now bears the buyer's name. The PPAT or buyer collects the updated certificate from BPN.

**Total timeline from AJB to new certificate:** 2-6 weeks depending on BPN office

## How to Choose a PPAT in Bali

### Jurisdictional Requirements

A PPAT must be appointed for the specific **kabupaten/kota** where the property is located. In Bali:

| Property Location      | Required PPAT Jurisdiction |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Canggu, Seminyak, Kuta | Kabupaten Badung           |
| Ubud, Tegallalang      | Kabupaten Gianyar          |
| Uluwatu, Nusa Dua      | Kabupaten Badung           |
| Sanur, Denpasar city   | Kota Denpasar              |
| Amed, Karangasem       | Kabupaten Karangasem       |
| Lovina, Singaraja      | Kabupaten Buleleng         |
| Tabanan, Tanah Lot     | Kabupaten Tabanan          |

**Important:** A PPAT registered in Denpasar cannot execute an AJB for a property in Ubud (Gianyar). Make sure your PPAT has jurisdiction where your property is located.

### Selection Criteria

When choosing a PPAT for your property transaction in Bali, verify:

1. **Active PPAT license** -- Check with IPPAT (Ikatan Pejabat Pembuat Akta Tanah) or BPN
2. **Correct jurisdiction** -- Must cover the kabupaten where the property sits
3. **Experience with foreign buyers** -- Not all PPAT offices regularly handle Hak Pakai or PT PMA transactions
4. **Dual license (Notaris/PPAT)** -- More convenient for handling all documentation
5. **English capability** -- Critical for understanding complex terms
6. **BPN relationship** -- A PPAT with good working rapport at the local BPN office can expedite registration
7. **Transparent fee structure** -- Get a written fee quote before engaging
8. **References** -- Ask other expats or property agents for recommendations

### Red Flags to Watch For

- PPAT unwilling to show their appointment letter (SK Pengangkatan)
- Vague or unusually low fee quotes (may indicate hidden charges later)
- Pressure to skip due diligence steps
- Suggesting you use a nominee arrangement (this is illegal and risky)
- Unwilling to provide a written engagement letter
- Operating outside their jurisdictional area

## Special Considerations for Foreign Buyers

### Hak Pakai Transfers

Foreigners in Indonesia can hold **Hak Pakai** (Right to Use) title directly. The PPAT process for Hak Pakai is the same as for other titles, with additional requirements:

- Buyer must hold valid **KITAS** or **KITAP**
- Property must already have Hak Pakai status, or be converted from Hak Milik/HGB
- PPAT verifies foreigner eligibility before executing AJB
- Maximum Hak Pakai duration: 30 years, extendable to 20+20

### PT PMA Property Purchases

When a **PT PMA** (foreign-owned company) purchases property, the PPAT works with the company entity:

- Company deed (akta pendirian) and latest amendments required
- NIB (business identification number) showing property-related KBLI codes
- Director authorization or shareholder resolution
- Company tax ID (NPWP)
- BPHTB paid by the company

The PT PMA typically holds property under **HGB** (Hak Guna Bangunan) or **Hak Pakai** title.

### Power of Attorney for Remote Transactions

If you cannot attend the AJB signing in person, a **notarial power of attorney** (surat kuasa notariil) can authorize someone to sign on your behalf. Requirements:

- Must be a specific power of attorney (not general)
- Must reference the specific property and transaction
- If executed overseas, requires apostille or legalization by Indonesian embassy
- The PPAT may require the original, not a copy

## Common PPAT-Related Problems and Solutions

### Problem 1: Certificate Mismatch

**Issue:** The certificate details do not match the actual property boundaries.
**Solution:** Conduct a **pengukuran ulang** (re-measurement) at BPN before AJB. Cost: IDR 1,000,000-5,000,000 depending on land size.

### Problem 2: Unpaid PBB Arrears

**Issue:** Property tax (PBB) has not been paid for years.
**Solution:** All arrears must be settled before AJB. The PPAT will verify PBB payment status. Budget for potential back-taxes.

### Problem 3: Double Certificate

**Issue:** Two certificates exist for the same land parcel.
**Solution:** This requires BPN resolution and potentially court proceedings. A good PPAT will catch this during the certificate check stage. Do not proceed with AJB until resolved.

### Problem 4: Inheritance Property Without Waris Agreement

**Issue:** Seller inherited the property but heirs have not formally agreed to the sale.
**Solution:** All legal heirs must sign a **Surat Keterangan Waris** (inheritance declaration) and consent to the sale. The PPAT will require this before AJB.

### Problem 5: PPJB Without AJB Follow-Up

**Issue:** You signed a PPJB years ago but never completed the AJB.
**Solution:** The PPJB may have an expiration clause. Engage a PPAT to review the PPJB terms and complete the AJB. You may need to renegotiate if conditions have changed significantly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between PPAT and notary in Indonesia?

A PPAT is authorized to create land deeds (akta tanah) for property transfers, mortgages, and land rights changes. A notary (notaris) handles general legal documents like contracts, powers of attorney, and company deeds. Many professionals hold both titles (Notaris/PPAT), but for property transfers, the PPAT function is legally required.

### How much does a PPAT charge for property transfer in Indonesia?

PPAT fees typically range from 0.5% to 1% of the property transaction value, with a government-set maximum of 1%. For a property worth IDR 5 billion, expect PPAT fees of IDR 25-50 million. Additional costs include BPN registration fees, stamp duties, and administrative charges.

### Can a foreigner use a PPAT directly in Indonesia?

Yes, foreigners can engage a PPAT directly for Hak Pakai (Right to Use) property transfers. The PPAT will verify that the foreigner holds a valid KITAS/KITAP and that the property has the correct title status. For transactions involving PT PMA companies, the PPAT works with the company entity.
