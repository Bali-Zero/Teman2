# The End of Easy Villa Permits

For years, Bali's building permit system was... flexible. Green zone land got developed anyway. Permits materialized through connections. Villas sprouted on rice paddies.

That era is ending.

---

## The 30-Second Brief

<InfoCard variant="error" title="Current Situation">
- **Green zone permits** largely frozen
- **Enforcement** dramatically increased
- **Demolition orders** being issued for violations
- **Affected areas**: Canggu, North Kuta, Ubud outskirts, Sanur edges
- **Timeline**: Started late 2025, intensifying in 2026
</InfoCard>

---

## What's a Green Zone?

Bali's spatial planning (RTRW) divides land into zones:

<InfoCard variant="info" title="Zone Classifications">
- **Green Zone (RTH/Kawasan Lindung)**: Protected — agriculture, conservation, no construction
- **Yellow Zone**: Limited development — residential with restrictions
- **Orange/Red Zone**: Commercial and tourism development allowed
</InfoCard>

The problem: Much of Bali's most desirable land is zoned green. And for years, that didn't stop development.

---

## Why the Crackdown Now?

Several factors converging:

1. **New governor** with different priorities
2. **Environmental pressure** from flooding and water issues
3. **Rice field loss** reaching critical levels
4. **International attention** on Bali's overdevelopment
5. **Digital systems** making permit fraud harder

---

## Areas Most Affected

<InfoCard variant="warning" title="High-Risk Zones">
- **Canggu**: Berawa, Pererenan, Babakan
- **North Kuta**: Umalas, Kerobokan Kelod
- **Ubud**: Tegallalang, Kedewatan outskirts
- **Sanur**: Northern edges
- **Tabanan**: Coastal areas
</InfoCard>

If you're looking at land in these areas, verify zoning before doing anything.

---

## What This Means for Buyers

**Before buying any land:**

1. Get official zoning certificate (Keterangan Zonasi)
2. Verify IMB/PBG can actually be issued
3. Check if neighboring structures have proper permits
4. Research recent demolition cases in the area
5. Work with notaris who understands current enforcement

**Red flags:**
- Seller says "permit is being processed"
- Below-market prices in desirable areas
- No existing structures on "development ready" land
- Promises to "help with connections"

---

## The Bali Zero Take

The days of building whatever you want wherever you want are over. This doesn't mean property investment in Bali is dead — it means it requires more due diligence.

**The new reality:**
- Properly zoned land is more valuable
- Existing permitted structures are premium
- Risk assessment is essential before any purchase
- "Fixer" culture is becoming dangerous

---

## Your Next Steps

1. **Verify zoning first** — before any serious negotiation
2. **Get independent legal review** — not seller's notaris
3. **Check neighboring compliance** — one demolition can trigger others
4. **Factor risk into price** — green zone land should be cheaper
5. **Consider alternatives** — properly zoned areas exist
6. **Walk away if unclear** — there's always other land

Before purchasing land in Bali:
