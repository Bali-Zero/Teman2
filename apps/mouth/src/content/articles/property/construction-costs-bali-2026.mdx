## What Does It Cost to Build in Bali in 2026?

Bali's construction market in 2026 reflects rising material costs, increased demand from foreign investors, and tighter building regulations. Whether you are building a personal residence, a rental villa, or a boutique hospitality project, understanding the true cost of construction is essential for realistic budgeting.

The headline number -- IDR 6-15 million per square meter -- only tells part of the story. The actual cost of a turnkey villa includes architect fees, engineering, permits, site preparation, the pool, landscaping, furnishing, and a healthy contingency budget for the surprises that inevitably arise in tropical construction.

This guide breaks down every cost component, helps you choose the right professionals, and prepares you for the realities of building in Bali.

## Architect Selection and Fees

### Fee Structures

| Fee Model                           | Rate                         | Best For                 |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Percentage of construction cost** | 5-12%                        | Most villa projects      |
| **Flat fee**                        | IDR 30-100M+                 | Simple or small projects |

| **Hourly consultation**             | IDR 1,500,000-5,000,000/hour | Minor modifications      |

### What Architect Fees Cover

A standard architectural service package includes:

**Design Phase (40% of fee):**

- Site analysis and concept development
- Schematic design (floor plans, elevations, sections)
- 3D renderings and material mood boards
- Client review and revision rounds (typically 3 included)
- Design development drawings

**Technical Phase (30% of fee):**

- Detailed construction drawings
- Structural coordination with engineer
- MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing) coordination
- Material specifications
- PBG submission drawings

**Supervision Phase (30% of fee):**

- Construction supervision (weekly or bi-weekly site visits)
- Material approval
- Progress monitoring
- Contractor coordination
- Final inspection and punch list

### Choosing an Architect in Bali

Bali has a thriving architectural community with both local and international firms. Consider:

- **Indonesian licensed architect (IAI member)** -- Required for PBG submission
- **Tropical design expertise** -- Critical for Bali's climate (cross-ventilation, humidity management, rain protection)
- **Portfolio relevance** -- Have they built similar projects? Ask for site visits to completed work
- **Contractor relationships** -- Experienced Bali architects know reliable contractors
- **Budget realism** -- A good architect designs within your budget, not their fantasy

**Tip:** Many foreign architects practice in Bali without an IAI license. They can design your villa, but the PBG submission must be stamped by a licensed Indonesian architect. This adds cost and complexity.

## PBG Permit: The New Building Permit System

### What Is PBG?

The **PBG (Persetujuan Bangunan Gedung)** replaced the old IMB (Izin Mendirikan Bangunan) system under **PP 16/2021**. It is a mandatory approval before starting any new construction, renovation, or building expansion.

### PBG Application Process

| Step                               | Timeline         | Cost (IDR)                 |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------- |
| **1. Document preparation**        | 1-2 weeks        | Included in architect fees |
| **2. Online submission via SIMBG** | 1 day            | Free                       |
| **3. Technical review**            | 1-2 weeks        | Free                       |
| **4. Retribusi payment**           | 1-3 days         | 3,000,000 - 12,000,000     |
| **5. PBG issuance**                | 1-5 days         | Included in retribusi      |
| **6. SLF (post-construction)**     | After completion | 2,000,000 - 5,000,000      |

**Total PBG cost:** IDR 5,000,000 - 15,000,000 depending on building size and location

### Required Documents for PBG

- Land certificate (SHM, SHGB, or Hak Pakai)
- Site plan (gambar situasi)
- Architectural drawings (signed by licensed architect)
- Structural calculations (signed by structural engineer)
- MEP drawings
- Environmental assessment (for larger buildings)
- Proof of land ownership or lease agreement
- KTP/KITAS of the owner
- NPWP (tax ID)
- Recommendation from local banjar (village council)

### Zoning and Building Regulations in Bali

| Regulation                        | Typical Requirement                                   |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **KDB (Building Coverage Ratio)** | 40-60% of land area                                   |
| **KLB (Floor Area Ratio)**        | 0.8-1.6 depending on zone                             |
| **Maximum height**                | 15 meters (approximately 3 stories)                   |
| **Setbacks**                      | 3-6 meters from property boundaries                   |
| **Green area**                    | Minimum 20-40% of land area                           |
| **Bali traditional architecture** | Some areas require traditional Balinese roof elements |

**Important:** Bali has specific regulations requiring elements of traditional Balinese architecture in certain zones. Your architect should be familiar with local requirements (Perda Provinsi Bali about building character).

## Contractor Selection

### Types of Contractors

| Type                             | Best For                             | Price Level | Risk Level  |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------- | ----------- |
| **Large construction company**   | Commercial projects, luxury villas   | Highest     | Lowest      |
| **Mid-size contractor**          | Standard villas, multi-unit projects | Mid-high    | Low-medium  |
| **Small contractor (pemborong)** | Budget projects, simple builds       | Low-mid     | Medium-high |
| **Daily labor (harian)**         | Small renovations, owner-supervised  | Lowest      | Highest     |

### Contractor Payment Structures

**Progress-based (recommended):**

- 20% upon contract signing (uang muka)
- 20% at foundation completion
- 20% at structural completion (roof)
- 20% at finishing stage
- 15% at practical completion
- 5% retention (released after 3-6 month defect liability period)

**Never pay more than 20% upfront.** This is the single most important rule in Bali construction.

### How to Vet a Contractor

1. **Visit completed projects** -- Not just photos; go to actual buildings they have completed
2. **Talk to previous clients** -- Both recent and from 2-3 years ago (to check for defects)
3. **Check legal status** -- Is the contractor a registered company (PT or CV)?
4. **Verify insurance** -- Workers' compensation (BPJS Ketenagakerjaan) for all workers
5. **Review past contracts** -- Ask for a sample contract to review
6. **Check equipment** -- A serious contractor owns basic equipment, not renting everything
7. **Assess workforce** -- Are they using their own team or subcontracting everything?

### Contract Must-Haves

Your construction contract should include:

- Fixed price or maximum guaranteed price with detailed BOQ (Bill of Quantities)
- Construction timeline with milestones
- Payment schedule tied to completed milestones
- Penalty clause for delays (0.1-0.5% of contract value per week, typical)
- Material specifications (brands, grades, quantities)
- Change order process and pricing
- Warranty period (minimum 12 months recommended)
- Dispute resolution mechanism
- Insurance requirements
- Site cleanliness and safety obligations
- Force majeure clause (particularly relevant for rainy season)

### Rainy Season Impact

Bali's rainy season (November through March) affects construction:

- **Foundation work** -- Can be delayed by waterlogged soil
- **Concrete pouring** -- Cannot pour during heavy rain; 1-2 day delays common
- **Roof installation** -- Dangerous in heavy rain and wind
- **Painting** -- Humidity affects drying and adhesion
- **Site access** -- Muddy roads can delay material delivery

**Budget tip:** Add 1-2 months to your timeline if construction spans the rainy season. Some owners start construction in April to catch the dry season for critical phases.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to build a villa in Bali per square meter?

In 2026, construction costs in Bali range from IDR 6 million/sqm (basic local quality) to IDR 15 million/sqm (premium international standard). Mid-range quality suitable for rental villas is IDR 8-10 million/sqm. These are construction-only costs; add 15-25% for architect fees, permits, pool, landscaping, and furnishing.

### Do I need a PBG permit to build in Bali?

Yes. The PBG (Persetujuan Bangunan Gedung) replaced the old IMB system under PP 16/2021. All new construction requires a PBG before building starts. The application is done through the SIMBG online system. Processing takes 2-4 weeks. Cost varies by building size, typically IDR 5-15 million including retribusi.

### How long does it take to build a villa in Bali?

A standard 2-bedroom villa (150-200 sqm) takes 8-12 months from foundation to handover. Add 2-3 months for design and permitting, plus 1-2 months for furnishing and landscaping. Total timeline from concept to move-in: 12-18 months. Rainy season (November-March) can add 1-2 months to construction.
