## The Facts

Indonesia has emerged as the dominant digital economy in Southeast Asia, a position its government and business leaders are actively working to consolidate through artificial intelligence adoption. The country's digital economy is forecast to reach hundreds of billions of dollars in gross merchandise value over the coming decade, with AI expected to be a core accelerant across sectors including e-commerce, fintech, logistics, agriculture, and healthcare.

The archipelago nation benefits from structural advantages that make it a compelling AI market: a population of over 280 million, more than 200 million internet users, a young demographic skew, and rapidly expanding mobile infrastructure. Indonesia ranks among the top five countries globally for social media usage and smartphone penetration, providing the data substrate that AI systems require to function and improve.

Government policy has increasingly aligned with the AI opportunity. Indonesia's national AI strategy, first formalised in 2020 and updated subsequently, targets investment in AI research, talent development, and the deployment of AI tools across public services. Several ministries have piloted AI-powered systems for permitting, tax administration, and citizen services, with mixed but improving results.

The private sector has moved aggressively. Indonesian tech unicorns including Gojek (now GoTo), Tokopedia, and Traveloka have embedded machine learning into their core products for years. A newer generation of AI-native startups is attracting venture capital from Singapore, the United States, and increasingly from the Gulf states, which view Indonesia as a strategic foothold in the Global South.

Bali plays a distinct role in this ecosystem. The island has evolved beyond its tourism identity to become a hub for digital nomads, remote workers, and tech entrepreneurs drawn by lifestyle quality, relative affordability, and proximity to the broader Southeast Asian market. Co-working spaces, startup communities, and international school networks have expanded substantially since 2022, reflecting sustained inflows of foreign digital talent.
