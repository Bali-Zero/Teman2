## The Facts

Digital Edge, a pan-Asian data center operator backed by Stonepeak Infrastructure Partners, has announced plans to develop what would be Indonesia's largest AI-optimized data center, with a projected investment of Rp10 trillion — approximately $620 million USD at current exchange rates. The facility is intended to serve as a cornerstone of Indonesia's national digital infrastructure strategy.

The project is part of a broader wave of hyperscale data center investment flooding into Indonesia. The country has attracted significant commitments from global technology giants in recent months, including announcements from Microsoft, Google, and Amazon Web Services, all of whom have pledged multi-billion dollar investments in Indonesian cloud and AI infrastructure. Digital Edge's announcement positions it within this competitive but expanding market.

AI-optimized data centers differ from conventional facilities in their hardware requirements. They are built to handle the intensive parallel processing demands of machine learning workloads, requiring specialized GPU clusters, high-density power delivery, and advanced cooling systems. The Rp10 trillion facility would represent a significant upgrade to Indonesia's current data center capacity, which has historically lagged behind regional peers such as Singapore and Malaysia.

Indonesia's government has actively encouraged this investment through its national digital transformation agenda. Regulations introduced in recent years require data localization for certain categories of data generated by Indonesian users, creating structural demand for domestic data center capacity. Foreign companies operating in Indonesia — including those using Indonesian legal entities to run digital businesses — are subject to these rules.

The specific location and operational timeline for the Digital Edge facility have not been publicly confirmed at the time of this report. However, greater Jakarta and Batam remain the dominant hubs for data center development in the archipelago, given their proximity to submarine cable landing stations and existing power infrastructure. Bali, while growing as a tech hub, does not currently host hyperscale data center operations.
