## The Facts

Bali has consistently ranked among the world's top destinations for expatriates, digital nomads, and long-term travelers for over two decades. The island's appeal cuts across demographics — from retirees seeking affordable living to younger remote workers drawn by co-working infrastructure, reliable internet in tourist corridors, and a well-established foreign community.

The cost of living in Bali remains significantly lower than most Western countries, with a comfortable lifestyle accessible at a fraction of the cost in cities like Sydney, London, or New York. Monthly living expenses for a single person, including accommodation in a mid-range villa, food, transport, and leisure, typically range from USD 1,200 to USD 2,500, depending on lifestyle choices and location within the island.

Bali's cultural richness — daily Hindu ceremonies, traditional arts, and a distinctive spiritual atmosphere — is frequently cited as a differentiating factor from other Southeast Asian destinations. Unlike purely commercial beach resorts, Bali's cultural calendar and local community engagement provide a sense of depth that encourages longer stays and return visits.

Infrastructure investment has also accelerated. Ngurah Rai International Airport handles direct routes from major hubs including Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Tokyo, Sydney, and increasingly, European cities. Connectivity improvements in Canggu, Seminyak, Ubud, and Sanur have made remote working more viable, reinforcing the island's position as a hub for the growing global remote workforce.

Indonesia's introduction of the Second Home Visa in 2022 and the Digital Nomad Visa — the latter still evolving in regulatory clarity — has formalized pathways for longer legal stays, signaling government recognition of the economic value of this demographic. The Bali provincial government has separately pursued tourism quality improvement policies aimed at attracting higher-spending, longer-staying visitors over high-volume budget tourism.
