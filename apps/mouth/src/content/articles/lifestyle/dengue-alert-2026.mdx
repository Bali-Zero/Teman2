# Dengue Alert: What You Need to Know

Bali's dengue numbers are climbing. 636 cases have been reported so far this year, and we're still in the early months.

This isn't a panic article. It's a preparation guide.

---

## The 30-Second Brief

<InfoCard variant="error" title="Current Situation">
  - **636 cases** reported in Bali (January 2026) - **Rainy season** increases
  mosquito breeding - **All areas** affected, not just rural zones - **No
  vaccine** widely available for travelers - **Insurance** is your primary
  protection
</InfoCard>

---

## Symptoms to Watch For

Dengue symptoms typically appear 4-10 days after being bitten:

<InfoCard variant="warning" title="Warning Signs">
  - High fever (40°C/104°F) - Severe headache - Pain behind the eyes - Muscle
  and joint pain - Nausea and vomiting - Skin rash (appears 2-5 days after
  fever) - Mild bleeding (nose or gums)
</InfoCard>

**Severe dengue** (dengue hemorrhagic fever) can be life-threatening. Seek immediate medical attention if symptoms worsen after fever subsides.

---

## Prevention Basics

1. **Use mosquito repellent** — DEET-based products work best
2. **Cover up** — long sleeves during dawn/dusk peak hours
3. **Eliminate standing water** — check your property weekly
4. **Use screens and nets** — especially in bedrooms
5. **Air conditioning** — mosquitoes don't thrive in cold, dry air

---

## Insurance: Non-Negotiable

<InfoCard variant="info" title="Coverage Requirements">
Your health insurance MUST cover:
- **Hospitalization** for dengue (can be 5-7 days)
- **ICU care** for severe cases
- **Blood transfusions** if needed
- **Medical evacuation** to Singapore if required

**Check your policy limits.** Dengue treatment can cost $5,000-$20,000 USD depending on severity.

</InfoCard>

## The Bali Zero Take

Dengue is endemic to Bali. It's not going away. The question isn't "if" you'll be exposed, but whether you're prepared when you are.

**The smart approach:**

- Assume you'll eventually get it
- Have proper insurance in place
- Know where the good hospitals are
- Don't ignore early symptoms
- Keep your property mosquito-free

---

## Your Next Steps

1. **Review your insurance** — confirm dengue coverage and limits
2. **Stock prevention supplies** — repellent, screens, nets
3. **Know your nearest hospital** — BIMC, Siloam, or Kasih Ibu
4. **Save emergency contacts** — your insurance hotline, doctor
5. **Brief your family** — everyone should know the symptoms
