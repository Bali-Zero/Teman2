## The Facts

Bali's immigration directorate has unveiled the Dharma Dewata Immigration Patrol Force, a purpose-built unit designed to conduct active, on-the-ground monitoring of foreign nationals across the island's most densely visited areas. The force will operate across four primary zones: Ubud, the island's cultural heartland; Seminyak and Canggu, favored by the digital nomad and lifestyle expatriate community; and Kuta, the traditional mass-tourism hub.

Officials framing the initiative have emphasized twin objectives: cultural preservation and tourist safety. The cultural protection angle addresses long-standing concerns about foreign visitors behaving disrespectfully at sacred sites — including documented incidents of nudity at temples, inappropriate conduct at cremation ceremonies, and social media stunts on holy grounds. The safety dimension is positioned as a response to a rise in tourist-related incidents, scams targeting foreigners, and informal economic activity operating outside legal frameworks.

The Dharma Dewata unit is distinct from general police or the existing civil service police (Satpol PP) in that it operates under immigration authority, giving it explicit jurisdiction over visa status, work permit verification, and foreign national conduct. This is operationally significant: the unit can detain individuals for immigration checks, not merely issue warnings or refer to other agencies.

Enforcement is expected to concentrate on known hotspots where informal foreign-run commercial activity has proliferated — wellness studios, surf schools, villa rental operations, and food and beverage outlets in Canggu and Seminyak in particular. Indonesian law prohibits foreigners from working without a valid work permit (KITAS/KITAP with work authorization) regardless of business ownership structure.

The announcement follows a series of high-profile deportations over the past 18 months targeting foreigners working illegally in Bali, and represents an institutionalization of what had previously been sporadic crackdown operations. By establishing a named, standing force rather than conducting periodic raids, authorities are signaling that elevated scrutiny is now the default operational posture, not the exception.
