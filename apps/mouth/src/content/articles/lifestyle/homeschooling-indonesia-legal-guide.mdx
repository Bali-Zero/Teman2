## Homeschooling in Indonesia: A Fully Legal Option

Homeschooling is legal, recognized, and increasingly popular in Indonesia. Under **Permendikbud No 129/2014**, the Ministry of Education classifies homeschooling as _pendidikan informal_ (informal education) conducted by the family. This is not a gray area or a legal loophole. It is an explicitly recognized form of education under Indonesian law.

For expat families in Bali, homeschooling offers a compelling alternative to both expensive international schools and the language challenges of Indonesian national schools. You maintain curriculum flexibility, schedule freedom, and the ability to travel while ensuring your children receive a quality education.

This guide covers the complete legal framework, registration process, curriculum options, equivalency exams, and the growing homeschooling community in Bali.

## The Legal Framework: What the Law Actually Says

Indonesian education law establishes three pathways:

| Category             | Indonesian Term      | Examples                         | Legal Basis            |
| -------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Formal education     | Pendidikan formal    | Public schools, private schools  | UU No 20/2003, Art. 14 |
| Non-formal education | Pendidikan nonformal | Learning centers (PKBM), courses | UU No 20/2003, Art. 26 |
| Informal education   | Pendidikan informal  | Homeschooling, family education  | UU No 20/2003, Art. 27 |

The key legislation is **UU No 20/2003** (National Education System Law), which in Article 27 states that informal education conducted by the family and environment is recognized and its results can be equated with formal education after passing equivalency assessments.

**Permendikbud No 129/2014** further regulates homeschooling specifically, establishing:

- Parents/families have the right to conduct education at home
- Homeschooled students can take equivalency exams (Kejar Paket)
- Results of informal education are recognized upon passing equivalency assessments
- Registration with Dinas Pendidikan is required

### Does This Apply to Foreign Families?

Yes. The regulations do not distinguish between Indonesian and foreign families. If you are legally resident in Indonesia (holding a KITAS or KITAP), you have the same right to homeschool your children. The key requirement is registration, not citizenship.

## How to Register Your Homeschool

Registration with **Dinas Pendidikan** (Education Office) at the kabupaten/kota level legitimizes your homeschool under Indonesian law.

### Step-by-Step Registration Process

1. **Visit your local Dinas Pendidikan office**
   - In Bali: Dinas Pendidikan Kabupaten Badung (for Canggu, Kuta areas), Dinas Pendidikan Kota Denpasar, or the relevant kabupaten office for your area
   - Bring a translator if your Bahasa Indonesia is limited

2. **Submit the required documents:**
   - Parent's KITAS and passport (copies)
   - Child's KITAS and passport (copies)
   - Birth certificate (translated to Bahasa Indonesia)
   - Statement of intent to homeschool (Surat Pernyataan)
   - Proposed learning plan or curriculum outline
   - Proof of address (SKTT or Surat Domisili)

3. **Receive acknowledgment letter**
   - Dinas Pendidikan issues a Surat Keterangan Terdaftar (Registration Certificate)
   - This document confirms your homeschool is registered

4. **Annual reporting** (recommended)
   - Submit annual progress reports to maintain active registration
   - This demonstrates ongoing educational activity

### Practical Reality

In practice, the registration process is straightforward but may require patience. Not all Dinas Pendidikan offices are experienced with homeschool registrations, particularly from foreign families. Bring all documents, be polite and persistent, and expect 1-3 visits to complete the process.

Some expat families in Bali choose to register through a **PKBM** (Pusat Kegiatan Belajar Masyarakat, Community Learning Center) rather than directly with Dinas Pendidikan. A PKBM acts as an umbrella organization that handles registration and provides access to Kejar Paket exams. This is often the easier path.

## Kejar Paket: The Equivalency Exam System

**Kejar Paket** is Indonesia's national equivalency examination program, and it is the critical bridge between homeschooling and formal recognition within the Indonesian education system.

### The Three Levels

| Kejar Paket Level | Equivalent To               | Age Requirement  | Exam Subjects                                                              |
| ----------------- | --------------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Paket A           | SD (Elementary, Grade 6)    | Minimum 12 years | Bahasa Indonesia, Math, Science, Social Studies, Civic Education, Religion |
| Paket B           | SMP (Junior High, Grade 9)  | Minimum 15 years | Above + English, Arts/Culture                                              |
| Paket C           | SMA (Senior High, Grade 12) | Minimum 18 years | Core subjects + elective track (IPA/Science or IPS/Social)                 |

### Why Kejar Paket Matters

A **Paket C certificate (ijazah)** is legally equivalent to an SMA diploma. This means:

- Your child can apply to Indonesian universities (UI, ITB, UGM, Udayana, etc.)
- The qualification is recognized by Indonesian employers
- It serves as proof of completed secondary education for government purposes
- It can be apostilled for international use

### How to Access Kejar Paket Exams

Kejar Paket exams are administered through **PKBM** (Community Learning Centers) or **SKB** (Sanggar Kegiatan Belajar, Learning Activity Centers). To register:

1. Contact a local PKBM in your area
2. Register as a Kejar Paket participant
3. Attend preparatory sessions (optional but recommended)
4. Sit for the examination during the scheduled exam period (typically twice per year)
5. Receive your ijazah upon passing

In Bali, several PKBMs serve the expat community and are experienced with foreign students taking Kejar Paket exams.

### Exam Preparation Tips

Kejar Paket exams follow the Indonesian national curriculum, so preparation is essential:

- Study past exam papers (soal-soal ujian) available online
- Hire an Indonesian tutor familiar with the Kejar Paket format
- Join a PKBM preparation class 3-6 months before the exam
- Focus heavily on Bahasa Indonesia, as all questions are in Indonesian
- For Paket C, choose between the IPA (science) or IPS (social science) track based on your child's strengths

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Not registering with Dinas Pendidikan**: While enforcement is minimal, having official registration protects you legally and enables Kejar Paket access
2. **Ignoring Bahasa Indonesia**: Even if using an English curriculum, your child benefits enormously from Bahasa Indonesia fluency for daily life in Indonesia
3. **Isolating your children socially**: Proactively build a social schedule through co-ops, sports, and community activities
4. **Forgetting about equivalency**: If you plan to stay in Indonesia long-term, ensure your child can access Kejar Paket for recognized qualifications
5. **Over-scheduling**: Bali's abundance of activities can lead to burnout. Maintain a balanced schedule with downtime

## Is Homeschooling Right for Your Family?

Homeschooling in Indonesia works best when:

- You want curriculum flexibility and personalized pacing
- Your family travels frequently within Southeast Asia
- You want to avoid the high cost of international schools
- Your child has specific learning needs not well-served by local schools
- You want to combine formal academics with Bali's experiential learning opportunities
- You are committed to providing consistent structure and socialization

It may not be ideal if both parents work full-time with no flexibility, if you prefer a completely hands-off approach to education, or if your child thrives in a traditional classroom environment.
