# How to Get a Driving License in Bali as a Foreigner

Renting a scooter in Bali is easy. Renting one *legally* is a different story.

In 2024, Bali police recorded 142 traffic accidents involving foreigners — a 35% increase from the previous year, with 21 fatalities. The response has been swift: expanded camera enforcement, targeted police operations in tourist areas, and a clear message from authorities that unlicensed driving will no longer be tolerated.

This guide explains exactly how the system works, what you need to drive legally, and why it matters far more than most people realize.

## The Two Paths: Residents vs. Tourists

Before anything else, you need to understand one fundamental rule:

**If you have a KITAS or KITAP** (residence permit), you can convert your foreign license to an Indonesian SIM. This guide walks you through the entire process.

**If you are a tourist** (visa on arrival, e-VOA, visa exemption, or B211 social visa), you **cannot** get an Indonesian driving license. Your only legal option is an International Driving Permit (IDP) — more on this below.

This is not a gray area. It is the law, and since 2025, the systems that enforce it have become significantly more sophisticated.

## What Is a SIM and Which One Do You Need?

SIM stands for *Surat Izin Mengemudi* — literally, your driving permit. Two types matter for foreigners:

- **SIM A** — for cars, SUVs, and vehicles up to 3,500 kg
- **SIM C** — for motorcycles and scooters (the one most people in Bali need)

If you want both, you apply for each one separately — two sets of documents, two fees, two processes. But you can do both on the same day at the same police station.

The SIM C has recently been subdivided into SIM C (under 250cc), SIM C1 (250-500cc), and SIM C2 (over 500cc), though most Bali police stations still issue the standard SIM C as of early 2026.

## Documents You Need (The Full List)

Gather everything before you go. Missing one document means coming back another day. Here is the complete list:

1. **Passport** — original plus photocopies of the identity page, visa page, and latest entry stamp
2. **KITAS or KITAP** — original plus photocopy
3. **Your foreign driving license** — must be current and valid. If it is not in English or Indonesian, you will need a sworn translation (*penerjemah tersumpah*)
4. **SKTT** (*Surat Keterangan Tempat Tinggal*) — your domicile certificate from the local civil registry (*Dukcapil*). This document links you to a specific address in Bali
5. **STM** (*Surat Tanda Melapor*) — police registration certificate confirming you have reported your presence to the local police station
6. **BPJS Kesehatan proof** — active membership in Indonesia's national health insurance program. This became mandatory for all SIM applications under Perpol No. 2/2023
7. **Passport photos** — 3x4 cm with red background (for the SKTT; the SIM photo is taken at the station)

If you do not yet have BPJS, you can register at dedicated counters available at most Satpas offices. If you do not have the SKTT, you will need to visit your local Dukcapil office first — this can take a separate trip.

## The Process, Step by Step

The entire conversion can be done in a single visit of 2-3 hours, provided your documents are ready. Here is how it works in practice:

### Step 1: Psychology Test

Before entering the police station, you visit the psychology testing office — usually a small building nearby (at Polresta Denpasar, it is about 300 meters to the right of the main entrance).

The test is a short questionnaire (about 20 questions) assessing your temperament and behavior in stressful driving situations. English versions are available at some locations. It takes 15-30 minutes.

**Cost:** Rp 60,000 - 100,000 per license type.

### Step 2: Medical Examination

Next door or nearby, a small clinic checks your blood pressure, vision, and color perception. Quick and straightforward — about 5-10 minutes.

**Cost:** Rp 25,000 - 50,000. If applying for both SIM A and SIM C, ask for two copies.

### Step 3: Registration at the Satpas

Enter the Satpas (*Satuan Penyelenggara Administrasi SIM* — the police driving license center). Arrive before 9:00 AM for the shortest queues.

**Important:** there is a dress code. Long trousers are required — no shorts, no sleeveless shirts, no beach attire. Closed shoes are recommended. Some stations do not allow blue clothing (it matches the photo background).

Submit your documents at the registration counter, fill out the application form, and receive a queue number. Some stations have a dedicated window for foreigners (*WNA*).

### Step 4: Pay the Government Fee

Pay at the Bank BRI counter inside or near the station. These are the official fees under PP 76/2020 — the same across all of Indonesia:

| License | New Issue | Renewal |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| SIM A (car) | Rp 120,000 | Rp 80,000 |
| SIM C (motorcycle) | Rp 100,000 | Rp 75,000 |

### Step 5: Theory Test

This is the part most foreigners worry about. The computer-based test presents 30 questions covering traffic signs, road rules, and safe driving behavior. You need 21 correct answers to pass.

The honest truth: the test is primarily in Bahasa Indonesia. English availability is inconsistent, even at Polresta Denpasar. You are generally allowed to bring a translator or a bilingual friend to help. Topics are practical — traffic signs, right of way, basic safety rules. Study materials are available at [korlantas.polri.go.id](https://korlantas.polri.go.id).

If you do not pass, you can retake the test after 7 days.

### Step 6: Practical Test (Usually Waived)

Here is the good news: **if you are converting a valid foreign driving license, the practical driving test is generally waived.** Multiple reports from Polresta Denpasar in 2024 and 2025 confirm this. Your existing license demonstrates driving competence.

If you are applying without a foreign license (first-time), the practical test involves cone courses, slalom, and the notorious figure-eight maneuver for motorcycles, or parking and reversing for cars.

### Step 7: Photo and Biometrics

Digital photograph, fingerprint scan, and signature capture. This takes a few minutes.

### Step 8: Your SIM Is Printed

The card is printed and handed to you the same day. From arrival to SIM in hand: 2-3 hours if everything goes smoothly.

## For Tourists: The IDP Rules

If you are visiting Bali on a tourist visa, here is what you need to know:

**You need two documents to drive legally:**
1. Your **original driving license** from your home country
2. An **International Driving Permit (IDP)** — obtained in your home country before traveling

An IDP is not a license. It is a certified translation of your existing license into multiple languages. It has **no legal standing on its own** — you must always carry both documents together.

### The Motorcycle Trap

This is where most tourists get caught: **your IDP only covers the vehicle categories on your home license.**

If your home country license is for cars only — which is the default in most Western countries — your IDP does **not** authorize you to ride a scooter in Indonesia. To legally ride a motorcycle, your original license must include a motorcycle endorsement:

- **USA:** "M" endorsement
- **UK:** Category A
- **Australia:** R-class
- **EU:** Category A or A2

Without the correct endorsement, riding a scooter with a car-only IDP is legally the same as riding without any license at all.

### Where to Get an IDP

Apply through your country's authorized automobile association before you travel:

- **USA:** AAA
- **UK:** Post Office or AA
- **Australia:** RACV, NRMA, or your state automobile club
- **EU:** Your national automobile club

Cost is typically $15-30 USD. Processing takes a few days to a few weeks depending on the country.

Be careful with online services advertising "international driving licenses" — many are scams selling unofficial documents with no legal value.

### ASEAN Citizens: A Special Advantage

Since June 1, 2025, citizens of ASEAN member countries (Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Vietnam, Philippines, and others) can drive in Indonesia with their domestic license alone for up to 90 days. No IDP needed. This is under the ASEAN mutual recognition agreement based on the 1985 Kuala Lumpur Agreement.

## Why This Matters: The Real Risks

Some people read all of this and think: "I will just risk it. The fine is only Rp 200,000 at a checkpoint."

The fine is the smallest of your problems.

### Insurance Invalidation

This is the risk that can ruin your finances. Every major travel insurance provider — World Nomads, Allianz, AXA, 1Cover, and others — includes a clause requiring compliance with local traffic laws. If you are involved in an accident while driving without a valid license or IDP:

- Your insurer **will deny the claim**
- Medical evacuation from Bali costs **$50,000 - $100,000 USD**
- Serious motorcycle trauma treatment can exceed **$200,000 USD**
- Even passengers may be uncovered if the driver is unlicensed

That Rp 100,000 SIM C — or the $15 IDP — looks very different against a potential six-figure medical bill with no insurance backing.

### Legal Consequences

Under Article 281 of UU 22/2009, driving without a valid license carries:
- Fines up to **Rp 1,000,000**
- Up to **4 months imprisonment**
- Your vehicle may be impounded

In accident cases, unlicensed drivers are almost always found at fault regardless of actual circumstances, and criminal charges can escalate to negligent injury or manslaughter.

### Electronic Enforcement Is Expanding

Bali's ETLE camera network is growing rapidly. As of late 2025, 40 cameras are operational, with expansion to 500 cameras ordered by the national traffic police chief. These cameras issue automated fines tied to license plates — no roadside interaction needed. The systems are now linked to immigration databases, meaning unpaid fines can flag you at airport departure checkpoints.

## Where to Go in Bali

### Polresta Denpasar

**Address:** Jl. Gunung Sanghyang No. 110, Denpasar Barat

The largest and most foreigner-friendly Satpas in Bali. Covers Denpasar, Kuta, and South Bali. Staff have extensive experience with foreign applications. This is where most visa agencies direct their clients.

**Hours:** Monday-Saturday, 08:00-15:00 WITA. Friday hours may be shortened. Closed Sundays and holidays.

### Polres Gianyar

**Address:** Jl. Ngurah Rai No. 6, Gianyar

Serves Ubud, Tegalalang, and surrounding areas. Smaller, less crowded, generally faster. The theory test may be Indonesian-only, but officers are reported to help with translation.

### Polres Badung

Covers Canggu, northern Kerobokan, and Mengwi. About 20 minutes north of Canggu. Despite being geographically convenient for many expats, most sources still recommend Polresta Denpasar for foreign applications.

**Tip:** Tuesday through Thursday are the least crowded days at all locations. Arrive before 9:00 AM.

**Important:** SIM Keliling (mobile renewal units) that you may see in parking lots and public spaces are for Indonesian citizens only. Foreigners cannot use them, even for renewals.

## What About Tourists Without Proper Documents?

If you are a tourist without an IDP (or without the correct motorcycle endorsement), you have practical alternatives that are safer, legal, and often more enjoyable:

- **Grab and Gojek** — motorcycle and car rides for Rp 8,000-25,000 for short trips
- **Private driver with car** — approximately $30-40 USD per day, perfect for temple tours and day trips
- **Bluebird taxis** — reliable metered service across Bali
- **Organized tours** — most include transportation

These options eliminate all licensing, insurance, and liability risk entirely.
