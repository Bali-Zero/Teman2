## Studying in Indonesia: An Affordable and Enriching Option

Indonesia is home to over 4,000 universities and higher education institutions, several of which rank among the best in Southeast Asia. For foreign students, studying in Indonesia offers an unusual combination: world-class education at a fraction of Western tuition costs, deep cultural immersion, and access to one of the world's most dynamic economies.

Whether you are pursuing a full degree, a semester exchange, or a cultural immersion program through the DARMASISWA scholarship, this guide covers everything you need to know about studying at Indonesian universities as a foreigner in 2026.

### Universitas Udayana: The Bali Option

For those already living in or planning to move to Bali, **Universitas Udayana** (UNUD) in Denpasar is the natural choice. Founded in 1962, UNUD offers programs across 13 faculties. Its strengths include:

- **Tourism and Hospitality**: One of Indonesia's top programs, leveraging Bali's position as a global tourism destination
- **Marine Science**: Research programs benefiting from Bali's marine biodiversity
- **Balinese Culture and Arts**: Unique programs not available elsewhere
- **Indonesian Language (BIPA)**: Well-established program for foreign students learning Bahasa Indonesia

UNUD's campus in Jimbaran offers a pleasant study environment with easy access to Bali's southern coast.

## The DARMASISWA Scholarship Program

**DARMASISWA** is Indonesia's flagship scholarship for international students, managed by the Ministry of Education. It is one of the easiest paths for foreigners to study in Indonesia.

### Program Details

| Aspect             | Details                                                                    |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Eligibility        | Citizens of countries with diplomatic relations with Indonesia, aged 17-35 |
| Duration           | 1 academic year (extendable)                                               |
| Coverage           | Tuition waiver + monthly stipend of IDR 2-2.5M                             |
| Focus Areas        | Indonesian language (BIPA), arts, music, dance, crafts, culinary arts      |
| Host Universities  | 70+ universities across Indonesia                                          |
| Application Period | February-April annually                                                    |
| Website            | darmasiswa.kemdikbud.go.id                                                 |

### How to Apply for DARMASISWA

1. **Check eligibility**: You must be a citizen of a country with diplomatic relations with Indonesia and not currently residing in Indonesia
2. **Choose your host universities**: Select up to 3 preferred universities from the list
3. **Prepare documents**: Passport, health certificate, academic transcripts, recommendation letters, motivation statement, recent photographs
4. **Apply online**: Submit through the DARMASISWA online portal during the application window
5. **Interview**: If shortlisted, attend an interview at the Indonesian embassy or consulate in your country
6. **Acceptance**: Receive notification of acceptance and host university placement
7. **Visa processing**: Apply for a VITAS (Limited Stay Visa) through the Indonesian embassy using your acceptance letter

### The KNB Scholarship (Full Degree)

For students seeking a full degree, the **KNB (Kemitraan Negara Berkembang)** scholarship is the premium option:

- Covers full tuition for a bachelor's, master's, or doctoral degree
- Monthly stipend of IDR 2.5-3.5M
- One-time settlement allowance
- Book allowance
- Health insurance
- Round-trip airfare
- Available at top Indonesian universities (UI, ITB, UGM, IPB, UNAIR)

KNB is more competitive than DARMASISWA but offers far greater benefits for committed students.

## The KITAS Pelajar (Student Stay Permit)

All international students in Indonesia must hold a **KITAS Pelajar** (Student Temporary Stay Permit). The process works as follows:

1. **Receive acceptance letter** from your Indonesian university
2. **Apply for VITAS** (Limited Stay Visa) at the Indonesian embassy or consulate in your home country, presenting your acceptance letter
3. **Arrive in Indonesia** and report to the local immigration office (Kantor Imigrasi) within 30 days
4. **Convert VITAS to KITAS**: Your university's international office will sponsor and assist with KITAS processing
5. **Receive KITAS card**: Valid for 1 year, renewable annually for the duration of your studies

### KITAS Pelajar Restrictions

- You may **not work** on a student KITAS (part-time work is technically prohibited)
- You must remain enrolled at your sponsoring university
- You must report address changes to immigration
- Renewal requires proof of continued enrollment and academic progress
- Overstaying your KITAS carries serious penalties (IDR 1M per day, up to deportation)

## Language Requirements

### Indonesian-Taught Programs

For programs taught in Bahasa Indonesia, you will need:

- **BIPA certificate** (Bahasa Indonesia untuk Penutur Asing) at level 4-5 (of 7)
- Most universities offer preparatory BIPA courses for incoming international students
- Some universities require a year of Indonesian language study before academic program entry

### English-Taught Programs

For international programs taught in English:

- **TOEFL iBT**: Minimum 61-80 (varies by university)
- **IELTS Academic**: Minimum 5.5-6.5 (varies by university)
- **TOEFL ITP**: Minimum 500-550 (accepted by some universities)

UI, ITB, and UGM have the most English-taught options, with full degree programs at bachelor's, master's, and doctoral levels.

## International Degree Recognition

A common concern for international students is whether their Indonesian degree will be recognized at home.

### Recognition by Region

| Region                   | Recognition Status         | Notes                                                                  |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ASEAN countries          | Fully recognized           | ASEAN Qualifications Reference Framework ensures mutual recognition    |
| Australia/New Zealand    | Generally recognized       | May require assessment by NZQA or relevant body                        |
| Europe (EU/EEA)          | Recognized with evaluation | Through NARIC centers in each country; Bologna compatibility improving |
| United States/Canada     | Recognized with evaluation | WES or ECE credential evaluation required                              |
| Middle East              | Generally recognized       | Strong bilateral agreements, especially for Islamic studies            |
| East Asia (Japan, Korea) | Recognized with evaluation | Bilateral agreements with specific universities                        |

For the strongest international recognition, degrees from UI, ITB, and UGM carry the most weight due to their global rankings and international accreditation partnerships.

## Student Life in Indonesia

Studying in Indonesia is not just about academics. The student experience offers deep cultural immersion:

- **Campus organizations (UKM)**: Indonesian universities have vibrant student activity units covering sports, arts, debate, volunteering, and more
- **Cultural events**: Regular cultural performances, festivals, and celebrations on campus
- **Field research opportunities**: Indonesia's biodiversity, cultural diversity, and economic dynamism provide unparalleled field research settings
- **Travel**: Student holidays allow exploration of Indonesia's 17,000+ islands, and budget airlines make domestic travel affordable
- **Community**: Indonesian students are famously hospitable and curious about international classmates

## Practical Tips for International Students

1. **Learn Bahasa Indonesia before arriving**: Even for English-taught programs, daily life is much easier with basic Indonesian
2. **Join the international student office orientation**: Every university has one; they handle visa, housing, and integration support
3. **Open an Indonesian bank account**: You will need this for stipend deposits and daily transactions
4. **Get a local SIM card**: Telkomsel or XL Axiata offer affordable student data plans
5. **Respect local customs**: Indonesia is diverse but generally conservative. Dress modestly on campus and during cultural events
6. **Budget carefully**: The low cost of living can lead to lifestyle inflation. Set a monthly budget and stick to it
7. **Network actively**: Indonesia's relationship-driven culture means personal connections are invaluable for both academic and professional success
