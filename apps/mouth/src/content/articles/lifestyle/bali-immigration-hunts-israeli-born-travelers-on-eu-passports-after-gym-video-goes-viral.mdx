## The Facts

Denpasar Immigration Office opened a formal investigation and placed two Israeli-born foreign nationals on its immigration wanted list after a viral social media video circulated showing their removal from a fitness center in Sukawati, Gianyar District, on August 19, 2026. Denpasar Immigration Chief R. Haryo Sakti confirmed on August 21 that the office's Intelligence and Enforcement Team had reviewed immigration records and conducted a site visit to the facility.

The two individuals were identified only by their initials. The first, IB, aged 23, was born in Israel and holds a Bulgarian passport. IB entered Indonesia through I Gusti Ngurah Rai International Airport on August 3, 2026, using a Visa on Arrival. The second individual, YBH, aged 22, was also born in Israel and holds a Romanian passport. YBH entered through the same airport on the same date on a visitor visa. A third foreigner was reportedly removed from the premises in the same incident, but that individual had not been positively identified in immigration records at the time of reporting.

Sakti said both individuals have been listed on the immigration wanted register — known in Indonesian bureaucratic terminology as the Daftar Pencarian Orang — for monitoring and formal questioning. Being placed on this list does not constitute a criminal charge but enables officers at any immigration checkpoint in Indonesia to detain an individual for interrogation and status review.

In a notable development, immigration officers are also investigating the fitness center owner who carried out the removal. This dual-track inquiry — targeting both the foreign nationals and the Indonesian business operator — suggests authorities are examining the full circumstances of the expulsion, not only the immigration status of those involved.

The incident reflects a wider climate of heightened scrutiny toward foreigners perceived to be of Israeli origin in Indonesia. The country does not maintain diplomatic relations with Israel and does not admit holders of Israeli passports. Israeli citizens who hold dual nationality and travel on a second passport are not explicitly barred, but the current geopolitical context — shaped by the ongoing conflict in Gaza and strong domestic public sentiment — has increased pressure on local authorities to respond visibly to incidents involving this community. The viral spread of the gym video directly preceded and apparently prompted the formal immigration response.

In today's Bali, any incident that goes viral can rapidly escalate into an institutional response, regardless of who was 'right' in the original dispute.

## In Practice

Foreign nationals born in Israel and traveling on EU or other third-country passports should understand that Indonesian immigration databases record place of birth. A valid non-Israeli passport does not erase that data point from an officer's screen. Under normal conditions this may not trigger any action, but in a politically charged environment, it can become grounds for questioning or detainment.

Visa on Arrival holders face particular exposure: this category offers minimal procedural protection if flagged. Holders cannot easily convert to a longer-stay visa once inside the country if an investigation is opened, and their legal options narrow quickly. Those on visitor visas (B211A) have slightly more formal standing but face the same enforcement framework.

Business owners — gyms, co-working spaces, restaurants, event venues — should understand that how they handle confrontations with foreign guests is now a potential compliance issue. A filmed expulsion shared on Instagram or TikTok can become the opening act of an immigration investigation that pulls the establishment into the process.

For all expats and long-stay visitors: avoid public confrontations. If you appear in viral content related to any dispute or incident, seek legal advice before engaging with immigration officers.
