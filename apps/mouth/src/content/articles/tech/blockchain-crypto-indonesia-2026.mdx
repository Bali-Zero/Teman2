## The Regulatory Shift: From Bappebti to OJK

Indonesia's approach to cryptocurrency took a major turn on January 10, 2025, when oversight of crypto assets officially transferred from Bappebti (the Commodity Futures Trading Regulatory Agency) to OJK (the Financial Services Authority). This was mandated by Law No. 4 of 2023 on the Development and Strengthening of the Financial Sector, commonly known as the P2SK Law.

Under Bappebti, crypto was classified as a commodity. Under OJK, it is regulated as a financial asset. This distinction matters because OJK applies stricter consumer protection rules, capital requirements for exchanges, and more rigorous audit standards. For expats trading crypto in Indonesia, the practical impact is better protection and clearer rules.

The transition period ran through most of 2025, during which existing Bappebti-licensed exchanges migrated their licenses to the new OJK framework. As of early 2026, all legally operating exchanges hold OJK registration.

One thing that has not changed: cryptocurrency is legal to trade and hold in Indonesia, but it is **not legal tender**. You cannot use Bitcoin to pay for your nasi goreng. The Indonesian rupiah remains the only legal tender, and Bank Indonesia has been very firm on this point.

### How Expats Can Open an Account

To register on a licensed Indonesian exchange as a foreigner, you typically need:

1. **Valid KITAS or KITAP** -- Tourist visas are generally not accepted for full KYC
2. **Passport** -- Original document scanned or photographed
3. **Indonesian phone number** -- Required for SMS verification
4. **Selfie with ID** -- Standard KYC step
5. **Indonesian bank account** -- For IDR deposits and withdrawals

The KYC process usually takes one to three business days. Some exchanges like Indodax have added English-language support for their verification flow, though the interface is primarily in Bahasa Indonesia.

### What About International Exchanges?

Exchanges like Binance (global), Coinbase, and Kraken are not licensed by OJK to operate in Indonesia. While accessing them via VPN is technically possible, it creates several problems: no legal recourse if funds are lost, potential violation of Indonesian regulations, and complications when moving funds back into the Indonesian banking system.

If you hold crypto on international exchanges from before your move to Indonesia, the practical approach is to keep those accounts but use a licensed local exchange for any new IDR-denominated trading.

## Crypto Taxation: PPh Final and VAT

Indonesia's crypto tax framework has been in place since May 2022 under PMK 68/2022 (Ministry of Finance Regulation). The rates are straightforward:

| Tax Type                        | Rate  | Applied To                          | Collection Method    |
| ------------------------------- | ----- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| PPh Final (Income Tax)          | 0.1%  | Every sale transaction              | Withheld by exchange |
| VAT                             | 0.11% | Every purchase transaction          | Withheld by exchange |
| PPh Final (unlicensed exchange) | 0.2%  | Sales on unregistered platforms     | Self-reported        |
| VAT (unlicensed exchange)       | 0.22% | Purchases on unregistered platforms | Self-reported        |

The key advantage of using licensed exchanges: taxes are automatically withheld and remitted. You do not need to file a separate crypto tax return for exchange-based transactions. The exchange handles it.

However, if you receive crypto as payment, mine crypto, or participate in DeFi yields, the tax treatment gets murkier. In general, income received in crypto should be declared at its IDR market value at the time of receipt and included in your annual SPT (tax return) under regular income tax rates.

### Tax Reporting Tips for Expats

- **Keep records**: Download transaction history from your exchange at least quarterly
- **Convert to IDR**: All tax reporting in Indonesia is in rupiah, regardless of the crypto asset
- **Declare holdings**: Crypto assets should be included in your annual wealth declaration (SPT 1770 or 1770S)
- **Consult a tax advisor**: Indonesian crypto tax rules interact with your overall tax residency status; if you are a tax resident (183+ days), worldwide income rules apply

## The Digital Rupiah: Indonesia's CBDC

Bank Indonesia has been developing the digital rupiah since 2022 under its "Project Garuda" initiative. The CBDC is designed in three layers:

1. **Wholesale CBDC** -- For interbank settlements between Bank Indonesia and commercial banks
2. **Retail CBDC** -- For public use, potentially through digital wallets
3. **Cross-border CBDC** -- For international settlement, integrated with ASEAN payment networks

As of early 2026, the wholesale layer has completed multiple pilot tests with major Indonesian banks including Bank Mandiri, BCA, and BRI. The retail layer is still in development, with a limited pilot expected in late 2026.

For expats, the digital rupiah will eventually mean faster domestic transfers, potentially cheaper remittance options, and a government-backed digital payment alternative to private wallets like GoPay and OVO. However, the practical impact is still a year or more away from being felt in daily life.

### Digital Rupiah vs Cryptocurrency

It is important to understand that the digital rupiah is not a cryptocurrency. It is a central bank digital currency, meaning:

- It is issued and controlled by Bank Indonesia
- Its value is pegged 1:1 to the physical rupiah
- It is legal tender (unlike crypto)
- It does not use a public blockchain
- There is no mining or staking

Bank Indonesia has been explicit that the digital rupiah is partly motivated by the desire to maintain monetary sovereignty in the face of private crypto adoption.

## DeFi, NFTs, and the Gray Areas

While exchange-based crypto trading is clearly regulated, decentralized finance (DeFi) and NFTs exist in a regulatory gray zone in Indonesia. OJK has not issued specific regulations for DeFi protocols, yield farming, or NFT marketplaces.

What this means in practice:

- **DeFi**: Using protocols like Aave or Uniswap is not explicitly prohibited, but there is no regulatory protection if you lose funds. Tax treatment of DeFi yields is unclear.
- **NFTs**: Creating and selling NFTs is not specifically regulated. The few Indonesian NFT marketplaces that existed have mostly pivoted to other products. If you sell NFTs for income, it should be declared as regular income.
- **Staking**: OJK has signaled that staking services offered by licensed exchanges will be regulated. Independent staking (running your own validator) has no specific rules.

The general regulatory direction is toward bringing more crypto activities under OJK's umbrella, so expect clearer rules for DeFi and NFTs in the coming years.

## Blockchain Beyond Crypto: Indonesian Use Cases

Indonesia's blockchain adoption extends beyond speculation. Several real-world applications are gaining traction:

### Supply Chain Verification

Indonesian agricultural exports (coffee, palm oil, seafood) are using blockchain for traceability. Companies like eFishery use blockchain to verify sustainable aquaculture supply chains, helping Bali-based restaurants prove the provenance of their seafood.

### Land Registry

The National Land Agency (BPN) has piloted blockchain-based land certificates in select regions. Given the complexity of Indonesian land ownership (HGB, HGU, Hak Milik), blockchain could significantly reduce fraud in property transactions -- a particular concern for expats investing in Bali real estate.

### Digital Identity

Indonesia's national digital identity initiative is exploring blockchain for secure identity verification. This could eventually simplify KYC processes for expats across banking, mobile registration, and government services.

### Trade Finance

Several Indonesian banks offer blockchain-based letters of credit for import/export transactions, reducing processing time from days to hours.

## Practical Security Tips for Crypto Holders in Indonesia

Holding crypto in Indonesia comes with unique security considerations:

1. **Use hardware wallets**: For significant holdings, store crypto offline. Ledger and Trezor devices ship to Indonesia via e-commerce platforms.
2. **Beware SIM swap fraud**: Indonesian phone numbers are vulnerable to SIM swapping. Use authenticator apps instead of SMS-based 2FA on your exchange accounts.
3. **Secure your email**: Use a strong, unique password and hardware keys for the email linked to your exchange accounts.
4. **Watch for scams**: Telegram and WhatsApp groups in Bali frequently promote "crypto investment opportunities" that are scams. If someone guarantees returns, walk away.
5. **Power and internet backup**: Bali's power grid can be unreliable. Do not make time-sensitive trades without backup power and internet.

## What to Expect in 2026 and Beyond

The Indonesian crypto regulatory landscape is evolving rapidly. Key developments to watch:

- **OJK exchange audits**: Expect stricter capital requirements and operational audits of licensed exchanges throughout 2026
- **DeFi regulation**: OJK has indicated that DeFi-specific rules are in development
- **Digital rupiah retail pilot**: Limited public testing expected by late 2026
- **ASEAN crypto framework**: Cross-border regulatory harmonization discussions are ongoing, which could simplify crypto use for expats moving between Southeast Asian countries
- **Tax clarity**: The Ministry of Finance is expected to update PMK 68/2022 to address staking, DeFi, and airdrops

Indonesia's crypto market has matured significantly. The days of Wild West regulation are over. For expats who follow the rules -- licensed exchanges, proper tax reporting, and security best practices -- Indonesia is a perfectly viable place to participate in the crypto economy.
