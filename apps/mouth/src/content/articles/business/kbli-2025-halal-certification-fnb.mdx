## KBLI 2025 and Halal Certification: Which Business Codes Require It in Indonesia

If you're running a food business in Indonesia—whether that's a restaurant in Bali, a beverage manufacturing plant in Jakarta, or an organic cosmetics brand—you need to know this: **halal certification is now mandatory**.

Under **UU 33/2014** (Undang-Undang Jaminan Produk Halal), all food, beverages, and certain other consumer products entering or circulating in Indonesia must be halal-certified, unless explicitly labeled as "TIDAK HALAL" (Not Halal).

With the **KBLI 2025 update** now in effect (transition deadline June 18, 2026), understanding which business classification codes require halal certification—and how to get it—is critical for legal compliance and market access.

This guide covers:

- Which KBLI codes require halal certification
- The BPJPH certification process (step-by-step)
- Cost breakdown and timeline
- Bali-specific considerations (alcohol, pork, foreign-owned F&B)
- Exemptions and penalties

---

## What is Halal Certification and Why is it Mandatory?

**Halal certification** verifies that a product complies with Islamic law (Sharia), meaning it:

- Contains no prohibited ingredients (pork, alcohol, blood, carnivorous animals)
- Is processed using halal methods (separate equipment, no cross-contamination)
- Follows supply chain integrity from raw materials to final product

**Legal Basis:**

- **UU 33/2014** (Undang-Undang Jaminan Produk Halal) - enacted October 17, 2014
- **PP 39/2021** (Peraturan Pemerintah) - implementing regulations
- **Enforcement deadline:** October 17, 2024 (5 years after enactment)

**Who issues certification?**

- **BPJPH** (Badan Penyelenggara Jaminan Produk Halal) - government authority under the Ministry of Religious Affairs
- **LPH** (Lembaga Pemeriksa Halal) - halal auditors (e.g., MUI, LPPOM MUI)
- **SIHALAL system** - online registration portal ([https://sihalal.go.id](https://sihalal.go.id))

## Which KBLI 2025 Codes Require Halal Certification?

Here's a breakdown of **KBLI categories** that fall under the halal certification mandate:

### Food Manufacturing (KBLI 10xxx)

**All food manufacturing codes require halal certification**, including:

| KBLI Code | Business Type                      | Halal Required? |
| --------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------- |
| 10111     | Meat processing (beef, poultry)    | Yes             |
| 10120     | Fish processing                    | Yes             |
| 10130     | Fruit and vegetable processing     | Yes             |
| 10191     | Bakery products                    | Yes             |
| 10310     | Fruit and vegetable oil production | Yes             |
| 10410     | Cooking oil and margarine          | Yes             |
| 10512     | Ice cream manufacturing            | Yes             |
| 10611     | Grain milling (rice, flour)        | Yes             |
| 10710     | Bread and pastries                 | Yes             |
| 10791     | Instant noodles                    | Yes             |
| 10821     | Confectionery (candy, chocolate)   | Yes             |

**Exemption:** If your product contains pork or other non-halal ingredients, you must label it **"TIDAK HALAL"** and follow non-halal product regulations (separate storage, clear signage).

### Beverage Manufacturing (KBLI 11xxx)

| KBLI Code | Business Type              | Halal Required?                      |
| --------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| 11010     | Non-alcoholic beverages    | Yes                                  |
| 11020     | Bottled water              | Yes                                  |
| 11030     | Soft drinks                | Yes                                  |
| 11040     | Fruit and vegetable juices | Yes                                  |
| 11050     | Coffee, tea, herbal drinks | Yes                                  |
| **11060** | **Alcoholic beverages**    | **NO** (exempt, but must be labeled) |

**Alcohol exemption:** Alcoholic beverages (beer, wine, spirits) are exempt from halal certification but must comply with labeling laws and excise regulations.

### Restaurants, Cafes, and Catering (KBLI 56xxx)

**All food service businesses require halal certification or non-halal labeling:**

| KBLI Code | Business Type                  | Halal Required?              |
| --------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| 56101     | Full-service restaurants       | Yes (or label "TIDAK HALAL") |
| 56102     | Fast food restaurants          | Yes (or label "TIDAK HALAL") |
| 56103     | Food stalls and street vendors | Yes (or label "TIDAK HALAL") |
| 56210     | Catering services              | Yes (or label "TIDAK HALAL") |
| 56301     | Cafes and coffee shops         | Yes (or label "TIDAK HALAL") |
| 56302     | Bars and nightclubs            | NO (if serving only alcohol) |
| 56309     | Other food service activities  | Yes (or label "TIDAK HALAL") |

**Bali-specific note:** Many restaurants in Bali serve pork (babi guling) or alcohol. These businesses must:

1. **Label menus** with "TIDAK HALAL" for non-halal dishes
2. **Store ingredients separately** (no cross-contamination)
3. **Train staff** on halal vs. non-halal handling

### Retail Food Sales (KBLI 47xxx)

| KBLI Code | Business Type                 | Halal Required?         |
| --------- | ----------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| 47111     | Supermarkets and hypermarkets | Yes (for products sold) |
| 47112     | Convenience stores            | Yes (for products sold) |
| 47113     | Grocery stores                | Yes (for products sold) |
| 47191     | Non-store retail (online)     | Yes (for products sold) |

**Note:** Retailers don't need halal certification for their _business_, but all food/beverage products they sell must be halal-certified or labeled "TIDAK HALAL".

### Cosmetics and Personal Care (KBLI 20xxx)

**Yes, cosmetics too!** Under UU 33/2014, cosmetics are subject to halal certification:

| KBLI Code | Business Type           | Halal Required?      |
| --------- | ----------------------- | -------------------- |
| 20231     | Cosmetics manufacturing | Yes (deadline: 2026) |
| 20232     | Personal care products  | Yes (deadline: 2026) |

**Why cosmetics?** Many cosmetics contain animal-derived ingredients (collagen, glycerin, carmine dye). Halal certification ensures no pork-derived or prohibited ingredients.

## The BPJPH Halal Certification Process: Step-by-Step

Here's how to obtain halal certification through **BPJPH** (Badan Penyelenggara Jaminan Produk Halal):

### Step 1: Register on SIHALAL System

1. Visit **[https://sihalal.go.id](https://sihalal.go.id)**
2. Create a business account (NIB, NPWP, company profile)
3. Submit a **Halal Product Registration Form** (Formulir Permohonan Sertifikat Halal)

**Documents required:**

- Company registration (PT PMA deed, NIB, TDP)
- Product list (name, ingredients, production process)
- Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) for each ingredient
- Production flowchart
- Halal Management System (HAS 23000) documentation (optional but speeds up approval)

### Step 2: Self-Declaration and Payment

1. Submit a **self-declaration** (Pernyataan Mandiri) confirming your product meets halal standards
2. Pay the **certification fee** (see cost breakdown below)

### Step 3: LPH Audit

**BPJPH assigns an LPH** (Lembaga Pemeriksa Halal) to conduct an on-site audit:

- Ingredient verification (supplier certificates)
- Production process inspection (equipment, storage, handling)
- Staff interviews (training on halal procedures)

**Duration:** 1-2 weeks (depending on business size and product complexity)

### Step 4: BPJPH Review and MUI Fatwa

1. LPH submits audit report to **BPJPH**
2. BPJPH requests a **fatwa** (religious ruling) from **MUI** (Majelis Ulama Indonesia)
3. MUI reviews the case and issues a halal determination

**Duration:** 1-3 months (MUI meets monthly to review cases)

### Step 5: Certificate Issuance

1. BPJPH issues a **Halal Certificate** (Sertifikat Halal) valid for **4 years**
2. You receive a **halal logo** to display on packaging, menus, and marketing materials
3. Certificate can be verified online via SIHALAL

**Total timeline:** 2-6 months from application to certificate

## Cost Breakdown: How Much Does Halal Certification Cost?

The cost of halal certification varies by **business size** and **product complexity**:

| Business Type   | Annual Revenue     | Certification Cost (IDR)            |
| --------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| Micro business  | < 500 million      | **FREE** (subsidized by government) |
| Small business  | 500M - 2.5 billion | 0 - 5 million                       |
| Medium business | 2.5B - 50 billion  | 5 - 15 million                      |
| Large business  | > 50 billion       | 15 - 50 million+                    |

**Additional costs:**

- **LPH audit fee:** IDR 2-10 million (depending on auditor and product count)
- **HAS 23000 certification:** IDR 5-15 million (optional, but recommended for faster approval)
- **Renewal fee:** 50% of initial cost (every 4 years)

**Government subsidy:** Micro and small businesses (under IDR 2.5 billion revenue) can apply for **subsidized or free certification** through BPJPH's SME program.

## Bali-Specific Considerations for Halal Certification

Bali presents unique challenges for halal compliance due to its Hindu-majority population and tourism-focused F&B industry:

### 1. Restaurants Serving Pork or Alcohol

**Problem:** Many Bali restaurants (babi guling, beachfront bars) serve pork or alcohol.

**Solution:**

- **Option A:** Don't get halal certification. Instead, label all menus/dishes as **"TIDAK HALAL"** (Not Halal).
- **Option B:** Separate operations—halal kitchen/menu + non-halal kitchen/menu (common in hotels).

**Legal requirement:** You must choose one or the other. Unlabeled non-halal food is a violation.

### 2. Foreign-Owned F&B Businesses (PT PMA)

**Challenge:** Many expat-owned cafes, bakeries, and restaurants in Bali don't realize halal certification applies to them.

**Reality check:**

- If you hold **KBLI 56101-56309** (restaurants/cafes), halal rules apply
- Foreign ownership doesn't exempt you
- No certification = must label "TIDAK HALAL"

### 3. Imported Ingredients

**Challenge:** Many Bali cafes import specialty ingredients (cheese, sauces, supplements) from abroad.

**Halal requirement:**

- Imported ingredients must have **halal certification from a recognized LPH** (e.g., JAKIM Malaysia, HFA Australia, IFANCA USA)
- BPJPH maintains a list of recognized international halal certifiers
- Check supplier certificates before importing

### 4. Tourism Marketing

**Opportunity:** Halal certification opens access to **Muslim tourist markets** (Middle East, Malaysia, Brunei).

**Bali halal tourism stats (2025):**

- 20% of Bali tourists are Muslim (Malaysia, Indonesia domestic, Middle East)
- Halal-certified restaurants see 30-40% higher bookings from Muslim travelers
- HalalTrip, Crescentrating, and other platforms prioritize halal-certified venues

## Exemptions: When is Halal Certification NOT Required?

Certain products and businesses are **exempt** from halal certification:

### 1. Alcoholic Beverages

**KBLI 11060** (beer, wine, spirits) is exempt. However:

- Must comply with excise tax (DJBC regulations)
- Must be labeled with alcohol content
- Cannot be marketed as "halal"

### 2. Pork Products (if labeled)

Pork-based products (KBLI 10111 - pork processing) are exempt **if clearly labeled**:

- Packaging must say **"MENGANDUNG BABI"** (Contains Pork) or **"TIDAK HALAL"**
- Must be stored separately from halal products
- Retailers must have separate display areas

### 3. Export-Only Products

Products manufactured in Indonesia but **only for export** (not sold domestically) are exempt. However:

- Must declare export-only status to BPJPH
- Cannot be sold in Indonesian market without certification

### 4. Non-Food Consumer Goods

**Furniture, electronics, textiles** (non-ingestible, non-topical products) are exempt. However:

- Cosmetics (topical) **are covered**
- Pharmaceuticals and supplements **are covered** (under separate BPOM + halal rules)

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

**What happens if you don't get halal certification?**

### Administrative Penalties

- **Warning letter** from BPJPH (first violation)
- **IDR 50-200 million fine** (repeat violations)
- **Business license suspension** (severe cases)
- **Product recall** (if sold without halal certification or non-halal labeling)

### Criminal Penalties (UU 33/2014, Article 56)

- **Prison:** Up to 5 years
- **Fine:** Up to IDR 2 billion
- **Applies to:** Businesses that falsely claim halal certification or intentionally mislabel products

### Market Consequences

- **Retailer rejection:** Major supermarkets (Alfamart, Indomaret, Hypermart) refuse non-certified products
- **Consumer backlash:** Social media complaints, boycotts
- **Loss of Muslim market:** 87% of Indonesia's population is Muslim

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. "I Serve Muslim Customers, So I'm Already Halal"

**Wrong.** Halal certification is a **legal requirement**, not a marketing choice. Even if your customers are 100% Muslim, you must have BPJPH certification or non-halal labeling.

### 2. "I'll Get Halal Certification Later"

**Risk:** If you're operating without certification (and without "TIDAK HALAL" labels), you're in violation of UU 33/2014. Start the process **now**—it takes 2-6 months.

### 3. "Halal Certification is Too Expensive"

**False.** Micro/small businesses (under IDR 2.5 billion revenue) can get **free or subsidized certification**. Even medium businesses pay only IDR 5-15 million—far less than potential fines or market loss.

### 4. "I'm in Bali, So Halal Rules Don't Apply"

**Wrong.** UU 33/2014 is a **national law**. Bali, Jakarta, or Papua—same rules. If you serve pork/alcohol, just label it "TIDAK HALAL". Don't ignore the law.

### 5. "My Supplier Says It's Halal"

**Not enough.** You need **documented halal certificates** from suppliers. Verbal assurances don't count. BPJPH auditors will ask for supplier certificates during the LPH audit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is halal certification mandatory for restaurants in Bali?

**Yes.** All restaurants (KBLI 56101-56309) must either obtain halal certification or explicitly label products as "TIDAK HALAL". Many Bali restaurants serving pork or alcohol must use non-halal labeling.

### How much does halal certification cost?

**Small businesses** (turnover under IDR 500M/year) can get subsidized or free certification. **Medium businesses** pay IDR 5-15 million. **Large companies** may pay IDR 15-50 million+ depending on product complexity.

### Can I sell non-halal food in Indonesia?

**Yes**, but it must be clearly labeled **"TIDAK HALAL"** with proper labeling, stored separately, and not mixed with halal products. This applies to pork, alcohol, and other non-halal items.

### Does cosmetics manufacturing need halal certification?

**Yes.** KBLI 20231-20232 (cosmetics and personal care manufacturing) requires halal certification under UU 33/2014. The deadline for cosmetics was 2026.

### How long does the halal certification process take?

**2-6 months** from application to certificate. The timeline depends on:

- Document completeness
- LPH audit scheduling
- MUI fatwa review (monthly meetings)

### What happens if I don't get halal certification?

**Consequences:**

- IDR 50-200 million fines
- Business license suspension
- Product recall
- Criminal penalties (up to 5 years prison, IDR 2 billion fine) for false halal claims

### Can I get halal certification for imported products?

**Yes**, but you need:

- Halal certificates from recognized international certifiers (JAKIM, HFA, IFANCA, etc.)
- BPJPH verification of foreign certificates
- Separate registration for each imported product

### Do bars and nightclubs need halal certification?

**No**, if they only serve alcohol (KBLI 56302). However, if they serve food, they must:

- Get halal certification for food items, OR
- Label food as "TIDAK HALAL"
