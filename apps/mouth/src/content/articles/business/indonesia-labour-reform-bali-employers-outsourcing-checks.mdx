## The Facts

Indonesia's latest labour-law development gives Bali employers a reason to review staffing arrangements carefully. The useful starting point is to separate what Parliament has reported from the detailed rules that can be checked in an official regulatory text.

DPR's report dated 8 October 2026 confirms that the Manpower Protection Bill was approved at its plenary meeting on 6 October. The report identifies outsourcing, fixed-term employment, wages, termination and foreign-worker use among its subjects. It also records a call for implementing regulations. That confirms a legislative development; it does not, by itself, establish every new operational deadline. [DPR's official report](https://www.dpr.go.id/kegiatan-dpr/berita/Nurhadi-Dorong-Pemerintah-Segera-Terbitkan-Aturan-Pelaksana-UU-Pelindungan-Ketenagakerjaan-70027)

## Distinguish the October development from existing outsourcing rules

A separate instrument, Manpower Minister Regulation 7/2026, was promulgated on 30 April 2026 and is listed as in force by the ministry. Its six supporting-work categories cover cleaning, food and beverage provision, security, drivers and worker transport, operational support, and supporting work in mining, oil, gas and electricity. It should not be reduced to an unqualified five-category list. [Official regulation and current status](https://jdih.kemnaker.go.id/peraturan/detail/3027/peraturan-menteri-ketenagakerjaan-nomor-7-tahun-2026)

Articles 4 and 5 require written agreement details and registration evidence. Article 4 also assigns worker-rights responsibilities to the outsourcing company and requires the client company to ensure those rights are met. Article 10 preserves existing agreements until expiry and requires existing work types and fields to adjust within two years of promulgation. These are provisions of the April regulation, not a newly verified October transition. [Regulation 7/2026, Articles 4, 5 and 10](https://jdih.kemnaker.go.id/asset/data_puu/2026pmnaker007.pdf)

## Build a workforce map before making changes

For a Bali restaurant, hospitality operator or other PT PMA, a useful internal review begins with a simple record: the work being performed, who employs the worker, which company supplies the service, where it is performed and when the agreement expires.

Keep the actual duties beside the contract description. A short label such as “support staff” does not explain what someone does throughout the working day. Ask the vendor to clarify any mismatch and have the applicable category assessed before renewing or expanding the arrangement.

This is a preparation step, not a conclusion that every outsourced role must become direct employment. A news headline is insufficient grounds to determine an individual worker's status or terminate an agreement.

## Put the vendor's documents beside your own records

As a practical document review, request the vendor's legal identity, relevant business authorisation, signed agreement and registration evidence. Identify who can provide payroll, social-security and worker-rights records when needed.

Check that the responsible person in your business can explain the arrangement. A contract stored with a vendor is difficult to evaluate if the operating team cannot connect it to the people and duties on site.

Where records are inconsistent, document the issue and obtain a review of the specific arrangement. Avoid promising an automatic conversion, exemption or grace period before the governing provisions have been identified.

## What still needs checking

Before relying on the October legislation for a staffing decision, obtain its final official text, commencement provisions, transitional rules and any relevant implementing measures. This article does not infer those details from parliamentary reporting.

The immediate task for employers is manageable: assemble the contracts, compare them with actual work, identify missing evidence and seek advice on the affected arrangements. Keeping that review specific is more useful than rebuilding an entire staffing model around an incomplete summary.

The October news development and the April outsourcing regulation are distinct. Both belong in the review, with their dates and legal status kept clear.
