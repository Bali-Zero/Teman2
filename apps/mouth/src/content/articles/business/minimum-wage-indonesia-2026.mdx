## How Indonesia's Minimum Wage System Works

Indonesia uses a decentralized minimum wage system where rates vary significantly by province and even by city/regency (kabupaten/kota). Understanding this system is essential for any PT PMA employer to ensure compliance and avoid criminal penalties.

### The Three Levels of Minimum Wage

| Level                     | Abbreviation                      | Set By                              | Applies To                            |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Provincial Minimum Wage   | UMP (Upah Minimum Provinsi)       | Governor                            | All workers in the province           |
| City/Regency Minimum Wage | UMK (Upah Minimum Kabupaten/Kota) | Governor (on Bupati/Mayor proposal) | Workers in that specific city/regency |
| Sectoral Minimum Wage     | UMSP/UMSK                         | Governor                            | Workers in specific industry sectors  |

**Key rule:** UMK must be equal to or higher than UMP. Employers must pay whichever is higher -- the UMP or the UMK of the regency where their business is located.

### How Minimum Wage Is Calculated

Under PP 51/2023 (Government Regulation on Wages), the annual minimum wage increase is calculated using the formula:

**New UMP = Current UMP x (1 + % increase)**

The percentage increase is determined by:

1. **Economic growth** (weighted)
2. **Inflation** (weighted)
3. **A specific index** considering purchasing power and labor market conditions

The government sets a national cap on the maximum increase percentage. For 2026, the national guideline allowed increases of 6-8% depending on provincial economic conditions.

## Understanding Your Salary Obligations

### What Counts as "Minimum Wage"

Minimum wage in Indonesia refers to the **base salary (upah pokok)** for a worker with zero years of experience in the lowest position. It specifically:

- **Includes:** Base monthly salary only
- **Does not include:** Overtime, allowances (transport, meals, housing), bonuses, THR, BPJS employer contributions

### Salary Structure Requirements

Under PP 51/2023, the base salary must be at least **75% of total take-home pay**. This means:

| Component                | Minimum                         | Maximum       |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ------------- |
| Base salary (upah pokok) | 75% of total                    | 100% of total |
| Fixed allowances         | 0%                              | 25% of total  |
| Non-fixed allowances     | Not counted toward minimum wage | Unlimited     |

**Example:** If a worker's total monthly package is IDR 4,000,000:

- Base salary must be at least IDR 3,000,000 (75%)
- Fixed allowances can be up to IDR 1,000,000 (25%)
- The base salary (IDR 3,000,000) must meet or exceed the applicable UMK

### Probation Period Wages

Employers may set a probation period of up to 3 months for new employees, but wages during probation **cannot be below minimum wage**. Unlike some countries, Indonesia does not allow a reduced "probationary wage."

## Sectoral Minimum Wages

Some provinces establish sector-specific minimum wages (UMSP/UMSK) that are higher than the general UMK. These typically apply to:

- Manufacturing
- Construction
- Mining
- Financial services
- Hospitality and tourism

In Bali, the hospitality and tourism sector may have a sectoral minimum that exceeds the standard UMK. Check with the local Disnaker for current sectoral rates.

## Exemption for Micro and Small Enterprises

Under the Cipta Kerja Law (UU 6/2023), micro and small enterprises (UMK/UMKM) may be exempted from minimum wage requirements if they meet certain criteria:

| Business Size    | Annual Revenue       | Exemption                         |
| ---------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Micro            | Up to IDR 2 billion  | May negotiate wages below UMP/UMK |
| Small            | IDR 2-15 billion     | May negotiate wages below UMP/UMK |
| Medium and Large | Above IDR 15 billion | Must comply with UMP/UMK          |

**Important for PT PMA:** Most PT PMA companies are classified as medium or large due to minimum capital requirements (IDR 10 billion authorized capital). The micro/small exemption rarely applies to foreign-owned companies.

## Employer Compliance Checklist

### Monthly Obligations

1. **Pay at least minimum wage** by the agreed payroll date
2. **Calculate overtime correctly:** 1.5x for first hour, 2x for subsequent hours (weekdays); 2x for first 8 hours, 3x for 9th hour, 4x for 10th+ hours (weekends/holidays)
3. **Withhold PPh 21** on total salary and allowances
4. **Pay BPJS contributions** (employer portion) on time
5. **Maintain payroll records** including payslips signed by employees

### Annual Obligations

1. **Review wages** against new UMP/UMK (announced in November/December)
2. **Adjust salaries** effective January 1 of the new year
3. **Update employment contracts** if wage structure changes
4. **File annual PPh 21 reconciliation** with DJP
5. **Report workforce data** to Disnaker (Wajib Lapor Ketenagakerjaan)

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

### Criminal Penalties

| Violation                      | Imprisonment             | Fine         |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------ |
| Paying below minimum wage      | 1-4 years                | IDR 100-400M |
| Not paying wages at all        | 1-4 years                | IDR 100-400M |
| Late wage payment (repeatedly) | Administrative sanctions | Varies       |

### Administrative Sanctions

The Manpower Office (Disnaker) can impose:

- Written warning
- Restriction of business activities
- Temporary suspension of business processes
- **Freezing of business license**

### Employee Remedies

Employees who are underpaid can:

1. File a complaint with Disnaker
2. Submit a case to the Industrial Relations Court (PHI)
3. Report to the police for criminal prosecution
4. The statute of limitations is 2 years for wage claims

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the minimum wage in Bali for 2026?

Bali's provincial minimum wage (UMP) for 2026 is IDR 3,156,300 per month. However, individual regencies set higher UMK rates: Badung IDR 3,472,000, Denpasar IDR 3,392,000, Gianyar IDR 3,275,000, and Tabanan IDR 3,210,000. Employers must pay at least the UMK of their operating regency.

### What happens if I pay below minimum wage?

Paying below minimum wage is a criminal offense under UU 13/2003 as amended by UU 6/2023. Penalties include imprisonment of 1-4 years and/or fines of IDR 100 million to IDR 400 million. The Manpower Office (Disnaker) can also impose administrative sanctions including business license suspension.

### Does minimum wage apply to foreign workers?

Yes, minimum wage applies to all workers in Indonesia regardless of nationality. However, foreign workers on KITAS typically earn well above minimum wage due to the expertise requirements for obtaining work permits. The RPTKA application requires disclosure of the foreign worker's salary.
