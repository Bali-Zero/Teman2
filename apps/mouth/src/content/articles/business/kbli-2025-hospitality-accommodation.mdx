KBLI 2025 restructures Indonesia's hospitality classification into 15 distinct codes — a significant change from KBLI 2020, which used different numbering and fewer categories. Every foreign investor planning to operate a hotel, villa, hostel, or guesthouse in Bali needs to understand the new codes before the **June 18, 2026** compliance deadline. The old codes (55110, 55120, 55193, etc.) no longer exist in KBLI 2025 and must be updated in your NIB.

The good news: **all hospitality codes allow 100% foreign ownership through a PT PMA**. The nuances lie in risk classification, authority level, and licensing obligations. This guide uses verified data from the production KBLI 2025 dataset (BPS 7/2025 + PP 28/2025).

If you are new to the KBLI system entirely, start with our [Beginner's Guide to KBLI 2025](/business/beginners-guide-kbli-2025) before diving into the sector-specific details below.

| -------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| 55110                | **55101–55105**      | Star hotels (now split by star rating) |

| 55191                | **55202**            | Youth Hostel                           |

| 55900                | **55901 / 55909**    | Long-term accommodation / other        |

If your current NIB still shows any of the old 551xx codes, it needs updating by June 2026.

## Star Hotels: Now Split by Rating (55101–55106)

The biggest structural change in this sector. KBLI 2020 had one code for all star hotels (55110). KBLI 2025 splits them:

- **55101** — 5-star (bintang lima)
- **55102** — 4-star (bintang empat)
- **55103** — 3-star (bintang tiga)
- **55104** — 2-star (bintang dua)
- **55105** — 1-star (bintang satu)
- **55106** — Non-star (Hotel Nonbintang / Hotel Melati)

All six are Medium-Low risk with Bupati/Walikota authority and automatic NIB issuance via OSS. The split is administrative — it allows the government to track the hotel market by category. For licensing purposes, the obligations are similar across all six codes: Sertifikat Laik Sehat, self-assessment document, and periodic activity reports.

**Practical implication:** If you currently operate under old code 55110, you need to determine your actual star rating and migrate to the matching 55101–55105 code. If you have no star rating, use 55106.

## Villas: The Flag Code (55203)

**55203** (Aktivitas Vila) is the flagship code for Bali's villa rental industry. It replaced old code 55193 from KBLI 2020 — same business, new number. Medium-Low risk, automatic OSS issuance, 100% PMA, Bupati/Walikota authority.

What you need to operate legally under 55203:

1. **PT PMA incorporation** — notary deed, AHU registration (~2–4 weeks)
2. **NIB via OSS** — select 55203, auto-issued (1–3 days)
3. **Sertifikat Standar** — auto-issued with NIB for Menengah Rendah
4. **KKPR** — spatial conformity from ATR/BPN (2–6 weeks if required)
5. **PBG** — building permit, replaces IMB since 2022 (4–12 weeks)
6. **Sertifikat Laik Sehat** — from Dinas Kesehatan (2–4 weeks)
7. **TDUP** — from Dinas Pariwisata Kabupaten

Typical total timeline: 3–5 months. Minimum PT PMA capital: IDR 10 billion stated (Rp 2.5B paid-up).

## The Exception: Condotel/Aparthotel (55204)

**55204** is the one code that behaves differently from all others in this sector:

- **Risk:** Menengah Tinggi (Medium-High) — the only one in this sector
- **Authority:** Gubernur (provincial government) — not Bupati/Walikota
- **Issuance:** 14 working days — not automatic
- **Extra obligation:** LSPr certification (Sertifikat Standar Usaha from accredited body)

The condotel model — apartments sold to individual investors but operated as a hotel — carries higher regulatory scrutiny because of the complexity of unit ownership, revenue sharing, and operational management. The Governor-level sign-off reflects this.

If you are developing a condotel in Bali, budget extra time for the licensing process and factor in LSPr certification costs.

## Other Codes Worth Knowing

**55201 — Homestay (Rumah Tinggal Sewa)**
The owner-occupied model: you live on the property and rent rooms to guests. Medium-Low risk, automatic, Bupati/Walikota. The defining legal requirement is that the owner resides on-site — without that, it's a villa (55203), not a homestay.

**55202 — Youth Hostel**
Dormitory-style accommodation with shared facilities. Medium-Low risk, automatic, Bupati/Walikota. Growing niche in Canggu, Kuta, and Nusa Penida.

**55300 — Campground / Glamping**
Covers campgrounds, caravan parks, and glamping. Medium-Low risk, 100% PMA. Strong growth in Tabanan, Kintamani, and East Bali highlands.

**55400 — Accommodation Intermediary**
For OTA (Online Travel Agency) businesses and booking platforms — not for operators. Different business model entirely.

**55901 / 55909 — Long-term / Other Accommodation**
Kost (boarding houses), dormitories, monthly rentals. Medium-Low risk. Different regulatory framework from short-term tourism codes — tenant protection rules apply.

## Authority Chain: Who Approves What

This is the detail that most guides get wrong. In KBLI 2025 for the hospitality sector:

- **Bupati/Walikota** (district/city level) — all codes except 55204
- **Gubernur** (provincial level) — 55204 only

For PT PMA registration, BKPM/OSS is always the entry point for the NIB. The authority listed above refers to who approves the Sertifikat Standar — the operational permit that actually allows you to receive guests.

## Compliance Obligations: The Core Requirements

Most hospitality codes share three core post-issuance obligations:

**1. Sertifikat Laik Sehat**
Health worthiness certificate from Dinas Kesehatan. Inspections cover water quality, sanitation, food handling (if applicable), pest control. Typically renewed annually.

**2. Dokumen Penilaian Mandiri**
Self-assessment covering facilities, HR, service quality, and management systems. You declare compliance; government verifies post-approval.

**3. Laporan Kegiatan Berkala**
Periodic activity reports to the relevant ministry — occupancy, staffing, operational compliance. Frequency varies by risk level.

**Additional for 55204 only:** LSPr certification (Sertifikat Standar Usaha from accredited body). Required before full operational approval.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Using old KBLI 2020 codes in your NIB**
This is the most urgent issue as of February 2026. If your NIB shows 55110, 55120, 55193, or any other old 551xx code, it needs updating. OSS integration of KBLI 2025 codes is ongoing — prepare now, execute as soon as OSS supports the migration.

**Choosing 55203 (villa) when operations are hotel-scale**
A villa complex with 20+ units, central reception, daily F&B, and hotel-like operations may be challenged during compliance inspection. Match your code to your actual operating model.

**Using 55201 (homestay) without living on-site**
The legal definition of homestay requires owner residency. Without it, you need 55203.

**Treating 55204 like other hospitality codes**
The Gubernur approval and 14-day processing time for condotels is a genuine operational difference. Plan accordingly.

**Ignoring Bali spatial planning (RTRW)**
Your KBLI code is irrelevant if your land is in a Green Zone where commercial accommodation is restricted. Verify zoning with Dinas Penataan Ruang before investing.

## Bali-Specific Considerations

**Investment threshold:** All PT PMA require minimum IDR 10 billion stated capital (Rp 2.5B paid-up), per PP 5/2021. This applies regardless of which hospitality code you select.

**Villa market saturation:** Canggu and Seminyak have significant oversupply. Better ROI areas in 2025–2026: Tabanan, Sidemen, Amed, north coast — lower land prices, growing demand.

**TDUP enforcement:** Dinas Pariwisata enforcement has intensified since 2023. Operating without TDUP in Canggu, Seminyak, and Kuta risks closure orders.

**Banjar approval:** Not a government permit but operationally essential. Neighborhood council consent affects staff, access, and community relations.
