KBLI 2025 introduces nine distinct codes for the hospitality and accommodation sector in Indonesia, and every foreign investor planning to operate a hotel, villa, hostel, or guesthouse in Bali needs to understand exactly which code applies to their business before the **June 18, 2026** compliance deadline. Getting the classification wrong does not just mean paperwork headaches — it determines your risk level, your licensing obligations, and whether your permit can be auto-approved through the Fiktif Positif mechanism.

The good news: **all nine hospitality codes allow 100% foreign ownership through a PT PMA**. The nuances lie in risk classification, compliance obligations, and which codes qualify for the fastest approval pathway. This guide breaks down every code, explains the practical differences, and shows you exactly how to choose the right one for your Bali accommodation business.

Three codes — homestays (55130), youth hostels (55191), and villas (55193) — qualify for Fiktif Positif automatic approval.

## Understanding Risk Levels: Why They Matter

The risk classification is not just a label. It directly determines the depth of government scrutiny your business faces during the licensing process.

### Low Risk (Rendah) — Codes 55120, 55130, 55191, 55192, 55193, 55194, 55199

Low-risk businesses benefit from a streamlined approval process. Your primary obligations are:

1. **Sertifikat Laik Sehat** — A health worthiness certificate confirming your property meets sanitation and safety standards. This is issued by the local health department (Dinas Kesehatan).
2. **Dokumen Penilaian Mandiri** — A self-assessment document where you declare compliance with accommodation standards covering facilities, human resources, service quality, and management systems.
3. **Laporan Kegiatan Berkala** — Periodic activity reports submitted to the relevant ministry, documenting occupancy, staffing, and operational compliance.

For low-risk codes, the government largely trusts your self-declaration and conducts post-approval verification rather than requiring pre-approval inspections.

### Medium Risk (Sedang) — Codes 55110, 55900

Medium-risk businesses face additional requirements beyond the three core obligations. Star hotels (55110) require LSPr (Lembaga Sertifikasi Profesi) standard certification, meaning your staff must hold recognized professional certifications in hospitality. The government conducts more thorough verification before granting operational approval.

Long-term accommodation (55900) carries medium risk because boarding houses and dormitories involve longer tenant relationships with different regulatory concerns around tenant rights, building safety codes, and fire compliance.

## Fiktif Positif: The Automatic Approval Advantage

Three hospitality codes qualify for Indonesia's **Fiktif Positif** mechanism: **55130** (Homestay), **55191** (Youth Hostel), and **55193** (Villa). These are among the 258 KBLI codes nationwide that benefit from this system.

Here is how it works: when you submit your permit application through OSS, the relevant government agency has a defined SLA (service level agreement) deadline to respond. If they fail to respond within that window, **your permit is automatically deemed approved**. The logic is straightforward — bureaucratic delay should not penalize compliant businesses.

For villa operators in Bali, this is significant. The villa sector is the single largest accommodation category on the island, and the Fiktif Positif mechanism means that a properly submitted application under KBLI 55193 cannot be held in indefinite limbo.

**Important caveat:** Fiktif Positif does not mean zero scrutiny. Post-approval inspections can still occur, and non-compliance discovered after automatic approval can result in permit revocation. File correctly the first time.

## Which Code Should You Choose? A Decision Guide

Choosing the wrong KBLI code is one of the most common and costly mistakes foreign investors make when setting up accommodation businesses in Bali. Here is how to match your business model to the right code.

### You Are Building or Operating a Star-Rated Hotel

**Use: 55110 — Hotel Bintang**

This code applies if your property will seek or holds a star rating from the Indonesian Hotel and Restaurant Association (PHRI) through the national star classification system. Star ratings (bintang 1 through bintang 5) come with mandatory standards for room count, facilities, staffing ratios, and service quality.

Choose this code only if you are genuinely pursuing a star rating. If you are building a boutique hotel with 15 rooms and no intention of seeking a star classification, 55110 is the wrong code — and its medium risk level means unnecessary compliance burden.

### You Are Running a Boutique or Budget Hotel

**Use: 55120 — Hotel Melati**

This is the workhorse code for the vast majority of small to medium hotels in Bali. Hotel Melati covers any hotel that does not hold or seek a star rating. Think: boutique hotels in Seminyak, surf lodges in Canggu, budget accommodations in Ubud.

The low risk classification makes this significantly easier to license than 55110, with the same 100% PMA allowance. If you are unsure whether your property qualifies as a star hotel, default to 55120.

### You Are Operating a Villa Business

**Use: 55193 — Vila**

The flagship code for Bali's massive villa rental industry. This covers properties that are marketed and operated as individual villas — typically standalone units with private pools, kitchens, and dedicated staff. The low risk classification combined with Fiktif Positif eligibility makes this the most investor-friendly accommodation code in the system.

**Critical distinction:** A villa managed as a hospitality business (daily cleaning, guest check-in/check-out, concierge services) belongs under 55193. A villa leased long-term to a single tenant without hospitality services is a property transaction, not an accommodation business, and falls under different KBLI codes entirely. See our [KBLI 2025 Bali Transformation Guide](/insights/business/kbli-2025-bali-transformation) for the full breakdown of this distinction.

### You Are Running a Homestay or Guesthouse

**Use: 55130 — Pondok Wisata** (for homestays) or **55199 — Penyediaan Akomodasi Jangka Pendek Lainnya** (for guesthouses)

This is where classification gets nuanced. A pondok wisata (homestay) implies that the owner lives on-site and rents out part of their residence to guests. It is a community-based tourism model. A guesthouse without an on-site owner-operator falls under the broader 55199 catch-all.

Both are low risk. But only 55130 qualifies for Fiktif Positif. If your business model genuinely fits the homestay definition, use 55130 for the faster approval pathway.

### You Are Building a Hostel or Backpacker Lodge

**Use: 55191 — Penginapan Remaja**

Youth hostels and backpacker accommodations with dormitory-style rooms, shared facilities, and communal spaces belong here. This code is low risk and Fiktif Positif eligible — a strong combination for the growing hostel market in areas like Canggu, Kuta, and Nusa Penida.

### You Are Developing a Glamping or Campsite Business

**Use: 55192 — Bumi Perkemahan**

Covers campgrounds, caravan parks, and glamping operations. Low risk, 100% PMA. This is a growing niche in Bali, particularly in the Tabanan, Kintamani, and East Bali highland areas.

### You Are Developing a Condotel or Aparthotel

**Use: 55194 — Apartemen Hotel**

Condotels — properties that function as hotels but are sold as individual apartment units to investors — use this code. Low risk, 100% PMA. The condotel model has specific regulatory requirements around unit ownership, revenue sharing, and operational management that differ from standard hotels.

### You Are Running a Boarding House, Kost, or Dormitory

**Use: 55900 — Penyediaan Akomodasi Lainnya**

This is the long-term accommodation code, covering monthly rentals like kost (boarding houses), dormitories, and similar establishments. Note the medium risk classification — long-term accommodation involves different regulatory concerns than short-term tourism stays, including tenant protection rules and different fire safety standards.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Using 55110 When 55120 Is Sufficient

Foreign investors often assume that registering under the star hotel code (55110) sounds more prestigious or professional. In reality, it triggers medium risk classification and LSPr certification requirements that add cost and complexity. Unless you are genuinely pursuing a star rating, 55120 (Hotel Melati) gives you the same 100% PMA with lower compliance burden.

### Mistake 2: Classifying a Managed Villa Under Property Codes

If you operate a villa with guest services — housekeeping, check-in, breakfast, pool maintenance — you are running a hospitality business. Registering under a property or real estate KBLI code to avoid hospitality regulations exposes you to tax benchmark mismatches. The DGT's Coretax system compares your profit margins against the benchmarks for your registered KBLI code. A "property" company reporting hospitality-level revenue patterns triggers automated audit flags.

### Mistake 3: Ignoring the Homestay vs. Guesthouse Distinction

The difference between 55130 (Pondok Wisata) and 55199 (Other Short-term) is not just semantic. Pondok Wisata carries Fiktif Positif eligibility; 55199 does not. But you cannot claim 55130 if the owner does not live on-site. Misclassification can result in permit challenges during post-approval inspection.

### Mistake 4: Overlooking the Long-term vs. Short-term Boundary

A property that rents monthly to long-term tenants (kost, boarding) falls under 55900, not the short-term accommodation codes. The reverse is also true — a kost that pivots to nightly Airbnb-style rentals without updating its KBLI code is operating outside its licensed classification.

### Mistake 5: Assuming OSS Is Ready for KBLI 2025

As of February 2026, the OSS (Online Single Submission) system has **not yet fully integrated KBLI 2025 codes**. Do not attempt to force a code update that could lock your NIB in an incomplete state. Prepare your documentation now — notarial deeds, compliance documents, code mapping — and execute the change once OSS is updated. Monitor BKPM announcements weekly.

## Bali-Specific Considerations

### Green Zone Restrictions

Bali's spatial planning regulations (RTRW) designate certain areas as Green Zones where commercial accommodation development is restricted or prohibited. Your KBLI code selection is irrelevant if your property sits in a zone that does not permit the intended use. Before committing to any accommodation investment, verify the zoning status of your land through the local Dinas Penataan Ruang (Spatial Planning Office).

Areas with known Green Zone restrictions include parts of Ubud's rice terrace corridor, the Ayung River valley, certain stretches of the Bukit Peninsula, and designated sacred areas near major temples. The 2024-2025 enforcement cycle has been notably stricter than previous years.

### Villa vs. Hotel Classification in Practice

Bali's villa market operates in a gray area that KBLI 2025 attempts to clarify. The practical test is operational: if your property operates like a hotel (front desk or check-in process, daily housekeeping, F&B service, guest activities), the government may argue it should be classified as a hotel (55110 or 55120) regardless of its physical form as a villa.

Conversely, a large villa complex with 20+ units, central management, and hotel-like operations may face pushback if registered under 55193 (Vila). The safest approach is to match your KBLI code to your actual operational model, not your property type.

### Investment Threshold

All PT PMA companies require a minimum investment of **IDR 10 billion** (approximately USD 600,000) per PP 5/2021. This applies regardless of which hospitality KBLI code you select. The investment is calculated per company, not per property — a single PT PMA can operate multiple accommodation units under one entity.

For detailed guidance on PT PMA setup and registration, see our [OSS Registration Guide](/insights/business/oss-registration-guide).

### Entertainment Tax (Pajak Hiburan) Exposure

Certain accommodation types — particularly villas and hotels that offer spa, wellness, or entertainment facilities — may trigger Pajak Hiburan obligations at the local level. The 2024 Constitutional Court ruling and subsequent regulations created ongoing uncertainty about entertainment tax rates for hospitality businesses. Your KBLI code selection can influence which local tax benchmarks apply.

## Compliance Obligations: The Three Pillars

Regardless of which code you select, most hospitality KBLI codes share three core compliance obligations:

### 1. Sertifikat Laik Sehat (Health Worthiness Certificate)

Issued by the local Dinas Kesehatan, this certificate confirms your property meets sanitation, hygiene, and health safety standards. Inspections cover water quality, waste management, food handling (if applicable), pest control, and general cleanliness. This is typically renewed annually.

### 2. Dokumen Penilaian Mandiri (Self-Assessment Document)

A structured self-assessment covering four domains: physical facilities and infrastructure, human resources and staffing qualifications, service quality standards, and management systems. You declare compliance, and the government verifies post-approval. The assessment criteria vary by code — star hotels face more detailed standards than homestays.

### 3. Laporan Kegiatan Berkala (Periodic Activity Reports)

Regular operational reports submitted to the relevant ministry, documenting occupancy rates, staffing levels, guest demographics, revenue metrics, and compliance status. The reporting frequency and detail level depend on your risk classification — medium risk businesses (55110, 55900) face more frequent reporting requirements.
