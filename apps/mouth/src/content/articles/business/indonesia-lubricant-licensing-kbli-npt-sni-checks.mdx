## The Facts

A lubricant business needs an accurate activity description before it needs a licensing checklist. Manufacturing, importing, distributing and recycling should not be treated as interchangeable simply because they involve products described as oil.

For investors reviewing an Indonesian venture from Bali, the first correction is important: BPS lists KBLI 2020 code 19291 as products from petroleum refining, within the broader group for other refined petroleum products. It is not titled “lubricant manufacturing and recycling.” [BPS: classification group 1929 and its component codes](https://klasifikasi.web.bps.go.id/app/view/kbli2020/1929)

## Start with the activity and the classification version

The OSS entry for KBLI 2020 code 19212 expressly describes manufacturing oil-based lubricating oil, oils and grease. The distinction matters when comparing a proposed production process with a consultant's checklist or a company's existing registration. [BPS: lubricant manufacturing, KBLI 2020 code 19212](https://klasifikasi.web.bps.go.id/app/view/kbli2020/19212)

These references identify the classification version being discussed. They do not mean an investor should blindly enter an old code into a new application. Confirm the applicable OSS version, conversion mapping and actual business scope when preparing the application.

Write down what the company will do before selecting a code: own a blending plant, use a contract manufacturer, import finished products, distribute them, or process used materials. Then assess each activity and location. A trading operation should not borrow a manufacturing checklist without checking the differences.

## Product registration is a separate question

The Directorate General of Oil and Gas describes the Nomor Pelumas Terdaftar, or NPT, as a number associated with a lubricant trade name that satisfies the applicable requirements. It is part of supervision of lubricant standards and quality in the domestic market. [Ditjen Migas: NPT guidance](https://migas.esdm.go.id/search?page=3&query=npt)

For a product review, compare the exact trade name, responsible company and validity details with the evidence supplied. A supplier's general statement that it is registered does not identify which product the document covers.

Keep this review separate from the business activity code. One concerns the entity's activities; the other concerns the product being placed on the market.

## Use the current SNI instrument and its actual scope

Industry Minister Regulation 8/2025 replaced Regulation 25/2018. BPK's official record lists its effective date as 24 July 2025. The regulation concerns mandatory standards for motor-vehicle lubricating oils; relying only on the older 2018 checklist can miss the replacement framework. [Official regulation record](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/321096/permenperin-no-8-tahun-2025)

Articles 2–4 identify covered standards and tariff headings and provide specific exceptions with supporting evidence. Testing and research samples have conditions; an exception should not be assumed merely because a shipment is small. Equally, the instrument should not be described as applying identically to every industrial lubricant or grease. [Regulation 8/2025, product scope and exceptions](https://www.peraturan.go.id/files/permenperin-no-8-tahun-2025.pdf)

## Prepare a product-by-product evidence pack

A practical pack should connect the activity description, manufacturing or supply arrangement, product specification, proposed use, brand, relevant registration and conformity evidence. Record which document supports each product and which questions remain unresolved.

For a PT PMA, separately verify the investment and licensing conditions for the chosen activity and location. This article does not establish a universal foreign-ownership percentage or a guaranteed licensing timetable.

The commercial decision should follow that evidence. Before committing to a launch date, ask what remains to be approved and what the relevant authorisation actually covers. This is an existing-rule compliance guide, not an announcement of a new October 2026 licence.
