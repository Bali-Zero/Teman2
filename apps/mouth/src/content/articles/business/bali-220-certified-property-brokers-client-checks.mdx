## The Facts

## A certification update, not a census of Bali's brokers

AREBI Bali says 220 property brokers have obtained competency certificates. NusaBali's report of 8 October 2026 quotes regional chairman Michael Hikma Gunawan at a competency-testing session in Denpasar. It also reports a target of 500 brokers taking competency tests by the end of the year. The 220 figure is an association-reported milestone, not a count of every broker operating in Bali. [NusaBali reporting, 8 October](https://www.nusabali.com/berita/233058/arebi-220-broker-properti-di-bali-sudah-tersertifikasi).

For foreign residents comparing a villa lease, property purchase or agency appointment, the useful question is who will handle the transaction and what evidence supports their professional role. A headline about certification is a starting point for checks, not an endorsement of an individual agent.

## What the official guidance says

The Ministry of Trade's account of its certification programme links the requirement to Permendag 33/2025. It says property-intermediation businesses must be supported by BNSP-certified broker personnel and identifies 5 October 2026 as the compliance deadline under Article 16. The programme involves the ministry, BNSP, AREBI and LSP BPI. [Ministry of Trade: certification programme](https://ditjenpdn.kemendag.go.id/berita/sertifikasi-kompetensi-pelaku-usaha-jasa-perdagangan-real-estat-bidang-perantara-perdagangan-properti).

Association membership and competency certification should therefore be checked separately. Ask for the certificate, holder's name, issuing institution, scope and validity information, rather than accepting an AREBI logo alone.

NusaBali also reports the chairman's statement about uncertified brokers working in tandem with certified brokers. That statement should not be treated as a blanket legal exemption for an otherwise noncompliant business. A proposed arrangement still needs to satisfy the applicable business and personnel requirements.

## Before appointing an intermediary

As a practical document check, match the name in the agency agreement to the person and business presenting the service. Ask who is authorised to market the property, negotiate terms and receive any payment. If several agents are involved, request a written explanation of their roles.

Keep the fee arrangement, payment instructions and supporting credentials together. Unclear answers about who receives a deposit should be resolved before money changes hands. These are due-diligence steps, not a claim that a certificate guarantees a safe transaction.

## Keep the property checks separate

Broker credentials do not establish the seller's title, a landlord's authority, permitted use of a building or a foreign buyer's eligibility for a particular legal structure. Those questions require their own documents and review.

This article addresses clients using a broker. It does not advise a foreign individual to work as a broker or a foreign-owned company to establish a brokerage. Eligibility to provide brokerage services is a separate issue. For a proposed lease or acquisition, ask the reviewer to identify the parties, the intended use and the rights being granted.

## Bali Zero take

Treat the certification update as a reason to make your checks specific. Record who showed which document and who reviewed it. The aim is a traceable decision before signing, rather than confidence based on a sales pitch.
