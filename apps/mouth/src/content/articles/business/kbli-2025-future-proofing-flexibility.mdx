## KBLI 2025 Future-Proofing: How to Choose Flexible Business Codes for Maximum Pivot Room

Your KBLI code isn't just administrative paperwork—it's a legal boundary that defines what your company can and cannot do in Indonesia. Choose the wrong code, and you might find yourself locked into narrow operations, unable to pivot when market conditions change or new opportunities emerge.

In this strategic guide, we'll show you how to select KBLI 2025 codes that maximize business flexibility, identify umbrella codes that cover broad activities, and avoid codes that will limit your growth.

## Why KBLI Flexibility Matters

### The Lock-In Problem

When you register your PT PMA through OSS, your KBLI codes become part of your NIB (Nomor Induk Berusaha). These codes determine:

- **What activities you can legally conduct** - Any business activity outside your registered KBLI codes is technically unauthorized
- **What licenses you must obtain** - Each code triggers specific licensing requirements
- **What tax treatments apply** - Different codes have different tax implications
- **What foreign ownership is allowed** - PP 28/2025 sets ownership limits per code

**Real-world impact:** A software company registered only under 62020 (software consulting) cannot legally offer software products (62010) or cloud infrastructure (63112) without amending their NIB.

### The Cost of Getting It Wrong

If you need to change your KBLI codes later:

**Time costs:**

- NIB amendment through OSS: 1-2 weeks
- New licensing applications: 2-8 weeks (depending on code)
- Total disruption: 1-3 months of administrative limbo

**Financial costs:**

- NIB amendment: Free through OSS
- New licenses: Rp 500,000 - Rp 5,000,000 per license
- Legal/consulting fees: Rp 5,000,000 - Rp 15,000,000
- Business interruption: Potentially much higher

**Strategic costs:**

- Client contracts may need amendment
- Bank accounts may require documentation updates
- Investor confidence can be shaken by "pivot chaos"

## The Umbrella Code Strategy

### What Makes a Code "Flexible"?

Flexible KBLI codes share these characteristics:

1. **Broad activity scope** - Cover multiple related business models under one code
2. **Technology-agnostic** - Don't specify particular tools, methods, or platforms
3. **Sector-spanning** - Can serve multiple industries
4. **Minimal licensing** - Don't require heavy permits that limit operations
5. **100% foreign ownership** - No Indonesian partner required (for PT PMA)

## Codes to AVOID for Flexibility

### High-Risk Codes That Lock You In

Some KBLI codes are so narrow or heavily regulated that they severely limit your ability to pivot:

| AVOID Code                               | Why It's Rigid                                                                 | Licensing Burden                                          |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **10xxx (Food Manufacturing)**           | Locks you into specific food products; changing product type requires new code | BPOM registration, halal certification, factory licensing |
| **86xxx (Healthcare Services)**          | Requires medical professionals; heavy Ministry of Health oversight             | Clinic license, medical director, annual audits           |
| **85xxx (Formal Education)**             | Ministry of Education approval; strict curriculum requirements                 | Teaching permits, Ministry approval, accreditation        |
| **64xxx (Financial Services)**           | OJK (Financial Services Authority) licensing; capital requirements             | OJK license (Rp 50M+ capital), ongoing compliance         |
| **62091 (Specific Industry Software)**   | Tied to particular industry; less flexible than 62010                          | Minimal licensing but narrow market                       |
| **18xxx (Printing Services)**            | Traditional printing only; doesn't cover digital services                      | Press license (potentially)                               |
| **21xxx (Pharmaceutical Manufacturing)** | Extremely strict BPOM rules; long approval timelines                           | BPOM manufacturing license, GMP certification             |

### Why These Codes Are Problematic

**Food manufacturing (10xxx) example:**

- You register 10790 (manufacture of other food products) to make granola bars
- Market changes, you want to pivot to protein shakes
- Protein shakes are classified under 10500 (manufacture of dairy products)
- You need a new KBLI code, new BPOM registration, potentially new factory certification
- Timeline: 3-6 months, cost: Rp 20,000,000+

**Healthcare (86xxx) example:**

- You open a physiotherapy clinic (86903)
- You want to add general medical services
- General medical services require 86101 (hospital activities) or 86102 (medical practice)
- These codes require medical doctors on staff, different licenses, Ministry of Health approval
- You can't pivot without essentially starting a new business line from scratch

## PP 28/2025 Impact on Flexibility

### How the New Regulations Changed the Game

PP 28/2025 (the 2025 Positive Investment List) significantly restructured many KBLI codes, splitting previously broad categories into more specific subcategories.

**What changed:**

**IT services (Section J62xxx):**

- **KBLI 2020:** 62010 covered very broad "computer programming"
- **KBLI 2025:** 62010 still broad but clearer definitions
- New subcodes added: 62091 (industry-specific software), 62099 (other software)
- **Impact:** 62010 remains flexible but watch for regulatory drift toward narrower definitions

**Real estate (Section L68xxx):**

- **KBLI 2020:** 68100 covered most real estate activities
- **KBLI 2025:** Split into 14 distinct codes (68101, 68102, 68103, etc.)
- **Impact:** Real estate operators now need multiple codes for diversified portfolios

**E-commerce (Section G47xxx):**

- **KBLI 2020:** 47911 covered most online retail
- **KBLI 2025:** 47911 (food e-commerce), 47912 (beverage), 47919 (other retail)
- **Impact:** General merchandise sellers use 47919; food/beverage sellers need specific codes

### Winners and Losers

**Codes that GAINED flexibility:**

- 62010 (software) - Explicitly covers AI/ML, blockchain
- 70201 (management consulting) - Broader scope confirmed
- 63999 (other information services) - Catch-all for digital services

**Codes that LOST flexibility:**

- Real estate codes (68xxx) - Much more granular
- Education codes (85xxx) - Stricter definitions
- Food retail (47xxx) - Split by product type

## How to Change KBLI Codes (If You Must)

### The OSS Amendment Process

**Step 1: Assess necessity**

- Can you operate under existing codes by interpreting them broadly?
- Is the new activity substantial or just occasional?
- Will the new code trigger problematic licensing?

**Step 2: Plan the transition**

- Identify new codes needed
- Research licensing requirements
- Budget time and money
- Inform clients/partners if necessary

**Step 3: Submit OSS amendment**

- Log into OSS RBA (Risk-Based Approach) system
- Navigate to NIB amendment
- Add/remove KBLI codes
- Submit supporting documents (business plan update, etc.)

**Step 4: Obtain new licenses**

- OSS will issue new license requirements based on new codes
- Apply for each required license
- Wait for approval (timeline varies)

**Step 5: Update operational documents**

- Inform tax office (if activities change substantially)
- Update business cards, website, contracts
- Notify bank if business activity description changes

### Realistic Timeline

- **Simple amendment (no new licensing):** 1-2 weeks
- **Moderate complexity (1-2 new licenses):** 1-2 months
- **Complex change (heavy licensing):** 2-4 months

### When to Just Register a New Company Instead

Sometimes it's faster and cleaner to set up a new PT PMA:

**Register new company if:**

- New activity requires incompatible licensing (e.g., adding healthcare to tech company)
- Foreign ownership limits differ (e.g., current company is 100% foreign, new code allows only 67%)
- You want to keep businesses legally separate for liability reasons
- The administrative burden of amendment exceeds new company setup cost

**Cost comparison:**

- NIB amendment + licensing: Rp 5M - Rp 20M
- New PT PMA setup: Rp 30M - Rp 50M (but you get a clean slate)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Choosing the Most Specific Code

**Wrong approach:** "I build e-commerce websites, so I'll register 62091 (industry-specific software for retail)."

**Problem:** Locks you into e-commerce only. Can't pivot to mobile apps, AI, or other software types.

**Right approach:** Register 62010 (general software programming) which covers e-commerce AND everything else.

### Mistake 2: Registering Too Many Codes

**Wrong approach:** "More codes = more flexibility, so I'll register 10 KBLI codes covering every possible activity."

**Problem:** Each code triggers licensing requirements. You'll drown in compliance overhead and may not be able to realistically maintain all licenses.

**Right approach:** 2-4 strategic codes that genuinely reflect your business model and realistic expansion plans.

### Mistake 3: Ignoring Licensing Implications

**Wrong approach:** "70201 (management consulting) covers HR consulting, perfect!"

**Problem:** If you later want to add recruitment services (78100), that code requires BPJS employment registration, LTKI (foreign worker placement) licensing if placing foreigners, and Ministry of Manpower oversight.

**Right approach:** Research licensing for ALL codes before selection. Choose codes where you can realistically comply with all requirements.

### Mistake 4: Not Planning for Scale

**Wrong approach:** "I'm starting small, I'll just register 1 code and add more later."

**Problem:** Adding codes later requires NIB amendment, potentially new capital injection (if moving to higher-risk activities), and business disruption.

**Right approach:** Register 2-3 codes from day one that cover your current + likely future activities. The upfront cost is minimal compared to amendment later.

### Mistake 5: Copying Competitors Without Context

**Wrong approach:** "Competitor X registered these codes, so I'll use the same ones."

**Problem:** Their business model, licensing capacity, and strategic goals may be completely different from yours.

**Right approach:** Understand WHY competitors chose their codes, then select based on YOUR specific business strategy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I change my KBLI code after registration?

Yes, but it requires updating your NIB through OSS, potentially obtaining new licenses, and may take 2-4 weeks minimum (up to 2-4 months if complex licensing is involved). It's significantly cheaper and faster to choose the right codes from the start.

### What are the most flexible KBLI codes?

The top flexible codes are:

- **62010** (software programming) - covers web, mobile, AI, blockchain, SaaS
- **70201** (management consulting) - covers strategy, operations, market research
- **47919** (general e-commerce) - covers most online retail
- **74100** (design activities) - covers graphic, UX/UI, branding, interior design
- **73100** (advertising) - covers digital marketing, social media, campaigns

### Should I register multiple KBLI codes?

Yes, for maximum flexibility. Most PT PMA companies register 2-5 codes. There's no extra government fee per code in the OSS system, but each code may trigger additional licensing requirements. The strategic benefit of flexibility usually outweighs the extra compliance burden.

### What KBLI codes should I avoid for flexibility?

Avoid:

- **Healthcare codes (86xxx)** - heavy Ministry of Health licensing, medical staff requirements
- **Formal education (85xxx)** - Ministry of Education approval, strict curriculum rules
- **Financial services (64xxx)** - OJK licensing, high capital requirements
- **Very specific manufacturing codes** - lock you into one product type
- **Codes requiring AMDAL** - environmental impact assessment adds 6-12 months to any change

### What happens if I operate outside my registered KBLI codes?

Operating outside your registered KBLI codes is technically unauthorized business activity. Consequences can include:

- Fines from Ministry of Trade or OSS authority
- Invalidation of contracts (clients can claim agreements are void)
- Tax complications (tax office may challenge deductions)
- Licensing revocation in severe cases
- Issues with banking (transactions may be flagged as suspicious)

In practice, enforcement is inconsistent, but the risk increases with company size and visibility. Safer to register correct codes upfront.

### How often can I change my KBLI codes?

Technically, there's no limit on how often you can amend your NIB. However:

- Each amendment takes time and administrative effort
- Frequent changes raise red flags with tax authorities and banks
- Some licenses have minimum validity periods (you can't just drop them immediately)
- Excessive changes suggest poor planning, which can hurt investor/lender confidence

Best practice: Choose flexible codes initially, make amendments only when genuinely necessary (major pivot or new business line).

### Do more KBLI codes mean higher government fees?

No. The OSS NIB registration fee structure is NOT based on number of KBLI codes. You pay the same government fee whether you register 1 code or 5 codes.

However:

- Each code may require separate licenses (which have individual fees)
- More codes = more compliance overhead (staff time, legal fees)
- Some codes trigger industry-specific taxes or levies

Total cost depends on WHICH codes you choose, not HOW MANY.

### Can I register broad codes like 62010 and narrow codes like 62091 together?

Yes, you can register both general and specific codes simultaneously. However, this is usually redundant:

- 62010 (general software programming) already covers the activities in 62091 (industry-specific software)
- Registering both doesn't give you additional flexibility
- It adds unnecessary complexity to your NIB

Better approach: Choose the broadest code that covers all your activities. Only register narrower codes if there's a specific regulatory or client requirement.

### What if my planned business model doesn't fit any KBLI code?

This is rare but can happen with very new business models (e.g., early cryptocurrency businesses, novel AI services). Options:

1. **Find the closest umbrella code:** Most innovative businesses can fit under broad codes like 62010 (software), 70201 (consulting), or 63999 (other information services)
2. **Register multiple related codes:** Combine 2-3 codes that together cover your activities
3. **Consult Ministry of Trade:** For truly novel businesses, you may need a formal interpretation from OSS/Ministry of Trade
4. **Wait for KBLI updates:** KBLI is revised every 5 years; very new activities may not have codes yet

Most "no code fits" situations are actually "I'm being too specific." Think broader about what your business actually does.
