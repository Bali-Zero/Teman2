A joint task force comprising **Tax Office (Ditjen Pajak)** and **Immigration officials** has begun surprise audits targeting PMA companies that hold residential freehold properties through local nominee arrangements in Badung Regency.

### What Happened

| Detail | Information |
|--------|-------------|
| **Location** | Pererenan, Badung Regency |
| **Properties affected** | 12 villas sealed |
| **Enforcement agencies** | Tax Office + Immigration (joint task force) |
| **Target** | PMA companies holding residential property via nominees |
| **Action taken** | Property sealing, document seizure |

The task force appears to be focusing on structures where:
- Foreign-owned PMA companies acquire **residential properties**
- Using Indonesian nationals as **legal nominees**
- For what appears to be **personal use**, not legitimate business operations

This practice exists in a legal gray area and the raids represent a **major escalation** in enforcement activity.

### High Risk

**You should be concerned if:**

- Your PMA company's **primary purpose** appears to be holding your villa
- You cannot demonstrate legitimate **business use** of the property
- Your property is in premium areas (Canggu, Seminyak, Pererenan, Uluwatu)

### Medium Risk

**Potential exposure if:**
- Your PMA company owns **multiple properties** including residential
- You mix personal use with business operations
- Your rental income is not properly reported

### Lower Risk (but not zero)

**Less immediate concern if:**
- You use proper **leasehold** structures (Hak Pakai, not freehold)

- You have proper documentation of business operations

2. **Access restricted** pending investigation

The sealing is typically done under tax enforcement authority, but Immigration presence suggests they're also building cases for visa violations.

## LEGAL BACKGROUND

### Why Nominee Structures Exist

Foreign individuals **cannot own freehold (Hak Milik) land** in Indonesia. This created a market for workarounds:

**Common structures:**
1. **Nominee arrangements**: Indonesian citizen holds property "on behalf of" foreigner
2. **PMA company ownership**: Company holds property, foreigner owns shares
3. **Hybrid structures**: PMA company using Indonesian nominees as shareholders/directors

### The Legal Problem

These structures have always existed in a gray area:
- Not explicitly legal
- Not always prosecuted
- Commonly used
- **Always risky**

The new enforcement suggests the gray area is **darkening**.

### What the Law Actually Says

**Agrarian Law (UUPA 1960) Article 21:**
- Only Indonesian citizens can hold Hak Milik (freehold)
- Foreign individuals limited to Hak Pakai (right to use) - maximum 80 years

**Investment Law implications:**
- PMA companies can own commercial property
- Residential property ownership by PMA is **questionable**
- Using nominees to circumvent foreign ownership restrictions is **high risk**

**Your situation:**
- PMA company owns 3 villas
- Properly rented through licensed entity
- Taxes filed and paid
- But Indonesian "partner" is actually nominee

**Your risk:** **HIGH**
- Nominee structure still problematic
- Better than Scenario 1 due to business operations
- Tax compliance helps but doesn't eliminate risk

**Recommended action:**
- Review shareholder agreements
- Document legitimate business operations
- Consider bringing nominee into genuine partnership or buying them out
- Ensure rental licenses are current

### Scenario 3: Long-term Leasehold Holder

**Your situation:**
- You hold a 25-year Hak Pakai lease
- Renewable for another 25 years
- No nominee involved
- Proper lease agreement registered

**Your risk:** **LOW**
- This is the legally compliant structure
- Not a target of current enforcement
- Standard legal protections apply

**Recommended action:**
- Maintain lease payments
- Keep documentation current
- Ensure lease is properly registered
- Consider extension timeline before expiry
