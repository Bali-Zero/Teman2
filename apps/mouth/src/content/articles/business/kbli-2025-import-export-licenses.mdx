## KBLI 2025 Import & Export Licenses: API-U, API-P, and Trade Permits by Business Code

**The nightmare scenario:** You've registered your PT PMA, invested IDR 10 billion, and ordered your first container of raw materials. The shipment arrives at Tanjung Priok. Customs rejects it. **Reason:** Your KBLI code doesn't match your import activities, and you don't have the right API license.

This isn't a hypothetical. It happens to dozens of foreign businesses every month. The problem? **Indonesia's import/export licensing system is directly tied to your KBLI classification**. Choose the wrong code during company registration, and you'll be blocked at customs—sometimes permanently.

With KBLI 2025 adding 2,661 new codes and reorganizing entire sectors, the stakes have never been higher. This guide decodes which business codes require import licenses (API-U vs. API-P), export permits, and how to navigate Indonesia's Lartas (prohibited/restricted goods) system without losing millions in detained shipments.

## Understanding Indonesia's Import License System

Indonesia has two primary import licenses, and they're **not interchangeable**:

### API-U (Angka Pengenal Importir Umum) – General Importer ID

**For:** Trading companies that import finished goods for resale.

**Eligible KBLI codes:**

- **46xxx (Wholesale trade)** – All subcategories
- **47xxx (Retail trade)** – Including e-commerce (47911)
- **77xxx (Rental and leasing)** – For importing equipment to rent

**What you can import:**

- Finished consumer goods
- Products ready for retail sale
- Pre-packaged items requiring no additional manufacturing

**What you CANNOT import with API-U:**

- Raw materials for production (use API-P)
- Industrial machinery for own-use manufacturing
- Goods intended for re-export without modification

**Registration:** Obtained automatically through OSS when your NIB includes trading KBLI codes and you apply for import facilities.

### API-P (Angka Pengenal Importir Produsen) – Producer Importer ID

**For:** Manufacturing companies importing raw materials, components, or machinery for production.

**Eligible KBLI codes:**

- **10xxx-33xxx (Manufacturing sector)** – All categories
- **41xxx-43xxx (Construction)** – For project-specific imports
- **72xxx (Scientific R&D)** – For research materials

**What you can import:**

- Raw materials for manufacturing
- Semi-finished goods for assembly
- Industrial machinery and equipment
- Spare parts for production equipment

**Critical restriction:** Goods imported under API-P **cannot be resold in their imported state**. You must demonstrate transformation/manufacturing. Customs audits verify this.

**Registration:** Requires proof of production facilities (factory lease/ownership, machinery invoices, production line photos). OSS will request these documents during NIB registration.

## API-U vs. API-P: The Critical Differences

| Aspect                  | API-U (General Importer)                 | API-P (Producer Importer)                  |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Primary purpose**     | Importing goods for resale               | Importing for manufacturing                |
| **Eligible businesses** | Trading companies, retailers, e-commerce | Manufacturers, producers                   |
| **Goods allowed**       | Finished products ready for sale         | Raw materials, components, machinery       |
| **Resale restriction**  | Allowed and expected                     | **Prohibited** – goods must be transformed |
| **Proof required**      | Business address, warehouse              | Factory facilities, production equipment   |
| **Customs scrutiny**    | Moderate (value/classification checks)   | **High** (production records audited)      |
| **KBLI alignment**      | Must be trade/retail code (46xxx/47xxx)  | Must be manufacturing code (10xxx-33xxx)   |
| **OSS integration**     | Direct approval if KBLI matches          | Requires facility verification documents   |

**Real case:** A Bali-based surf shop (KBLI 47640 – Retail of sports goods) registered with API-U to import surfboards. **No issue.** A year later, they wanted to import raw fiberglass and resin to manufacture custom boards. Customs rejected it—API-U doesn't cover raw materials. **Solution:** They had to register a second company with KBLI 30992 (Manufacture of sports goods) and obtain API-P.

## Export Permits and KBLI 2025

Not all exports require permits, but certain goods and sectors do. Here's the breakdown:

### ET (Eksportir Terdaftar) – Registered Exporter

**Required for:**

- Agricultural products (coffee, spices, palm oil)
- Mining products (coal, minerals)
- Forestry products (wood, rattan)
- Fishery products

**Eligible KBLI codes:**

- **01xxx-03xxx** (Agriculture, forestry, fishery)
- **05xxx-09xxx** (Mining and quarrying)
- **10xxx-12xxx** (Food manufacturing – if exporting processed goods)
- **46xxx** (Wholesale trade – export-oriented)

**Registration:** Through OSS after company formation. Requires proof of export capacity (contracts, facility audit, product samples).

### Export Incentives Tied to KBLI

Indonesia offers **tax holidays** and **super deductions** for export-oriented businesses. Eligibility depends on your KBLI code:

**Tax holiday eligible sectors (PP 55/2019):**

- Manufacturing (10xxx-33xxx) with minimum 30% export commitment
- Economic infrastructure (energy, telecom)
- High-tech industries (electronics, pharma)

**Super tax deduction (PMK 128/2019):**

- Vocational training for export workforce
- R&D for export product development

**Note:** Your KBLI must clearly demonstrate export focus. A retail code (47xxx) won't qualify—you need a manufacturing or wholesale code with documented export activities.

## Lartas: Prohibited and Restricted Goods

**Lartas (Larangan dan Pembatasan)** are government restrictions on importing/exporting specific goods, **regardless of your API license type**. These are enforced at customs and require additional permits.

### Common Lartas Categories and KBLI Codes Affected

#### 1. Food and Beverages

**Requires:**

- **BPOM ML (Nomor Izin Edar)** – Food/drug distribution permit
- **Halal certification** (mandatory since Oct 2024, PP 39/2021)
- **Karantina clearance** – For agricultural products

**Affected KBLI codes:**

- 10xxx-11xxx (Food/beverage manufacturing)
- 46311-46339 (Wholesale of food/beverage)
- 47111-47199 (Supermarket/retail with food)
- 47911 (E-commerce selling food)

**Processing time:** 3-6 months for BPOM registration (product testing required).

#### 2. Electronics and IT Equipment

**Requires:**

- **SNI (Standar Nasional Indonesia)** – Indonesian National Standard certification
- **SDPPI/Postel type approval** – For telecom/radio frequency devices

**Affected KBLI codes:**

- 26xxx (Electronics manufacturing)
- 46510-46599 (Wholesale of electronics)
- 47410-47499 (Retail of electronics)

**Processing time:** 1-3 months for SNI, 2-4 months for Postel.

**Common issue:** Importing smartphones without IMEI registration results in device blocking by Postel.

#### 3. Textiles and Apparel

**Requires:**

- **Permendag quota allocation** (annual import quotas)
- **Verified importer status** (audit by Ministry of Trade)

**Affected KBLI codes:**

- 13xxx-14xxx (Textile/apparel manufacturing)
- 46410-46499 (Wholesale of textiles)
- 47510-47599 (Retail of textiles)

**Processing time:** Quota applications open annually (usually Q4 for next year).

#### 4. Chemicals and Hazardous Materials

**Requires:**

- **K3L permit** (Environmental and safety license)
- **Import permit from Ministry of Industry**
- **MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet)** documentation

**Affected KBLI codes:**

- 20xxx (Chemical manufacturing)
- 21xxx (Pharmaceutical manufacturing)
- 46750 (Wholesale of chemical products)

**Processing time:** 2-4 months (includes environmental impact assessment).

#### 5. Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices

**Requires:**

- **BPOM pharma license** (for drugs)
- **AKL/AKD** (Medical device approval)
- **Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP)** certification

**Affected KBLI codes:**

- 21001 (Pharmaceutical manufacturing)
- 32501 (Medical instrument manufacturing)
- 46460 (Wholesale of pharma/medical)

**Processing time:** 6-12 months (clinical trials may be required).

## The KBLI-Customs Alignment Problem

**The most expensive mistake:** Registering your company with a KBLI code that doesn't match your actual import/export activities.

### Real Case Studies

**Case 1: The Misclassified Manufacturer**

A furniture company registered as KBLI 47591 (Retail sale of furniture). They assumed this covered importing raw wood to build furniture. **Result:** Customs rejected their wood imports because retail codes require API-U, which only covers finished goods. **Fix:** Had to register a new company with KBLI 31001 (Manufacture of furniture) and API-P. Cost: IDR 150 million in detained goods + 6 months delay.

**Case 2: The E-commerce Trap**

An online fashion store (KBLI 47911 – E-commerce retail) imported garments from China. First shipment cleared fine. Second shipment was flagged because their OSS profile showed no import facility registration. **Result:** Permendag required them to obtain API-U formally, submit warehouse proof, and pay retroactive fines. Cost: IDR 45 million + 2 months shipment delay.

**Case 3: The Export Incentive Denial**

A coffee processing company registered as KBLI 46380 (Wholesale of beverages) to export specialty coffee. Applied for tax holiday. **Rejected:** Wholesale codes don't qualify—only manufacturing codes do. **Fix:** Re-registered as KBLI 10831 (Tea and coffee processing) to qualify. Cost: 1 year lost tax holiday benefits (estimated IDR 2 billion in tax savings).

### How to Avoid KBLI-Import Mismatch

**Step 1: Reverse-engineer your license needs**

Before choosing KBLI codes, map out:

- What are you importing? (Raw materials → API-P, Finished goods → API-U)
- What are you exporting? (Agricultural/mining → ET required)
- Any Lartas goods? (Add 3-12 months to timeline)

**Step 2: Choose the right KBLI hierarchy**

If you're manufacturing AND trading:

- **Primary KBLI:** Manufacturing code (10xxx-33xxx) → Qualifies for API-P
- **Secondary KBLI:** Wholesale/retail code (46xxx/47xxx) → Qualifies for API-U

Example: A garment factory that also runs an e-commerce store:

- Primary: KBLI 14111 (Manufacture of men's outerwear)
- Secondary: KBLI 47911 (E-commerce retail)
- Result: Eligible for both API-P (import fabrics) and API-U (import finished accessories to resell)

**Step 3: Verify during NIB application**

When applying for NIB through OSS:

1. Check the "Import Facility" box
2. System will auto-generate required permits based on KBLI
3. Upload supporting documents:
   - For API-P: Factory address, machinery invoices, production flow diagram
   - For API-U: Warehouse address, business plan showing trading activities

**Step 4: Pre-clearance with Customs**

Before your first shipment, file a **PIB (Pemberitahuan Impor Barang)** test submission with Customs. They'll verify:

- KBLI code matches goods
- API license is active
- Lartas permits are complete

This catches issues before your container arrives.

## Step-by-Step: Obtaining Import License After Company Formation

Assuming your PT PMA is already registered with correct KBLI codes:

### For API-U (Traders)

**Week 1-2:**

1. Log into OSS (oss.go.id)
2. Navigate to "Perizinan Berusaha" → "Fasilitas Impor"
3. Select API-U
4. Upload:
   - Warehouse address proof (rental agreement or ownership certificate)
   - Business plan showing import-for-resale activities
   - NPWP and NIB
5. Submit

**Week 2-3:**

- OSS auto-verifies if KBLI matches (46xxx/47xxx)
- If approved, API-U is added to your NIB certificate
- Download updated NIB

**Week 3-4:**

- Register with Customs (CEISA system) using NIB
- Appoint customs broker
- File first PIB for test clearance

**Total time:** 3-4 weeks

### For API-P (Manufacturers)

**Week 1-3:**

1. Gather facility documentation:
   - Factory address proof
   - Machinery purchase invoices
   - Photos of production line (timestamped)
   - Production flow diagram (raw material → finished product)
2. Log into OSS → "Fasilitas Impor" → Select API-P
3. Upload all facility docs
4. Submit

**Week 3-5:**

- OSS may request on-site verification (for high-risk industries)
- Verifier visits factory within 2-3 weeks
- Verification report submitted

**Week 5-6:**

- API-P approved and added to NIB
- Download updated NIB certificate

**Week 6-8:**

- Register with Customs (CEISA)
- Submit production plan to Customs (required for API-P audit trail)
- File first PIB with detailed HS codes

**Total time:** 6-8 weeks

## Common Mistakes That Trigger Import Denial

### Mistake 1: Using API-P Goods for Resale

**Scenario:** A furniture manufacturer (KBLI 31001, API-P) imports teak wood. They make 100 tables, but sell 50 raw wood planks to another carpenter.

**Problem:** API-P prohibits reselling imported raw materials. Customs audits caught this via cross-referencing import records with sales invoices.

**Penalty:** IDR 200 million fine + API-P suspension for 6 months.

### Mistake 2: Importing Lartas Goods Without Pre-Approval

**Scenario:** An e-commerce business (KBLI 47911, API-U) imports Korean skincare products. No BPOM registration.

**Problem:** All cosmetics require BPOM ML permit **before** import. Customs will not release goods without it.

**Penalty:** Goods detained indefinitely or destroyed. No refund on shipping/customs fees.

### Mistake 3: KBLI Code Too Vague

**Scenario:** A company registered as KBLI 46900 (Wholesale of non-specialized goods) imports electronics, textiles, and food.

**Problem:** Customs flagged the import as suspicious—how can one company handle such diverse Lartas categories? Triggered deep audit.

**Result:** 4-month clearance delay while providing individual Lartas permits for each product category.

**Fix:** Use specific KBLI codes for each product line (46510 for electronics, 46410 for textiles, 46311 for food).

### Mistake 4: No Customs Broker Pre-Registration

**Scenario:** A startup imports their first container. Tries to clear Customs without a registered broker.

**Problem:** Indonesia requires PPJK (Pengusaha Pengurusan Jasa Kepabeanan) – licensed customs broker – for commercial imports. DIY clearance is only allowed for small value imports (<USD 1,500).

**Penalty:** Shipment held at port. Daily storage fees (IDR 500k-2 million/day) accumulate.

## Tax Implications of Import/Export KBLI Codes

### Import Duties and KBLI Alignment

Indonesia uses HS codes (Harmonized System) for customs duties, but **Customs cross-checks HS codes against your KBLI**.

**Example:**

- You import "coffee beans" (HS code 0901.11)
- Your KBLI is 47111 (Supermarket retail)
- **Customs question:** Why is a retailer importing raw coffee beans? (Should be roasted/packaged for retail)
- **Result:** Audit trigger. You may need to justify or provide proof of packaging facility.

**Proper alignment:**

- KBLI 10831 (Coffee processing) → Can import raw beans (HS 0901.11) for roasting
- KBLI 47111 (Supermarket) → Should import packaged coffee (HS 2101.11)

### Export Tax Incentives

**PPh 22 Import Tax:**

- API-U: 2.5% of import value (prepaid income tax)
- API-P: 0.5% of import value (lower rate for manufacturers)

**Export Tax Exemption:**

- Companies with KBLI 46xxx (export-oriented wholesale) can apply for PPh 22 export exemption
- Requires proof of minimum 50% revenue from exports

**Super Deduction (150% tax deduction):**

- Available for export-focused manufacturers
- Eligible KBLI: 10xxx-33xxx with documented export contracts
- Deduction applies to vocational training and R&D costs for export products
