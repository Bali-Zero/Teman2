## The Facts

## Define what the business will actually do

A company distilling essential oils, a trader buying finished bottles and a brand commissioning production from another manufacturer should not assume they need identical business classifications or operating documents.

The official OSS description for KBLI 20294 covers essential oil manufacturing and gives examples including clove, nutmeg, lemongrass and patchouli oils. The listing is a starting point for matching the production activity, not proof that every essential-oil-related business falls into the same category. [Primary OSS listing](https://oss.go.id/id/kbli/detail/e4bc5542-3dfd-4b24-81ec-f22516311182).

## Check the project, not just the five-digit code

Before committing to a site or equipment, document the intended inputs, process, output, capacity and location. Use those facts to check the current OSS scope and the licences, certificates and supporting requirements applicable to the project.

Do not infer one universal risk tier, environmental document or processing timeline for all facilities from the code alone. A location review should also establish whether the proposed activity is compatible with the site and which spatial and environmental requirements apply.

For a foreign-investment project, review the permitted activity, ownership conditions, company structure and investment requirements together. A general statement that an industry is attractive does not establish that a specific foreign-investment proposal is eligible.

## Industrial reporting is a separate workstream

The Ministry of Industry explains that Permenperin 13/2025 governs submission of industrial and related data through SIINas. A manufacturing business should identify the reporting obligations applicable to it and assign responsibility for maintaining the information. [Primary Ministry of Industry explanation](https://imc.kemenperin.go.id/berita/kemenperin-sosialisasikan-permenperin-132025-tentang-pelaporan-data-industri-melalui-siinas).

Keep the facility's operational records consistent with its actual production. Registering a company does not, by itself, explain whether all project-specific operating and reporting requirements have been met.

## Export documents depend on the goods and transaction

Indonesian Customs explains the relationship between an NIB and customs access for importers and exporters. Its guidance should be used when checking the exporting entity's registration rather than assuming a generic additional “APE” document is always required. [Primary customs guidance](https://www.beacukai.go.id/registrasi-kepabeanan).

Customs also provides guidance on supporting documents for export declarations. Confirm the declaration route, goods classification and applicable restrictions for the actual shipment. [Export-document guidance](https://beacukai.go.id/mandatory-upload-dokap-peb).

Ask the destination-market importer and relevant authorities which product, safety, origin or quarantine documents apply. A phytosanitary certificate, a buyer's laboratory specification and an Indonesian business licence serve different purposes; none should be presented as universally interchangeable or universally required.
