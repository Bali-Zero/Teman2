## The Facts

Bali continues to attract a significant wave of American expatriates drawn by the island's combination of natural beauty, relatively low cost of living compared to US metros, and a maturing ecosystem of high-end sustainable accommodation. The eco-luxury villa segment has expanded considerably in recent years, with properties increasingly designed to meet international environmental standards while commanding premium rental and sale prices.

For US citizens, establishing legal residency in Indonesia requires navigating a layered visa framework. The most commonly used pathways include the Second Home Visa, which allows stays of up to ten years for qualifying applicants who can demonstrate sufficient financial assets, and the Digital Nomad Visa category, which targets remote workers. Each pathway carries distinct requirements regarding proof of income, health insurance coverage, and sponsorship obligations.

Property ownership in Indonesia remains restricted for foreigners under national land law. Americans and other non-Indonesian nationals cannot hold Hak Milik, or freehold title. Instead, the available legal structures include Hak Pakai, a right-to-use title available to foreign individuals holding a valid residence permit, leasehold arrangements typically structured for 25 to 30 years with extension options, and nominee ownership via an Indonesian entity, though the latter carries significant legal risk and is no longer recommended under current regulatory guidance.

The eco-luxury segment in Bali — particularly in areas such as Canggu, Ubud, Pererenan, and the Bukit Peninsula — has seen villa rental yields remain competitive despite global economic headwinds. Short-term rental platforms continue to drive demand, though local regulations in several banjar and kabupaten jurisdictions are tightening rules around villa licensing, zoning compliance, and environmental permits.

Tax obligations for US expats in Bali are notably complex because the United States taxes its citizens on worldwide income regardless of residency. Americans living in Bali must continue filing US federal returns while also potentially incurring Indonesian income tax obligations if they meet local residency thresholds. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion and Foreign Tax Credit mechanisms exist to mitigate double taxation, but professional cross-border tax advice is strongly recommended.
