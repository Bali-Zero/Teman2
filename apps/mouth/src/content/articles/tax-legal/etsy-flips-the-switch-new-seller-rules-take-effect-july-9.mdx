## The Facts

Etsy, the global marketplace for handmade and vintage goods, rolled out a revised seller policy framework effective July 9, 2026. The changes were flagged in advance through the platform's seller communications and are understood to affect identity verification requirements, tax information collection, and the mechanics of how Etsy remits collected taxes in jurisdictions where it operates as a marketplace facilitator.

Etsy has been on a multi-year trajectory of tightening compliance infrastructure, particularly around cross-border selling. Since 2021, the company has progressively expanded its tax collection and remittance obligations across dozens of markets, effectively acting as the tax collector on behalf of sellers in those jurisdictions. The July 2026 updates appear to extend that architecture further, with a particular emphasis on seller identity validation and the accuracy of tax identification data submitted at account level.

For sellers operating from Indonesia, the mechanics are layered. Etsy already collects Indonesian VAT (PPN) at the point of sale for digital goods delivered to Indonesian buyers under the country's PMSE (Perdagangan Melalui Sistem Elektronik) framework, which has been in force since 2020. However, sellers based in Indonesia who export physical or digital goods internationally face a different question: how Etsy's platform-side tax logic interacts with their own Indonesian income tax obligations and, where applicable, their business licensing status.

The platform's shift toward requiring more granular tax identification from sellers — including taxpayer identification numbers (NPWP in Indonesia's case) — mirrors a broader global trend of tax authorities pressuring marketplace operators to become de facto compliance agents. The OECD's Model Rules for Reporting by Platform Operators, adopted by an expanding number of countries, provides the regulatory scaffolding for this approach.

Sellers who have historically operated informally — running an Etsy shop as a side income without formally registering a business entity or declaring earnings to the Indonesian Directorate General of Taxes (DJP) — are most exposed to the compliance gap that tighter platform rules illuminate. The platform itself does not determine Indonesian tax liability, but the data it now holds increasingly overlaps with what tax authorities can access under automatic exchange of information agreements.
