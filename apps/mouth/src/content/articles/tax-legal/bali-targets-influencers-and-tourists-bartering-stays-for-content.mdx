## The Facts

Indonesian immigration authorities in Bali have moved to enforce existing visa regulations against a practice that has become widespread in the island's hospitality and lifestyle economy: foreign nationals on visitor visas accepting complimentary accommodation, meals, spa treatments, or experiences in exchange for social media posts, reels, or promotional content.

Under Indonesian law, a visitor visa — whether the B1 Visa on Arrival or the C1 Tourist visa — does not authorize any form of economic activity on Indonesian soil. The critical legal threshold is not whether money changes hands, but whether any economic value is transferred. Receiving a free villa stay worth IDR 5 million in exchange for an Instagram post constitutes, in the eyes of Indonesian immigration and tax authorities, the performance of a commercial service without the appropriate work authorization.

Bali's immigration office, operating under the Directorate General of Immigration (Ditjen Imigrasi), has coordinated enforcement through the Dharma Dewata Task Force, a multi-agency operation launched in April 2026 that combines immigration, police, and regional government resources. The task force has already recorded hundreds of deportations and detentions of foreign nationals across Bali in the first months of 2026, with influencer-related violations forming a growing subset of cases.

The legal basis for enforcement is longstanding. Law No. 6 of 2011 on Immigration prohibits foreigners from engaging in work or business activities inconsistent with their visa type. Separately, Indonesia's income tax framework under Law No. 36 of 2008 on Income Tax treats barter arrangements as taxable income — a point that implicates both the foreign content creator and the Indonesian business providing the complimentary service, which may be liable for withholding obligations.

Authorities have signaled that the crackdown is not limited to obvious social media personalities with large followings. Micro-influencers and individuals who negotiate complimentary arrangements privately, even without formal sponsorship contracts, are equally exposed. Immigration officers have begun reviewing social media accounts of detained or flagged foreigners as part of standard enforcement procedure, using publicly visible posts and tagged locations as evidence of unauthorized economic activity during a visitor visa stay.
