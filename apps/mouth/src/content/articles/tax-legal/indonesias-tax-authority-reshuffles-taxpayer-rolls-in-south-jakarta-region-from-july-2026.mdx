## The Facts

Indonesia's Directorate General of Taxes (Direktorat Jenderal Pajak, DJP) has announced a reorganisation of taxpayer registration across the South Jakarta Regional Tax Office — known as Kanwil DJP Jakarta Selatan or Kanwil Jaksus — set to take effect on 1 July 2026. The measure involves a redistribution of registered taxpayers among the various Tax Service Offices (Kantor Pelayanan Pajak, KPP) operating under the regional umbrella.

The restructuring is part of DJP's broader modernisation drive, which in recent years has included the consolidation of tax administration units, the digital transformation of compliance workflows, and the redistribution of taxpayer loads to improve service efficiency. South Jakarta represents one of the highest-density tax jurisdictions in Indonesia, home to a significant concentration of foreign-invested companies, multinational offices, and high-net-worth individual taxpayers.

Under the reorganisation, specific taxpayer files — identified by their Nomor Pokok Wajib Pajak (NPWP) — will be transferred between KPPs within the Kanwil Jaksus area. This means that some taxpayers will see a change in their registered KPP, their designated Account Representative (AR), and potentially the physical office address to which they must direct correspondence and in-person consultations.

While the DJP has not yet published the full technical detail of which NPWP ranges or entity categories will be reassigned to which KPP, the July 2026 effective date gives taxpayers and their advisers a window of approximately six to seven weeks from the current date to prepare. Historical DJP reorganisations of this type have been accompanied by official Keputusan Direktur Jenderal Pajak (Director General of Taxes Decree) notices, which enumerate the specific KPP transfers.

Taxpayers directly affected are typically notified through the DJP Online system (djponline.pajak.go.id), via their registered email address, or through their assigned Account Representative. Failure to follow up on a KPP transfer can lead to misrouted tax returns, delayed refunds, and — in more serious cases — unprocessed SPT filings that trigger administrative penalties under the General Tax Provisions Law (UU KUP).
