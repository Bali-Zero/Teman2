# E-VOA 2026: Electronic Visa on Arrival for Indonesia - Complete Guide

Planning a trip to Indonesia? The **E-VOA (Electronic Visa on Arrival)** is the most popular short-term visa option for tourists, offering **60 days of stay**, extendable up to **120 days total**. In 2026, Indonesia has streamlined the process with a fully online application system, eliminating most airport queues.

This guide covers everything you need to know: how to apply online, eligible nationalities, costs, extension procedures, and how E-VOA compares to other visa options like the free visa and B211A.

---

## What is E-VOA?

**E-VOA (Electronic Visa on Arrival)** is Indonesia's online visa application system for short-term tourists. Instead of paying at the airport upon arrival (the old VOA system), you now apply online **before your flight** and receive approval electronically.

### Key Features

- **Duration:** 60 days (extendable twice for 30 days each = 120 days max)
- **Cost:** IDR 500,000 government fee (~USD 32)
- **Eligible countries:** 90+ nationalities
- **Processing time:** Usually 1-3 business days
- **Application:** 100% online at [molina.imigrasi.go.id](https://molina.imigrasi.go.id)
- **Purpose:** Tourism, family visits, social/cultural activities
- **Work restrictions:** **No work allowed** (including remote work for Indonesian companies)

### E-VOA vs Old VOA System

The traditional "Visa on Arrival" required payment at airport immigration counters, often causing long queues. E-VOA shifts this process online, so you arrive with pre-approved authorization.

**Key difference:** You still pay the same IDR 500K fee, but online instead of at the airport.

## How to Apply for E-VOA Online

### Step-by-Step Application Process

#### 1. Prepare Required Documents

**Mandatory documents:**

- Valid passport (minimum 6 months validity from entry date)
- Passport biographical page scan (clear, readable)
- Recent passport photo (digital, white background)
- Return/onward flight ticket
- Proof of accommodation (hotel booking or invitation letter)
- Credit/debit card for payment

**Tips:**

- Passport scan should show name, photo, passport number, and expiry date clearly
- Photo must meet ICAO standards (no glasses, neutral expression)
- Flight ticket doesn't need to be within 60 days (you can extend)

#### 2. Create Account on MOLINA System

Visit [molina.imigrasi.go.id](https://molina.imigrasi.go.id) and register:

- Enter email and create password
- Verify email via confirmation link
- Log in to start application

#### 3. Fill Out E-VOA Application Form

**Personal information:**

- Full name (exactly as in passport)
- Date and place of birth
- Nationality and passport number
- Current address and phone number

**Travel details:**

- Intended arrival date and port of entry (e.g., Ngurah Rai Airport Bali)
- Accommodation address in Indonesia
- Purpose of visit (select "Tourism")
- Sponsor/inviter details (if applicable, e.g., hotel or friend)

**Common mistakes to avoid:**

- Name spelling doesn't match passport exactly
- Passport number typo
- Selecting wrong entry port
- Uploading blurry documents

#### 4. Upload Documents

- Passport biographical page (JPEG/PNG, max 2MB)
- Passport photo (JPEG/PNG, max 500KB)
- Flight ticket (PDF or screenshot)
- Accommodation proof (booking confirmation)

**Pro tip:** Use high-resolution scans. Blurry or incomplete uploads cause delays.

#### 5. Pay IDR 500,000 Fee

Payment methods:

- Credit/debit card (Visa, Mastercard)
- Indonesian bank transfer
- International payment gateways

**Total cost:** IDR 500,000 (government fee only, no agent markup if applying yourself)

#### 6. Wait for Approval

- **Normal processing:** 1-3 business days
- **Check status:** Log in to MOLINA and view "Application Status"
- **Approval notification:** Sent via email with downloadable E-VOA approval letter

#### 7. Download and Print E-VOA

Once approved:

- Download E-VOA approval letter (PDF)
- Print **2 copies** (one for airline check-in, one for immigration)
- Bring printed copies + digital backup on phone

**Important:** Airlines will check your E-VOA before boarding. Without it, you may be denied boarding.

## Arrival Procedure at Indonesian Airport

### At Check-In (Departure Airport)

Airline staff will verify:

- Valid passport (6+ months validity)
- E-VOA approval letter (printed or digital)
- Return/onward flight ticket

**No E-VOA = No boarding** (some airlines may allow free visa entry if you're eligible, but don't risk it).

### At Indonesian Immigration

1. **Queue at "Visa on Arrival" or "E-VOA" counter** (NOT "Visa-Free" line)
2. **Present:**
   - Passport
   - Printed E-VOA approval letter
   - Customs declaration form (filled on plane)
3. **Immigration officer:**
   - Scans E-VOA barcode
   - Stamps passport with 60-day validity
   - Takes your photo and fingerprints
4. **Collect luggage and exit**

**Processing time:** Usually 5-10 minutes (much faster than old VOA queues).

## Common E-VOA Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

### 1. Not Applying Early Enough

**Mistake:** Applying 1 day before flight, approval delayed, flight missed.

**Solution:** Apply **at least 5-7 days before** departure. Normal processing is 1-3 days, but delays happen (weekends, public holidays, document issues).

### 2. Passport Validity Issues

**Mistake:** Passport expires in 4 months, E-VOA rejected.

**Solution:** Passport must be valid for **6 months from entry date** (not 6 months from application date).

### 3. Name Spelling Errors

**Mistake:** Application name: "John Smith Jr." Passport name: "John Smith Junior" → Mismatch rejection.

**Solution:** Copy name **exactly** as it appears in passport, character by character.

### 4. Blurry Document Uploads

**Mistake:** Passport scan is dark/blurry, text unreadable → Application pending forever.

**Solution:** Use high-resolution scans (300 DPI minimum). Check that passport number and photo are crystal clear.

### 5. Wrong Port of Entry

**Mistake:** E-VOA states "Jakarta CGK," but you actually fly into "Bali DPS" → Immigration confusion.

**Solution:** Select the **actual airport** where you'll first enter Indonesia. If plans change, reapply with correct port.

### 6. Assuming E-VOA = Work Permit

**Mistake:** Working remotely for Indonesian company on E-VOA → Deportation risk.

**Solution:** E-VOA is **strictly tourism**. Remote work for foreign companies in a gray area (not officially legal, but rarely enforced). For Indonesian employment, you need KITAS + work permit.

**See also:** [Work Permit Requirements](work-permit-requirements) and [KITAS Explained](understanding-kitas-permits)

### 7. Missing Extension Deadline

**Mistake:** E-VOA expires tomorrow, immigration office closed → Overstay.

**Solution:** Set calendar reminders **14 days before expiry**. Apply early. If cutting it close, use a visa agent.

## E-VOA FAQs

### Can I enter Indonesia without E-VOA if I'm eligible for visa-free entry?

**Yes.** If your nationality qualifies for **both** E-VOA and visa-free entry, you can choose visa-free for stays up to 30 days. However, visa-free is **not extendable**, so if there's any chance you'll stay longer, get E-VOA.

### Can I apply for E-VOA after arriving in Indonesia?

**No.** E-VOA must be applied **before departure**. If you arrive without a visa, you can only use visa-free entry (if eligible) or face immigration issues.

### Can I work remotely on E-VOA?

**Officially, no.** E-VOA is for tourism only. Remote work for **foreign companies** (laptop on beach) is a gray area—not explicitly legal, but rarely enforced. However, working for **Indonesian companies** or **Indonesian clients** is illegal on E-VOA and risks deportation.

**For legal remote work:** Consider B211A or investor KITAS (though these are also tourism visas technically).

**See:** [Digital Nomad Visa Options](digital-nomad-visa-b211a)

### What happens if my E-VOA expires?

**Overstay penalties:**

- **Fine:** IDR 1,000,000 per day (up to IDR 30,000,000 maximum)
- **Immigration ban:** 6 months to lifetime, depending on overstay duration
- **Deportation:** If overstay exceeds 60 days

**Always extend or exit before expiry.** Set reminders.

**See:** [Visa Overstay Consequences](visa-overstay-consequences)

### Can I do a border run to reset my E-VOA?

**Yes, but...** You can exit Indonesia (e.g., fly to Singapore) and return with a **new E-VOA** (apply online again). This gives you another 60 days.

**However:**

- Frequent border runs attract immigration scrutiny
- If immigration suspects you're working illegally, they may deny entry
- Border runs are expensive (flights, accommodation)

**Better option:** Use B211A for longer stays without border runs.

**See:** [Border Runs to Extend Stay](border-runs-extend-stay)

### Can I convert E-VOA to another visa type while in Indonesia?

**No.** E-VOA cannot be converted to KITAS, investor visa, or work permit. You must **exit Indonesia** and apply for a different visa from outside.

**Example:** If you want to start a business, you'll need to:

1. Exit Indonesia (E-VOA ends)
2. Apply for investor KITAS from overseas
3. Re-enter with KITAS

**See:** [KITAS Sponsorship Guide](understanding-kitas-permits)

### Can family members apply for E-VOA?

**Yes.** Each person needs their own E-VOA application (including children). There's no "family E-VOA."

**Cost:** IDR 500,000 per person (so a family of 4 = IDR 2,000,000).

**Children under 2:** May travel visa-free on some airlines, but check with immigration.

### What if my E-VOA application is rejected?

**Common rejection reasons:**

- Passport validity less than 6 months
- Blurry/incomplete documents
- Name mismatch
- Previous immigration violations

**What to do:**

1. **Check rejection reason** in MOLINA portal
2. **Fix the issue** (e.g., upload clearer scan, correct name)
3. **Reapply** (pay another IDR 500,000 fee)

**If urgent:** Contact Indonesian embassy or use a visa agent for troubleshooting.
