## The Facts

Indonesia's immigration authority has stepped up its public messaging around the concept of a 'Global Citizen of Indonesia,' framing it as a tool to enhance the archipelago's competitive position in attracting international talent, capital, and expertise. The statement, attributed to immigration officials and reported by Antara News, positions the initiative within a broader national agenda to modernise Indonesia's approach to foreign residency.

The 'Global Citizen of Indonesia' framing is not entirely new. It has been discussed in policy circles as an aspirational category that could encompass various visa and residency instruments already in existence — including the Second Home Visa (B211C), the Limited Stay Permit (KITAS), and the more recently introduced digital nomad provisions. What is notable is the immigration directorate's explicit linkage between this concept and national economic competitiveness, a framing that mirrors approaches taken by regional competitors such as Thailand, Malaysia, and the UAE.

Indonesia has made measurable strides in recent years to modernise its residency framework for foreigners. The Second Home Visa, launched in late 2022, allows eligible applicants to reside in Indonesia for up to five or ten years, provided they meet financial thresholds — typically proof of funds equivalent to IDR 2 billion (approximately USD 125,000) in an Indonesian bank account. The visa does not currently confer the right to work for a local employer but has been widely used by remote workers and retirees.

Officials have signalled interest in expanding pathways that allow skilled foreigners to contribute more directly to the Indonesian economy, including through investment vehicles and knowledge-transfer arrangements. The immigration directorate has emphasised that such frameworks must balance openness with the protection of the domestic labour market, a consistent tension in Indonesian immigration policy.

At the time of publication, no formal legislative or regulatory amendment has been announced in connection with the 'Global Citizen of Indonesia' branding. It remains to be seen whether the concept will crystallise into a distinct visa category or serve primarily as a communications umbrella for existing instruments. Observers note that Indonesia's immigration reforms have historically moved at a measured pace, with implementation timelines often extending well beyond initial policy announcements.
