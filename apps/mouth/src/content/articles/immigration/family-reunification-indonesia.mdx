## Family Reunification in Indonesia: No Single Visa, Multiple Strategies

Unlike many Western countries, Indonesia does not have a dedicated "family reunification" visa category. There is no single application that brings your entire extended family to live with you. Instead, each family member must qualify for their own visa type based on their personal circumstances.

This means bringing your family to Indonesia requires a strategic approach. Your spouse and children have clear pathways (E33F and E33E dependent KITAS). Parents have options depending on their age. Siblings, unfortunately, have very limited paths.

Let us break down the options for each family member.

## Spouse and Children: The Straightforward Path

### Spouse: E33F Dependent KITAS

Your spouse is the easiest family member to bring to Indonesia. The E33F spouse dependent KITAS is automatically available when you hold a valid work KITAS.

**Requirements:**

- Marriage certificate (legalized and translated)
- Your active KITAS as the primary holder
- Spouse's passport (18+ months validity)
- Passport photos
- Sponsor letter from your company

**Validity:** Same duration as the primary KITAS holder (1-2 years)
**Work rights:** None (see our guide on spouse dependent work rights)
**Cost:** IDR 5-10 million including agent fees

### Children: E33E Dependent KITAS

Children under 18 (or under 21 if unmarried and in full-time education) can receive E33E dependent KITAS.

**Requirements:**

- Birth certificate (legalized and translated)
- Parent's active KITAS
- Child's passport
- School enrollment letter (for children in education)

**Validity:** Same as parent's KITAS
**Special note:** Children born in Indonesia to KITAS-holding parents have a separate process (see our newborn registration guide)

## Bringing Parents to Indonesia

This is where it gets more complex. Your options depend primarily on your parents' age.

### Parents Aged 55+: E311A Retirement KITAS

The **E311A Retirement KITAS** is the most practical long-term option for elderly parents. Indonesia actively welcomes retirees and has a well-established retirement visa program.

**Requirements:**

| Requirement         | Details                                                    |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Age                 | 55 years or older                                          |
| Financial proof     | Pension or savings of USD 2,500/month (or equivalent)      |
| Health insurance    | Valid international or Indonesian health insurance         |
| Accommodation       | Proof of rental or ownership of accommodation in Indonesia |
| Health declaration  | Medical fitness statement                                  |
| Statement of intent | Declaration not to work in Indonesia                       |
| Indonesian sponsor  | Local sponsor (individual or agent)                        |

**Key advantages for parents:**

- No work requirement (it is a retirement visa)
- Renewable annually
- Can lead to KITAP after 5 consecutive years
- Relatively affordable compared to work KITAS
- No RPTKA or DKP-TKA needed

**Practical considerations:**

- Health insurance can be challenging for elderly parents (premiums increase with age)
- Some insurance providers have upper age limits
- Medical evacuation coverage is important in Indonesia
- Parents should have access to quality healthcare (Bali and Jakarta have international hospitals)

### Parents Under 55: Limited Options

If your parents are not yet 55, the retirement visa is not available. Here are the alternatives:

#### Option 1: B211 Social/Cultural Visit Visa

**Best for:** Parents visiting for extended periods (up to 180 days)

| Feature            | Details                            |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Initial validity   | 60 days                            |
| Extensions         | Up to 4 extensions of 30 days each |
| Maximum stay       | 180 days per visit                 |
| Work authorization | None                               |
| Cost               | IDR 2-5 million per extension      |

**Limitations:**

- Must leave Indonesia after 180 days
- Not a permanent solution
- Cannot be converted to KITAS
- Must re-apply after each departure

#### Option 2: Second Home Visa (SHV)

The **Second Home Visa** was introduced to attract wealthy long-term residents.

**Requirements:**

- Bank deposit of IDR 2 billion (approximately USD 125,000) in an Indonesian bank
- Valid for 5 years (extendable for another 5 years)
- No age restriction
- No work authorization

**Best for:** Parents with significant financial resources
**Limitation:** The IDR 2 billion deposit must remain in the Indonesian bank for the visa duration

#### Option 3: Work KITAS (If Applicable)

If your parent has professional skills and a company willing to sponsor them, they could apply for their own work KITAS (E25B or E23). This requires:

- A company willing to submit RPTKA
- Position that justifies foreign expertise
- Full RPTKA, DKP-TKA, and KITAS process

This is rarely practical for parents but worth mentioning for completeness.

## Bringing Siblings to Indonesia

Here is the difficult truth: **there is no dependent visa for siblings.** Indonesian immigration law only recognizes spouse and children as dependents. Your brother or sister must qualify for a visa independently.

### Possible Paths for Siblings

| Visa Option                | Requirements                      | Practical?             |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| B211 Social/Cultural Visit | Sponsor letter, max 180 days      | Short-term only        |
| E25B Director KITAS        | Own/join a PT PMA as director     | If they want to work   |
| E23 Employee KITAS         | Job offer from Indonesian company | If qualified           |
| E28A Investor KITAS        | IDR 10B+ investment               | If wealthy             |
| E311A Retirement KITAS     | Age 55+, USD 2,500/month          | If retired             |
| E33G Remote Worker         | Remote job with overseas employer | If digital worker      |
| Second Home Visa           | IDR 2B bank deposit               | If financially capable |

**Reality check:** Unless your sibling has independent means, professional skills, or retirement eligibility, long-term residence in Indonesia is difficult to arrange.

## Multi-Family KITAS Applications: Managing the Process

When applying for KITAS for multiple family members simultaneously, coordination is essential.

### Recommended Application Sequence

**Step 1:** Primary KITAS holder (the working spouse) applies first. All dependent KITAS applications require an active primary KITAS as a prerequisite.

**Step 2:** Spouse E33F application, submitted alongside or shortly after the primary KITAS. Most agents handle these as a package.

**Step 3:** Children's E33E applications, which can be submitted simultaneously with the spouse's application.

**Step 4:** Parents' E311A applications (independent process, can run in parallel with the above).

### Document Preparation Checklist

Prepare these documents for the entire family to avoid delays:

**For all family members:**

- Passports with 18+ months validity
- Passport photos (4x6 cm, red/blue background per immigration requirements)
- Legalized and translated civil documents (marriage, birth certificates)
- Health insurance policies

**For the primary KITAS holder:**

- Company documents (NIB, AHU, deed)
- RPTKA approval
- Employment contract

**For the spouse (E33F):**

- Marriage certificate (apostilled/legalized)
- Sworn translation in Indonesian (by certified translator)
- Sponsor letter from the primary holder's company

**For children (E33E):**

- Birth certificates (apostilled/legalized)
- School enrollment letter (if applicable)
- Custody documents (if parents are separated)

**For parents (E311A):**

- Pension statements or bank statements (6 months)
- Health insurance policy
- Accommodation proof (rental agreement or property ownership)
- Medical fitness declaration
- Indonesian sponsor details

### Timeline for Multi-Family Applications

| Family Member   | Process          | Timeline                  |
| --------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------- |
| Primary holder  | RPTKA + KITAS    | 4-6 weeks                 |
| Spouse (E33F)   | Dependent KITAS  | 2-3 weeks (after primary) |
| Children (E33E) | Dependent KITAS  | 2-3 weeks (after primary) |
| Parents (E311A) | Retirement KITAS | 3-5 weeks (independent)   |

**Total estimated timeline:** 6-10 weeks for the entire family, if applications are coordinated.

## Practical Tips for Family Relocation

1. **Start with documents** - Begin apostilling and translating documents months before the move
2. **Health insurance first** - Secure family health insurance before applying for any KITAS
3. **School enrollment** - If bringing children, secure school places early (international schools in Bali fill up fast)
4. **Housing** - Arrange family-suitable accommodation before arrival
5. **Bank accounts** - Open Indonesian bank accounts as soon as KITAS cards are issued
6. **Emergency planning** - Know the nearest international hospital and have evacuation insurance
7. **Legal counsel** - Use a single immigration agent for all family members to ensure coordination
