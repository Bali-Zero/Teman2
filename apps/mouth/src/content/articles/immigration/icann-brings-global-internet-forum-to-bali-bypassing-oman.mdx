## The Facts

The Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN), the non-profit body responsible for coordinating global internet naming systems and IP address allocation, has confirmed that its Global Internet Forum will be held in Bali, Indonesia, replacing the previously designated host city of Muscat, Oman.

ICANN convenes multiple high-profile public meetings each year, gathering government representatives, civil society organisations, the technical community, and private sector actors to deliberate on internet governance policy. These gatherings are among the most significant in the global digital policy calendar and typically attract several thousand delegates from across the world.

The forum's relocation to Bali represents a notable shift in the geographic centre of gravity for global internet governance discussions toward Southeast Asia. Indonesia is home to one of the world's largest internet user populations — estimated at over 200 million — and Jakarta has in recent years sought a more prominent seat at the international digital policy table, including through engagement with the UN Internet Governance Forum (IGF) process.

Bali has previously hosted major international summits, including the G20 Leaders' Summit in November 2022, establishing the island's operational infrastructure for large-scale diplomatic and multilateral events. The Nusa Dua convention district in particular has been configured to host events of this size and profile.

No further detail on the precise dates, specific venue, or the reasons for the substitution of Oman were available in early reporting. The reasons for the venue change have not been publicly confirmed by ICANN at the time of publication.
