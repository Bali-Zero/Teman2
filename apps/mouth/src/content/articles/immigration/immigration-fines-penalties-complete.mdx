## Overview of Indonesia's Immigration Enforcement System

Indonesia's immigration enforcement is governed primarily by Undang-Undang Nomor 6 Tahun 2011 tentang Keimigrasian (Immigration Law No. 6 of 2011) and its implementing regulations. The Direktorat Jenderal Imigrasi (Directorate General of Immigration) under the Ministry of Law and Human Rights enforces these rules through a system of administrative fines, detention, deportation, and entry bans.

Understanding these penalties is not just about avoiding punishment. It is about protecting your ability to live and work in Indonesia long-term. A single serious violation can result in years of being banned from entering the country, which can destroy business operations, family arrangements, and retirement plans.

## Complete Fine and Penalty Schedule

### Overstay Fines

| Overstay Duration | Daily Fine        | Cumulative Maximum    | Additional Consequences                       |
| ----------------- | ----------------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 1-7 days          | IDR 1,000,000/day | IDR 7,000,000         | Fine only, exit permitted after payment       |
| 8-30 days         | IDR 1,000,000/day | IDR 30,000,000        | Fine + mandatory immigration interview        |
| 31-60 days        | IDR 1,000,000/day | IDR 60,000,000        | Fine + possible detention + blacklist warning |
| 61+ days          | IDR 1,000,000/day | IDR 500,000,000 (cap) | Mandatory deportation + blacklist 1-5 years   |

The IDR 1,000,000 per day rate applies uniformly regardless of visa type. Whether you overstay an E-VOA, B211, or KITAS, the daily rate is the same. The cumulative cap of IDR 500,000,000 means that after 500 days of overstay, the fine stops accumulating, but by that point deportation and a lengthy blacklist entry are guaranteed.

### Work Permit Violations

| Violation                        | Administrative Penalty         | Criminal Penalty                     | Blacklist     |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ------------- |
| Working without any permit       | Deportation                    | Up to 5 years prison + IDR 500M fine | 1-5 years     |
| Working outside KITAS scope      | KITAS revocation + deportation | Up to 5 years prison + IDR 500M fine | 1-3 years     |
| Employing illegal foreign worker | Company fine IDR 500M          | Up to 5 years prison                 | N/A (company) |
| Using someone else's work permit | Deportation                    | Up to 5 years prison + IDR 500M fine | 2-5 years     |

Working without a permit is treated as one of the most serious immigration violations. Under Article 122 of UU 6/2011, both the foreign worker and the employer face criminal liability. Immigration regularly conducts raids on businesses in tourist areas, coworking spaces, and restaurants in Bali, particularly in Canggu, Seminyak, and Ubud.

### Reporting Violations

| --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |

| Sponsor failure to report foreign worker departure  | IDR 25,000,000-50,000,000         |

| Failure to report lost/damaged KITAS card           | IDR 5,000,000 + replacement fee   |

### Expired Document Penalties

| Document                              | Grace Period     | Penalty for Expiry                                                           |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| KITAS (stay permit)                   | None             | Overstay fines begin immediately (IDR 1M/day)                                |
| STM (Exit-Reentry Permit)             | None             | Cannot re-enter Indonesia; must apply for new visa from abroad               |
| Passport (expired while in Indonesia) | 30 days to renew | No immigration fine, but cannot extend/renew any visa until passport renewed |
| SKTT (Domicile Registration)          | 14 days          | IDR 2,000,000-5,000,000 administrative fine                                  |
| Wajib Lapor (Annual Reporting)        | 14 days          | IDR 5,000,000 administrative fine                                            |

## Deportation: Triggers, Process, and Consequences

### What Triggers Deportation

Deportation (deportasi) is an administrative action, not a criminal punishment. It can be ordered by the head of any immigration office or by the Director General of Immigration. The following situations trigger deportation:

1. **Overstay exceeding 60 days** without valid justification
2. **Working without a valid work permit** (KITAS with RPTKA authorization)
3. **Criminal conviction** in Indonesia
4. **Involvement in activities threatening public order** or national security
5. **Violation of visa conditions** (e.g., conducting business on a tourist visa)
6. **False documents or statements** in visa applications
7. **Being declared an undesirable person** by the immigration authority
8. **Failure to pay accumulated overstay fines** when financial penalties are imposed

### The Deportation Process Step by Step

1. **Detention** - You are taken to an Immigration Detention Center (Rumah Detensi Imigrasi) or held at the immigration office. Indonesia has 13 detention centers nationwide.
2. **Administrative review** - Immigration officers review your case and documents. You may be interviewed.
3. **Deportation order** - The head of the immigration office issues a formal deportation order (Surat Keputusan Deportasi).
4. **Travel document verification** - Immigration verifies your passport validity and arranges departure.
5. **Departure** - You are escorted to the airport and placed on a flight. You must pay for your own ticket. If you cannot pay, the government covers the cost and may pursue reimbursement.
6. **Blacklist entry** - Your name is entered into the immigration blacklist system with a specified duration.
7. **Exit stamp** - Your passport receives a deportation exit stamp, which is visible to immigration authorities of other countries.

## The Immigration Blacklist System

### How the Blacklist Works

Indonesia maintains a centralized immigration blacklist database called the Sistem Informasi Manajemen Keimigrasian (SIMKIM). When you are blacklisted, your passport number, name, date of birth, and nationality are entered. Every immigration checkpoint (airport, seaport, land border) checks incoming passengers against this database.

### Blacklist Duration by Violation

| Violation Type                              | Typical Blacklist Duration |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Minor overstay (under 30 days), fines paid  | 6 months - 1 year          |
| Moderate overstay (30-60 days), fines paid  | 1 - 2 years                |
| Severe overstay (60+ days) with deportation | 2 - 3 years                |
| Working without permit                      | 1 - 5 years                |
| Criminal deportation                        | 3 - 5 years                |
| Security threat                             | 5 years - permanent        |
| Drug-related offenses                       | 5 years - permanent        |
| Repeat offender (second deportation)        | 5 years - permanent        |

### Can You Check if You Are Blacklisted?

There is no public online tool to check your blacklist status. The methods available are:

1. **Apply for a new visa** - If your application is rejected citing "immigration record," you are likely blacklisted
2. **Visit an Indonesian embassy** - Consular officers can check the system
3. **Hire an immigration lawyer in Indonesia** - They can submit a formal inquiry to the Directorate General of Immigration
4. **Contact your country's embassy in Jakarta** - They can make inquiries on your behalf through diplomatic channels

## How to Pay Immigration Fines

### Step-by-Step Fine Payment Process

1. **Report to the nearest immigration office** (Kantor Imigrasi) in your area. Do not attempt to leave Indonesia without paying. You will be stopped at the airport.
2. **Immigration officer calculates your fine** based on the number of overstay days multiplied by IDR 1,000,000.
3. **Receive a payment order** (Surat Perintah Bayar) with the exact amount and a billing code.
4. **Pay via bank transfer** at Bank BRI, Bank Mandiri, or Bank BNI using the billing code. Some offices also accept payment via the SIMPONI (Sistem Informasi PNBP Online) platform.
5. **Return to the immigration office** with proof of payment (bank receipt).
6. **Immigration officer stamps your passport** with an exit permit notation.
7. **You must leave Indonesia within 7 days** of fine payment for overstay cases.

### Accepted Payment Methods

- Bank transfer (BRI, Mandiri, BNI) using SIMPONI billing code
- Cash payment at the immigration office cashier (some offices only)
- ATM transfer using the billing code
- Mobile banking transfer using the billing code

Credit cards and foreign bank transfers are generally not accepted for fine payments.

## The Appeals Process

### Administrative Objection (Keberatan Administratif)

If you believe a fine or deportation order is unjust, you have the right to file an administrative objection. This must be filed within 14 days of receiving the decision.

**Process:**

1. Submit a written objection letter (Surat Keberatan) to the head of the immigration office that issued the decision
2. Include supporting documents that justify your objection (medical certificates, force majeure evidence, etc.)
3. The immigration office must respond within 30 working days
4. If the objection is rejected, you can escalate to the Directorate General of Immigration in Jakarta

### Valid Grounds for Appeal

- **Medical emergency** that physically prevented you from leaving or extending your visa (hospital admission records required)
- **Natural disaster** that disrupted transportation or government services
- **Government processing delay** where you applied for an extension before expiry but immigration failed to process it in time
- **Administrative error** by immigration officials (wrong dates, incorrect calculations)
- **Force majeure** events such as airline cancellations, airport closures, or pandemic-related travel restrictions

### Grounds That Will NOT Work

- "I didn't know the rules" - Ignorance of immigration law is never a valid defense
- "My agent told me it was fine" - You are personally responsible regardless of agent advice
- "I was too busy" - Personal convenience is not grounds for appeal
- "The office was far away" - Distance to an immigration office is not considered force majeure

## Prevention: How to Avoid All Immigration Penalties

### Set Up a Compliance Calendar

1. **90 days before visa expiry** - Begin renewal/extension process or plan departure
2. **60 days before** - Submit KITAS extension application if applicable
3. **30 days before** - Confirm processing status; escalate if delayed
4. **14 days before** - Final check; if extension not approved, prepare to exit
5. **7 days before** - If no extension, book departure flight
6. **Day of expiry** - You must have a valid stay permit or be on an outbound flight

### Document Checklist for Compliance

- Passport valid for at least 18 months at all times
- Current visa/KITAS/KITAP with confirmed expiry date noted in your calendar
- Valid STM (Exit-Reentry Permit) if you plan to travel and return
- Current SKTT (Domicile Registration) from local kelurahan
- Completed Wajib Lapor (annual reporting at immigration office)
- Digital copies of all documents stored in cloud storage

### Emergency Contacts

If you find yourself in an immigration emergency (detained, overstay discovered, facing deportation), take these steps immediately:

1. **Contact your country's embassy or consulate** - They can provide consular assistance
2. **Hire an immigration lawyer** - Not an agent, but a licensed lawyer (advokat) who specializes in immigration law
3. **Do not sign documents you do not understand** - Request translation or wait for legal counsel
4. **Remain calm and cooperative** - Resisting or being aggressive with immigration officers will worsen your situation
5. **Document everything** - Take photos of documents, note officer names and badge numbers, keep all receipts

## Special Circumstances

### COVID-19 and Pandemic Extensions

While the pandemic-era automatic extensions have ended, Indonesia demonstrated willingness to grant force majeure extensions during extraordinary circumstances. If a similar situation arises, monitor official announcements from the Directorate General of Immigration and be prepared to apply for emergency extensions through your nearest immigration office.

### Minors and Dependent Visa Holders

Children on dependent KITAS (E33E) are subject to the same overstay rules as adults. However, fines for minors are assessed against the parent or guardian sponsor. Deportation of minors follows special procedures that involve coordination with child welfare authorities and the relevant embassy.

### Death of a KITAS Holder

If a KITAS holder passes away in Indonesia, the sponsor or next of kin must report the death to the immigration office within 14 days. The KITAS is cancelled, and no overstay fines are assessed. Failure to report may result in administrative penalties against the sponsor.
