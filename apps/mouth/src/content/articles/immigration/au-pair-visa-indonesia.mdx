## The Short Answer: No Au Pair Visa Exists in Indonesia

Let us be direct: **Indonesia does not have an au pair visa program.** There is no visa category, no cultural exchange framework, and no regulatory pathway for foreign nationals to work as au pairs in Indonesian households.

This comes as a surprise to many European and North American families who are familiar with well-established au pair programs in their home countries. In the EU, the au pair system is formalized under the European Agreement on Au Pair Placement. In the United States, the J-1 visa covers au pair exchanges. Indonesia has nothing equivalent.

If you are planning to move to Indonesia and hoping to bring along an au pair from your home country, or if you are a young person hoping to au pair with a family in Bali, this guide explains why it does not work and what your alternatives are.

## Why Indonesia Does Not Have an Au Pair Program

Several factors explain the absence of a formal au pair system:

### 1. Domestic Work Is on the Negative Investment List

The Ministry of Manpower maintains a list of positions that are **prohibited for foreign workers**. Domestic work, childcare, and household management positions fall into this category. The rationale is simple: Indonesia has a large workforce of domestic helpers, and there is no skills gap that would justify bringing in foreign workers for these roles.

### 2. No Cultural Exchange Framework for Domestic Work

Au pair programs internationally are structured as cultural exchanges, not employment. Indonesia's immigration system does not recognize this distinction for household work. There is no "cultural exchange" visa that covers living with a family and providing childcare.

### 3. RPTKA Cannot Be Obtained for Domestic Positions

Even if a family wanted to sponsor a foreign au pair through the formal work permit system, the RPTKA (Foreign Worker Employment Plan) application would be rejected because:

- Domestic work is a prohibited position for foreigners
- No company structure exists (families are not PT PMA companies)
- No skills transfer plan is possible (domestic work does not require foreign expertise)
- The Ministry of Manpower would see no justification for the position

### 4. Labor Law Complexity

Indonesian labor law for domestic workers (UU Ketenagakerjaan and UU PPRT) is complex and evolving. Applying this framework to a foreign cultural exchange participant would create legal complications that the system is not designed to handle.

## What Happens with Informal Au Pair Arrangements

Despite the legal barriers, some foreign families in Indonesia attempt informal au pair arrangements. Here is what this looks like and why it is risky:

### Common Informal Scenarios

| Scenario                        | How It Works                                                               | Legal Risk                                       |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Friend or relative "helping"    | A family member or friend comes on a tourist visa and helps with childcare | High - unauthorized work on visit visa           |
| Gap year student "volunteering" | Young person comes on B211 visa and lives with family                      | High - if they perform regular domestic duties   |
| Online au pair matching         | Family finds an au pair through international platforms                    | Very High - no legal basis whatsoever            |
| Former nanny from home country  | Family brings their nanny from abroad on visit visa                        | Very High - clear employment without work permit |

### Risks for the Foreign "Au Pair"

1. **Deportation** - If immigration discovers the arrangement, the au pair faces deportation
2. **Immigration blacklist** - 1-5 year ban from entering Indonesia
3. **Detention** - Can be held at an immigration detention facility
4. **No legal protection** - Without a work permit, the au pair has zero labor rights
5. **No insurance coverage** - Travel insurance typically excludes employment-related activities
6. **Criminal charges** - Under Immigration Law, unauthorized work carries criminal penalties

### Risks for the Host Family

1. **Fines** - Employing a foreign worker without proper permits
2. **Sponsor liability** - If the family sponsored the visit visa, they bear responsibility
3. **Criminal penalties** - Up to IDR 400 million for employing unauthorized foreign workers
4. **KITAS complications** - Your own immigration status could be affected
5. **Community reporting** - Neighbors or local staff may report the arrangement to authorities

### How It Gets Discovered

- Local staff (Indonesian nanny, driver, housekeeper) notice and report
- Neighbors observe a young foreigner regularly caring for children
- Social media posts showing the au pair in domestic settings
- Immigration random checks in residential areas
- Visa extension interviews where the au pair's activities are questioned

## Legal Alternatives for Childcare in Indonesia

### Option 1: Hire an Indonesian Nanny (Recommended)

This is by far the most practical, legal, and cost-effective solution. Indonesia has a large, experienced domestic worker workforce.

**Advantages:**

- Completely legal, no immigration issues
- Very affordable (IDR 2-5 million/month for live-in nanny)
- Indonesian nannies are often experienced with international families
- Many speak basic English, especially in Bali and Jakarta
- Cultural immersion for your children

**How to find a good nanny:**

- Word of mouth from other expat families
- Agency referrals (nanny placement agencies in major cities)
- Community groups (Bali expat groups, school parent networks)
- Online platforms (local job boards)

**Employment considerations:**

- Draft a simple employment agreement
- Provide fair wages (above minimum wage)
- Include meals, accommodation (if live-in), and health coverage
- Register with BPJS (social security) - technically required
- Give standard Indonesian holidays off

### Option 2: Hire an Indonesian Babysitter (Part-Time)

If you do not need full-time childcare:

| Service                        | Typical Cost                | Availability          |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Part-time babysitter (4-6 hrs) | IDR 100,000-200,000/day     | Easy to find          |
| Evening babysitter             | IDR 100,000-150,000/evening | Moderate availability |
| Vacation care helper           | IDR 200,000-300,000/day     | Book in advance       |

### Option 3: International Schools with Extended Care

Many international schools in Indonesia offer:

- Before and after school care programs
- Holiday camps and programs
- School bus services
- Extracurricular activities until 4-5 PM

This reduces the need for a full-time nanny while your children are school-age.

### Option 4: Bring a Nanny on a Work KITAS (Extremely Difficult)

While theoretically possible to bring a foreign nanny on a work KITAS, in practice this is nearly impossible because:

1. The family would need to establish a company (PT PMA or CV) that employs the nanny
2. The position must not be on the negative list (domestic work is on the list)
3. RPTKA would need to be approved (almost certainly rejected)
4. Cost would be enormous (RPTKA + DKP-TKA + KITAS = USD 5,000+ per year minimum)
5. The nanny would need to qualify as a "skilled worker"

**Bottom line:** This path is not viable for domestic childcare.

### Option 5: Remote Childcare Support

If your concern is maintaining educational or cultural continuity:

- Online tutoring from teachers in your home country
- Virtual language classes for your children
- Remote educational support through international platforms

These do not require any visa or immigration arrangement since the service providers are not in Indonesia.

## Special Considerations for Bali

Bali has the largest concentration of foreign families in Indonesia, and the childcare ecosystem is well-developed:

### Bali-Specific Resources

- **Nanny agencies in Seminyak, Canggu, and Ubud** specialize in matching nannies with international families
- **English-speaking nannies** are more readily available in Bali than elsewhere in Indonesia
- **Parent community groups** on Facebook and WhatsApp are active and helpful for nanny referrals
- **International schools** (Green School, Bali Island School, Canggu Community School) have strong parent networks

### Cost of Living Advantage

One of the reasons the au pair concept does not translate to Indonesia is economics. In Europe, au pairs are affordable childcare for middle-class families. In Indonesia, full-time domestic help is already accessible:

| Service                     | Monthly Cost (Bali)           | Monthly Cost (Europe au pair)         |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Live-in nanny               | IDR 3-5 million (USD 190-310) | EUR 300-400 pocket money + room/board |
| Part-time nanny             | IDR 2-3 million (USD 125-190) | N/A (au pairs are live-in)            |
| Live-in nanny + housekeeper | IDR 5-8 million (USD 310-500) | N/A                                   |

The economics simply do not support importing foreign domestic help when local help is readily available and affordable.

## What About Bringing a Family Helper on a Family Reunion Visit?

Some families ask about bringing a helper "as part of the family" on a visit visa. This does not work:

- Visit visas do not authorize any form of work
- Immigration officers are trained to spot this arrangement
- The helper is not a family member in immigration terms
- This approach has led to deportations in documented cases
