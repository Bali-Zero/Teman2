## The Facts

Indonesia's immigration apparatus has been operating at elevated activity levels throughout the first quarter of 2026, with the Directorate General of Immigration (Ditjen Imigrasi) issuing regular weekly advisories that track enforcement sweeps, policy clarifications, and procedural updates affecting both in-country visa holders and incoming travelers.

The week of April 16, 2026 falls within a period of heightened scrutiny following a series of high-profile deportation cases in Bali, where authorities have been conducting targeted operations against foreigners allegedly working without appropriate permits, overstaying their authorized periods, or engaging in activities inconsistent with their visa classifications. Bali's immigration offices — particularly Denpasar and Ngurah Rai — have historically been among the most active enforcement nodes in the national system.

Indonesia operates a complex multi-tiered visa architecture. Short-stay visitors typically enter on a Visa on Arrival (B211A) or the Electronic Visa on Arrival (e-VoA), both permitting stays of 30 days extendable once for a total of 60 days. Longer-term residents utilize instruments including the KITAS (Izin Tinggal Terbatas, or Limited Stay Permit), the relatively new Second Home Visa (C6), or investor-linked permits tied to PT PMA company structures. Each category carries distinct work authorization parameters, and violations — even inadvertent ones — can result in fines, detention, or multi-year entry bans.

Authorities have in recent months emphasized that holders of tourist and visit visas who are found to be performing compensated work — including remote work for foreign employers — may be subject to enforcement action. While Indonesia has not yet introduced a formally codified digital nomad visa equivalent to those issued by Thailand or Portugal, the Second Home Visa has functionally served that population, though its work authorization status remains a subject of legal interpretation.

Indonesia's immigration law framework is governed primarily by Law No. 6 of 2011 on Immigration and its implementing regulations. Penalties for overstays are graduated: administrative fines accrue per day, with detention possible for extended violations. Deportation carries an automatic re-entry ban of six months to five years depending on the severity of the infraction.
