## The Facts

Bali's international tourist arrivals have risen approximately 10% in recent months, a figure that stands in marked contrast to broader anxieties about the impact of global conflict on long-haul leisure travel. The data points to a travel market that continues to prioritise the island's appeal — cultural richness, natural environment, and relatively accessible visa pathways — over geopolitical uncertainty in more distant theatres.

The uptick arrives during a period when Middle East airspace disruptions have forced flight rerouting and, in some cases, left travellers stranded at Ngurah Rai International Airport. Indonesia's immigration authorities responded under an established legal framework: PP No. 45 Tahun 2024 (Pasal 5 Ayat 5 and Pasal 7 Ayat 1) provides the legal basis for waiving immigration fees and overstay fines for foreigners facing force majeure conditions — a provision known informally as the 'Zero Rupiah' overstay policy. Ngurah Rai Immigration applied this provision in 2026 to protect tourists whose departure was blocked by regional airspace closures.

Complementing this, Permenkumham No. 22 Tahun 2023 (Pasal 151 and 154) mandates the issuance of Emergency Stay Permits (Izin Tinggal Keadaan Terpaksa) for foreigners whose permits expire but who cannot depart due to unexpected events, non-natural disasters, or force majeure. This regulation ensures that legal status is preserved rather than penalised during crisis situations beyond a traveller's control.

The primary entry mechanisms supporting continued tourist flow remain the Visit Visa (Visa Kunjungan) and Visa on Arrival (VOA), governed by Permenkumham No. 22 Tahun 2023 and Permenkumham No. 11 Tahun 2024. These instruments keep Bali accessible to the broad leisure and short-stay market that drives the headline arrival numbers.

At the provincial level, Bali's regional government continues to operate its tourist levy under Perda Provinsi Bali No. 2 Tahun 2025 — a replacement for the earlier Perda 6/2023 — which requires all foreign tourists entering the province to pay a levy of Rp 150,000. Revenue is earmarked for the protection of Balinese culture and the natural environment. The levy has not functioned as a deterrent; the 10% growth figure suggests visitor sensitivity to the charge remains low.
