## The Facts

A short video depicting a foreign national operating DJ equipment atop Mount Batur — an active volcano in Kintamani, Bali, revered as a spiritual axis by the Balinese Hindu community — spread rapidly across social media platforms, triggering a swift and intense public response.

Mount Batur is not merely a tourist trekking destination. The mountain is home to Pura Ulun Danu Batur, one of Bali's six most sacred directional temples (sad kahyangan), and the summit itself is considered spiritually charged ground. For the Balinese, the mountain represents the dwelling of Dewi Danu, goddess of lakes and rivers, and is deeply integrated into the island's cosmological framework.

The video prompted immediate condemnation from Balinese cultural figures, local government officials, and community leaders, who characterized the act as disrespectful and a violation of the adat — customary law — that governs conduct in sacred spaces. Several officials called for the individual to be identified, sanctioned, and potentially deported.

The incident is part of a pattern of high-profile foreigner misconduct cases that have tested Bali's hospitality in recent years. Authorities have previously deported foreign nationals for offenses including posing nude at sacred sites, riding motorbikes recklessly, and working illegally. Indonesia's Directorate General of Immigration has stated on multiple occasions that it will not hesitate to use deportation powers against visitors who show disrespect toward Indonesian culture and law.

Bali's Governor and local regency heads have repeatedly issued public statements warning that the island will not tolerate behavior that degrades its spiritual heritage. The viral nature of such incidents amplifies pressure on enforcement bodies to respond visibly and quickly, setting precedents that send a signal to the broader expatriate and tourist population about the boundaries of acceptable conduct.
