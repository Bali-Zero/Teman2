## The Facts

Australia and New Zealand consistently rank among Bali's top three tourism source markets. In a typical pre-pandemic year, Australians alone account for well over one million arrivals at Ngurah Rai International Airport, making them the single largest foreign visitor cohort. New Zealand, while smaller in absolute numbers, punches above its weight on per-capita spending and average length of stay.

Currently, both nationalities enter Indonesia under the Visa on Arrival (VoA) scheme, which allows a 30-day stay extendable once for an additional 30 days, at a fee of IDR 500,000 (approximately USD 31) per entry. The process is handled at designated immigration counters upon arrival and, since 2022, is also available through the e-VOA portal in advance. While operationally straightforward, the fee and the administrative step represent a threshold absent for travellers heading to competing regional destinations such as Thailand, Vietnam, and Malaysia, which already offer visa-free entry to Australian and New Zealand citizens.

Discussions around granting full visa-free access to selected high-value tourism markets have surfaced periodically within Indonesian government circles, most recently as part of broader efforts to accelerate post-pandemic tourism recovery and compete with Southeast Asian neighbours. Any formal shift would likely be implemented via a revision to Government Regulation (Peraturan Pemerintah) or a Presidential Regulation governing the Bebas Visa Kunjungan (BVK) list, which currently covers approximately 90 nationalities.

No official decree has been published confirming the change, and the Directorate General of Immigration has not issued a formal policy statement as of the time of this writing. Reports circulating in Balinese and Indonesian travel media cite government sources and tourism industry stakeholders flagging the proposal as under active consideration, but timelines remain unconfirmed.

The diplomatic backdrop matters: Indonesia and Australia maintain a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership, and the two governments have been expanding bilateral cooperation across trade, defence, and people-to-people links. A visa-free corridor would align with Indonesia's stated target of 14 to 16 million foreign tourist arrivals annually, a goal that requires meaningful reductions in entry barriers across high-volume origin markets.
