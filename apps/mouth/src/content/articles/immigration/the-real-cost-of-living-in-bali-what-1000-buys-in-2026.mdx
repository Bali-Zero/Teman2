## The Facts

Bali has long carried a reputation as one of Southeast Asia's most affordable destinations for foreign residents, but that perception is increasingly at odds with ground-level economic reality. The Indonesian island's cost of living has shifted meaningfully over the past several years, driven by post-pandemic tourism surges, increased demand for quality housing, and broader inflationary pressure across the Indonesian economy.

At the US$1,000 per month threshold — roughly IDR 16 million at current exchange rates — a foreign resident in Bali can expect a modest but functional lifestyle, provided expectations are calibrated carefully. Accommodation in popular expat corridors such as Seminyak, Canggu, and Ubud will consume the largest share of this budget. A basic but decent one-bedroom villa or apartment in these areas typically runs between IDR 4 million and IDR 8 million per month, depending on proximity to the coast, amenities, and lease duration. Longer-term contracts of six to twelve months generally yield more favorable pricing.

Food costs vary significantly depending on lifestyle choices. A foreigner relying primarily on local warungs and markets can eat well for IDR 1.5 million to IDR 2.5 million per month. However, frequent meals at Western-oriented cafes and restaurants, which have proliferated in the Canggu and Seminyak corridors, can push food expenditure to IDR 4 million or more.

Transportation in Bali remains relatively affordable by international standards. A monthly scooter rental runs approximately IDR 500,000 to IDR 700,000, with fuel costs modest for moderate daily use. Ride-hailing apps such as Grab and Gojek serve most tourist and expat zones and remain competitively priced for shorter journeys.

The remaining budget must account for utilities, internet connectivity, health insurance, and discretionary spending. Electricity costs, particularly for air-conditioned accommodations, can be a significant variable. Reliable high-speed internet, now essential for remote workers, adds another IDR 300,000 to IDR 600,000 monthly. Private health insurance — strongly advisable for any foreign resident without local employer coverage — is often not factored into entry-level budget estimates but represents a meaningful ongoing expense. At US$1,000 per month, there is little margin for unexpected costs, medical emergencies, or the social lifestyle that draws many to Bali in the first place.
