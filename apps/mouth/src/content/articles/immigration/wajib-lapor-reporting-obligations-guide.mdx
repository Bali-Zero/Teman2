# Wajib Lapor 2026: Your Reporting Obligations as a KITAS Holder in Indonesia

If you hold a KITAS (Kartu Izin Tinggal Terbatas) or KITAP (Kartu Izin Tinggal Tetap) in Indonesia, **Wajib Lapor** (mandatory reporting) is one of your most critical ongoing obligations. Missing these reports can result in fines, delays, or even denial of your KITAS renewal.

This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about Wajib Lapor in 2026: what to report, when, where, required documents, online options, and the consequences of non-compliance.

## What is Wajib Lapor?

**Wajib Lapor** literally means "mandatory report" in Indonesian. It's a legal requirement for all foreign nationals holding KITAS or KITAP to periodically report their presence, address, and activities to the local immigration office (Kantor Imigrasi).

### Legal Basis

Wajib Lapor is mandated by:

- **Immigration Law No. 6/2011** (Undang-Undang Keimigrasian)
- **Government Regulation No. 31/2013** on Immigration Implementation Regulations
- **Ministerial Regulation on Foreign Nationals Reporting** (Peraturan Menteri)

These regulations establish the reporting requirements, timelines, and penalties for non-compliance.

### Who Must Report?

**All KITAS holders**, regardless of visa type:

- Work KITAS (e25/e26)
- Investor KITAS
- Retirement KITAS
- Spouse KITAS
- Dependent KITAS
- Student KITAS

**KITAP (permanent residence) holders** also have reporting obligations, though requirements may differ slightly.

**Exemption:** Holders of temporary visit visas (B211A, tourist visa, visa on arrival) are NOT required to do Wajib Lapor. This obligation only applies to residence permit holders.

## Types of Wajib Lapor: What You Need to Report

There are three main types of reporting obligations for KITAS holders:

### 1. Quarterly Report (Every 3 Months)

**Frequency:** Every 90 days from your KITAS issuance or last report date

**Purpose:** Confirm your continued presence in Indonesia and current address

**Required documents:**

- Original passport
- Original KITAS card
- Previous SKLD (Surat Keterangan Lapor Diri) - your last report receipt
- Proof of address (rental agreement, hotel booking, or sponsor letter)
- Completed reporting form (provided at immigration office)

**Output:** You'll receive an **SKLD (Surat Keterangan Lapor Diri)** - a reporting certificate stamped by immigration. Keep this safe; you'll need it for your next report and KITAS renewal.

**Timeline:** Report within 30 days before or after your due date (60-day window total). However, reporting late may result in fines.

### 2. Address Change Report (Within 30 Days)

**Trigger:** Any change of residence address

**Timeline:** Must report within **30 days** of moving

**Required documents:**

- Original passport
- Original KITAS card
- Previous SKLD
- New proof of address (new rental agreement, homeowner letter, or hotel booking)
- Completed address change form

**Output:** You'll receive an **SKTT (Surat Keterangan Tempat Tinggal)** - a residence certificate with your new address.

**Important:** If you move to a different city or province, you may need to report to the immigration office in your new location AND update your KITAS sponsor if applicable.

### 3. Travel Reporting (Within 24 Hours of Return)

**Trigger:** Return from international travel

**Timeline:** Must report within **24 hours** of re-entering Indonesia

**Required documents:**

- Original passport (with new entry stamp)
- Original KITAS card
- Previous SKLD
- Flight details or boarding pass (as proof of travel dates)

**Output:** Immigration will note your return date. This may be combined with your quarterly report if the timing aligns.

**Note:** Some immigration offices are lenient on this 24-hour rule, but technically it's required. If you're a frequent traveler, ask your local immigration office about their specific requirements.

## How to Report: Step-by-Step Process

### Traditional In-Person Reporting

**Step 1: Gather Documents**

- Passport (original)
- KITAS card (original)
- Previous SKLD (if you have one)
- Proof of address
- Photocopies of all documents (2 copies each)

**Step 2: Go to Immigration Office**

- Arrive during office hours
- Take a queue number (if applicable)
- Wait for your number to be called

**Step 3: Submit Documents**

- Hand over your documents at the reporting counter
- Immigration officer will review your documents
- You may be asked to fill out a reporting form

**Step 4: Interview (Brief)**

- Officer may ask about your activities in Indonesia
- Questions are typically routine: "Where do you live?", "What do you do?", "Any problems?"
- Answer honestly and briefly

**Step 5: Receive SKLD**

- Officer will stamp and sign your SKLD
- Keep this document safe
- Note your next reporting due date (3 months from today)

**Processing time:** 30 minutes to 2 hours, depending on queue length

**Cost:** Usually free, but some offices may charge a small administrative fee (IDR 50,000-100,000)

### Online Reporting (Where Available)

As of 2026, **some** immigration offices offer online Wajib Lapor through:

- **Immigration Mobile App** (M-Imigrasi)
- **Official immigration website** (layanan.imigrasi.go.id)

**Availability:** Online reporting is NOT available at all locations. Bali (Ngurah Rai), Jakarta, and Surabaya immigration offices have piloted online reporting, but implementation varies.

**How to check:** Contact your local immigration office or check their website to confirm if online reporting is available for your visa type and location.

**Online process (if available):**

1. Download M-Imigrasi app or visit immigration website
2. Register account (linked to your passport and KITAS number)
3. Submit reporting form online
4. Upload scanned documents (passport, KITAS, proof of address)
5. Receive digital SKLD via email or app (usually within 1-3 business days)

**Limitations:** First-time reporting typically requires in-person visit. Online reporting is usually only available for subsequent quarterly reports, NOT for address changes or travel reporting.

## Timeline & Due Dates: When to Report

### Calculating Your Quarterly Report Due Date

**Method 1: From KITAS issuance date**

- KITAS issued: January 15, 2026
- First report due: April 15, 2026 (90 days later)
- Second report due: July 15, 2026
- Third report due: October 15, 2026

**Method 2: From last SKLD date**

- Last report: February 10, 2026
- Next report due: May 10, 2026

**Grace period:** You can report up to 30 days before or after your due date without penalty (in theory). However, immigration officers may note late reporting on your record.

**Best practice:** Report within 1-2 weeks before your due date to avoid any issues.

### Address Change Timeline

**Scenario:** You move from Denpasar to Ubud on March 1, 2026.

**Action required:** Report address change by **March 31, 2026** (within 30 days).

**Consequence of late reporting:** Fine of IDR 100,000-300,000 per day, up to a maximum of IDR 5,000,000.

### Travel Reporting Timeline

**Scenario:** You return from Singapore on a Monday at 10:00 PM.

**Action required:** Report to immigration by **Tuesday 10:00 PM** (within 24 hours).

**Reality:** Many immigration offices do not strictly enforce the 24-hour rule for travel reporting, but technically you can be fined for late reporting.

**Pro tip:** If you travel frequently, ask your immigration officer if you can combine travel reporting with your quarterly report to simplify the process.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Forgetting Your Report Due Date

**Problem:** Many expats lose track of their 90-day reporting cycle.

**Solution:**

- Set recurring calendar reminders (every 3 months)
- Take a photo of your SKLD and note the due date
- Use apps like "Immigration Tracker" (available for Android/iOS) to track your reporting schedule

### 2. Reporting at the Wrong Immigration Office

**Problem:** You must report at the immigration office that has jurisdiction over your current address. If you live in Ubud but report at the Denpasar airport immigration office, your report may not be accepted.

**Solution:** Verify the correct office based on your address. Call ahead if unsure.

### 3. Not Bringing Original Documents

**Problem:** Immigration requires original passport and KITAS. Photocopies alone are not sufficient.

**Solution:** Always bring originals + photocopies.

### 4. Missing Previous SKLD

**Problem:** Immigration uses your previous SKLD to verify your reporting history. If you lose it, you may face delays.

**Solution:**

- Keep all SKLDs in a safe folder
- Take photos of each SKLD as backup
- If lost, request a duplicate (may require written explanation letter)

### 5. Not Reporting Address Changes

**Problem:** If you move and don't update your address within 30 days, you can be fined and your KITAS renewal may be delayed.

**Solution:** Report address changes immediately. Even if you're moving within the same city, update your address.

### 6. Assuming Online Reporting is Available Everywhere

**Problem:** Online reporting is NOT available at all immigration offices.

**Solution:** Contact your local immigration office to confirm availability before relying on online reporting.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

### Late Quarterly Report

**Fine:** IDR 100,000-500,000 per missed report

**Additional consequences:**

- Negative note on your immigration record
- Potential KITAS renewal delay or denial
- Immigration officer may require written explanation letter

### Late Address Change Report

**Fine:** IDR 100,000-300,000 per day late, up to IDR 5,000,000 maximum

**Additional consequences:**

- Penalty stamp on your KITAS (visible to future immigration officers)
- May block KITAS renewal until fine is paid and address is updated

### Late Travel Report

**Fine:** IDR 50,000-200,000 (typically less strict than other violations)

**Additional consequences:** Usually minimal unless you have multiple violations.

### Severe Non-Compliance

If you miss **multiple** reporting deadlines or fail to report for more than 6 months, consequences can include:

- KITAS suspension or cancellation
- Deportation order (in extreme cases)
- Ban on future Indonesian visa applications (1-5 years)

**Reality check:** Immigration officers typically give warnings for first-time minor violations. However, repeat offenders or those with extended non-compliance face serious penalties.

## How Missed Reports Affect KITAS Renewal

When you apply to extend or renew your KITAS, immigration will review your **entire reporting history**.

### What Immigration Checks

1. **Quarterly report compliance:** Did you report every 3 months?
2. **Address reporting:** Did you report address changes within 30 days?
3. **Travel reporting:** Did you report returns from international travel?
4. **Fine payment:** Have you paid all fines for late reporting?

### Impact on Renewal

**Clean record:** Fast processing, no issues.

**1-2 late reports:** Immigration may issue a warning and still approve renewal, but processing may take longer.

**3+ late reports or unpaid fines:** High risk of KITAS renewal denial or requirement to restart the application process from scratch.

**Failure to report for 6+ months:** KITAS may be automatically cancelled, requiring you to leave Indonesia and reapply from your home country.

### How to Fix Reporting Gaps

**Step 1:** Visit your local immigration office and explain the situation.

**Step 2:** Bring all original documents and request to "report late" (lapor terlambat).

**Step 3:** Pay any fines immediately.

**Step 4:** Obtain SKLDs for all missed periods (if possible).

**Step 5:** Write a formal explanation letter (surat keterangan) explaining why you missed reports (work travel, medical emergency, etc.).

**Step 6:** Submit this documentation with your KITAS renewal application to demonstrate you've resolved the issue.

## Tips for Smooth Reporting

### 1. Keep a Reporting Calendar

Use Google Calendar or a similar app to set recurring reminders:

- 2 weeks before due date: "Prepare documents for Wajib Lapor"
- 1 week before due date: "Go to immigration this week"
- Due date: "Wajib Lapor due TODAY"

### 2. Use a Sponsor or Agent

Many expats use immigration agents to handle Wajib Lapor:

- **Cost:** IDR 200,000-500,000 per report
- **Benefit:** Agent handles queues, paperwork, and potential issues
- **Recommended for:** Busy professionals, those with language barriers, or anyone who finds the process stressful

**How it works:** You provide documents to agent → Agent goes to immigration on your behalf → Agent returns with SKLD.

**Note:** You still need to provide original documents and sign forms. The agent cannot report without your passport and KITAS.

### 3. Build a Wajib Lapor Document Folder

Create a physical or digital folder with:

- Passport scans (biodata + visa pages)
- KITAS scan (both sides)
- All previous SKLDs
- Current proof of address
- Passport photos (keep extras on hand)

Update this folder after each report.

### 4. Learn Basic Indonesian Phrases

You don't need fluency, but basic phrases help:

- "Saya ingin lapor diri" (I want to report)
- "Ini dokumen saya" (Here are my documents)
- "Kapan harus lapor lagi?" (When do I need to report again?)
- "Terima kasih" (Thank you)

Most immigration officers speak some English, but showing effort to speak Indonesian is appreciated.

### 5. Be Polite and Patient

Immigration offices can be bureaucratic and crowded. Maintain a calm, respectful attitude. If there's an issue with your documents, don't argue—politely ask what's needed and return with corrected documents.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I report early if I'm traveling?

**Yes.** You can report up to 30 days before your due date. This is helpful if you'll be out of the country during your reporting window.

### What if I'm in the hospital on my report due date?

**Contact immigration immediately.** Call your local immigration office and explain the situation. Bring a hospital letter (surat keterangan dokter) when you're able to report. Immigration typically grants extensions for medical emergencies.

### Do I need to report if I'm on a KITAP (permanent residence)?

**Yes, but less frequently.** KITAP holders typically report annually instead of quarterly. Requirements vary by immigration office, so confirm with your local office.

### Can my spouse report on my behalf?

**No.** Wajib Lapor requires the KITAS holder to appear in-person (or use authorized agent). Your spouse cannot report for you unless they have formal power of attorney.

### What if I lose my SKLD?

**Request a replacement.** Visit your immigration office with:

- Original passport
- Original KITAS
- Written explanation letter (in Indonesian or English)
- Possible fee (IDR 50,000-100,000)

Immigration will issue a duplicate SKLD.

### Can I report at a different immigration office if I'm traveling in Indonesia?

**No.** You must report at the immigration office that has jurisdiction over your registered address. If you've moved, update your address first (SKTT), then report at the new immigration office.

### Does Wajib Lapor apply to diplomats and official visa holders?

**No.** Diplomatic and official visa holders (visa diplomatik, visa dinas) are exempt from Wajib Lapor. Their presence is monitored through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.
