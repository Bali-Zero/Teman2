## The Facts

Indonesia's Golden Visa program, launched in 2022 under Government Regulation No. 39/2021 and operationalised through the Directorate General of Immigration, has added a dependent spouse category designated E31B. This sub-classification sits beneath the primary Golden Visa holder's status, designated E31A or simply E31, and grants the spouse long-stay residency without requiring independent investment qualification.

The Golden Visa itself is a multiple-stay visa issued for five or ten years, designed to attract foreign capital, talent, and retirees. The primary holder must meet investment thresholds — generally USD 350,000 for a five-year individual visa or USD 700,000 for the ten-year equivalent when investing in state instruments, or higher when depositing into Indonesian-incorporated companies. The dependent E31B classification extends derived residency rights to the legally married spouse of such a primary holder.

Under the E31B framework, the dependent spouse does not need to independently satisfy financial thresholds. Instead, their eligibility is anchored to the primary holder's active Golden Visa status. Should the primary visa lapse, be revoked, or not be renewed, the dependent status is expected to follow suit, making the principal holder's continued compliance the critical variable for family continuity.

Applications for the dependent spouse visa are processed through the Directorate General of Immigration, with documentation requirements typically including a valid passport, a certified and apostilled marriage certificate, evidence of the primary holder's active E31 status, and recent photographs meeting immigration standards. Processing is conducted at Indonesian embassies and consulates abroad or, for those already in-country on another legal status, through a status conversion at the local immigration office.

The introduction of a structured dependent category represents a maturation of Indonesia's Golden Visa architecture. Earlier iterations of the program left family reunification arrangements somewhat ambiguous, with spouses often resorting to Social Visa or KITAS routes that carried lower prestige, shorter validity, and more administrative burden. The E31B formalises what had been a practical gap in the framework.
