## The Facts

Indonesia's D12 visa is a purpose-built immigration instrument designed specifically for foreign nationals who need to conduct pre-investment activities before formally committing capital to the country. Unlike standard tourist or business visit visas, the D12 is structured around the investment lifecycle — acknowledging that serious investors require multiple visits, extended stays, and ongoing access to local partners, government offices, and prospective sites before any final commitment is made.

The Multiple Entry designation is the visa's defining feature. Holders are permitted to enter and exit Indonesia repeatedly within the validity period without needing to reapply for each journey. This is a critical distinction for investors who may be based in Singapore, Hong Kong, or other regional hubs and need to shuttle between their home base and Indonesia as negotiations and assessments progress.

The D12 falls under Indonesia's broader effort to attract foreign direct investment (FDI) by reducing administrative barriers. Indonesia has been actively reforming its immigration and business licensing framework in recent years, with the Job Creation Law (Omnibus Law) of 2020 serving as the legislative backbone for many of these liberalizations. The D12 visa represents the immigration side of this broader reform agenda.

Applicants for the D12 typically must demonstrate a credible intent to invest, which may include documentation such as a statement of investment purpose, evidence of financial capacity, and details of the business sector being explored. The visa is processed through Indonesian diplomatic missions abroad or, in certain cases, through authorized sponsors within Indonesia.

The validity period and permitted stay duration under the D12 are structured to provide meaningful runway for the pre-investment process. While specific parameters can vary based on individual applications and current ministerial regulations, the visa is generally understood to offer substantially more flexibility than a standard single-entry business visit visa. Investors should verify current validity terms directly with the Directorate General of Immigration or a licensed immigration consultant, as Indonesian visa regulations are subject to periodic ministerial updates.
