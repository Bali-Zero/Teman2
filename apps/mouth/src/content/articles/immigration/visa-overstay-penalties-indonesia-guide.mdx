# Visa Overstay Indonesia 2026: Penalties, Fines, Detention, and Resolution

**Overstaying your visa in Indonesia is expensive, stressful, and can result in deportation and re-entry bans.** Since 2024, the fine is **IDR 1,000,000 per day** with a maximum of 60 days before automatic deportation proceedings. If you're reading this because you've overstayed or are about to—don't panic, but act immediately.

This guide covers everything you need to know: fine schedules, detention procedures, how to resolve overstay situations, emergency extensions, and how to avoid overstaying in the first place.

## What Constitutes Visa Overstay in Indonesia?

**Visa overstay occurs when you remain in Indonesia beyond:**

1. **Visa validity date** on your visa sticker or e-Visa
2. **Extension deadline** if you previously extended your visa
3. **KITAS/KITAP expiration** for residence permit holders
4. **STM (Exit-Reentry Permit) deadline** if you left and returned

**Example scenarios:**

- **Tourist visa (B211A):** 60-day validity, you stayed 75 days = **15 days overstay**
- **Social-cultural visa:** Extended once (120 days total), you stayed 140 days = **20 days overstay**
- **KITAS holder:** Expired January 15, you're still in country on February 10 = **26 days overstay**

**Grace periods:** There are **NO grace periods**. Even 1 day over counts as overstay.

### Current Fine Structure (Since 2024)

**Base fine:** IDR 1,000,000 per day
**Maximum fine period:** 60 days (IDR 60,000,000 total)
**Beyond 60 days:** Deportation proceedings begin automatically

**Old system (pre-2024):** IDR 300,000 per day
**New system (2024-present):** IDR 1,000,000 per day

**Why the increase?** Indonesian government aims to deter visa abuse and align penalties with regional standards (Thailand: THB 500/day, Malaysia: MYR 300/day, Vietnam: USD 25/day).

## Immigration Detention: What Happens After 60 Days?

Once you exceed 60 days overstay, the situation escalates from "administrative violation" to "immigration case" with detention and deportation.

### Detention Process

**Step 1: Immigration summons or arrest**

- If you self-report: scheduled detention at immigration facility
- If discovered: immediate arrest and transfer to detention center
- **Detention centers:** Rudenim (Rumah Detensi Imigrasi) facilities in major cities

**Step 2: Case processing (7-30 days)**

- Investigation by immigration officers
- Document verification and home country embassy notification
- Deportation order preparation
- **You remain detained during this period**

**Step 3: Deportation execution**

- Immigration purchases your one-way ticket (you reimburse them)
- Escorted to airport by immigration officers
- Passport stamped with deportation record
- **Entry ban begins immediately**

### Detention Conditions

**Facilities vary by location, but generally:**

- Shared rooms (4-10 people per room)
- Basic meals provided (Indonesian food)
- Limited outdoor time (1-2 hours per day)
- No internet access in most facilities
- Visitors allowed (limited hours)

**Costs you pay:**

- Daily detention fee: IDR 50,000 - 150,000 per day
- Flight ticket: Cost of one-way ticket to home country
- Administrative processing: IDR 500,000 - 2,000,000
- Total: Often IDR 10,000,000 - 30,000,000 depending on ticket cost

**Duration:** Average 2-4 weeks from detention to deportation, but can extend to 2-3 months in complex cases.

## Entry Bans and Blacklist: How Long Are You Banned?

### Ban Duration by Offense

| Offense Type                              | First Offense                   | Second Offense      | Third Offense            |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Overstay 1-60 days (resolved voluntarily) | Warning only (no automatic ban) | 1-year ban possible | 2-year ban likely        |
| Overstay 61-90 days (deportation)         | 1-year ban                      | 2-year ban          | 5-year ban               |
| Overstay 91-180 days (deportation)        | 2-year ban                      | 5-year ban          | 10-year ban or permanent |
| Overstay 180+ days (deportation)          | 5-year ban                      | 10-year ban         | Permanent ban            |
| Immigration fraud or criminal activity    | 10-year ban or permanent        | Permanent ban       | Permanent ban            |

**Key distinctions:**

**"Warning only"** = Immigration officer discretion. If you self-report within 1-30 days overstay, pay fines immediately, and demonstrate genuine mistake (medical emergency, flight cancellation), you may avoid formal blacklisting. **But it's not guaranteed.**

**"Automatic ban"** = Triggered by deportation. Once you're deported (61+ days overstay or caught by authorities), the ban is automatic and non-negotiable.

### How to Check if You're Banned

1. **Visit Indonesian embassy/consulate** in your home country before booking flights
2. **Submit online inquiry** via https://www.imigrasi.go.id (Indonesian only, requires assistance)
3. **Request clearance letter** if applying for new visa after previous overstay

**Cannot check directly at airport.** If you're blacklisted, you'll be denied boarding at your departure airport or denied entry upon arrival in Indonesia.

## How to Resolve Visa Overstay: Step-by-Step Process

If you realize you've overstayed or are about to overstay, follow this process immediately.

### Option 1: Self-Report to Immigration (1-60 Days Overstay)

**Best for:** Short overstays where you can still exit voluntarily.

**Steps:**

1. **Go to nearest immigration office** (Kantor Imigrasi)
   - **Bali:** Jl. Raya Tuban, near Ngurah Rai Airport
   - **Jakarta:** Multiple offices; check https://www.imigrasi.go.id
   - **Bring:** Passport, visa documentation, proof of reason (flight tickets, medical records if applicable)

2. **Report to "Pelanggaran" (Violation) desk**
   - Explain situation honestly
   - Officer calculates overstay duration and fine amount
   - You receive **surat teguran** (warning letter) and payment notice

3. **Pay fine at designated bank**
   - Immigration will direct you to specific bank (usually BNI or BRI)
   - Pay exact amount in Rupiah
   - **Keep receipt—critical for exit clearance**

4. **Return to immigration office with receipt**
   - Submit payment proof
   - Officer stamps your passport with clearance
   - You receive **exit clearance letter**

5. **Exit Indonesia within 7 days**
   - Book flight immediately
   - Present clearance letter at airport immigration
   - **Do not delay—clearance expires after 7 days**

**Timeline:** 1-3 days if no complications
**Cost:** Overstay fine + bank fees (~IDR 10,000)

### Option 2: Emergency Extension (BEFORE Overstay Occurs)

**Best for:** You realize your visa is expiring in 1-5 days and can't exit in time.

**Steps:**

1. **Visit immigration office 1-3 days before expiration**
   - **Do not wait until expiration day**—offices may be closed or too busy

2. **Request "emergency extension"** (perpanjangan darurat)
   - Explain reason: flight cancellation, medical issue, family emergency
   - Provide evidence: flight cancellation email, hospital letter, death certificate, etc.

3. **Pay emergency extension fee**
   - **Cost:** IDR 500,000 - 1,000,000 depending on visa type
   - **Extension granted:** Usually 7-14 days (NOT full visa extension period)

4. **Exit within extension period**
   - This is a **grace period only**, not a proper visa extension
   - Cannot be extended again—you must exit

**Success rate:** 70-80% if you have legitimate documentation
**Denial:** If denied, you must exit immediately (before midnight on expiration day) or face overstay penalties

### Option 3: Emergency Flight and Force Majeure

**Best for:** Visa expires today or already expired by 1-3 days, but you have proof of unavoidable circumstances.

**Force majeure examples:**

- Natural disaster (earthquake, volcano eruption, tsunami)
- Medical emergency requiring hospitalization
- Flight cancellation due to airline bankruptcy or airport closure
- Death of family member in Indonesia requiring immediate presence

**Steps:**

1. **Gather documentation immediately**
   - Hospital discharge papers with admission/discharge dates
   - Flight cancellation official notice from airline
   - Police report (if applicable)
   - Embassy letter (if they're assisting)

2. **Go to immigration office with documents**
   - Request **force majeure consideration**
   - Officer reviews case and decides: waive fine OR reduce fine OR no leniency

3. **If approved:**
   - Fine waived or reduced (50-100% reduction possible)
   - Exit clearance issued immediately
   - Must exit within 3-7 days

4. **If denied:**
   - Pay full overstay fine
   - Follow standard self-report process (Option 1)

**Success rate:** 40-60% depending on evidence quality and officer discretion
**Critical:** Medical emergencies are most likely to be approved; "I forgot" or "I was traveling" are almost never approved.

## What Happens if You Try to Leave at the Airport?

**Scenario:** You overstayed, didn't resolve it with immigration office, and show up at Ngurah Rai Airport hoping to "just pay and go."

**What actually happens:**

1. **Immigration officer scans passport at exit checkpoint**
   - System flags overstay immediately (red alert on screen)

2. **You're escorted to airport immigration office**
   - Separate room, often in basement or restricted area
   - **You cannot board your flight**

3. **Immigration officer calculates fine and issues detention notice**
   - You pay overstay fine on the spot (if under 60 days)
   - **OR** you're detained if over 60 days or unable to pay

4. **If you pay fine:**
   - Officer may allow exit on next available flight (if you can rebook immediately)
   - **OR** officer requires you to return to city immigration office first

5. **If you can't pay or exceed 60 days:**
   - You're transferred to Rudenim detention center
   - Deportation process begins
   - Flight is forfeited (non-refundable)

**Bottom line:** Attempting to resolve overstay at airport is **high-risk**. Immigration officers have less discretion, process is slower, and you may miss your flight even if fine is payable.

## How to Avoid Visa Overstay: Prevention Tips

**The best strategy is never overstaying in the first place.** Here's how to stay compliant:

### 1. Track Your Visa Expiration Date

**Use multiple reminders:**

- Phone calendar: 30 days before, 14 days before, 7 days before, 3 days before
- Email reminder service (Calendly, Google Calendar)
- Physical calendar at your accommodation

**Common mistakes:**

- Confusing **visa issue date** with **entry date** (validity starts from entry, not issue)
- Forgetting **extension deadline** after extending visa
- Miscounting days (always count arrival day as Day 1)

**Pro tip:** Add visa expiration date to your phone lock screen or home screen widget.

### 2. Extend Your Visa Early

**Extension timeline:**

- Tourist visa (B211A): Extend 7-14 days before expiration
- Social-cultural visa: Extend 14-21 days before expiration
- KITAS: Renew 30-60 days before expiration

**Why early extension matters:**

- Immigration offices are often crowded (1-3 day processing delays)
- Public holidays can delay processing
- Document issues (photo quality, payment problems) need time to resolve

**Never assume "I'll extend tomorrow"—extend as soon as you're eligible.**

### 3. Book Flights with Buffer Time

**Flight booking strategy:**

- Book departure flight **3-7 days before** visa expiration (not on expiration day)
- Choose refundable or flexible tickets (in case visa extension needed)
- Avoid last-minute bookings during high season (July-August, December-January)

**Buffer time accounts for:**

- Flight cancellations or delays
- Visa extension delays
- Unexpected personal circumstances

### 4. Understand Visa Types and Limitations

**Common confusion:**

- **Visa-free entry (30 days):** Cannot be extended—must exit before day 30
- **VOA (Visa on Arrival, 30 days):** Can extend once (30 days more, 60 total)
- **e-VOA (35 days):** Can extend once (30 days more, 65 total)
- **B211A (60 days):** Can extend 4 times (60 days per extension, 300 days total)

**Rule:** Know your visa's extension eligibility **before entering Indonesia**. Plan accordingly.

### 5. Monitor KITAS Renewal Status Actively

**For KITAS holders:**

- **Do not rely on sponsor company alone**—monitor your own renewal timeline
- Request status updates every 7 days from sponsor during renewal process
- If renewal is delayed beyond expiration date, get **KITAS extension letter** from immigration

**Employer negligence is not a defense:** If your employer fails to renew your KITAS on time and you overstay, **you're still liable** for overstay fines. (You can sue employer later, but immigration will penalize you first.)

## Overstay FAQs

### Can I extend my visa after it has already expired?

**No.** Once your visa expires, you're in overstay status. You cannot extend an expired visa. You must:

1. Resolve overstay by paying fines at immigration office
2. Exit Indonesia immediately
3. Apply for new visa from outside Indonesia

**Exception:** Force majeure cases (hospitalized, natural disaster) may receive emergency exit clearance, but not visa extension.

### Will overstay fines appear on my passport?

**Yes and no.**

- **Visible stamp:** Immigration officer stamps your passport with overstay clearance code (looks like regular exit stamp to most people, but immigration officers worldwide recognize it)
- **Digital record:** Overstay is recorded in Indonesian immigration database and shared with Interpol/ASEAN immigration systems
- **Future visa applications:** Overstay record may affect visa approvals for Indonesia and other countries (Australia, Singapore, etc.)

**Moral:** Even if you pay fines and exit, overstay creates a permanent immigration record.

### Can I come back to Indonesia immediately after resolving overstay?

**Technically yes, if:**

- Overstay was under 60 days (no deportation)
- You paid all fines and exited voluntarily
- You were not formally blacklisted

**BUT:**

- Immigration officers at entry may question you
- You may be denied entry at officer's discretion (even without formal ban)
- Recommend waiting 30-90 days before returning to avoid scrutiny

**If deported:** You cannot return until ban period expires (1-5 years minimum).

### What if I can't afford to pay the overstay fine?

**Options:**

1. **Contact your embassy** - Some embassies provide emergency loans to citizens (must be repaid)
2. **Borrow from friends/family** - Wire transfer or Western Union
3. **Negotiate payment plan** - Some immigration offices allow installment payments (rare, requires approval)
4. **Accept detention** - If you truly cannot pay, you'll be detained until your embassy arranges repatriation

**Warning:** Do not attempt to leave without paying. You'll be detained at airport and situation worsens.

### Does travel insurance cover overstay fines?

**No.** Standard travel insurance does not cover visa overstay penalties because overstay is considered a **preventable violation**, not an insurable risk.

**What travel insurance DOES cover:**

- Medical emergencies (hospital bills)
- Flight cancellations due to covered reasons (illness, natural disaster)
- Trip interruption (if you need to stay longer due to medical reasons, but does NOT cover overstay fines resulting from extended stay)

**Always check policy fine print.** Some policies explicitly exclude "immigration penalties" from coverage.
