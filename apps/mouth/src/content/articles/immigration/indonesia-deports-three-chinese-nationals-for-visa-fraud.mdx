## The Facts

Indonesian immigration authorities deported three Chinese nationals after determining they had violated the country's immigration laws through visa fraud, according to ANTARA News, Indonesia's official state news agency. The case adds to a pattern of enforcement actions that Direktorat Jenderal Imigrasi — the Directorate General of Immigration under the Ministry of Law and Human Rights — has intensified in recent years.

Visa fraud in the Indonesian context typically encompasses a range of violations: using tourist visas (most commonly the B211A or Visa on Arrival) to engage in paid employment or business activities; presenting forged or altered immigration documents; misrepresenting the purpose of a visit on arrival declarations; and operating under-the-table employment arrangements without securing the required work permit (IMTA) and the accompanying residence permit (KITAS or ITAS).

Chinese nationals represent one of the largest groups of foreign visitors and workers in Indonesia. In recent years, Bali and other economic corridors have seen a marked increase in Chinese-owned or Chinese-staffed businesses operating in sectors ranging from hospitality and retail to construction and mining — some of which have drawn scrutiny for employing workers without valid authorization documents.

Indonesia's immigration enforcement framework is anchored in Law No. 6 of 2011 on Immigration (Undang-Undang Keimigrasian), which grants authorities broad powers to detain, deport, and permanently blacklist foreign nationals found in violation. Deportation orders are typically accompanied by entry bans that can range from one year to a permanent prohibition, depending on the severity of the offense.

Operasi Jagratara, the Directorate General of Immigration's recurring nationwide sweep targeting undocumented or improperly documented foreign nationals, has netted hundreds of individuals annually since its institutionalization. Local immigration offices (Kantor Imigrasi) at the regency level carry independent authority to initiate inspections, detentions, and deportation proceedings, meaning enforcement is not limited to major entry points such as Ngurah Rai International Airport.
