## The Facts

Indonesia operates a layered visa system that serves a spectrum of foreign nationals — from short-term tourists to long-term investors — each with distinct eligibility criteria, durations, and conditions. The country's immigration framework is governed primarily by Law No. 6 of 2011 on Immigration, with subsequent implementing regulations issued by the Directorate General of Immigration under the Ministry of Law and Human Rights.

At the entry level, citizens of eligible countries may arrive under a Visa-Free arrangement for short stays, typically up to 30 days for tourism purposes. Indonesia recognises bilateral and multilateral visa exemptions with dozens of countries, though the list is periodically reviewed based on reciprocity and security considerations. Separately, a Visa on Arrival (VoA) is available at designated international ports — including Ngurah Rai International Airport in Bali — for nationals of qualifying countries, currently priced at IDR 500,000 and valid for 30 days with one extension of 30 days possible.

For those seeking longer stays, the B211A Social-Cultural Visit Visa — commonly used by digital nomads and remote workers — allowed stays of up to 60 days with multiple extensions. However, Indonesian immigration authorities have been progressively refining the visa-on-arrival and visitor visa categories, with the B211A being phased out in favour of the C1 Tourist Visa category under ongoing regulatory updates. Travellers should verify current processing pathways before making plans.

The KITAS (Kartu Izin Tinggal Terbatas), or Limited Stay Permit, serves foreign nationals with longer-term legitimate purposes: employment, investment, retirement, marriage to an Indonesian citizen, or participation in government programmes. KITAS categories vary significantly in their sponsorship requirements, processing timelines, and associated rights. The KITAP (Permanent Stay Permit) becomes available to qualifying holders after several consecutive years of holding a valid KITAS.

Indonesia's Second Home Visa, introduced under Presidential Regulation No. 37 of 2022, was designed to attract high-net-worth individuals by offering up to 10-year stays upon placing a substantial deposit in a designated Indonesian bank — the threshold set at IDR 2 billion for the initial tier. The programme targets retirees, investors, and affluent remote workers who can demonstrate financial self-sufficiency without taking up formal employment.
