## The Facts

Indonesian Minister of Law and Human Rights Yusril Ihza Mahendra has publicly declared an end to the fast-track processing option for ITAS (Izin Tinggal Terbatas, Limited Stay Permit) and ITAP (Izin Tinggal Tetap, Permanent Stay Permit) for foreign nationals. The announcement, reported by Suara Surabaya, positions the decision as a core component of the government's campaign to eradicate pungli — pungutan liar, or illegal levies — from the immigration apparatus.

Pungli has been endemic in Indonesian bureaucracy for decades, and immigration services have historically been among the sectors most affected. The 'jalur cepat' — fast track — referred both to formally offered premium processing tiers and, more commonly, to the informal practice of cash payments made to immigration officers or intermediaries in exchange for accelerated document handling. For foreign nationals, this created a two-tiered system where ability to pay, rather than procedural merit, could determine how quickly a residence permit was issued or renewed.

Yusril's directive signals that the Prabowo administration intends to standardize the immigration queue. Under the new policy, all ITAS and ITAP applications are to be processed through a single, uniform channel. The ministry frames this as restoring equal treatment under the law and eliminating the financial incentive for immigration officials to discriminate between applicants based on informal payments received.

ITAS and ITAP together cover the full spectrum of long-term legal residence in Indonesia. ITAS is granted to foreigners holding sponsored working or investment purposes and is typically issued for periods of six months to two years, renewable within Indonesia. ITAP, a more senior status, is available to foreigners who have held ITAS for a qualifying period and grants a more stable five-year renewable residency status. Both documents are administered by the Directorate General of Immigration under the Ministry of Law and Human Rights.

The timing of this announcement reflects a broader anti-corruption posture within the new administration. President Prabowo Subianto took office in October 2024 and has publicly committed to institutional reform across ministries. Yusril, a veteran legal figure, has positioned the Ministry of Law and Human Rights as an early mover in this agenda. Whether the formal abolition of fast-track channels will translate into measurable reductions in informal payments — or simply shift the dynamic underground — remains to be seen and will depend heavily on enforcement depth and officer accountability mechanisms.
