## The Facts

Mardani Ali Sera, a member of Indonesia's House of Representatives (DPR) serving on Commission II, which oversees home affairs and regional administration, publicly criticized the government for what he described as an indiscriminate sell-off of state land assets to attract foreign capital. His remarks were directed at the government's rollout of the Golden Visa program and the accompanying commitment by Minister of Agrarian Affairs and Spatial Planning Agus Harimurti Yudhoyono (AHY) to guarantee land rights for Golden Visa holders.

Under the Golden Visa framework, eligible foreign nationals who invest a defined minimum capital in Indonesia receive a long-stay limited visa granting residency privileges. Under Permenkumham 11/2024, which amends Permenkumham 22/2023, individual foreign investors can qualify for the Golden Visa (Index E28/E28C) by purchasing luxury apartments or condominiums valued at a minimum of USD 1,000,000. The program is framed by the government as a mechanism to deepen foreign direct investment and signal Indonesia's openness as an investment destination.

The land rights guaranteed under the scheme include Hak Milik Atas Satuan Rumah Susun (strata title over individual apartment units), Hak Guna Usaha (HGU, or business land use rights), and other rights enumerated in Government Regulation No. 18 of 2021. At the new national capital Nusantara (IKN), HGU concessions have been granted for periods reaching 190 years — a tenure Mardani characterized as spanning seven generations. Presidential Regulation 76/2024 (Perpres 76/2024) governs the Land and Investment Allocation Task Force responsible for granting, revoking, and reallocating these state land concessions to both domestic and foreign-invested business entities.

Indonesian immigration law places a categorical limit on one class of rights: under Article 39 of Law No. 6 of 2011 on Immigration, as amended by Law No. 63 of 2024, foreign nationals are strictly prohibited from acquiring freehold title (Hak Milik) over Indonesian land. The Golden Visa framework operates within this constraint, offering use-based rights rather than outright ownership. In Bali specifically, Perda Bali 2/2025 imposes a regional minimum purchase threshold of IDR 5 billion for foreign nationals acquiring real estate under Hak Pakai or strata title, a figure substantially higher than the national baseline of IDR 2 billion set under Permenkumham 22/2023.

Mardani argued that the sustained government drive to attract foreign conglomerates by opening access to state land risked deepening agrarian inequality and triggering land conflicts. He framed the policy as a structural imbalance between investor incentives and the unmet land needs of Indonesian citizens. No legislative vote or formal reversal of the program was announced alongside his statement.

## Bali Zero Take

### Our Analysis

The DPR criticism is a political signal worth tracking, but it does not currently alter the legal framework governing how foreign nationals hold property in Bali. For foreign investors and residents, the operative reality remains unchanged: Hak Milik is categorically unavailable to foreigners. What the Golden Visa and Second Home Visa programs provide is a structured, legally defined pathway to Hak Pakai and strata title — rights that are real and enforceable under current law.

The risk to watch is political trajectory, not immediate regulatory change. Indonesia has tightened or reversed investment-friendly land policies before when domestic pressure has built sufficiently. The 190-year HGU concessions at IKN are a legitimate legislative target, and if that debate gains momentum, it could eventually reframe the broader conversation around foreign land access rights — including residential categories.

For Bali-based clients, the immediate priority is compliance, not alarm. The IDR 5 billion regional minimum under Perda Bali 2/2025 is firm and non-negotiable. Nominee ownership arrangements carry elevated legal and political exposure in the current environment and should be avoided entirely.

## In Practice

Foreign nationals in Bali holding or pursuing property positions should factor the following into their planning. The IDR 5 billion threshold set by Perda Bali 2/2025 is Bali's own rule and overrides the lower IDR 2 billion national baseline — there is no regional exemption. The Golden Visa (E28/E28C) requires a minimum USD 1,000,000 investment in qualifying residential property at the national level; in Bali, the stricter regional threshold applies concurrently. The Second Home Visa (E33A) sets a national baseline of IDR 2 billion, but Bali's Perda 2/2025 figure controls locally.

Outright freehold title (Hak Milik) remains categorically unavailable to foreign nationals under Article 39 of UU 6/2011 as amended. Arrangements designed to simulate Hak Milik through nominees or similar structures carry serious legal exposure under Indonesian law and increasing political risk as the agrarian debate intensifies. HGU rights — relevant to business investors — are a separate category from residential Hak Pakai and require active business registration. The IKN HGU controversy discussed in the article does not currently affect residential property rights in Bali.
