## The Facts

Indonesia's immigration authorities have moved to eliminate fast-track processing options for foreign nationals seeking residency and stay permits, in a policy shift announced by a senior minister. The decision marks a reversal from recent years in which successive governments had introduced expedited pathways aimed at attracting high-net-worth individuals, digital nomads, and foreign investors.

The fast-track mechanism had allowed qualifying applicants — typically those meeting financial thresholds or holding sponsored employment contracts — to receive approval for their Temporary Stay Permit (KITAS) or equivalent documentation in a compressed timeframe compared to the standard administrative queue. Processing times under the accelerated route had been cited as a key competitive advantage over regional peers such as Thailand, Malaysia, and the Philippines, which have each launched their own premium residence programs in recent years.

Indonesia had intensified its efforts to attract long-stay foreigners following the pandemic, launching the Second Home Visa (C8) in 2022 and promoting so-called digital nomad provisions for remote workers. The fast-track element was viewed as operationally central to those programs: without it, the appeal of paying premium fees in exchange for certainty on timelines diminishes substantially.

The minister's statement did not provide an effective date or specify whether existing applications in the fast-track queue would be grandfathered under old rules or rerouted to standard processing. That ambiguity is likely to create a short-term surge of queries to immigration consultants and law firms as applicants seek clarity on their individual cases.

Indonesia's immigration policy has undergone significant turbulence since 2023, with several high-profile enforcement actions against overstayers, a tightening of the rules governing the on-arrival VOA visa used as a de facto long-stay instrument, and a formal review of the Second Home Visa scheme amid questions over whether its financial requirements were being enforced rigorously. The removal of fast-track processing fits a broader pattern of the current administration consolidating control over immigration flows and signaling that the era of easy-entry premium lanes is closing.
