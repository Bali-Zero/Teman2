## The Facts

On the evening of Thursday, September 18, 2026, a joint enforcement team drawn from the Class I Special Immigration Office at Ngurah Rai (Kantor Imigrasi Kelas I Khusus TPI Ngurah Rai), the Bali Regional Office of the Directorate General of Immigration (Kanwil Ditjen Imigrasi Bali), and the Bali Regional Police (Polda Bali) launched simultaneous raids across three of the island's most densely populated expat zones: Canggu, Seminyak, and Umalas. The operation commenced at 17:00 WITA and resulted in the detention of 13 foreign nationals.

Authorities stated the operation was triggered by a combination of public tip-offs and the proactive monitoring of suspicious online activity. Officials who appeared at a subsequent press briefing included Muhammad Novyandri, Head of the Ngurah Rai Immigration Office; Hendarsam Marantoko, Director General of Immigration; and Ramdhani, Head of the Bali Regional Office of the Directorate General of Immigration. The presence of the Director General signals this was not a routine local action.

In Canggu, officers entered a private villa where they found a Russian national identified by the initials IP. IP is alleged to have been actively misusing a Remote Worker residence permit—a visa category designed to attract digital professionals working for foreign employers—to advertise and sell commercial sex services via WhatsApp. Officers subsequently traced an IP address linked to the online activity to a nearby apartment, where they detained a Ukrainian national identified as OG. OG is suspected of acting as an organiser and facilitator of prostitution services. Immigration records show that OG's investor residence permit (ITAS) had expired on October 14, 2025, meaning he had been residing in Indonesia unlawfully for approximately eleven months at the time of his arrest.

The operation then extended to Seminyak, where the joint team was tracking a dedicated online promotional website linked to further commercial sex services. Full details from the Seminyak and Umalas components of the raid had not been published at the time of reporting, but a total of 13 foreign nationals were detained across all three areas, all of them suspected of misusing their residence status.

All detainees face immigration enforcement proceedings under Indonesian law, which provides for deportation and, in cases of deliberate permit misuse, a potential multi-year re-entry ban. The all detainees are described as having misused their legally issued residence permits rather than entering illegally, underlining that the operation targeted permit misuse, not undocumented entry.

## Bali Zero Take

### Our Analysis

This operation is a clear marker of where Bali's immigration enforcement has arrived in 2026. Authorities are no longer relying on physical inspections or neighbour tip-offs alone: they are actively tracing IP addresses, monitoring WhatsApp activity, and scanning online promotional platforms, then cross-referencing findings against permit databases in near real time. The detention of a Remote Worker visa holder is especially significant. That permit category was introduced to attract legitimate digital talent, and its inclusion in an enforcement operation of this scale sends a direct message: novel permit categories carry the same obligations as any other, and misuse of purpose will be treated as a hard immigration violation.

The ITAS expiry case is equally instructive for our clients. An investor permit that lapsed in October 2025 went unaddressed for nearly a year—but when enforcement caught up, the context was as damaging as it could possibly be. The lesson is not that overstay is tolerated; the lesson is that when it surfaces, it surfaces at the worst moment. Permit renewal deadlines are non-negotiable.

The multi-agency, Director General-level format of this operation confirms a structural shift: Bali is moving from reactive enforcement to proactive, intelligence-led operations. Clients should internalise this as the new baseline, not an isolated event.
