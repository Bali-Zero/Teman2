## The Facts

Indonesia's immigration framework offers two primary residence permit tracks for foreign retirees: the Retirement KITAS, a temporary stay permit requiring annual renewal, and the Retirement KITAP, a permanent stay permit that provides multi-year or indefinite residency once eligibility thresholds are met. The KITAP — Kartu Izin Tinggal Tetap — represents the upper tier of Indonesia's legal residence hierarchy for foreigners.

To qualify for a Retirement KITAP, an applicant must generally have held a valid Retirement KITAS for a minimum of five consecutive years without significant interruption. This tenure requirement is a hard prerequisite: immigration authorities expect applicants to demonstrate genuine long-term commitment to residing in Indonesia rather than using the country as a periodic base.

Financial thresholds form the second pillar of eligibility. Retirement KITAS holders must demonstrate proof of passive income or pension funds deposited in an Indonesian bank account — the commonly cited figure is a minimum monthly income equivalent of roughly USD 1,500, though this benchmark has varied in practice and may be adjusted at the discretion of the sponsoring immigration agent or regional Imigrasi office. A clean criminal record, valid health insurance, and a local sponsor or guarantor are also standard requirements across the application.

The KITAP, once granted, is typically valid for five years and renewable, with some holders ultimately eligible for an indefinite stay permit. Crucially, KITAP holders are permitted to re-enter Indonesia freely without needing to apply for a re-entry permit each time — a practical advantage that regular KITAS holders do not enjoy.

However, the Retirement KITAP does not confer the right to work. Indonesia maintains a strict separation between its retirement and employment visa categories. Retirees found to be engaging in paid or unpaid professional activities risk permit revocation and potential deportation. The permit is designed exclusively for individuals whose income derives entirely from foreign pensions, savings, or investments held outside Indonesia's domestic labor market.
