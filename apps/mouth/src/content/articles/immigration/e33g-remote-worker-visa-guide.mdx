# E33G Remote Worker Visa 2026: Indonesia's Digital Nomad Visa

If you're a remote worker earning USD 60,000+ per year from a foreign employer or clients, the E33G Remote Worker visa (also called "Visa Pekerja Jarak Jauh") is Indonesia's official digital nomad visa. It allows you to live in Bali or anywhere in Indonesia for 1-2 years without needing to set up a company, get RPTKA work authorization, or pay monthly DKP-TKA fees.

This guide covers everything you need to know about E33G requirements, costs, application process, and tax implications as of 2026.

<InfoCard variant="warning" title="2026 Update (Source: imigrasi.go.id)">
**Hybrid KITAS System (since May 2025):**

Indonesian Immigration now uses a **hybrid system** for KITAS applications:

- **Online Phase**: Initial application, document upload, payment via imigrasi.go.id portal
- **In-Person Phase**: Biometrics (fingerprints, photo), original document verification, physical KITAS collection

**Important**: Physical presence in Indonesia is still required for biometrics. E33G visa is valid for 90 days from issuance - you must enter Indonesia within that period. Upon entry, ITAS (KITAS card) and re-entry permit are issued automatically.

</InfoCard>

## What is E33G Remote Worker Visa?

<InfoCard variant="note" title="E33G Remote Worker Visa (Digital Nomad Visa)">
A Limited Stay Permit (ITAS/KITAS) for foreign nationals who:
- Work remotely for a company or clients OUTSIDE Indonesia
- Earn minimum USD 60,000 per year (USD 5,000/month)
- Do not work for Indonesian companies or entities
- Have valid employment contract or proof of remote income

**Key benefits:**

- No company setup required in Indonesia
- No RPTKA (work authorization) needed
- No monthly DKP-TKA fees (saves USD 100/month)
- Much cheaper than working KITAS (IDR 13M vs 34.5M)
- Valid 1-2 years, renewable
- Can sponsor spouse and children
  </InfoCard>

### E33G vs Tourist Visa

Many digital nomads in Bali operate on tourist visas (B211A or E-VOA extensions), but this comes with risks:

| Aspect                     | E33G Remote Worker   | Tourist Visa (B211A)          |
| -------------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Legal to work remotely** | ✅ Yes               | ❌ No (gray area)             |
| **Duration**               | 1-2 years            | Max 180 days                  |
| **Cost (offshore)**        | IDR 13,000,000       | ~IDR 5,500,000 (6 extensions) |
| **Renewable**              | Yes, indefinitely    | No (must leave Indonesia)     |
| **Can sponsor family**     | Yes                  | No                            |
| **Bank account**           | Easy to open         | Very difficult                |
| **Risk of deportation**    | Very low             | Medium (if caught working)    |
| **Exit requirement**       | No (re-entry permit) | Must leave every 180 days     |

**Reality check:** While many nomads work on tourist visas in Bali, immigration crackdowns have increased. E33G provides legal peace of mind and is more cost-effective long-term.

### How to Prove Income

Immigration accepts several forms of proof:

1. **Employment Contract** - From foreign employer showing salary of USD 5,000+/month
2. **Bank Statements** - Last 3-6 months showing consistent deposits of USD 5,000+/month
3. **Client Contracts** - If freelance, contracts with foreign clients totaling USD 60K+/year
4. **Tax Returns** - Previous year's tax return showing USD 60K+ income
5. **Company Registration** - If you own a foreign company, proof of business + bank statements

<InfoCard variant="warning" title="Income Source MUST Be Foreign">
**Critical:** Your income must come from:
- Company established OUTSIDE Indonesia
- Clients located OUTSIDE Indonesia
- Remote work performed for foreign entities

**You CANNOT:**

- Work for Indonesian companies (even if paid in USD)
- Provide services to Indonesian clients
- Earn income from Indonesian sources
- Freelance for Indonesian businesses

Violation = visa revocation + possible deportation.

</InfoCard>

## Tax Implications

<InfoCard variant="warning" title="Tax Residency Warning">
**183-Day Rule:**

If you stay in Indonesia for **more than 183 days in a 12-month period**, you become an Indonesian tax resident. This means:

1. **You must register for Indonesian NPWP (tax number)**
2. **You may owe Indonesian income tax on worldwide income** (progressive rates up to 35%)
3. **You must file annual Indonesian tax returns**

**Tax treaty benefits:** Many countries have tax treaties with Indonesia to prevent double taxation. Consult a tax advisor about:

- Tax treaty benefits with your home country
- Foreign tax credits
- PT setup for tax optimization
  </InfoCard>

### Tax Residency Strategies

| Strategy                 | Pros                         | Cons                                |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Stay < 183 days/year** | No Indonesian tax obligation | Must leave Indonesia regularly      |
| **Accept tax residency** | Live in Bali full-time       | Pay Indonesian tax (up to 35%)      |
| **Set up PT for income** | Lower corporate tax (22%)    | Requires company setup + compliance |
| **Use tax treaty**       | Reduce double taxation       | Requires professional tax advice    |

**Bali Zero recommendation:** Consult with a tax advisor BEFORE applying for E33G if you plan to stay 183+ days. We can refer you to tax specialists who work with digital nomads.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Working for Indonesian Clients

**WRONG:**

- "I have E33G so I can freelance for Bali businesses"
- "I'll just invoice through my foreign company"

**RIGHT:**

- E33G income MUST come from foreign sources only
- Even if paid in USD, Indonesian clients = violation
- Need E25B Working KITAS to work for Indonesian entities

### 2. Overstaying VITAS

**WRONG:**

- "VITAS is valid 90 days, I can enter anytime"
- Then forget about it and try to enter 4 months later

**RIGHT:**

- VITAS is valid 90 days FROM ISSUANCE DATE
- Must enter Indonesia before VITAS expires
- If VITAS expires, must reapply (pay again)

### 3. Not Tracking Tax Residency

**WRONG:**

- "I'm on a visa, immigration handles my taxes"
- Stay 300 days/year, never register for NPWP

**RIGHT:**

- Immigration ≠ tax office
- Track your days in Indonesia carefully
- Register for NPWP if > 183 days
- File tax returns if required

### 4. Freelancing Without Proof

**WRONG:**

- Apply with "I'm a freelancer" and no documentation
- Bank statements show random transfers with no explanation

**RIGHT:**

- Need CONTRACTS with foreign clients
- Bank statements showing regular income
- Tax returns or business registration as proof
- Payment processor statements (PayPal, Wise, etc.)

### Can I switch from tourist visa to E33G while in Indonesia?

**Yes, but it's complicated:**

1. **Onshore conversion** (inside Indonesia) costs IDR 14M (vs IDR 13M offshore)
2. You need to be on a **long-term visa** (B211A with extensions), not a short-term VOA
3. Processing can take longer (2-3 weeks)
4. **Easier option:** Exit Indonesia, apply for E33G from abroad, re-enter with VITAS

**Bali Zero recommendation:** If you're on a tourist visa and want E33G, do a visa run (Singapore, KL, Bangkok) and apply for E33G offshore. Cheaper and faster.

### What if I want to work for BOTH foreign and Indonesian clients?

You need **two separate arrangements:**

1. **E33G for foreign income** - Keep working for US/EU/other clients
2. **PT + E25B for Indonesian income** - Set up Indonesian company, get working KITAS

**Reality:** Most nomads just keep Indonesian clients as "foreign clients" (invoice through their home country company). But this is technically a gray area. If you're earning significant Indonesian income, set up a PT and get E25B.

### Can I bring my partner (unmarried)?

**No.** E31 family visa requires:

- Spouse: Marriage certificate
- Children: Birth certificate showing you as parent

**Unmarried partners** must apply for their own visa:

- E33G (if they also earn USD 60K+/year remotely)
- B211A tourist visa (max 180 days)
- Or another visa type they qualify for

**Bali Zero cannot sponsor unmarried partners as "dependents".**

### Do I need to show USD 60K every year?

**Yes, for renewal.**

When you renew E33G:

- Must provide updated bank statements (last 6 months)
- Must show continued income of USD 60K+/year
- If income drops below USD 60K, visa may not be renewed

**What if income drops?**

- Consider B211A tourist visa (6 months max)
- Or leave Indonesia and return when income recovers
- Or switch to E28A investor KITAS (if you invest IDR 10B+ in PT PMA)

**When to choose each:**

- **E28A:** Have IDR 10B+ to invest in PT PMA, want pure investor status
