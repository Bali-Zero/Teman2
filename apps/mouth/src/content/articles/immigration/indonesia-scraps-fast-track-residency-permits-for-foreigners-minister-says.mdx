## The Facts

Indonesia's Coordinating Minister for Law, Human Rights, Immigration and Corrections, Yusril Ihza Mahendra, has declared that fast-track channels for foreign residency permits have been abolished. The statement, reported by Liputan6, marks a significant shift in how the Directorate General of Immigration manages applications from foreign nationals seeking to live in the country.

The term 'jalur cepat' — literally 'fast lane' — has historically referred to expedited processing pathways that allowed certain categories of applicants, often investors or high-net-worth individuals, to move through the bureaucratic pipeline at an accelerated pace. These mechanisms were distinct from the standard KITAS and KITAP application queues available to the general foreign population.

Yusril currently oversees immigration policy as part of the Prabowo Subianto administration's coordinating ministry structure, giving his statements direct policy weight. His remarks suggest a deliberate push toward uniform processing standards, eliminating any perception of preferential treatment in the immigration system.

The announcement comes against the backdrop of Indonesia's broader effort to overhaul its immigration architecture. In recent years, the government introduced instruments such as the Golden Visa — a long-term stay permit targeting investors and global talent — which carried its own premium processing track. Whether those Gold Visa pathways are affected by this policy shift has not been immediately clarified in available reporting.

The immigration directorate has faced scrutiny over procedural inconsistencies and, in some documented cases, corruption at processing stages. Eliminating formal fast-track mechanisms could be read as part of a transparency and anti-corruption push, though the practical effect on processing times for all categories of applicant remains to be seen.
