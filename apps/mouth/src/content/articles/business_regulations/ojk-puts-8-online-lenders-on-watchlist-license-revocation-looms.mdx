## The Facts

Indonesia's Otoritas Jasa Keuangan (OJK), the country's integrated financial services regulator, has moved eight peer-to-peer (P2P) online lending platforms—colloquially known as 'pinjol,' short for pinjaman online—onto its special supervision list, according to a report by Suara.com. The designation signals that the regulator has found material compliance failures at these operators and is prepared to revoke their business licenses if corrective action is not taken within a defined remediation window.

Special supervision (pengawasan khusus) is a formal, escalated regulatory status in the OJK framework, distinct from routine monitoring. It is typically triggered by breaches that include inadequate capital buffers, failure to meet minimum equity thresholds, persistent violations of borrower protection rules, data governance failures, or inability to demonstrate sound risk management. Platforms placed in this category are publicly identifiable, which itself constitutes a significant commercial liability—borrowers and lenders can withdraw, accelerating any liquidity stress.

The P2P lending sector in Indonesia is governed primarily by OJK's regulations on technology-based lending services. OJK maintains an official registry of licensed platforms, currently managed in coordination with the Asosiasi Fintech Pendanaan Bersama Indonesia (AFPI), the sector's self-regulatory organization. Any platform removed from that registry loses the legal right to intermediate loans, and existing loan books face uncertain resolution procedures.

OJK has progressively tightened oversight of the fintech lending space following a wave of consumer complaints, predatory collection practices, and insolvencies in prior years. The regulator has revoked dozens of licenses since 2022, culling the sector from over a hundred registered operators to a significantly smaller, theoretically more robust cohort. The eight platforms named in the current round represent a continuation of that consolidation drive.

The names of the eight platforms were not independently confirmed at the time of publication. OJK typically discloses special supervision decisions through its official website and periodic press releases, and the current cohort is expected to be formally listed there. Platforms under special supervision are generally given a defined period—often 30 to 90 days—to remediate identified deficiencies before the regulator proceeds to license cancellation.
