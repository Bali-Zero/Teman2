## The Facts

Foreign-owned companies in Indonesia — structured as PT PMA, or Perseroan Terbatas Penanaman Modal Asing — are required by law to maintain a registered business address that appears in their Articles of Association (Akta Pendirian) and is endorsed by the relevant regional government through a Domicile Letter, known as Surat Keterangan Domisili Perusahaan or SKDP. This address anchors the company's legal identity and is tied to the company's NIB (Nomor Induk Berusaha), business license classification, and tax registration.

Virtual offices have become a widely accepted solution for meeting this requirement, particularly during the early stages of a company's establishment when a physical office may be premature or cost-prohibitive. A virtual office provider supplies a formal street address in a commercially zoned building, along with ancillary services such as mail handling, phone answering, and access to meeting rooms on an as-needed basis.

However, not all virtual office arrangements are legally equivalent. The provider itself must hold a valid SIUP (Surat Izin Usaha Perdagangan) and operate from premises zoned for commercial or mixed-use activity. Residential addresses — including villa and homestay addresses — are categorically ineligible for PT PMA registration. Regional governments, including Bali's, have tightened enforcement of zoning compliance in recent years, and registrations tied to non-compliant addresses risk rejection or subsequent invalidation.

The BKPM (Badan Koordinasi Penanaman Modal, now operating as the Investment Coordinating Board under the Ministry of Investment) processes PT PMA registrations through the OSS (Online Single Submission) system. The OSS platform links the declared address to its administrative kelurahan and kecamatan, and cross-references it against regional spatial planning data. Discrepancies between the declared address and OSS zoning data are a common cause of registration delays.

Beyond initial registration, companies using virtual offices must also maintain the arrangement in good standing throughout their operational life. Lapses in the virtual office contract — or a change of address that is not reflected through a formal Akta Perubahan (amendment to the Articles of Association) — can create compliance gaps that surface during tax audits, licensing renewals, or due diligence by banks and business partners.
