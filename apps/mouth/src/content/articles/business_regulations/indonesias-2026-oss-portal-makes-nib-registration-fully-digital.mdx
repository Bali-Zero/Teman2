## The Facts

Indonesia's government has continued its push toward a fully digital business registration ecosystem, with the 2026 iteration of the Online Single Submission (OSS) platform—accessible at oss.go.id—now enabling business owners to obtain their Nomor Induk Berusaha (NIB) without a single visit to a central government office.

The NIB, or Business Identification Number, is the foundational license for any legally operating business in Indonesia. Introduced as part of the government's single-window investment and licensing reform, it serves as the master registration document that unlocks access to downstream permits—sector-specific licenses, import rights, and in some cases fiscal incentives. Without a valid NIB, no business entity in Indonesia can operate legally or access further government services.

The 2026 OSS portal has been engineered for deeper inter-agency integration. Back-end systems now connect directly with the Directorate General of Trade (Kemendag), the Investment Coordinating Board (BKPM), and local Cooperative Offices (Dinas Koperasi). This integration is designed to reduce the manual transfer of data between agencies that historically caused delays and discrepancies in registration records, a problem that added days or weeks to the process.

To begin the process, applicants must prepare four core documents in digital format: a national identity card (KTP or e-KTP for Indonesian nationals), an active NPWP (Nomor Pokok Wajib Pajak, the Indonesian tax identification number), a Surat Keterangan Domisili Usaha (SKDU)—a business domicile certificate issued by the local kelurahan or kecamatan office—and a minimum of two photographs of the business premises, showing both the exterior and interior.

Registration begins with account creation on oss.go.id using a valid email address and mobile phone number. The platform employs OTP (one-time password) verification, which can fail in areas with weak mobile connectivity. Once logged in, users navigate to the 'Pendaftaran Usaha' (Business Registration) menu, where they must classify their enterprise using the Klasifikasi Baku Lapangan Usaha Indonesia (KBLI)—Indonesia's standardized business activity code system. The KBLI code selected directly determines which additional permits, if any, will be required beyond the NIB itself. According to Statistics Indonesia (BPS) data from 2025, approximately 62 percent of micro-enterprises in North Sumatra were still operating with physical documentation, underscoring that the shift to digitized records remains a practical hurdle across many parts of the country. The OSS system applies nationwide—the same portal and process govern business registration in Bali, Java, Sumatra, and all other islands. Once issued, the NIB does not carry an expiration date under current regulations, but must be updated whenever material changes occur to ownership, address, or business classification.
