# CHAPTER: The Fitness & Padel Club

**1. The Dream**

Lukas had a body built by iron and a business plan built by spreadsheets. A former semi-pro athlete from Munich, he had spent his thirties running a chain of boutique gyms across Bavaria—the high-end kind, with cold plunges and InBody scanners and a waitlist. He had watched, with the predatory attention of a man who reads markets, the single hottest trend to hit Bali in years: padel. The courts were multiplying across Canggu and Uluwatu like mushrooms after rain. Every new one had a queue. Every queue was full of exactly his demographic—affluent, fitness-obsessed expats and tourists with money to burn on a membership.

His dream fused the two things he understood best. A premium fitness and padel club in Pererenan: four glass-walled padel courts, a serious strength-and-conditioning gym, a recovery zone with sauna and ice baths, a smoothie bar, and a members' community of the island's most committed athletes. He had the operational playbook memorized from his Bavarian chain. He had identified the land. He had run the numbers until they sang.

He arrived at the consultant's office in athletic wear, radiating the certainty of a man who had never lost a deal he'd prepared for. "Fitness center and padel courts," he said. "Premium club. I think it's 93116 for the gym, maybe 93199 or a sport-facility code for the courts. Let's get the PMA moving—this market won't wait."

**2. The Reality Check**

"The market won't wait," the consultant agreed. "But the province already moved, and it moved against you."

Lukas's certainty flickered. "Meaning?"

"93116—*Fasilitas Pusat Kebugaran/Fitness Center*, the fitness-center code—is blocked for new foreign PMA in Bali. It went down with the broad moratorium on the thirteenth of May, 2026. Nationally it's wide open, a hundred percent. Provincially, in Bali, it's locked."

"That's insane. Do you know how much money is flowing into fitness here right now? Padel alone is—"

"I know exactly how much," the consultant said. "And that's part of why it's blocked. The moratorium sealed the low and medium-low risk activities, and a fitness center at the large PMA scale lands in exactly that risk band—Rendah/Menengah-Rendah on every scope. The province looked at the categories that were low-barrier, replicable, and suddenly very lucrative because of foreign demand, and it closed them to new foreign ownership. Your gym is a poster child: low regulatory complexity, exploding demand, and a perfect target for the province to reserve for local entrepreneurs riding the same wave."

Lukas pulled up the sport-facility codes he'd researched. "Fine. Forget the gym box. The padel courts—those are sport facilities. 93114, 93124, 93199. What's the status?"

The consultant nodded slowly. "Now you're in more honest but messier territory. Several of those sport-facility and club codes—the lapangan (court/field) codes, the 'other sports facility' codes—carry a *needs-review* status in our data: they have no clean OSS scale row, which means the moratorium verdict isn't cleanly determined and the registrability is genuinely uncertain rather than a clean yes. That's not a green light; it's a flashing amber. You'd be building a multi-court facility on codes whose foreign-PMA status is unresolved—and 'unresolved' is a dangerous foundation for poured concrete."

"So the single hottest fitness trend on the island," Lukas said slowly, "is either blocked or unresolved for a foreigner."

"For the *core* fitness-center code, blocked. For the sport-facility codes around it, unresolved. Neither is the clean open door you walked in expecting. The demand is real. The doors are not open."

**3. The Blueprint: The Fitness Fact Sheet**

The trap that catches fitness founders is the assumption that booming demand implies an open door. In Bali the relationship can invert: the hotter and more replicable the trend, the more likely the province has moved to keep it for locals.

Here is the verified, ground-truth reality.

> ### THE KBLI BATTLEGROUND: FITNESS & SPORT FACILITIES
>
> *Crucial context: these codes are 100% open on the National Investment List. The obstacle is the Bali provincial moratorium plus unresolved scale data on several sport codes.*
>
> **THE LOCKED DOOR — THE GYM**
> *   **93116 — Fasilitas Pusat Kebugaran/Fitness Center:**
>     *   *National Status:* 100% PMA Open.
>     *   *Bali Status:* **BLOCKED** for new PMA (moratorium 13 May 2026). At Besar scale, OSS risk is Rendah/Menengah-Rendah on every scope—inside the sealed band.
>
> **THE AMBER DOORS — THE COURTS & CLUBS**
> *   **93114 — Fasilitas Lapangan (Courts/Fields):** *needs review* — no clean OSS scale row; foreign-PMA registrability unresolved.
> *   **93124 — Klub Tenis Lapangan (Racquet Club):** *needs review* — same unresolved status (the closest natural home for a padel club).
> *   **93127 — Klub Kebugaran/Fitness Dan Binaraga (Fitness/Bodybuilding Club):** *needs review* — unresolved.
> *   **93199 — Aktivitas Lainnya Berkaitan dengan Olahraga (Other Sports Activities):** *needs review* — unresolved.
>
> **THE LESSON IN THE NUMBERS**
> *   "Blocked" and "needs review" are both *not* "open." A booming market does not mean a registrable code.
> *   Unresolved status on a capital-heavy build (courts, gym equipment) is a flashing amber, not a green light.

**4. The Money**

A fitness-and-padel club is one of the most capital-intensive lifestyle businesses a founder can attempt—and that weight is precisely what makes the regulatory uncertainty so dangerous. Padel courts are real construction: specialized surfaces, glass walls, lighting, drainage. A serious gym means tens of thousands of dollars in equipment. Add the recovery zone, the build-out, the lease on a large parcel, the minimum paid-up capital every PMA must commit, and the staffing—and you have a multi-hundred-thousand-dollar commitment in fixed, immovable infrastructure.

Now pair that with a *blocked* core code and *unresolved* surrounding codes, and the financial picture turns hazardous. With 93116 blocked, the gym box simply cannot be the registered activity. With the court codes unresolved, building a padel facility means committing heavy capital to infrastructure whose foreign-PMA registrability is not confirmed—the worst kind of bet, because the money goes into the ground (literally) before the regulatory answer is clean. Lukas's spreadsheets were flawless on revenue and silent on the one variable that mattered: whether he could legally own the thing the spreadsheets described.

The workaround founders reach for—*register some adjacent, clearly-open recreation or education code and run the gym under it*—is a code mismatch. If the substance is a fitness center, registering it as something else does not make 93116's blockage disappear; it adds enforcement risk to an already heavy investment.

**5. The Timeline**

For the gym code, 93116, there is no timeline in Bali—it's blocked, so there is nothing to schedule. For the court codes, the timeline is worse than long: it is *contingent*. Lukas couldn't draw a Gantt chart for the padel facility because the first milestone—confirm that a foreign PMA can register this activity in this province—is unresolved. You cannot schedule construction against an unconfirmed permission.

This is the cruelty of "needs review" for a heavy builder: it is not a clean no (which would let him walk away and redeploy his capital) and not a clean yes (which would let him build). It is a maybe, and a maybe is the one answer you cannot pour concrete on. His honest timeline question was not "how fast can I build the courts?" but "do the court codes resolve to a yes before I commit, and am I willing to wait—or walk—until they do?"

**6. The Bali Angle**

Fitness sits squarely in the island's anxiety about foreigners capturing the most profitable, most replicable corners of the lifestyle economy. Padel didn't exist in Bali a few years ago; now it's a goldmine, and the province watched that goldmine fill almost entirely with foreign operators serving foreign customers. Closing the fitness-center code and leaving the sport codes unresolved is the province applying the brake: keeping the next wave of these high-demand, low-complexity lifestyle businesses available to Balinese entrepreneurs rather than letting them be absorbed by arriving foreigners with deeper pockets.

The deeper Bali Angle for Lukas is that his greatest asset—being early-ish to a booming trend with capital and operational expertise—is exactly what the province is reacting against. The island is not trying to stop fitness from existing; it is trying to stop the pattern where every lucrative new lifestyle category becomes foreign-owned by default. The blocked gym and the amber courts are the province saying: *this wave will not break entirely in foreign hands.* Reading that as policy, not malfunction, is what separates a founder who plans intelligently around the rules from one who burns capital fighting them.

**7. The Pitfall**

The pitfall that could have cost Lukas a six-figure facility was the most natural assumption a market-driven founder can make: *demand this strong must mean the door is open.* He reasoned backward from the queues at the padel courts—if everyone's doing it and everyone's making money, surely it's permitted. But in Bali, visible booming demand for a low-barrier business is often a *predictor* of closure, not a guarantee of access. The province closes the doors people are crowding through.

The second pitfall is treating "needs review" as a soft yes. An optimistic builder hears "not blocked" and starts pouring concrete. But unresolved is not open; it is the regulatory equivalent of unmapped water, and the heaviest, most immovable investments are the worst things to float on it.

The crucial lesson: in Bali, never let market heat substitute for code verification, and never treat capital-heavy as a reason to rush an unresolved code. The more concrete a business requires, the more certainty its code must carry before the first truck arrives. Lukas knew the fitness market cold. What he nearly missed is that the very thing he was best at spotting—an exploding, low-barrier, high-margin trend—is precisely the kind of thing the province moves to close. The dream of the club is not impossible. But it cannot be built on a blocked core code and a ring of unresolved ones, and the discipline is to confirm the door before you build the room.

**8. The Way Through**

Lukas had come in certain, then watched that certainty drain. The consultant let the silence sit, then turned the conversation from what was closed to what was not. "The province closed the *fitness-center box*," she said. "It did not close fitness. It did not close sport, or coaching, or recovery, or food. What it closed is one low-barrier code at large scale. Around that single locked door there are doors that are genuinely open to a foreign PMA — verified open, not amber. None of them is a clone of the club you drew. Each is a different, honest business that gets you most of the way to the same place, if you are willing to build the thing the code actually describes."

She wrote on the whiteboard.

> ### THE OPEN ROUTES — VERIFIED ALTERNATIVES
>
> *Every code below is confirmed registrable for a foreign PMA in Bali in our ground-truth data — unlike 93116 (blocked) and the 93114/93124/93127/93199 ring (unresolved). "Registrable" is the floor, not the finish line: each sits in a higher-risk band than the sealed one, and each carries its own sector permit. Open means you can start, not that you can skip the licensing.*
>
> **ROUTE 1 — Build the arena, not the "fitness center." `93113 — Fasilitas Gelanggang/Arena.`** The padel courts are, in substance, a sport arena — a managed multi-court facility. 93113 describes exactly that and is open to PMA. This is the most honest home for the courts, *provided the registered, operated reality is an arena facility* (court hire, events, tournaments) and not a fitness center wearing an arena's name. The reason it is open where 93116 is closed is that it lands in a higher-risk band — which means more building, fire, and sport-facility compliance, not less. The construction itself (specialised surfaces, glass, drainage) has its own open service codes and SBU construction licensing; the arena code does not absorb them.
>
> **ROUTE 2 — Sell the program, not the gym. `85510 — Pendidikan Olahraga dan Rekreasi`** (sports & recreation education). Lukas's true edge is not the equipment — it is the Bavarian operational playbook: coaching, strength-and-conditioning programming, padel academy. A sports-education PMA can own and run *that* legally: coaches, classes, certifications, the brand. It needs education-sector permits and accredited trainers — this is not a self-declared activity. It does not, by itself, grant you the right to operate a fitness-center floor; it grants you the right to teach and coach.
>
> **ROUTE 3 — Own the recovery zone as wellness. `96230 — Aktivitas SPA, Sauna, dan Pemandian Uap.`** The sauna, ice baths, and recovery suite are a wellness facility in their own right and have a clean open PMA code. Run as a genuine day-spa / recovery business it stands on its own and complements the rest — but it is a health-adjacent activity with its own health/tourism permits, and it covers recovery, not the gym floor.
>
> **ROUTE 4 — Capture the members at the bar. `56101 — Penyediaan Makanan di Bangunan Tetap` (food service) and `56301 — Aktivitas Bar`.** The smoothie bar, the community café, the members' social hub — the part of the club where margin and loyalty actually live — are open to PMA. These carry food-safety and (for the bar) beverage licensing; an F&B code is a licence to serve food and drink, never a back-door licence to run the blocked gym beside it.
>
> **ROUTE 5 — The partner structure (no single code does this alone).** Split ownership along the line the province actually drew. A Balinese-owned local company (UMKM) holds the *blocked storefront* — the fitness-center operation the moratorium reserves for locals — while Lukas's PMA owns what is genuinely his to own: the brand, the coaching program (Route 2), the recovery wellness (Route 3), and a management/licensing agreement for know-how. This is a real commercial relationship with a real local principal who carries real risk and real upside — not a borrowed name. It is harder, slower, and requires a partner you trust; that difficulty is the point, and it is the difference between a structure and a sham.

> **WHAT IS *NOT* A ROUTE**
> - **The nominee.** A Balinese "owner" who is really Lukas with a borrowed name is not Route 5 — it is the crime Route 5 is designed to avoid. If the local partner carries no risk and makes no decisions, you have built a fiction the province can unwind.
> - **The code mismatch.** Registering 93113, 85510, or any open code and then quietly running a full fitness-center underneath it is the exact trap §4 and §7 warned about. Running activity Z under code Y does not make 93116's blockage vanish; it stacks enforcement risk on top of buried capital. The registered reality must match the operated reality.
> - **The fake upgrade.** Adding a second open KBLI does not "automatically upgrade" your licence class or waive a sector permit. Every one of these codes needs its own health, tourism, construction (SBU), or education approval. None of these routes "fully circumvents" the moratorium — they reach an adjacent legal goal, they do not defeat the rule.

The principle Lukas needed was the inverse of the one he walked in with. He had assumed that booming demand opens doors; in Bali, demand this visible is what *closes* them. The way through is not to find a cleverer name for the closed business — it is to ask, honestly, *which of the things I actually do is legal for me to own here*, and to build that. Lukas could not own a foreign fitness center in Pererenan. But he could own the academy that trains its members, the recovery suite that heals them, the bar where they gather, the arena where they play — and, with a genuine local partner, a stake in the floor itself. That is a smaller, cleaner, more defensible empire than the one on his spreadsheet, and it is one he can actually keep.

*Before you sign anything, scan to check the live status of KBLI 93116 against the open routes — 93113, 85510, 96230, 56101, and 56301 — on the Nuzantara platform, and confirm the sector permit each one demands.*
