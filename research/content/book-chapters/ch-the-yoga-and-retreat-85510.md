# CHAPTER: The Yoga & Retreat Studio

**1. The Dream**

Amara had come to Bali to heal and ended up wanting to build. A burned-out trauma therapist from California, she had arrived in Ubud for a three-week retreat and stayed nine months, finishing her two-hundred-hour yoga teacher training and then a breathwork certification and then a sound-healing apprenticeship under a teacher whose name she said with reverence. Somewhere in the rice terraces she had found the thing every retreat brochure promises and almost none deliver: she had actually changed.

So her dream was not a business in the way Lukas's gym was a business. It was a *calling* with a P&L attached. A retreat center in the hills above Ubud—a shala with a view of the valley, a few guest bungalows, a program of week-long immersions blending yoga, breathwork, and trauma-informed somatics. She would host twelve guests at a time, charge serious money for serious transformation, and live the life she'd come here to find while helping others find it too.

She knew the economics were real—a well-run retreat can clear tens of thousands of dollars per cohort. But she also knew, from a hundred horror stories whispered in Ubud cafés, that Bali had become a minefield of blocked codes. Villas blocked. Cafés blocked. Consulting blocked. She arrived at the consultant's office already grieving a dream she assumed was dead.

"I want to run yoga and wellness retreats," she said, bracing. "Teaching, programs, immersions. I'm guessing it's blocked like everything else. Just tell me how bad it is."

**2. The Reality Check**

For the second time that month, the consultant got to deliver the rarest line in his profession.

"This one's open," he said.

Amara blinked. "Open."

"The core of what you're describing—organizing yoga teacher trainings, breathwork programs, surf schools, recreational and sports education—lives under 85510, *Pendidikan Olahraga dan Rekreasi*, sports and recreation education. Nationally it's a hundred percent open. And in Bali it is *not* blocked by the moratorium. At the large PMA scale, its OSS risk lands at Medium-High/High—above the low-risk band the province sealed. The teaching-and-program side of your retreat is one of the doors that's actually open."

Amara, who had walked in grieving, felt something unfamiliar: hope, with a catch she could already sense. "You said 'the teaching side.' What's the catch?"

"You're sharp. The catch is the *bungalows*. Your dream has two halves, and they live under two different codes. The yoga programs, the trainings, the immersions—that's education, 85510, open. But the part where guests *sleep on your property*—that's accommodation, and short-term lodging in Bali is a minefield. Villa (55203), homestay (55201), other short-stay (55209)—those carry blocked or closed verdicts. So the verdict on your retreat depends entirely on how you structure the *sleeping*, not the teaching."

"So I can teach, but I can't house them?"

"You can teach freely. Housing them under your own PMA, on your own land, under a blocked accommodation code—that's where it gets hard. The clean version of your business is the *program*: you run the retreat as recreation-and-wellness education under 85510, and you partner with an existing, properly-licensed accommodation provider for the beds rather than trying to register the lodging yourself. The retreat industry is full of operators who own the program and rent the venue. The moment you try to also own the blocked accommodation code, you walk out of the open door and into the locked one."

For the first time, Amara understood that her dream was not one business but two stapled together—and that one staple was open and the other was a trap.

**3. The Blueprint: The Wellness Education Fact Sheet**

The insight that saves retreat founders is learning to separate the *program* from the *property*. The teaching is open; the lodging is the minefield. Most failed retreat PMAs die not because the wellness work was blocked, but because the founder bundled blocked accommodation into the same company.

Here is the verified, ground-truth reality.

> ### THE KBLI BATTLEGROUND: WELLNESS, YOGA & RETREATS
>
> *Crucial context: all codes below are 100% open on the National Investment List. The Bali verdicts differ sharply between the teaching half and the lodging half.*
>
> **THE OPEN DOOR — THE PROGRAM**
> *   **85510 — Pendidikan Olahraga dan Rekreasi (Sports & Recreation Education):**
>     *   *National Status:* 100% PMA Open.
>     *   *Bali Status:* **OPEN.** Not blocked—at Besar scale, OSS risk is Medium-High/High, above the sealed band.
>     *   *What it covers:* Non-degree training, camps, and immersions in sport and recreation—explicitly including yoga teacher trainings, surf schools, and recreational instruction.
>
> **THE MINEFIELD — THE LODGING**
> *   **55203 — Villa:** **CLOSED** to PMA (no Besar scale / reserved for UMKM).
> *   **55201 — Homestay (Pondok Wisata):** **CLOSED** (reserved for Indonesian citizens).
> *   **55209 — Other Short-Term Accommodation:** **CLOSED** to PMA (no Besar scale).
>
> **THE LESSON IN THE NUMBERS**
> *   Your retreat is two businesses: teaching (open) and lodging (mostly closed). Keep them separate. Own the program; partner for the property.
> *   The wellness work is welcome. The accommodation is the trap.

**4. The Money**

The economics of the program-only model are genuinely attractive, and—rare for this book—the regulation and the money point the same direction. A retreat run as 85510 education is relatively light: your assets are your teachers, your curriculum, your brand, and the shala space (which you can lease rather than own as registered accommodation). The cost is the PMA formation, the minimum paid-up capital, the program build-out, and marketing—not a hotel's worth of bedrooms and the blocked codes that come with them.

The dangerous version of the math is the one Amara had in her head walking in: build the whole compound, bungalows and all, on her own land, under her own company. That model staples a blocked accommodation code onto an open education code—and the blocked half poisons the whole structure. The financially *and* legally sound version is to spend on the program and the brand, and to source the beds through partnership with an accommodation provider who already holds the right license. You capture the high-margin part of the retreat (the transformation people pay for) without sinking capital into the part the province has closed.

The temptation to dodge—*register the education code and quietly run accommodation on the side*—is precisely the bundling the authorities watch for. If guests sleep on your property as part of your paid program, that is an accommodation activity regardless of what your PMA is registered as. Keep the sleeping with a licensed partner and the model stays clean.

**5. The Timeline**

Because 85510 is genuinely open, Amara's timeline was a real, finishable one—corporate formation, the education licensing, the program setup. By Bali standards it is on the gentler end: no commercial kitchen, no padel-court construction, no hotel build. A program-led retreat company can come together in a span measured in weeks-to-months rather than the open-ended limbo of a blocked code.

The timeline only breaks if she insists on owning the lodging. The moment she tries to register blocked accommodation, the clean schedule dissolves into the no-timeline of a locked door. The structural discipline—own the program, partner for the property—is also what keeps the timeline real. The founders who finish are the ones who built the open business; the ones who stall are the ones who couldn't let go of owning the beds.

**6. The Bali Angle**

There is a deep and fitting logic to why the province left wellness *education* open while closing the lodging around it. Bali's identity—its global brand, its gravitational pull—is spiritual and recreational. Yoga, healing, transformation: this is the island's own cultural export, and welcoming foreigners who come to teach and deepen that tradition (responsibly, in genuine exchange) aligns with how Bali sees itself. The open door for 85510 is, in a sense, the island saying *yes* to the part of the foreign influx that honors what Bali already is.

The closed accommodation codes are the other half of the same philosophy: the *land* and the *lodging*—the physical, rooted, finite assets—are what the province is determined to keep in local hands. So the policy, read whole, is coherent and even generous: come, teach, run your immersions, build your program and your brand—but do not also become the landlord. For Amara, the Bali Angle is almost a blessing in disguise. The island is open to her calling and closed to her becoming a property owner—which, if she's honest, separates the part of the dream that was about service from the part that was about acquisition. Build on the part the island welcomes.

**7. The Pitfall**

The pitfall for the retreat founder is the inverse of every other chapter in this book: not assuming a door is open when it's closed, but assuming everything is closed and nearly walking away from a door that's genuinely open. Amara arrived having so thoroughly absorbed the blocked-code horror stories that she almost didn't ask—she came to grieve, not to build. The pessimism that protects you from the villa trap can rob you of the retreat opportunity if you let it harden into "everything is blocked."

The mirror pitfall is the bundling trap: hearing "yoga retreats are open!" and racing to build the full compound, bungalows included, under one company—stapling the blocked accommodation code to the open education one and poisoning the whole structure. "The retreat is open" is true of the *teaching* and false of the *lodging*, and the founder who doesn't separate the two builds a company with a locked door inside it.

The crucial lesson: a single "business" can span multiple KBLI codes with opposite verdicts, and your job is to find the seam. Amara's retreat was one dream made of two codes—one open, one closed. The discipline that saves her is structural: register and grow the open half (85510 education), and partner for the closed half (accommodation) rather than trying to own it. The wellness work the island welcomes; the beds it reserves. Build the part Bali says yes to, and don't let either pessimism or bundling cost you a dream that, this time, is genuinely available.
