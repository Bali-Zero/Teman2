# Your Annual Shareholders' Meeting Is Now a Public Filing

**A 2026 compliance notice for every Indonesian company — from Bali Zero's Corporate Desk**

---

For years, the annual shareholders' meeting was a formality. Directors prepared a report, shareholders approved it, the minutes went into a drawer. Nothing was filed. Nothing happened.

**That era ended on 17 December 2025.**

Under a new regulation from the Ministry of Law — **Permenkum No. 49 of 2025** — your annual report approval must now be recorded by a notary and filed into the government's corporate system. What used to be an internal ritual is now a public filing with real sanctions. The first cycle caught by the new rules is your **2025 financial year**, and the clock is already running.

This notice explains, in plain terms, what you must do, by when, and what happens if you don't.

---

## The two dates that matter

The obligation is a chain of two deadlines. Miss the first and the second becomes impossible.

**1 — Approve the annual report by 30 June 2026.**
Indonesian company law (**UU No. 40 of 2007**, the Company Law) has always required the directors to present the annual report — financial statements, activity summary, supervisory notes — to the **General Meeting of Shareholders (RUPS)** within **six months of the end of the financial year**. For a company with a 31 December year-end, six months lands on **30 June**.

**2 — File the notarized approval into SABH within 30 days of the notary's deed.**
This is the new part. Under **Article 16 of Permenkum 49/2025**, the shareholders' approval of the annual report must be:

- recorded in a **notarial deed** (*akta notaris*) — not a signed PDF, an actual deed made by a notary; and
- submitted to the Ministry of Law's **SABH system** (the AHU Online platform) **within 30 days, counted from the date the deed is signed** (*tanggal penandatanganan akta*), not from the meeting date.

The filing is done **by the notary**, not by you directly.

> **In practice:** if your RUPS approves the report and the deed is signed on 30 June 2026, the notary has until **30 July 2026** to file it into SABH. That is why the real outer edge of this obligation is late July — but the chain only works if the meeting happens by 30 June. There is no separate "30 July deadline" in the law; it is simply 30 June plus the notary's 30-day filing window.

---

## What happens if you ignore it

The penalty under **Article 17 of Permenkum 49/2025** is not a fine. It is something that hurts a working company far more.

First, the Ministry issues a **written warning** (*teguran tertulis*). Then, if you still don't file, it **blocks your company's access to SABH** (*pemblokiran akses SABH*) — the single system through which *every* corporate legal change in Indonesia flows.

A blocked company keeps existing, but it is **legally frozen**. While blocked, you cannot:

- appoint or replace directors or commissioners;
- transfer shares or change shareholders;
- amend your company deed — including any KBLI or activity changes;
- update your registered address or capital.

And because immigration and licensing systems read the same corporate data, the freeze spreads: you will hit walls renewing **work permits and KITAS** for foreign staff and updating your **OSS licensing records**.

The freeze almost always reveals itself at the worst possible moment — mid-transaction, mid-hire, mid-renewal — when you suddenly need a corporate change and discover the door is shut.

---

## If your company did nothing in 2025, this still applies to you

This is the trap that catches the most owners, so read it twice.

The obligation attaches to the **legal entity, not to its turnover.** A PT that traded nothing in 2025 — a holding company for a villa, a venture that never launched, a structure kept "just in case" — still owes its shareholders an annual report (even one showing zero revenue) and still owes the SABH filing.

Indonesia has thousands of quietly dormant PTs. Their owners will mostly discover the block only when they try to **sell, restructure, or close** — and find they cannot, because a report they never thought mattered was never filed.

**If you own a sleeping PT, wake it up once this month: one short report, one deed, one filing. Then it sleeps again, compliant.**

## Who has a lighter path

- **PT Perorangan** (single-owner micro companies): no shareholders' meeting and no notarial deed are required. The financial report is filed electronically through SABH directly — the lighter regime, by design.
- **Companies with a non-December financial year:** your six-month clock runs from *your* year-end. The 30 June date is simply the deadline for the December majority.
- **Companies that prefer not to gather in person:** the Company Law (**UU 40/2007, Article 91**) allows shareholders to approve in writing — a circular resolution — without a physical meeting, provided everyone signs. Many notaries are processing the June wave exactly this way. Your notary will confirm the format; the deadline does not care whether the approval happened around a table or by circulating signatures, only that the deed exists and gets filed.
