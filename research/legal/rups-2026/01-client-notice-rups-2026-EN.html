THE CHANGE

A formality became a filing

For years, the annual shareholders' meeting was a ritual. Directors prepared a report, shareholders approved it, the minutes went into a drawer. Nothing was filed. That era ended on 17 December 2025.

Under a new regulation from the Ministry of Law — Permenkum No. 49 of 2025 — your annual report approval must now be recorded by a notary and filed into the government's corporate system. What used to be internal is now a public filing with real sanctions. The first cycle caught by the new rules is your 2025 financial year, and the clock is already running.

01

The two dates that matter

The obligation is a chain of two deadlines. Miss the first and the second becomes impossible.

1 — Approve the annual report by 30 June 2026

Indonesian company law (UU No. 40 of 2007, the Company Law) has always required directors to present the annual report — financial statements, activity summary, supervisory notes — to the General Meeting of Shareholders (RUPS) within six months of the end of the financial year. For a 31 December year-end, six months lands on 30 June.

2 — File the notarized approval into SABH within 30 days of the notary's deed

This is the new part. Under Article 16 of Permenkum 49/2025, the shareholders' approval must be recorded in a notarial deed (akta notaris) — not a signed PDF — and submitted to the Ministry of Law's SABH system (AHU Online) within 30 days, counted from the date the deed is signed (tanggal penandatanganan akta), not from the meeting date. The filing is done by the notary, not by you directly.

IN PRACTICE — WHERE "30 JULY" COMES FROM

If your RUPS approves the report and the deed is signed on 30 June 2026, the notary has until 30 July 2026 to file it into SABH. That is why the real outer edge of this obligation is late July — but the chain only works if the meeting happens by 30 June. There is no separate "30 July deadline" in the law; it is simply 30 June plus the notary's 30-day filing window.

02

What happens if you ignore it

The penalty under Article 17 of Permenkum 49/2025 is not a fine. It is something that hurts a working company far more.

First, the Ministry issues a written warning (teguran tertulis). Then, if you still don't file, it blocks your company's access to SABH (pemblokiran akses SABH) — the single system through which every corporate legal change in Indonesia flows.

A BLOCKED COMPANY IS LEGALLY FROZEN

While blocked, you cannot appoint or replace directors or commissioners; transfer shares or change shareholders; amend your company deed (including any KBLI or activity changes); or update your registered address or capital. Because immigration and licensing systems read the same corporate data, the freeze spreads to work-permit and KITAS renewals for foreign staff and to OSS licensing updates. It usually reveals itself at the worst moment — mid-transaction, mid-hire, mid-renewal.

03

If your company did nothing in 2025, this still applies to you

This is the trap that catches the most owners, so read it twice. The obligation attaches to the legal entity, not to its turnover. A PT that traded nothing in 2025 — a holding company for a villa, a venture that never launched, a structure kept "just in case" — still owes its shareholders an annual report (even one showing zero revenue) and still owes the SABH filing.

Indonesia has thousands of quietly dormant PTs. Their owners will mostly discover the block only when they try to sell, restructure, or close — and find they cannot, because a report they never thought mattered was never filed.

06

The bottom line

This is not a difficult obligation. It is cheap to meet and expensive to miss. It needs a closed set of 2025 accounts, one shareholder decision, and a notary appointment — secured before everyone else in the country wants the same slot.

The pattern behind it is the story of Indonesian compliance in 2026: obligations that used to live on paper now live in connected systems, and the systems enforce automatically what officials used to overlook indefinitely.
