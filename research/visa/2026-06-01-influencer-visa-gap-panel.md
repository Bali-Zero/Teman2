## II. Deconstruction & Statutory Verification of the Thesis

### 1. The C5A "Visa Kunjungan Konten Kreator"
*   **Regulatory Basis:** Created under **Keputusan Menteri Imigrasi dan Pemasyarakatan Nomor M.IP-08.GR.01.01 Tahun 2025 tentang Klasifikasi Visa** (effective June 2, 2025). This decree reorganized Indonesian visas, reducing indices from 133 to 110.
*   **The "Data Belum Tersedia" Claim:** The thesis argues the visa is non-operational because the public-facing e-Visa portal (`evisa.imigrasi.go.id`) occasionally shows "Data Belum Tersedia" or "Data tidak ditemukan." 
    *   *Verification:* **Fragile.** In Indonesian administrative law, a ministerial decree (*Kepmen*) establishes the legal force. The website's display errors are typical of database synchronization lag, or related to the requirement of a local guarantor (*penjamin*) dashboard. Licensed visa agencies routinely process C5A applications onshore and offshore through their professional sponsor portals.
    *   *Legal Scope:* C5A is a **Visit Visa (Visa Kunjungan)**. Under general immigration rules, Visit Visas strictly prohibit the holder from performing work that results in local remuneration, wages, or commercial compensation. While C5A provides a legal shield for *filming/creating content* in public, it does not permit local monetization.

### 2. The E33G Remote Worker Visa
*   **Requirements:** Requires a contract (*perjanjian kerja*) with a company registered outside Indonesia (*perusahaan di luar Indonesia*), proof of an annual income of at least **USD 60,000**, and a minimum personal bank balance of **USD 2,000**.
*   **The "Forced Fit" Claim:** The thesis is **highly sound** here. A freelance influencer with a "loose bundle" of brand deals and fluctuating AdSense revenue cannot supply the single, continuous foreign employment contract required during the application process. Attempting to present fabricated or loosely structured contracts to bypass this violates visa application integrity under **Pasal 123 UU 6/2011** (providing false information to obtain a stay permit).
*   **The Local Restriction:** E33G strictly prohibits selling goods or services to Indonesian individuals or companies. Any local partnership violates E33G conditions.

### 3. UU No. 6 Tahun 2011 tentang Keimigrasian: Pasal 122 (Criminal Liability)
The thesis correctly identifies the dual-criminalization mechanism under Indonesian law:
*   **Pasal 122 Huruf a:** Targets the foreigner.
    > *"Dipidana dengan pidana penjara paling lama 5 (lima) tahun dan pidana denda paling banyak Rp500.000.000,00: setiap Orang Asing yang dengan sengaja menyalahgunakan atau melakukan kegiatan yang tidak sesuai dengan maksud dan tujuan pemberian Izin Tinggal yang diberikan kepadanya."*
*   **Pasal 122 Huruf b:** Targets the local enabler (hotels, villa owners, local brands, or visa sponsors).
    > *"Dipidana dengan pidana penjara paling lama 5 (lima) tahun dan pidana denda paling banyak Rp500.000.000,00: setiap orang yang menyuruh atau memberikan kesempatan kepada Orang Asing menyalahgunakan atau melakukan kegiatan yang tidak sesuai dengan maksud atau tujuan pemberian Izin Tinggal yang diberikan kepadanya."*
*   *Verification:* **Indisputably Sound.** This is the exact statutory weapon used by immigration officers to prosecute or deport influencers and fine/imprison local business owners who host them.

### 4. Barter as Work (Bekerja)
*   **Regulatory Basis:** Under **Undang-Undang Nomor 13 Tahun 2003 tentang Ketenagakerjaan** (Manpower Law), a labor/work relationship (*hubungan kerja*) exists if there are elements of job description, instruction, and compensation (*upah*). 
*   *Administrative Practice:* The Directorate General of Immigration interprets *upah* (wages/compensation) broadly. It includes **compensation-in-kind** (*imbalan dalam bentuk barang/jasa*), such as a free villa stay or complimentary meals in exchange for social media exposure (endorsements/collaborations). 
*   Because this exchange has commercial value for the local business, it is classified as commercial work in Indonesia. Thus, "barter" is legally treated as working without a work permit (*TA01 / RPTKA*) and stay permit for work.

### 5. Operasi & Patroli Dharma Dewata 2026
*   **Verification:** **Indisputably Sound.** The *Satgas Dharma Dewata* was officially inaugurated on **April 15, 2026**, by the Directorate General of Immigration specifically to address foreign immigration violations in Bali's creator hotspots (Canggu, Ubud, Uluwatu, etc.). Within the first month, they apprehended and processed **62 WNA** (Foreign Nationals) for deportation and entry bans (*penangkalan*). The enforcement mechanism is highly active, leveraging public reports (*PIMPASA* - Petugas Imigrasi Pembina Desa) and social media monitoring.

### 6. C7 and C7C Visas
*   **Verification:** **Sound.** The C7 index is a Single-Entry Visit Visa for Art and Culture. Under the 2025 decree, **C7C** is for "Practical Skill Demonstration" (e.g., chefs, photographers, yoga instructors participating in workshops or live events). It requires a specific local event sponsor and is non-extendable (valid for 30 days), making it completely unsuitable for ongoing lifestyle influencers.

## III. Fragile Points & Legal Counterarguments

An adversarial lawyer or the state could challenge the thesis on the following grounds:

### Counterargument 1: The Foreign Single-Member LLC Workaround (The E33G Loophole)
A pure influencer is technically a freelancer, but they can easily adapt to the E33G requirements by incorporating a **Single-Member LLC** in a jurisdiction like Delaware, Wyoming, or Singapore.
1. The influencer's LLC contracts with foreign brands and collects AdSense revenues.
2. The LLC issues a formal employment contract to the influencer (the individual) as an "Executive Content Director" or "Creative Consultant" with a salary exceeding USD 60,000/year.
3. The influencer submits this contract and the LLC's bank statements to the Indonesian immigration portal.
4. **Result:** The influencer is now legally a remote employee of a foreign corporation. They can live in Bali under the E33G visa, creating content for their LLC, which then distributes it globally. 
*   **Legal Fragility of Thesis:** This demonstrates that a "pure influencer" (monetized globally via AdSense and foreign brands) *does* have a clean, operational path under E33G, provided they adopt a basic corporate wrapper.

### Counterargument 2: The PT PMA (Foreign Direct Investment) Route
The thesis states there is "NO clean, purpose-built, OPERATIONAL visa category." This is legally untrue if we include the corporate route.
1. The influencer establishes an Indonesian company: **PT PMA (Penanaman Modal Asing)** under KBLI code **73101 (Periklanan / Advertising)** or **59111 (Film/Video Production)**.
2. The PT PMA sponsors the influencer for a **Working KITAS (ITAS Kerja / index C312 / E-series)**.
3. **Result:** Under this setup, the influencer is a legal director/employee of their own Indonesian company. The PT PMA can legally sign local contracts, accept local payments, execute barter agreements with local hotels, and pay the influencer a legal local salary while paying local corporate and income taxes.
*   **Legal Fragility of Thesis:** While the financial barrier is high (minimum investment plan of IDR 10 billion), it is a completely clean, purpose-built, and operational category for influencers wishing to conduct business locally in Indonesia.
