# Bali Tourist Visa Policy Updates — Q1-Q2 2026 Snapshot

> Window: 1 Jan 2026 — 15 May 2026 (with carry-over context from H2 2025 still in force).
> Audience: foreign tourist planning Bali trip 3-12 months ahead + already-on-island short-stay foreigner needing extension or switch + Bali-based travel/property professional.
> Hard rule: every number / regulation citation in body is verified by at least 2 independent web sources within this research turn. Unverified items live in section 9 "Open questions".

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The first half of 2026 is not a quiet stretch for Bali tourist-visa policy. Five concurrent forces are reshaping how short-stay foreigners experience the island:

1. **Enforcement first, not rules first.** The Dharma Dewata Immigration Task Force launched 15 April 2026 at Renon Field, Denpasar. Between 1 January and 12 April 2026 Bali Immigration recorded **165 deportations + 62 administrative detentions**. The pattern continued through May — DG Imigrasi Hendarsam Marantoko, at the Dharma Dewata launch event (cited in The Bali Times, April 2026): *"Foreign nationals must be aware that Immigration authorities are present, monitoring, and ready to take enforcement action."*
2. **Visa-free policy under review.** Following the 8 May 2026 Batam raid (210 foreigners arrested at an online scam compound — 125 Vietnamese, 84 Chinese, 1 Myanmar national), DG Imigrasi announced on 14-15 May 2026 a review of the ASEAN visa-free (BVK) scheme. Quote (Hendarsam Marantoko, Jakarta Post 15 May 2026): *"Cases of foreigners involved in illegal activities … bring about [the need] for us to evaluate [the scheme]."* No specific Permenkumham revocation announced yet.
3. **Visa code system fully matured.** Indonesia's transition from 133 legacy visa types to the streamlined 110-type system (started January 2024) is now operationally mature in 2026. The old **B211A tourist visa is officially retired**; tourists now enter on **C1 (Tourist)** or **B1 (Visa on Arrival)** indices. Several Bali-side actors still casually refer to "B211A" in marketing copy — this is no longer an Indonesian visa product.
4. **IKN Golden Visa threshold cut 80%** (per multiple secondary reports as of January 2026 — primary source DJI / Otorita IKN press release pending). The Government reportedly reduced the Nusantara Golden Visa thresholds from USD 25M → **USD 5M (5-year)** and USD 50M → **USD 10M (10-year)**. This is investor-track, not tourist — but it is the highest-profile new visa headline of Q1 2026 and consumers conflate it with general "tourist policy". *Confirm current thresholds at evisa.imigrasi.go.id before publication.*
5. **Influencer/creator economy in the crosshairs.** April-May 2026 enforcement made explicit that even unpaid sponsored content, brand collabs, yoga teaching, photography assignments, and volunteer arrangements on a tourist visa qualify as economic activity under Law 6/2011 — punishable by detention, deportation, and entry ban.

The carousel + article should frame Q1-Q2 2026 as **"the year enforcement caught up with the rulebook"**, not as a wave of new laws. The rules are essentially the same as 2024-2025; the difference is visibility and consequence.

## 3. Per-Visa-Type Detail (state as of 15 May 2026)

### 3.1 Visa on Arrival / e-VOA (Index B1)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Fee | **IDR 500,000** (≈ USD 32-35 at current FX) |
| Initial stay | 30 days |
| Extension | Once, +30 days (paid IDR 500,000 again at immigration office) |
| Max stay | 60 days |
| Eligible nationalities | 97 countries (expanded over the past 18 months) |
| Online platform | `evisa.imigrasi.go.id` (official) — VFS Global runs `indonesiavoa.vfsevisa.id` as authorised partner |
| Q1-Q2 2026 change | **Extensions now require in-person attendance at immigration office** with biometric capture (fingerprints + photo). Online-only extension is no longer available since mid-2025. |
| Permitted activities | Tourism, social visits, transit. Explicitly NOT: paid work, work for foreign employer while physically in Indonesia, sponsored content, brand collabs |

Note on old "molina" portal: the user-facing reference for many Bali Zero guides used to be `molina.imigrasi.go.id`. Current canonical immigration portal is `evisa.imigrasi.go.id`.

### 3.2 Tourist Visit Visa (Index C1 — replaces old B211A)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Single Entry, tourism purpose only |
| Sponsor | Indonesian sponsor required (agency or local citizen) |
| Initial stay | 60 days |
| Extensions | Up to 2× +60 days each → max 180 days total |
| Fee | Varies by agency; government component IDR 2,000,000 + 2× extensions IDR 2,000,000 each (approximate, agencies bundle differently) |
| Q1-Q2 2026 change | None directly on C1. But the **C1 (Tourist) cannot be used for business meetings** — for business activities you need C2 (Business Visit). This was implicit before but is now enforced cleanly under the 110-type system. |
| Permitted activities | Same restriction as VoA: no paid work, no remote work for foreign employer while physically in Indonesia |

### 3.3 Visa-Free (Bebas Visa Kunjungan / BVK)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Stay | 30 days, NOT extendable, NOT convertible to other visa types onshore |
| Eligible | Per Perpres 95/2024 baseline (13 countries): 9 ASEAN full members (Brunei, Cambodia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam) + Timor-Leste (ASEAN accession candidate, separate bilateral) + Suriname + Colombia + Hong Kong. Brazil + Turkey added via Permenkumham eff 3 July 2025 (number pending verification). Peru status: unconfirmed — appears in Jakarta Post 15 May 2026 cited list but no primary Permenkumham identified; investigate (§9 Open Question 5). |
| Legal basis | Perpres 95/2024 ttg Bebas Visa Kunjungan (effective 29 August 2024, replaces Perpres 21/2016) + Permenkumham eff 3 July 2025 (specific Brazil-Turkey addition; exact Permenkumham number pending primary-source verification) |
| Q1-Q2 2026 change | **Under formal government review since 14 May 2026** post-Batam raid. No revocation announced yet. Reciprocity remains the key criterion (Indonesia grants BVK only where the partner already grants BVK to Indonesian passports). |

### 3.4 Other indices relevant to tourist-to-resident pivot

These are out-of-scope for a "tourist visa" carousel but appear when tourists ask "what next?":

- **C2** — Business Visit Visa (meetings, conferences, not paid work)
- **E33G** — Digital Nomad / Remote Worker KITAS (launched 1 April 2024). Requirements: minimum income USD 60,000/year, 1-year validity, fee IDR 7,000,000, processing 7-14 working days. Cannot work for Indonesian employers or take payment from Indonesian clients.
- **E33B** — Golden Visa KITAS (5/10 year). Passive investment threshold: USD 350,000 (5-yr), USD 700,000 cash OR USD 1,000,000 property (10-yr). Plus minimum overseas income USD 5,000/month or equivalent savings.
- **E28C / Nusantara Golden Visa** — IKN-focused. From January 2026: USD 5M (5-yr) / USD 10M (10-yr).
- **B11 / Second Home Visa** — IDR 2 billion deposit (≈USD 130,000) held in state bank, 5 or 10 years. Deposit earns interest; refundable on visa expiry. Legal basis: Permenkumham 29/2021 + SE Ditjen Imigrasi IMI-0740.GR.01.01/2022 (effective 24 December 2022). PNBP fee per PMK 2/2022 (Rp 3 juta primary + Rp 2 juta per dependant).

## 4. Tourist Tax — Pungutan Wisatawan Asing

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Amount | **IDR 150,000** (≈USD 10) per foreign visitor, per entry to Bali |
| Legal basis | Perda Bali 6/2023 (as amended by Perda Bali 2/2025), effective 14 February 2024 |
| Payment portal | `lovebali.baliprov.go.id` (official) + Love Bali mobile app |
| Frequency | Once per visit (not per day, not per visa) |
| Applies to | **All foreign visitors regardless of visa type** — BVK, VoA, C1, KITAS holders, transit |
| Q1-Q2 2026 change | Rate unchanged. Enforcement is gradually tightening — some entry-point QR-code spot checks at Ngurah Rai. App errors and unofficial third-party portals charging inflated rates remain a hazard (multiple advisory sources flag this as the #1 tourist-tax problem). |
| Exemptions | Diplomatic passports, official-business permits, certain transit categories (verify via Love Bali portal — list maintained by Bali provincial government) |

## 5. Enforcement & Overstay (state as of 15 May 2026)

### 5.1 Overstay penalties

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Daily fine | **IDR 1,000,000 per day** (≈USD 65), no grace period |
| Legal basis | Government Regulation 28/2019 (sets daily rate) |
| Cap before deportation | 60 days overstay → daily fine applies; **beyond 60 days → automatic deportation + blacklisting** |
| Payment channels | Immigration offices, airports, IMPAS online portal |
| Q1-Q2 2026 change | UU 63/2024 (Immigration Law Amendment, in force during 2025) raises entry-ban ceiling to **10 years + extendable for another 10 years** (effective 20-year max ban). Biometric exit checks at Ngurah Rai detect overstay automatically — no avoidance via "didn't get scanned out". |

### 5.2 Activities reclassified as work (April-May 2026 enforcement clarifications)

Even on a valid B1/C1 tourist visa, the following can trigger violation findings:

- Posting sponsored social-media content (paid or in-kind compensation)
- Brand collaboration deliverables (photo/video shoots for a Bali brand)
- Yoga teaching (paid or volunteer with consideration)
- Photography assignments
- DJ sets at venues
- Influencer events (receiving lodging/meals/experiences as compensation)
- Volunteer work that produces economic value for an Indonesian entity

Authorities have stated explicitly (Travel And Tour World, Outlook Traveller, Curly Tales): "receiving benefits such as free lodging, meals or experiences can still count as economic activity."

### 5.3 Geographic enforcement footprint

Dharma Dewata Task Force operating zones (April-May 2026):
- Ubud
- Seminyak
- Canggu
- Kuta

These are exactly the high-density expat/nomad zones — the geography is not accidental.

### 5.4 Nationality breakdown — violation profile

Source: Bali Regional Immigration Office statements (multi-source via Travel And Tour World, Arab News). The three nationalities most cited in 2026 violation reports:
1. Russia
2. Australia
3. United States

(Note: this is *violation incidence*, not absolute arrival share. Australia leading arrival numbers does not equal Australia leading violation rate.)

## 9. Open Questions / Unverified Claims (for devils-advocate to investigate)

The following items appeared in 1 source only or in low-confidence aggregator-style sources and need second-source verification before going into a public-facing carousel:

1. **"97 countries eligible for VoA"** — sourced from Lets Move Indonesia 2026 update. The historical number was 92 (per Permenkumham 22/2023 baseline). The expansion to 97 is plausible but I have not independently verified the supplementary Permenkumham number that added the 5 extra countries.

2. **"Influencer/volunteer crackdown began April 2026"** — sourced from multiple secondary outlets (Outlook Traveller, Travel And Tour World, Curly Tales) but no Ditjen Imigrasi primary-source press release directly cited in research. This is a high-stakes editorial claim — needs primary-source verification before publication.

3. **"Top violators: Russia, Australia, United States"** — Bali Regional Immigration Office statement reported by secondary outlets. Not verified directly via DJI press release in this research turn.

4. **Investor KITAS shareholding threshold raised from IDR 1B → IDR 10B** — one aggregator source (balibusinessbuilders.com). This is out-of-scope for tourist visa but if Subhi wants a follow-up article it needs separate verification.

5. **"Suriname, Hong Kong, Colombia, Peru" listed as visa-free** — appeared in Jakarta Post 15 May 2026 listing of countries cited as under review. The historical reciprocal arrangements for these jurisdictions are plausible (HK and Colombia particularly) but Peru's status post-Permenkumham 9/2025 needs verification. SSEK Law Firm article suggested Peru was added together with Brazil/Turkey but the imigrasi.go.id primary press release (which I could access by search snippet) named only Brazil and Turkey explicitly. **Investigate**: was Peru added in a separate Permenkumham or did it grandfather from a pre-2025 bilateral?

6. **B211A vs C1 transition timing**: existing Bali Zero repo article `bali-immigration-law-what-expats-need-to-know-in-2026` (published 22 March 2026) still uses "B211A" terminology. This is internally inconsistent with `bali-2026-the-rules-every-visitor-must-know-before-landing` (8 April 2026) which states B211A is abolished. The 2024 transition is real; the March 2026 article appears to be out-of-date copy. Recommend internal correction post-carousel publication.

7. **e-VOA biometric requirement timing** — described as "mid-2025" by aggregator sources but no specific Permenkumham or DJI circular number identified in this research. For the carousel we can say "since mid-2025" but for a Mouth newsroom article a precise legal citation would be stronger.
