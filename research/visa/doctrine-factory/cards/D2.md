## 5. RESOLVED 2026-08-17 — CF-1: D2 duration citation disagreement

**Resolved by the E3a CF-1 resolution fast-follow** (`research/visa/doctrine-factory/claims/
e3a-cf1-resolution.md`, new claim `CL-D2-04`, live NB-2 query `E3A-CF1-Q1-EXT-COUNT`, citation-audit
`VERIFIED`). Original OPEN-CONFLICT text (as merged in PR #4250, carried here for the audit trail) is
below the resolution.

**Resolution**: both Reading A and Reading B were asking the wrong question. **Permenkumham 11/2024 Pasal
95 Ayat (3) sets NO numeric cap on the NUMBER of extensions** — the only constraint it states is
60-days-per-extension and a 180-day cumulative-per-stay ceiling. The query's own answer makes this
explicit: *"la legge non scrive letteralmente 'massimo 2 volte', ma tale limite è la conseguenza
matematica diretta del tetto insuperabile dei 180 giorni"* — i.e. "2 extensions" is an **arithmetic**
consequence of 60+60+60=180, never a figure the statute itself states. Corroborated by Pasal 95 **Ayat
(7)** (the VoA extension mechanism, same article): identical drafting pattern — day-per-extension +
cumulative ceiling, no extension-count language — evidence the whole article regulates extension limits
via duration math only, for every ITK sub-case it covers, D1/D2 included.

**Consequence for the two readings**: Reading A ("2×60-day extensions") is the correct **operative
arithmetic** to reach 180 days from a 60-day base, but is imprecise as stated — it is not itself a legal
figure, only the typical path. Reading B ("1×60-day extension only") is simply incorrect as a *cap*
statement, but is not "wrong" in the sense of citing a contradicting authority — it never had a citation to
begin with (§3.1.5 pointer-resolvability was checked: the dissenting `E2A-D12-VS-D2` cell IS bracket-cited
`[2, 29]` to internal guide `0c22e859-...`, so it is not simply discarded as unverified — it is marked
`SUPERSEDED` under `source-hierarchy-draft.md` §3.1.2, cross-level supersession: an L6 internal guide vs.
the still-valid L2 ministerial regulation Reading A itself rests on). **Neither reading is "the winner" —
the fact both were contesting does not exist in primary law.**

**Resolution mechanism**: `source-hierarchy-draft.md` §3.1.2 (cross-level supersession), not §3.1.3
(same-tier escalation) as this card originally framed it, and not §3.1.5 (pointer-non-resolvability
exclusion) as an internal working draft of the fast-follow briefly misapplied before self-correcting (see
`e3a-cf1-resolution.md`'s Disposition section for the full audit trail of that correction).

**Correction (kimi-k3 delta review, 2026-08-17)**: the dissenting cell's `[2, 29]` bracket-citation
resolves to `0c22e859-...` via the `citations` field of the `E2A-D12-VS-D2` record in
`response-log.jsonl` itself (structured, NB-2-provided mapping) — not via `e2a-citation-audit.json` as an
earlier version of this section stated: that audit's prose-pointer extractor only resolved the compound
citation's first number (`[2]`) and never lists `[29]` at all (neither resolved nor unresolved). The
resolution itself (`[29]→0c22e859`, L6) is independently correct; the artifact attribution was imprecise
and is corrected here. Also worth recording: the dissenting cell carried **two** wrong claims, not one —
"one 60-day extension only" AND "capped at 180 cumulative days per calendar year" (the category error,
separately resolved before this fast-follow) — §3.1.2's supersession disposes of both consistently, since
both rest on the same superseded L6 source.

**OWNER-ESCALATION marker: REMOVED.** The pinpoint above (Pasal 95(3) verbatim + explicit "no literal
cap" disclaimer + Pasal 95(7) structural corroboration) genuinely settles the extension-count question —
this is not a same-tier disagreement any escalation protocol still applies to.
