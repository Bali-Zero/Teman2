# C5A Local-Work Prohibition — Legal Sources (2026-06-01)

> **Scope.** This brief documents ONE narrow point for a Bali Zero editorial piece:
> under the **C5A "Visa Kunjungan Konten Kreator"**, a foreign creator may CREATE
> social-media content but is **prohibited from any value-exchange with an Indonesian
> beneficiary — including non-monetary compensation (barter: free villa/hotel stay,
> sponsored experience, services in kind)**. The legal hinge is the phrase **"imbalan,
> upah, atau sejenisnya"** ("reward, wage, **or the like**") in the visa-classification
> *Larangan*, reinforced by the Indonesian-immigration doctrine that what matters is
> **(a) the SOURCE of the benefit and (b) the BENEFICIARY of the activity — not whether
> money changed hands.**
>
> **Honesty note up front (read before citing).** The official Directorate General of
> Immigration page for C5A — `imigrasi.go.id/wna/daftar-visa-indonesia/C5A` — was
> empirically fetched on 2026-06-01 and shows the title **"C5A Visa Kunjungan Konten
> Kreator"** but a body that reads **"Data Belum Tersedia"** ("Data Not Yet Available")
> with no Hak/Larangan published. So the **C5A-specific** *Larangan* text **cannot be
> quoted from the live public portal.** It is therefore reconstructed below from (i) the
> **identical, published** *Larangan* clause of the sibling **C5** (media/pers) row, (ii)
> the published **E33G** row, both of which ARE live, and (iii) a verbatim extraction of
> the Kepmen 2025 classification table held in Bali Zero's NB-2 NotebookLM ground-truth
> (source_id `0c7e2212`). Every clause is tagged with its extractability status.

## Section 1 — C5A *Larangan* verbatim (ID + EN) + source + extractability note

### 1a. The operative prohibition clause (the hinge)

**C5A-specific (Kepmen M.IP-08.GR.01.01 Tahun 2025, visa-classification table)** —
`[UNVERIFIED on public portal — extracted from Bali Zero NB-2 ground-truth, source_id 0c7e2212]`:

> **ID (verbatim, NB-2 extraction):** "Larangan: 3) Menerima imbalan, upah, atau sejenisnya dari perorangan atau korporasi di Indonesia"
>
> **EN (translation):** "Prohibition: 3) Receiving **reward, wage, or the like** from an individual or corporation **in Indonesia**."

**C5 — "Visa Kunjungan Media dan Pers" (the published sibling row that proves the
pattern)** — `[VERIFIED — live, text-extractable from imigrasi.go.id, fetched 2026-06-01,
section "Ketentuan lain"]`:

> **ID (verbatim):** "Orang Asing dengan izin tinggal dari Visa ini dilarang menjual barang atau jasa atau menerima imbalan, upah, atau sejenisnya atas kerja/usahanya dari perorangan atau korporasi di Indonesia."
>
> **EN (translation):** "A Foreign National with a stay permit from this Visa is **prohibited from selling goods or services, or from receiving reward, wage, or the like for their work/business from an individual or corporation in Indonesia.**"

This single sentence is the doctrinal core. It contains TWO independent bans joined by
"atau" (or):
1. **"menjual barang atau jasa"** — selling goods or services (the local-commerce ban); and
2. **"menerima imbalan, upah, atau sejenisnya ... dari perorangan atau korporasi di Indonesia"** — receiving *reward, wage, or the like* from an Indonesian person or company (the local-benefit ban).

The phrase **"imbalan, upah, atau sejenisnya"** is broader than salary: **"imbalan"** =
reward/consideration (not necessarily money); **"upah"** = wage; **"atau sejenisnya"** =
"or the like / or anything of that kind". The residual catch-all "atau sejenisnya"
is what sweeps in **non-monetary** value (a comped villa night, a sponsored experience,
services in kind) — there is no "cash only" limitation in the text.

> **Extractability of the C5A row, stated plainly:** the C5 clause above is **published
> and live**; the **C5A** clause is **not yet published on the public C5A page** (still
> "Data Belum Tersedia" 11+ months after the index was created). For an airtight
> editorial citation, quote the **C5** clause verbatim (it is live and identical in
> structure) and state that the C5A row carries the same *Larangan* family per the
> Kepmen classification table. Do **not** present the C5A-specific wording as if pulled
> from the live portal — it is not there.

### 1b. The E33G contrast (why the wording differs, and what it teaches)

**E33G — "Visa Rumah Kedua Pekerja Jarak Jauh" (remote worker / "second home")** —
`[VERIFIED — live, text-extractable, fetched 2026-06-01, section "Ketentuan lain"]`:

> **ID (verbatim):** "Anda dilarang tinggal di wilayah Indonesia melebihi jangka waktu izin tinggal, melakukan pekerjaan yang tidak sesuai dengan izin tinggal, melakukan penjualan barang atau jasa, terkecuali diperlukan dalam pekerjaannya"
>
> **EN (translation):** "You are prohibited from staying in Indonesian territory beyond the stay-permit period, from **doing work that does not conform to the stay permit**, from **selling goods or services, except where required in [the performance of] their [remote] work**."

E33G's "Persyaratan khusus" (special requirement) — `[VERIFIED — live]`:

> **ID (verbatim):** "Perjanjian kerja dengan perusahaan yang didirikan **di luar Wilayah Indonesia**" — and proof of "penghasilan ... senilai paling sedikit US$60.000 per tahun".
>
> **EN:** "Employment agreement with a company **established outside Indonesian territory**" + income ≥ US$60,000/yr.

**Why this contrast matters for the piece:** E33G *permits* the underlying work because
its **source is foreign** (foreign employer, foreign-paid) and is explicitly *not* a
local sale — note the carve-out "terkecuali diperlukan dalam pekerjaannya". By contrast,
C5/C5A flatly ban receipt of any **imbalan/upah/sejenisnya from an Indonesian person or
company**. The two rows together demonstrate that the operative variable is **source +
beneficiary**, not the medium of payment.

## Section 2 — "Upah / imbalan includes in-kind" — UU 13/2003 verbatim + analysis

Source: **UU No. 13 Tahun 2003 tentang Ketenagakerjaan, Pasal 1**
`[VERIFIED — text-extractable from id.wikisource.org; cross-checked against the primary
UGM/Kemenperin PDF]`. The most precise read requires THREE definitions, not one:

**Pasal 1 angka 30 — Upah (verbatim ID):**
> "Upah adalah hak pekerja/buruh yang diterima dan **dinyatakan dalam bentuk uang** sebagai imbalan dari pengusaha atau pemberi kerja kepada pekerja/buruh yang ditetapkan dan dibayarkan menurut suatu perjanjian kerja, kesepakatan, atau peraturan perundang-undangan, termasuk tunjangan bagi pekerja/buruh dan keluarganya atas suatu pekerjaan dan/atau jasa yang telah atau akan dilakukan."
>
> **EN:** "Wage (Upah) is the right of a worker/labourer, received and **expressed in the form of money** as a reward from the entrepreneur or employer ... for work and/or services that have been or will be performed."

**Pasal 1 angka 3 — Pekerja/buruh (verbatim ID):**
> "Pekerja/buruh adalah setiap orang yang bekerja dengan menerima **upah atau imbalan dalam bentuk lain**."
>
> **EN:** "A worker/labourer is any person who works in return for a **wage or reward in another form** [i.e. in-kind / non-monetary]."

**Pasal 1 angka 4 — Pemberi kerja (verbatim ID):**
> "Pemberi kerja adalah orang perseorangan, pengusaha, badan hukum, atau badan-badan lainnya yang mempekerjakan tenaga kerja dengan membayar **upah atau imbalan dalam bentuk lain**."
>
> **EN:** "An employer is a natural person, entrepreneur, legal entity, or other body that employs labour by paying a **wage or reward in another form**."

**Analysis (this is the load-bearing nuance — handle precisely in the piece):**
- The *narrow* term **"upah"** (angka 30) is, on its face, **monetary** ("dinyatakan
  dalam bentuk uang"). If the editorial relied ONLY on the labour-law definition of
  *upah*, a literalist could argue a free villa is "not upah" because no money moves.
- **BUT** the legal concepts that actually define WHO is a *worker* and WHO is an
  *employer* (angka 3 and angka 4) both explicitly include **"imbalan dalam bentuk lain"**
  — *reward in another form*, i.e. **non-monetary / in-kind**. Indonesian labour law
  itself therefore treats an in-kind reward as enough to constitute an employment
  relationship.
- This dovetails exactly with the immigration *Larangan*'s **"imbalan, upah, atau
  sejenisnya"**: the immigration text deliberately lists **"imbalan"** FIRST (the broad,
  not-necessarily-monetary term) and adds **"atau sejenisnya"** (or the like). It is not
  limited to "upah"/cash. A comped villa night given by an Indonesian villa in exchange
  for a Reel is an **"imbalan ... dari ... korporasi di Indonesia"** — squarely inside
  the ban.

> **Citable formulation for the piece:** "Indonesian labour law defines a *worker* as
> anyone who works for *a wage or reward in another form* (UU 13/2003 Pasal 1 angka 3),
> and an *employer* as anyone who pays *a wage or reward in another form* (angka 4). The
> immigration prohibition mirrors this: it bans receiving *imbalan, upah, atau sejenisnya*
> — *reward, wage, or the like*. In-kind value (a free stay) is *imbalan*. No money needs
> to change hands."

## Section 3 — Source-vs-beneficiary doctrine — UU 6/2011 + PP 34/2021 verbatim

### 3a. UU No. 6 Tahun 2011 tentang Keimigrasian

`[VERIFIED — Pasal 1 from jdih.kemenkeu.go.id HTM; Pasal 122 from imigrasi.go.id]`

**Pasal 1 angka 21 — Izin Tinggal (verbatim ID):**
> "izin yang diberikan kepada Orang Asing oleh Pejabat Imigrasi atau pejabat dinas luar negeri untuk berada di Wilayah Indonesia."

**Pasal 1 angka 18 — Visa (verbatim ID):**
> "keterangan tertulis yang diberikan oleh pejabat yang berwenang ... yang memuat persetujuan bagi Orang Asing untuk melakukan perjalanan ke Wilayah Indonesia dan menjadi dasar untuk pemberian Izin Tinggal."

**Pasal 1 angka 26 — Penjamin (verbatim ID) — the "beneficiary/responsibility" anchor:**
> "orang atau Korporasi yang bertanggung jawab atas keberadaan dan **kegiatan** Orang Asing selama berada di Wilayah Indonesia."
>
> **EN:** "a person or Corporation responsible for the **presence and the activity** of a Foreign National while in Indonesian territory."

> The statute thus frames a foreigner's lawful stay around their **kegiatan** (activity),
> not their salary. The permit authorises specific *activity*; the *Penjamin* is
> responsible for that *activity*. This is why immigration polices the **substance of the
> activity and who benefits**, not the existence of a paycheck.

**Pasal 122 — the penalty for activity inconsistent with the permit (verbatim ID):**
> "Dipidana dengan pidana penjara paling lama 5 (lima) tahun dan pidana denda paling banyak Rp500.000.000,00 (lima ratus juta rupiah):
> a. setiap Orang Asing yang dengan sengaja menyalahgunakan atau **melakukan kegiatan yang tidak sesuai dengan maksud dan tujuan pemberian Izin Tinggal** yang diberikan kepadanya;
> b. setiap orang yang menyuruh atau memberikan kesempatan kepada Orang Asing menyalahgunakan atau melakukan kegiatan yang tidak sesuai dengan maksud atau tujuan pemberian Izin Tinggal yang diberikan kepadanya."
>
> **EN:** "Punished with imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to Rp500,000,000:
> (a) any Foreign National who intentionally misuses or **carries out activity not in
> accordance with the purpose of the Stay Permit** granted to them; (b) any person who
> orders or gives a Foreign National the opportunity to do so."

> Note (b): the Indonesian sponsor/villa/agency that *enables* the unauthorised activity
> is exposed to the **same** penalty as the foreigner. Relevant to any Bali Zero advisory
> that touches a local business offering a barter deal.

### 3b. PP No. 34 Tahun 2021 tentang Penggunaan Tenaga Kerja Asing

`[VERIFIED — text-extracted via pdftotext from the provincial primary copy
(llg-bwi.org); abstract cross-checked on peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/161898]`

**Pasal 1 angka 1 — TKA (verbatim ID) — "work" is defined by intent, not by payment form:**
> "Tenaga Kerja Asing yang selanjutnya disingkat TKA adalah warga negara asing pemegang visa **dengan maksud bekerja** di wilayah Indonesia."

**Pasal 1 angka 2 — Pemberi Kerja TKA (verbatim ID) — the in-kind hinge again:**
> "Pemberi Kerja TKA adalah badan hukum yang didirikan berdasarkan hukum Indonesia atau badan lainnya yang mempekerjakan TKA dengan membayar **upah atau imbalan dalam bentuk lain**."
>
> **EN:** "An Employer of a TKA is a legal entity established under Indonesian law, or other body, that employs a TKA by paying **a wage or a reward in another form** [in-kind]."

**Pasal 1 angka 4 — RPTKA (verbatim ID):**
> "Rencana Penggunaan Tenaga Kerja Asing yang selanjutnya disingkat RPTKA adalah rencana penggunaan TKA pada jabatan tertentu dan jangka waktu tertentu."

**Pasal 4 ayat (1) (verbatim ID) — work must be inside a formal employment relationship:**
> "TKA hanya dapat dipekerjakan oleh Pemberi Kerja TKA dalam **hubungan kerja** untuk jabatan tertentu dan waktu tertentu, serta memiliki kompetensi sesuai dengan jabatan yang akan diduduki."

> **Synthesis of 3a + 3b — the doctrine, stated for the piece:**
> 1. Immigration ties lawful stay to **kegiatan** (the permitted *activity*) and to a
>    responsible **Penjamin/beneficiary** (UU 6/2011 Pasal 1 angka 26), and criminalises
>    *activity that does not match the permit* (Pasal 122) — payment is never an element
>    of the offence.
> 2. The labour framework defines an "employer of a TKA" as anyone who provides a **wage
>    OR reward in another form** (PP 34/2021 Pasal 1 angka 2; UU 13/2003 Pasal 1 angka 4).
> 3. Therefore: a foreigner who performs promotional work *for the benefit of an
>    Indonesian business* and receives an in-kind reward *from that Indonesian business*
>    has (i) performed *kegiatan* inconsistent with a content-creator *visit* visa, and
>    (ii) entered a relationship that, in substance, is employment by an Indonesian
>    beneficiary requiring a work permit/RPTKA — **regardless of whether cash moved.**
>    What disqualifies it is the **Indonesian source/beneficiary**, not the form of pay.

### 3c. Official enforcement framing (the doctrine in the regulator's own words)

`[SECONDARY — Ditjen Imigrasi official Instagram, reported by BaliNews.id, 23 May 2026;
no individual official named — the source attributes it to "Instagram resmi Ditjen
Imigrasi"]`:

> **ID (verbatim as reported):** "Aktivitas yang menyerupai pekerjaan dan memberikan manfaat ekonomi, **meskipun tidak dibayar**" — prohibited on a tourist visa; and the assessment criterion: **"Yang dinilai bukan hanya apakah seseorang dibayar atau tidak, tetapi juga tujuan kedatangan, bentuk kegiatan, dan dampak ekonominya."**
>
> **EN:** "Activity that resembles work and gives economic benefit, **even if unpaid**" is prohibited; "what is assessed is not only whether a person is paid or not, but also the **purpose of arrival, the form of the activity, and its economic impact.**"

`[SECONDARY — WhatsNewIndonesia, 25 May 2026; TheAsianAffairs, 26 May 2026]`: both
report that **barter agreements** (complimentary villa/hotel/beach-club/gym/restaurant
stays exchanged for promotional content) are **illegal on tourist visas**, and that
"payment is no longer the defining factor — even if no money changes hands, authorities
may still consider the activity illegal if it generates promotional or economic value
for a business." The **Dharma Dewata Immigration Patrol Task Force** (launched April 2026,
monitoring Canggu/Ubud + social media) is the enforcement vehicle.

> **Important honesty caveat for the editorial.** The secondary press says a creator
> doing such collaborations "should hold a C5A." It does **NOT** establish that a
> barter deal *with an Indonesian beneficiary* becomes **permitted** once you hold C5A.
> The C5A *Larangan* (Section 1a) independently bans receiving *imbalan/upah/sejenisnya*
> **from an individual or corporation in Indonesia**. So the correct statement is: C5A
> legalises the *act of creating content*; it does **not** legalise *taking in-kind
> reward from an Indonesian business*. Do not let the piece imply C5A "unlocks" barter.

## Section 4 — PLAIN-LANGUAGE DEFINITION (5 bullets, citable)

What the C5A "Visa Kunjungan Konten Kreator" **BANS** and **PERMITS** — for an Indonesian
content-creator beneficiary:

1. **PERMITTED — creating content.** A C5A holder may shoot, film and produce
   social-media content in Indonesia. That creative act is the visa's purpose
   ("Visa Kunjungan Konten Kreator"). *(Basis: the visa category itself, Kepmen
   M.IP-08.GR.01.01/2025; sibling C5 "Dengan visa ini, anda dapat ... peliputan".)*

2. **PERMITTED — being paid by a FOREIGN source.** Income from outside Indonesia — a
   foreign brand, a foreign agency, Google AdSense (Google Ireland/US), a foreign
   employer — does not trigger the prohibition, because the ban is specifically on
   reward **"dari perorangan atau korporasi DI INDONESIA."** *(The E33G row confirms the
   same logic: it requires "perjanjian kerja dengan perusahaan yang didirikan di luar
   Wilayah Indonesia.")*

3. **BANNED — selling goods or services locally.** Selling any product or service while
   on this visa is prohibited: **"dilarang menjual barang atau jasa."** *(Basis: C5
   *Ketentuan lain*, live; same family for C5A.)*

4. **BANNED — any reward from an Indonesian person or company, INCLUDING in-kind/barter.**
   Receiving **"imbalan, upah, atau sejenisnya ... dari perorangan atau korporasi di
   Indonesia"** is prohibited. **"Imbalan ... atau sejenisnya"** (reward ... or the like)
   covers **non-monetary** compensation — a free villa/hotel stay, a sponsored experience,
   services in kind — because Indonesian labour law itself counts **"imbalan dalam
   bentuk lain"** (reward in another form) as compensation (UU 13/2003 Pasal 1 angka 3-4).
   **No money has to change hands for it to be banned.**

5. **WHY — it's the SOURCE and the BENEFICIARY, not the cash.** Indonesian immigration
   judges the **substance** of the activity: *purpose of arrival, form of activity, and
   economic impact* — and criminalises activity inconsistent with the permit
   (UU 6/2011 Pasal 122), with the enabling Indonesian party exposed to the same penalty
   (Pasal 122 letter b). Work that benefits an **Indonesian business** and is rewarded by
   that **Indonesian business** is, in substance, local employment requiring a work
   permit/RPTKA (PP 34/2021) — barter does not escape this.

### One-line takeaway for the dispatch

> *C5A lets you **make** the content. It does **not** let you **take** anything — cash
> OR a free villa — **from an Indonesian business** for it. The law looks at where the
> reward comes from and who benefits, not at whether money changed hands.*
