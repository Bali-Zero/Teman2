# Indonesia C5A — Visa Kunjungan Konten Kreator (Social Media Content Creator Visit Visa)

## Question

What does the Indonesian C5A "Content Creator" visa cover — legal basis, eligibility, documents, sponsor, fees, duration, allowed/forbidden activities, tax exposure, and how Bali Zero should advise clients (YouTubers, IG/TikTok creators, indie filmmakers, sponsored influencers, video journalists) choosing between C5A and adjacent indices (B211A/C1, C5, C7B, C14, E33G)?

## TL;DR (3 bullets)

- C5A is a single-entry **Visa Kunjungan** specifically for foreign social media content creators (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, podcasts, vlog/livestream) operating with foreign-source monetization; 60 days initial, two extensions of 60 days each, max 180 days; sponsor REQUIRED operationally (PT/PT PMA or eligible Indonesian individual registered on evisa portal); base fee Rp 2.000.000 per the PP 45/2024 tariff schedule for non-tourism visit visas (supersedes PMK 9/2022, effective 17 Dec 2024).
- Legal stack: introduced under Permenkumham No. 22 Tahun 2023 visa restructuring, modified by Permenkumham No. 11 Tahun 2024, then formally re-codified under **Kepmen M.IP-08.GR.01.01 Tahun 2025** (ditetapkan 1 May 2025, effective 2 June 2025, siaran pers 13 Jun 2025, PDF released 13 Aug 2025) which collapsed 133 indices into 110 and confirmed C5A as the dedicated index for "Visa Kunjungan Konten Kreator".
- C5A is NOT a work permit, NOT a remote-worker KITAS, NOT a film/production permit (C14), and NOT a journalism press visa (C5). Misuse on a B1/B211A/C1 tourist visa during Dharma Dewata Task Force operations 2025–2026 has triggered deportations and 10-year blacklists (Bonnie Blue case); 186 foreigners apprehended in Operation Wira Waspada Jan 2025 (Bali alone). Article 122 UU 6/2011: 5 years imprisonment + Rp 500.000.000 fine for visa-purpose misuse (verbatim verified).

## Findings

### 1. Identity of the visa

- **Official code**: **C5A**.
- **Bahasa Indonesia official name**: *Visa Kunjungan Konten Kreator*.
- **English official rendering** (per Ditjen Imigrasi siaran pers and SSEK/Lexology/ABNR cited above): "Social Media Content Creator Visit Visa" / "Content Creator Visit Visa".
- **Taxonomy position**: belongs to **Visa Kunjungan** (visit visa) family — single-entry (Bahasa: *kunjungan sekali perjalanan*). Distinct from **Visa Tinggal Terbatas (ITAS/KITAS)** which includes E33G remote worker; distinct from **Visa Tinggal Tetap (ITAP/KITAP)** permanent stay. Under MOIC Decree 2025 the index sits within the C-series visit visas alongside C1 (tourism), C2 (business meetings), C5 (journalism), C7-series (arts/performance), C11 (exhibitions), C14 (film/TV production).

### 3. Eligibility / Persyaratan

**Who can apply**: foreign nationals whose primary professional activity in Indonesia falls within "digital content creation for social media platforms" — practically:

- YouTubers (vlog, travel, lifestyle, tutorial)
- Instagram and TikTok creators (Reels, short-form video)
- Podcasters (audio-only or video podcast)
- Livestreamers (Twitch, YouTube Live, Kick)
- Indie social-first videographers/photographers monetizing personal channels
- Influencers executing brand collabs paid by **foreign** brands

**Who must NOT apply on C5A** (must use a different visa):

- **Accredited journalists** (newsroom-employed, press card holder) → C5 Journalist Visa, requires Temporary Press ID from Kementerian Luar Negeri.
- **Film/documentary production crews** (commercial shoots, music videos, reality TV, documentaries with industrial scale) → C14 Visa Kunjungan Pembuatan dan Produksi Film, which mandates a separate *Surat izin dari instansi pemerintah* (film permit).
- **Artists, musicians, performers** with paid live appearances → C7-series (C7A music, C7B arts demonstrations, C7C non-music skills incl. fan meetings, cooking demos per the 2025 Kepmen update).
- **Remote employees of foreign companies** with USD 60k+ annual income wanting 1+ year residence → **E33G Remote Worker KITAS** (1-year, renewable up to 5 times).
- **Content creators paid directly by Indonesian entities** (sponsored by an Indonesian brand depositing IDR in an Indonesian-registered bank account) → no C-series visit visa permits this; requires Work KITAS (E23-series) with IMTA work permit and PT sponsor.

### 4. Duration

- **Initial grant**: 60 days from arrival date.
- **Extensions**: 2 × 60-day extensions, processed online via the sponsor on evisa.imigrasi.go.id.
- **Maximum continuous stay**: 180 days per single C5A issuance.
- **Single-entry**: leaving Indonesia voids the visa; re-entry requires a fresh application (no multiple-entry equivalent in the C5-series; multi-entry tier sits under D-series visa kunjungan beberapa kali perjalanan, but no D-equivalent exists for content creators as of 2026-05-25).
- **Application activation window**: visa must be used (i.e. entry stamp obtained) within 90 days of issue, consistent with C-series practice.

**C5A-specific confirmation** of 60+60+60 extension structure: Visa-Indonesia.com verbatim: *"You get a single-entry, typically starting with 60 days. You can extend twice for 60 days each time, giving you potentially 180 days total."* This sibling-index pattern (C1, C2, C4, C14) is confirmed for C5A by every operating agency (LMI, Flado, PNB, Visa-Indonesia, BaliVisas) and is consistent with the 2025 Kepmen general visa kunjungan extension framework (60+60+60 = 180 days max).

### 5. Sponsor — Regulatory vs Operational

**Regulatory text (NB-2 verbatim, citing Permenkumham 22/2023 framework)**: *"Tidak disyaratkan adanya entitas sponsor perusahaan lokal; pemohon diwajibkan melampirkan bukti status sebagai kreator konten (portofolio atau profil media sosial) serta bukti kemampuan finansial/penghasilan minimal USD 2.000 per bulan."*

**Operational reality (evisa.imigrasi.go.id portal + multiple agency convergent reports)**: every operating immigration practitioner — PNB Immigration Law Firm, Visa-Indonesia, LetsMoveIndonesia, BaliVisas, Flado, Emerhub — confirms that the e-visa application FORM requires a Penjamin (guarantor/sponsor) field linked to an Indonesian NIK/NIB. The form cannot be submitted without selecting an Indonesian guarantor.

- **PNB Immigration Law Firm (verbatim)**: *"To apply for the C5A, you need to find an Indonesian sponsor — a company or individual eligible to sponsor this visa index. You can't apply without one. The sponsorship needs to come from legal entities based in Indonesia including companies, NGOs, and educational institutions, or Indonesian individuals for family visits."*
- **Visa-Indonesia.com (verbatim)**: *"You need sponsorship from an Indonesian company or person. This follows the visa index rules, and you apply online before you enter the country."*

**Reconciliation**: The regulatory text in Permenkumham 22/2023 may not explicitly mandate a *corporate* sponsor (allowing Penjamin Perorangan / individual sponsorship), but the e-visa application is technically impossible without a Penjamin of some kind. NB-2's framing "*tidak disyaratkan adanya entitas sponsor perusahaan lokal*" is best read as "no corporate-entity sponsor strictly required" (i.e. an individual Indonesian penjamin suffices), NOT "no sponsor at all required". This nuance was missed in the earlier draft.

**Bali Zero practical advice**: clients planning C5A must have either (a) a PT/PT PMA agency sponsor (Bali Zero corporate sponsorship), or (b) an Indonesian individual willing to be Penjamin Perorangan (rare, since this carries legal liability for compliance). The 2026 immigration system **digitally links the application to the sponsor's NIK/NIB**, making the sponsor **legally liable for the foreign national's compliance** (overstay, visa misuse). Operation Wira Waspada (Jan 2025) inspected 267 PMA companies with revoked NIB; 74 of them in Bali were still actively sponsoring; 126 foreigners under their sponsorship; 48 individuals from fictitious-company sponsorships were deported.

**Bali Zero implication**: when sponsoring C5A as PT PMA, verify NIB status is **active** before submission. Use of an "agency sponsor" who is also a problematic PMA is a Wira Waspada red flag.

### 6. Required documents

Practical checklist (synthesized from NB-2 + Visa-Indonesia + LetsMoveIndonesia + Flado + PNB Immigration; verbatim Imigrasi C5A page returned "Data Belum Tersedia" at last fetch — Bali Zero should re-verify against the live evisa portal at application time):

1. Passport valid ≥ 6 months beyond the planned stay (per Imigrasi general visa kunjungan rule)
2. Recent color photograph (passport standard, white background)
3. Return ticket OR onward ticket evidence
4. **Bank statement minimum USD 2,000 (or equivalent) for last 3 months** — *operational standard* per evisa.imigrasi.go.id FAQ for visa kunjungan family. **NB-2 text** says *"penghasilan minimal USD 2.000 per bulan"* (monthly income USD 2,000) — these are TWO DIFFERENT standards. See §Disagreements. Practical Bali Zero advice: provide BOTH (3-month bank statement showing ≥ USD 2,000 balance + recent income statement / tax return / contracts showing ≥ USD 2,000/month income). This conservative dual-proof approach satisfies both interpretations.
5. **Content creator portfolio** — links to active monetized profiles (YouTube channel ID, Instagram handle, TikTok handle, podcast feed) with audience metrics
6. **Surat Sponsor (sponsorship letter)** from PT/PT PMA or eligible individual containing: full name + company name of sponsor; sponsor's Indonesian address, phone, email; applicant's full name, nationality, passport number; relationship between sponsor and applicant; stated purpose ("creating social media content"); intended dates and locations
7. Hotel booking OR accommodation confirmation for first week
8. Travel/health insurance covering Indonesia (minimum USD 25,000 medical coverage typical practice)
9. Statement of activities (deklarasi kegiatan) — what the creator plans to film, post, monetize

### 7. Application process

**Step-by-step** (consolidated from PNB Immigration Law Firm + visa-indonesia + LetsMoveIndonesia):

1. **Pre-application**: Indonesian sponsor registers (or already registered) on https://evisa.imigrasi.go.id/.
2. **Sponsor initiates application** in e-visa portal on behalf of the foreign content creator, selecting index C5A.
3. **Document upload**: passport scan + photo + bank statement + portfolio links + sponsor letter + insurance + accommodation proof.
4. **Payment**: through designated bank using kode billing generated by the system (Rp 2.000.000 base fee per PP 45/2024 schedule, plus any e-visa convenience surcharge).
5. **Processing time**: official portal commits 5 working days after payment received; expedited service via agencies typically 3–4 business days; community reports suggest 5–10 working days end-to-end.
6. **Issuance**: e-visa PDF delivered to applicant email; printable QR code.
7. **Entry**: applicant arrives at designated Indonesian point of entry; immigration officer scans QR; entry stamp grants 60 days.
8. **Extensions** (60 days × 2): apply through sponsor on evisa portal at minimum 14 days before expiry; biometrics at local Kantor Imigrasi (Denpasar for Bali); cannot leave Indonesia while extension is pending.

### 9. What you can / cannot do on C5A

**Allowed**:

- Filming, photography, video editing on Indonesian soil for personal social media channels
- Publishing content monetized via **foreign platforms paying foreign-account creators** (YouTube AdSense paid to a non-Indonesian bank, Patreon, Twitch subs, Instagram Creator Fund where applicable)
- Brand collaborations with **foreign companies** paying outside Indonesia
- Destination vlogs, lifestyle content, travel content
- Personal podcasts and livestreams
- Standard tourism activities (sightseeing, meeting friends/family)

**Forbidden**:

- Receiving any salary, wage, or fee from an **Indonesian-registered entity** or Indonesian individual (per NB-2 and UU 6/2011 work-permit doctrine)
- Hosting paid events, fan meetings, workshops with paid attendance (those require C7C and an event permit)
- News reporting / accredited journalism (C5 territory)
- Industrial film/TV production using Indonesian locations (C14 territory + filming permit)
- Selling goods or services within Indonesia
- Bartering content for free meals, free hotel stays, free villa stays — Bali immigration now classifies these as "undocumented commercial transactions" per the 2026 Dharma Dewata Task Force enforcement framing (TheAsianAffairs, ivisa.com)

### 10. Tax implications

- **183-day rule (per PwC Tax Summaries Indonesia)**: a foreign national becomes Indonesian tax resident if they are physically present in Indonesia >183 days within any 12-month period OR demonstrate intent to reside.
- **Rolling-window mechanic (M1 — important nuance)**: the 183-day threshold is calculated on a **rolling 12-month window**, NOT a calendar year. Two back-to-back C5A stays of 180 days each, spaced 4 months apart, will cumulatively exceed 183 days within any 12-month rolling window — triggering tax residency retroactively. Bali Zero advisors must compute cumulative Indonesian presence on a rolling-window basis, not "this calendar year".
- A C5A holder using the **full 180-day maximum** is **just below** the residency threshold by 3 days — designed boundary. If the creator stacks a C5A + a back-to-back B211A or another visit visa pushing past 183 cumulative days, **they become tax resident** and theoretically subject to worldwide income tax (PPh).
- For non-resident foreigners (under 183 days), only **Indonesian-source income** is taxed at PPh 26 (20% withholding). Since C5A explicitly forbids Indonesian-source compensation, the practical PPh 26 exposure on the visa itself is zero — but accepting any sub-rosa Indonesian sponsorship payment in IDR creates simultaneous visa violation + undeclared tax liability.
- **Bali Zero red flag**: creators planning to stay >183 cumulative days/year should be advised to either (a) cap their stay or (b) shift to E33G remote worker KITAS with full tax planning (E33G holders are tax residents and must register NPWP; the 4-year worldwide income exemption for "specific expertise" generally does not extend to content creators per the MUC and ILA Global guidance unless they qualify under STEM expertise certificates).

### 12. Edge cases and gray zones

1. **Brand collaboration with an Indonesian brand paying offshore**: a Bali surf brand pays a US creator via a US bank — legal gray zone. The economic activity ("promoting an Indonesian brand on Indonesian soil") arguably constitutes Indonesian-source income regardless of routing. Conservative position: this is C5A misuse and should trigger Work KITAS planning. Aggressive position: foreign-source payment = compliant. Bali Zero recommendation: **avoid the gray zone**; require either offshore brand or proper Work KITAS.
2. **Livestream monetization** (Twitch subs, YouTube Live super chats from Indonesian viewers): platform pays the creator via foreign bank; the creator is filming inside Indonesia. Same gray zone as brand collaboration; the source of payment is the platform (foreign) not the viewer.
3. **Selling merchandise via creator e-commerce**: shipping from Indonesia = clear violation. Drop-shipping with no Indonesian touch + foreign fulfillment = arguably compliant but commercially risky to advertise publicly.
4. **Filming in protected or restricted areas** (Borobudur, Komodo National Park, Raja Ampat): C5A does NOT waive the requirement for site-specific *izin pengambilan gambar* from BKSDA, Ministry of Environment, or the relevant cultural agency. Creators filming temple/national park content must obtain those permits separately from the visa.
5. **Adult content / "edgy" content**: explicitly prohibited per UU Pornografi (UU 44/2008) and demonstrated by the **Bonnie Blue / Tia Billinger 10-year blacklist** case (2026). Even with a C5A, the **content nature** is subject to UU Pornografi and UU ITE; the visa does not pre-clear content that would violate Indonesian morality law.
6. **Family-bringing**: C5A is single-applicant; spouse/children on C-family-of-visit-visa would file separate visit visa applications. No automatic dependent stream like E31 attached to E33G.

### 15. Risk flags & compliance — verbatim Article 122 UU 6/2011

**Article 122 UU No. 6 Tahun 2011 tentang Keimigrasian** — verbatim Bahasa Indonesia (sourced from official imigrasi.go.id Bab XI Ketentuan Pidana page):

> *"Dipidana dengan pidana penjara paling lama 5 (lima) tahun dan pidana denda paling banyak Rp500.000.000,00 (lima ratus juta rupiah):*
>
> *a. setiap Orang Asing yang dengan sengaja menyalahgunakan atau melakukan kegiatan yang tidak sesuai dengan maksud dan tujuan pemberian Izin Tinggal yang diberikan kepadanya;*
>
> *b. setiap orang yang menyuruh atau memberikan kesempatan kepada Orang Asing menyalahgunakan atau melakukan kegiatan yang tidak sesuai dengan maksud atau tujuan pemberian Izin Tinggal yang diberikan kepadanya."*

**English translation (Bali Zero)**: "Punished with imprisonment of up to 5 (five) years and a maximum fine of Rp 500,000,000.00 (five hundred million rupiah): (a) every foreign national who intentionally misuses or conducts activities not in accordance with the purpose of the Residence Permit granted to them; (b) every person who instructs or provides opportunity to a foreign national to misuse or conduct activities not in accordance with the purpose of the Residence Permit granted to them."

**Citation**: Pasal 122 huruf (a) dan huruf (b), Undang-Undang Nomor 6 Tahun 2011 tentang Keimigrasian, Bab XI Ketentuan Pidana.

**Penalty number confirmation**: the 5-year imprisonment + Rp 500.000.000 fine figures cited in the earlier draft are CONFIRMED verbatim against the official Imigrasi page. The earlier draft's hedge "must be re-verified verbatim before client-facing legal opinion" is now satisfied.

**Sister articles for context** (verbatim verified via same Imigrasi page):

- **Pasal 119**: Orang Asing tanpa Dokumen Perjalanan dan Visa sah atau menggunakan Dokumen Perjalanan palsu — pidana penjara paling lama 5 tahun dan denda paling banyak Rp 500.000.000.
- **Pasal 123**: Orang yang memberikan surat/data palsu untuk memperoleh Visa/Izin Tinggal — pidana penjara paling lama 5 tahun dan denda Rp 500.000.000.
- **Pasal 124**: Menyembunyikan Orang Asing berada tidak sah — pidana 2 tahun penjara dan/atau Rp 200.000.000; Izin Tinggal habis berlaku — kurungan 3 bulan atau denda Rp 25.000.000.

**Other risk flags**:

- **Overstay penalty (UU 6/2011)**: Rp 1.000.000 per day overstay (administrative); >60 days overstay can trigger criminal prosecution and 10-year re-entry ban.
- **Digital patrols**: Dharma Dewata Task Force monitors trending hashtags + geolocations + sponsorship disclosures + "Paid Partnership" tags + brand mentions on Indonesian-located posts.
- **Blacklist tiers**: 6 months to lifetime depending on severity. Bonnie Blue precedent = 10-year blacklist for monetized content on tourist visa + content nature violation.
- **Sponsor liability**: per Pasal 122 huruf (b), the Indonesian sponsor (penjamin) faces the SAME 5-year imprisonment + Rp 500M penalty if found to have "given opportunity" to the foreign national to misuse the visa. PT PMA sponsoring C5A and the foreigner overstays = NIB scrutiny + possible revocation + criminal exposure for directors. Bali Zero must run NIB-active + Wira-Waspada-clean checks before accepting a C5A sponsorship instruction.
- **Social media own-goal**: a creator posting "I'm working in Bali on a tourist visa" or "free villa in exchange for content" provides Imigrasi the evidence directly. C5A clients must be briefed on **language hygiene** in their posts.

## Disagreements / open questions

1. **NB-2 says "tidak disyaratkan adanya entitas sponsor perusahaan lokal"**, while every operating immigration agency (LMI, Flado, PNB, Visa-Indonesia, BaliVisas) confirms a sponsor (Penjamin) IS required on the e-visa form. Resolution: the regulatory text likely means "no *corporate-entity* sponsor strictly required — individual Penjamin Perorangan suffices" rather than "no sponsor at all". The e-visa portal mandates a Penjamin field linked to NIK/NIB. Bali Zero working assumption: **sponsor IS operationally mandatory** (corporate PT/PT PMA preferred for liability and Wira-Waspada compliance; individual Penjamin theoretically allowed but rare and high-risk for the individual).

2. **Imigrasi.go.id C5A page returned "Data Belum Tersedia"** at fetch 2026-05-25. The official detail page is empty. The C7C and C14 pages have full detail. This is an outstanding documentation gap. Bali Zero should monitor for the page to be populated.

3. **C5A-specific PDF text not retrieved** in this session — the Kepmen 2025 PDF on kemenimipas.go.id requires direct download and was not parsed. The 110-index list verbatim Bahasa from the Kepmen would be the canonical source for any future Bali Zero legal opinion that requires citing the exact Bahasa text of "Visa Kunjungan Konten Kreator" line. Action item §Checklist.

4. **Official C5A-specific PNBP line item in IDR** not yet published — the Rp 2.000.000 figure derives from the umbrella non-tourism 60-day visit visa tariff under PP 45/2024. The Imigrasi C5A landing page (when populated) is the canonical source. Bali Zero should monitor for a dedicated C5A line.

5. **Income proof standard — USD 2,000/month income vs USD 2,000 3-month savings (H3 unresolved nuance)**: NB-2 verbatim says *"penghasilan minimal USD 2.000 per bulan"* (monthly income USD 2,000). Visa-Indonesia.com says *"showing you have sufficient funds (usually around 2,000 USD per month of stay)"*. evisa.imigrasi.go.id FAQ for general visa kunjungan family says "USD 2,000 bank statement for last 3 months". These are THREE different standards. Most conservative reading for Bali Zero clients: provide BOTH a 3-month bank statement showing ≥ USD 2,000 monthly balance AND recent income evidence (tax return, contracts, channel revenue statements) demonstrating ≥ USD 2,000/month income. This dual-proof approach satisfies the strictest interpretation and gives the immigration officer no ambiguity ground for rejection.

6. **60+60+60 = 180 days for C5A specifically (H4 partial)**: confirmed via Visa-Indonesia.com verbatim AND sibling-index pattern (C1/C2/C4/C14 all use this structure). NO C5A-explicit Kepmen text retrieved in this session naming the extension structure. Working assumption: 60+60+60=180 days applies to C5A. Risk: a future Kepmen amendment could carve a different rule. Bali Zero should re-verify at the official C5A landing page when it gets populated.

7. **Permenkumham 11/2024 role in C5A (H1 reduced claim)**: Public summaries of Permenkumham 11/2024 (Meridian Hukum, BPK official record) describe it as procedural amendments to 22/2023 — adjusting visa kunjungan activity list and various provisions. **No explicit content-creator clause was located in publicly accessible summaries**. Earlier draft's framing of 11/2024 as "Further refinement of visa procedures and golden-visa adjacencies" is consistent with the available evidence; its role in the C5A specific stack is procedural (not introducing or modifying the C5A index directly). Bali Zero should access the full Permenkumham 11/2024 PDF (via regulasip.id or peraturan.go.id) for any future legal opinion that requires citing specific pasal.

8. **133→110 index count — single primary source acknowledged (H2)**: The 133→110 figure is sourced from the Ditjen Imigrasi siaran pers 13 Jun 2025 (primary). SSEK, ABNR, Lexology, Safeguard Global, VoI all cite this same press release (not independent confirmations — secondary commentary all converges on the same primary). The Kepmen 2025 PDF itself is the canonical authority on the actual 110-index list, but the PDF was not parsed in this session. Bali Zero should treat "133→110" as a press-release-attributed figure pending verbatim Kepmen verification.

9. **Tax 4-year worldwide-income exemption** under PMK 18/2021 for "specific expertise" almost certainly excludes content creators (STEM-focused), but ILA Global and Emerhub differ on edge cases (e.g. creators with tech-channel content). For high-revenue creators considering E33G + tax structuring, separate research capture is warranted.
