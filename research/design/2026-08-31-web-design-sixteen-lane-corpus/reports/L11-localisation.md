## Executive summary

1. Indonesian commerce UI (Tokopedia/Shopee/BCA) is genuinely dense and saturated — but government sites (imigrasi.go.id, oss.go.id) that also serve a nervous, first-time user are *not*: they run navy/white, generous whitespace, and lead with a human-contact channel (WhatsApp, video call) before any form. Bali Zero should look like the government/bank register, never the marketplace register — density reads as "selling," restraint reads as "official," and the buyer needs to feel the second one.
2. Use **language names, never flags** ("EN · ID", not 🇬🇧🇮🇩) — this is now settled UX doctrine, not a style choice, and Indonesia is the textbook edge case that breaks flags (Bahasa Indonesia has no unambiguous single-country flag pairing the way, say, Japanese does).
3. There is no published English→Indonesian expansion percentage from any localization vendor I could verify — treat 20–35% as a working assumption from adjacent Malay-family and Slavic-family data, and design every button/label with min-width + wrap, never fixed width. The single most dangerous word in this product's vocabulary is "all-inclusive" — Indonesian has no one-word equivalent.
4. Formal written Bahasa Indonesia is not a register Bali Zero has to invent — BCA's own homepage literally reads "**Senantiasa di sisi Anda**" (always by your side) and OSS's reads "**Lancar dan aman kelola usaha dengan NIB**" (manage your business smoothly and securely with an NIB). That is the exact voice: *Anda*, short declarative sentences, the benefit named plainly, no exclamation marks.
5. Skip the batik-pattern-as-wallpaper move entirely. The one verified example of a serious Indonesian studio handling heritage identity without tourist cliché (Thinking*Room's Mangkunegaran royal-house identity work) treats the source material as a *typographic and structural* reference, not a decorative skin — that is the transferable lesson, not any specific motif.

## 1. The Indonesian web as it really is

### Finding 1 — Commerce is loud, banking/government is restrained, and the split is deliberate, not accidental

**The named example.** Traveloka (traveloka.com/en-id, VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31) runs a **white-dominant, photography-led** homepage with a horizontally-scrolling deal carousel, standardized cards (image + concise title + price), and trust numbers stated once ("50M+ Downloads, 1M+ Reviews," 4.6–4.7 star badges) rather than repeated everywhere. Contrast that with the academic comparison of Shopee/Tokopedia/Lazada (Usability *User Interface* study, ANDHARUPA Jurnal DKV, publikasi.dinus.ac.id, VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31; and the Medium case study "Shopee Redesign," VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31), where **29.2% of study participants named clutter — icons, banners, ads stacked without hierarchy — as their explicit usability complaint**, and multiple independent redesign case-studies exist specifically to fix it. Meanwhile Bank Central Asia's own site, bca.co.id (VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31), runs white background, dark text, a handful of stacked promotional banners, and OJK/Bank Indonesia/LPS regulatory-compliance statements stated in full sentences, not badges. imigrasi.go.id (VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31) and oss.go.id (VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31) go further: navy/white, card-based service categories, generous whitespace, and — this is the load-bearing detail — **both lead with named human-contact channels** (imigrasi lists phone/email/in-person; OSS literally lists "WhatsApp," "Email," "Tatap Muka" [face-to-face], "Panggilan Video" [video call] as first-class contact options, not a buried footer link).

**The measurable rule.** Density in Indonesian digital design is a *category signal*, not a cultural universal: marketplaces (Shopee/Tokopedia) use density to communicate "deals happening now," and it is explicitly a complaint, not a preference, when it exceeds what the task needs (29.2% clutter complaint rate cited above). Institutions that need to be trusted with money or legal status (BCA, imigrasi, OSS) use restraint plus an explicit, named human-contact path as the actual trust mechanism — which lines up exactly with Nielsen Norman Group's "Trustworthy Design" research (nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design, VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31): their four durable trust factors, stable "for 17+ years," are design quality, **upfront disclosure of price/fees before checkout**, comprehensive/correct content, and **connection to external, third-party-verifiable sources** (reviews, press) — not visual richness.

**What to steal for Bali Zero.** All three surfaces (home, GARUDA VOA, Visa Oracle verdict) should read like OSS/imigrasi/BCA, not Shopee: white/near-white ground, one accent colour (the red), cards used for *segmentation* (the four "Start where you are" doors) not for promotion, and — directly copying the OSS pattern — a **named human contact channel visible before the user has to commit to a form**, especially on the Visa Oracle verdict screen where the copy already promises "a named human who takes the case after payment." Put that name and a WhatsApp affordance where OSS puts its four contact icons, not buried in a footer.

**What to avoid.** The fad version is "let's make it feel local by adding a countdown timer / flash-sale badge / red urgency banner" — that is the Shopee vocabulary bleeding into a regulated-services site, and it directly undermines the "the price is the whole price, no guaranteed-approval hype" promise in the brief. Density and urgency markers are commerce-category signals in Indonesia; a licensed agency importing them reads as exactly the kind of visa-scam-adjacent operator the target user is afraid of.

## 2. Language switching

### Finding 2 — Flags are wrong for language switchers, and Indonesia is a textbook case of why

**The named example.** flagsarenotlanguages.com (VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31) collects real developer testimony on this exact failure mode; one contributor discusses Indonesia specifically, explaining he used flags *plus* language names for Bahasa/English because Indonesia was a low-risk two-language case, but the site's own conclusion, stated flatly, is "**unless the content is specific to that country — don't use flags**." SimpleLocalize's 2025 UX guide (VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31) converges on the same rule: use the language's **native name** ("Bahasa Indonesia," not "Indonesian," not a flag), because a flag encodes a *country* while a switcher needs to encode a *language*, and the mapping is not 1:1 (one country can have several languages; one language spans several countries/flags).

**The measurable rule.** Concretely: label the control with the language's own endonym or the ISO code pair, not iconography — "EN · ID" or "English · Bahasa Indonesia," never 🇬🇧/🇮🇩. Placement: header (desktop) or inside the primary nav/hamburger (mobile) — both sources agree this is where users expect to look, and it must never be auto-hidden behind a settings menu. Auto-detection: default to the browser/`Accept-Language` header on first visit, but always leave the switcher visibly reachable — Traveloka's own live pattern (VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31) shows this exactly: a persistent "IDR | EN" control in the top-right header, always visible, not a one-time interstitial.

**What to steal for Bali Zero.** A persistent "EN · ID" text toggle in the header, on all three surfaces, same position every time (top-right, next to or above the phone/WhatsApp CTA). Auto-detect on first load from browser locale, but never force a redirect the user can't undo in one tap — GARUDA VOA and Visa Oracle in particular must let a user mid-flow switch language without losing form state, since the audience is explicitly "often afraid of being scammed" and a broken language switch mid-payment-flow is exactly the kind of glitch that reads as untrustworthy.

**URL structure and the SEO consequence.** Google's own hreflang documentation (developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions, VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31) is explicit that **Google does not use hreflang or the `lang` attribute to detect a page's language** — it uses its own content-language algorithms — and that hreflang's only job is to tell Google *which URL to serve to which searcher*, requiring **bidirectional annotation** (if EN page X links to ID page Y via hreflang, Y must link back to X, or the pair "may be ignored or not interpreted correctly"). Google's guidance does not prescribe subdirectory vs subdomain vs ccTLD — that choice is operational, not an SEO requirement. For balizero.com specifically: use a subdirectory (`/id/`) over a subdomain or a cookie-only switch — a cookie-based switch is invisible to Googlebot and to any crawler-based hreflang discovery, which is precisely the failure mode Google's own docs warn about (a page that "exists" only behind client-side state has no stable, crawlable, hreflang-linkable URL). `/id/` also matches the existing `balizero.com` path family already documented for this project (kita/my/prime/knowledge subdomains) without adding a fourth DNS-level surface to maintain.

**What to avoid.** Auto-redirecting on geo-IP with no visible override (a Bali-based Australian tourist on a hotel Wi-Fi geo-located to Jakarta gets force-served Indonesian with no escape); and — the specific Indonesia trap — using the Indonesian flag to mean "this is the Indonesian-market site" when the actual switch is a *language* switch, conflating market and language the way flagsarenotlanguages.com's own case studies warn against.

## 3. Text expansion and layout stress

### Finding 3 — No vendor publishes an Indonesian-specific expansion number; the honest move is to design for the failure mode, not a percentage

**The named example.** I checked four dedicated localization-industry sources for an Indonesian-specific figure: LocaleProof's 2026 "Text Expansion by Language" cheat sheet (localeproof.com/blog/text-expansion-by-language, VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31), i18nAgent's UI-overflow guide (i18nagent.ai/en/blog/handling-app-translation-text-expansion, VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31), POEditor's UI localization guide (VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31), and PeMad's own Indonesian-localization-agency guide (pemad.or.id/ui-translation-for-translating-content-in-applications, VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31, itself an Indonesian company). **None of the four gives a Bahasa Indonesia-specific percentage.** The published numbers cluster around Romance/Germanic/Slavic languages: Spanish/French/Italian/Portuguese/Polish/Arabic/Hebrew at 15–30% (SimpleLocalize, VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31), German/Finnish at 30–40%, Russian 15–30%, and LocaleProof's general rule of thumb for **short UI strings specifically** ("reserve 35–50% extra width") — the highest-risk category, which is exactly buttons and nav labels.

**The measurable rule (honest version).** Treat 25–35% as a *working assumption* for Bahasa Indonesia short strings, reasoning from adjacency (Indonesian, like the Romance languages in the cited data, uses particles and auxiliary words English compresses into single morphemes) and from spot-checking the product's own vocabulary below — but flag this as an assumption to verify with a native copywriter doing an actual side-by-side count, not a cited study.

**The actual word list this product needs**, checked against ordinary Indonesian usage (FROM-MEMORY — general bilingual vocabulary, not a fetched source, and worth a native-speaker sanity check before shipping):

| English | Bahasa Indonesia | Expansion risk |
|---|---|---|
| Apply | Ajukan / Mengajukan permohonan | Low (imperative) → severe if the noun-phrase legal form is used |
| Submit | Kirim / Serahkan | Low |
| Upload | Unggah (often left as "Upload" in casual Indonesian tech UI — code-switching is normal here) | None if code-switched |
| Extension | Perpanjangan | Moderate (12 vs 9 characters, and it's the label on a small pill/badge in the VOA tracking flow) |
| All-inclusive | *No single word* — "Sudah termasuk semua" / "Harga sudah termasuk semuanya" | **Severe** — a 2-word compound adjective becomes a 3–4 word clause |
| Tracking | Pelacakan (formal) / Lacak (imperative, short — "Lacak pesanan") | Low if the imperative form is used |
| Verdict | Keputusan / Hasil (legal register may want "Putusan") | Moderate, and register-sensitive — see Section 4 |

The one genuinely dangerous term is **"all-inclusive"** on the GARUDA VOA price card: English fits a badge in one word-pair; Indonesian needs a clause. This is precisely the layout stress case Text Expansion in UI Localization (Translated.com / simplelocalize.io, VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31) describes: "teams should move away from fixed widths for text containers and rely on minimum and maximum constraints instead," with i18nAgent's concrete techniques (VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31) — intrinsic/content-based sizing over hardcoded width, default text wrapping (a button that fits one line in English "might need to gracefully wrap to two lines" in the target language), and icon/text pairs positioned with relative not static constraints so the icon moves with the expanding label.

**What to steal for Bali Zero.** Never hardcode a pixel/rem width on the GARUDA VOA price badge or the Visa Oracle verdict pill — size to content with a generous min-height that tolerates two lines. Pseudo-localize during design (append ~30% padding text to every EN string and look at it before Indonesian copy exists) rather than discovering the break only when real Indonesian copy lands. On the 360–390px Android viewport this brief specifies, a two-line wrap is *recoverable*; a truncated ellipsis on a price or a verdict word is not — never truncate the words "all-inclusive," "verdict," or a price figure.

**What to avoid.** The fad fix is a fixed-width button with `overflow: hidden; text-overflow: ellipsis` "because it works for English" — on this specific vocabulary that silently clips exactly the two words (the price-inclusivity claim, the verdict) that the entire trust proposition of the product depends on making visible in full.

## 4. Register and formality

### Finding 4 — Formal written Bahasa Indonesia already has a bank-and-government voice, and it is directly quotable

**The named example.** Two live, verified strings, fetched today: BCA's homepage headline copy (bca.co.id, VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31) — "**Senantiasa di sisi Anda**" (always by your side) and a service line, "**Kelola beragam transaksi finansial**" (manage a variety of financial transactions) — alongside its regulatory disclosure sentence stated in full: "Maksimum nilai simpanan yang dijamin LPS per Nasabah per Bank adalah Rp2 miliar." OSS's homepage hero (oss.go.id, VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31): "**Lancar dan aman kelola usaha dengan NIB**" (manage your business smoothly and securely with an NIB). On the pronoun question specifically: Bahasakita's reference grammar (VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31) and Talkpal's grammar note (VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31) both independently describe *Anda* as "a fairly recent [1950s] invention... slightly trendy, a bit formal... widely used in advertising and public announcements" and explicitly the register for "polite written scripts such as news articles, posters" and institutional communication — versus *kamu*, reserved for peers/friends/family.

**The measurable rule.** BCA's copy is the template: *Anda* used sparingly (not in every sentence — Indonesian formal register drops the pronoun where the verb/context already makes the subject clear, unlike English which needs "you" in almost every sentence), short declarative clauses, the concrete benefit named as a noun phrase up front ("Lancar dan aman..." — smooth-and-secure comes *before* the mechanism, "NIB"), and numbers/regulatory facts stated as complete sentences rather than compressed into badges. No exclamation marks in either example. No imperative-command tone ("Daftar sekarang!" marketing register) — both read as declarative fact.

**What to steal for Bali Zero.** *Anda* throughout, in the BCA/OSS register: benefit-first sentence structure, regulatory facts (the notary/tax-agent licensing, the "government fee included" claim) stated as full sentences the way BCA states its LPS guarantee, not as a small-print asterisk. This matches the brief's own English H1 voice ("Most people moving to Bali pick the wrong visa in the first month") — that same flat, factual, slightly blunt register translates naturally into *Anda*-register Indonesian; it does not translate into a friendly *kamu* register, which would undercut the "licensed agency, not a hospitality brand" positioning the brief explicitly asks for.

**What to avoid.** *Kamu* anywhere on a page discussing money, legal status, or government fees — it is not "friendlier," it reads as a downgrade in institutional seriousness to an Indonesian reader exactly the way an immigration lawyer texting "hey bestie" would read to an English one. Equally avoid the opposite fad — bureaucratic legalese copied verbatim from a government form (long nominalized noun-phrases, passive voice) just to "sound official"; BCA and OSS are proof that formal *and* plain-spoken are not in tension in Indonesian institutional writing.

## 5. Indonesian visual identity without cliché

### Finding 5 — The transferable move is treating heritage as structure, not skin, and one verified studio shows exactly that distinction

**The named example.** Thinking*Room Inc., a Jakarta/Bandung branding studio (thinkingroominc.com, VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31), lists a real client project titled **"Mangkunegaran — Redefining Today's Javanese Royals"** — identity work for one of Java's actual royal houses, alongside "Adidas Office Jakarta — Representing Indonesia to the Global Sports Brand." The site itself, notably, states no manifesto about "avoiding cliché" — it doesn't need to; the project *titles themselves* signal the posture (redefining a historical institution *for today*, not decorating a modern brand with historical motifs). Separately, Indonesia Design Week 2025 ran under the explicit theme **"Ideantity"** — described (indonesiaexpat.id, VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31) as encouraging "the design community to rediscover and amplify Indonesia's unique character through design" — a theme-title that is itself evidence the national conversation among Indonesian designers in 2025 was actively about identity-without-cliché as a live, named problem, not a solved one.

**The measurable rule.** The distinguishable pattern across both examples: Indonesian identity, done seriously, is used as a **structural or typographic reference** (proportion systems, historical letterforms, court hierarchy/protocol as an information-architecture metaphor) rather than as a **literal decorative motif** (a batik pattern as a background texture, a wayang silhouette as a hero illustration, the Garuda as a logo mascot). The tourist-marketing version — which this lane was specifically asked to distinguish — treats these elements as *surface pattern applied to Western layout*; the restrained version treats them as *source material for structure*, and the structure is what survives translation into a UI grid.

**What to steal for Bali Zero.** Red-and-white ("Merah Putih") as the identity is already the right instinct — it is the flag, not a folkloric motif, and it needs zero cultural explanation to either an Indonesian or an expat audience, unlike batik/wayang which carry meanings a foreign buyer won't read correctly anyway. Use it the way BCA and OSS use their institutional palettes: as a disciplined two-tone system (one saturated red as the single accent, white/near-white as the field), not as a pattern. If any traditional-motif reference is used at all (which is not required here), the Thinking*Room lesson says: extract a *structural* idea — e.g., the hierarchical, single-column "verdict" reveal on the Visa Oracle page could take its pacing cue from how formal Indonesian institutional documents sequence a decision (finding stated, then basis, then next step) rather than from any visual iconography at all.

**What to avoid.** Batik-pattern backgrounds, wayang-shadow-puppet silhouettes as hero art, and the Garuda used as a decorative logo mascot — this is precisely the "tourism marketing" register the brief warns Bali Zero is not ("never hospitality fluff"). It is also the fad most likely for an AI image generator to reach for by default (temple silhouettes, batik borders, a stylized Garuda) unless explicitly prohibited, given how heavily those motifs dominate stock-photo and tourism-brand training data — worth naming as an explicit exclusion in any image-generation brief for this project, the same way the CONTRACT document itself warns "whatever the brief supplies, the models return."

## 6. Practical formats

### Finding 6 — Each format has one correct convention per audience, verified against a live bank and cross-checked against general reference data

| Element | Indonesian convention | Notes / source |
|---|---|---|
| **Currency** | `Rp 790.000` — period as thousands separator, "Rp" prefix, space or no space (BCA/major banks use no space: "Rp2 miliar" in the LPS disclosure quoted above, VERIFIED-LIVE fetched) | The exact opposite of US convention (comma vs period) — this is not optional; a raw "790,000" reads as 790 to an Indonesian eye. Verified via BCA copy + a developer reference (dev.to formatRupiah example, VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31, "Rp1.500.000"). |
| **Time zones** | Bali = **WITA (UTC+8)**; Jakarta = WIB (UTC+7); Papua = WIT (UTC+9) | VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31. GARUDA VOA's "tracking until Immigration decides" timestamps must be labeled WITA explicitly for the Kerobokan office context, not left ambiguous — a tourist reading a bare timestamp will default-assume their home timezone. |
| **Phone numbers** | Country code **+62**, drop the domestic trunk "0" for international display: a domestic 08xx becomes +62 8xx | VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31. Any WhatsApp CTA number on the site should be pre-formatted as `+62 8xx-xxxx-xxxx` so it dials correctly for both an Indonesian using a local dialer and a foreign visitor's phone. |
| **Names** | Do not assume a Western given-name/family-name split — many Indonesians (notably Javanese and Balinese naming customs) use a single name or a non-Western structure | FROM-MEMORY (unverified against a specific 2025–2026 source this session, but well-established and already load-bearing for any form design) — relevant to the GARUDA VOA passport-upload form: a rigid "First name / Last name" pair will reject or mis-map a real, valid Indonesian passport name. Use a single "Full name (as in passport)" field matching the passport machine-readable-zone convention instead. |
| **Dates** | `31 Agustus 2026` (day, full month name, year) is the standard formal written form | FROM-MEMORY (unverified against a fetched source this session) — worth a quick spot-check against the live oss.go.id or imigrasi.go.id date stamps before shipping, since this is a low-risk but easily-verified fact. |

**What to steal for Bali Zero.** All three surfaces should hardcode `Rp` + period-separated thousands (never a raw number, never comma-separated) for the GARUDA VOA price and the Visa Oracle verdict price; timestamp every tracking/status update with an explicit "WITA" suffix; and build the passport-upload form's name field as a single free-text "Full name (as passport)" field, not split first/last — this is a real, common intake failure mode for foreign-visa products and a one-line fix.

**What to avoid.** Copying a Western checkout form's First-Name/Last-Name split onto the GARUDA VOA passport-upload step without checking — Indonesian government forms (as seen in the imigrasi/OSS UX) and international passport MRZ standards both already accommodate single-name holders; a form that doesn't will silently reject a valid Indonesian applicant.

## 7. The fad — what reads as tone-deaf to an actual Indonesian reader

**The named example / rule, combined (this section is a pattern-list, not one finding).** Drawing directly on everything verified above:

1. **Flag icons as the language switcher** — flagsarenotlanguages.com's own conclusion (VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31), and specifically wrong for a two-language EN/ID site because it signals "which country's site is this" when the actual choice is "which language do you read."
2. **Batik/wayang/Garuda as decoration** rather than structure — the tourism-marketing register the brief already flags as forbidden, and the opposite of what the one verified serious Indonesian identity project (Thinking*Room's Mangkunegaran work) actually does.
3. **Marketplace urgency mechanics (countdown timers, flash-sale red badges, "X people bought this")** on a page selling a regulated government service — this is the specific clutter pattern 29.2% of Shopee/Tokopedia study participants flagged as a complaint (VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31), and importing it into a licensed-agency context reads as exactly the manipulative-agent behavior the target audience already fears.
4. ***Kamu*-register copy** on anything involving money or legal status — reads as a register downgrade, not warmth (Bahasakita/Talkpal, VERIFIED-LIVE searched 2026-08-31).
5. **A hidden or buried human-contact channel.** Every trustworthy Indonesian institutional site checked here (BCA, OSS, imigrasi) puts a named, reachable human-contact path (phone, WhatsApp, video call) in the primary content, not the footer — omitting this, or replacing it with a chatbot-only funnel, is the single clearest tell of a low-trust site to this audience, per both the OSS pattern and NNGroup's "upfront disclosure" trust factor (VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31).
6. **Literal, word-for-word English→Indonesian translation of marketing idioms** ("all-inclusive," "your one-stop shop") — these have no natural Indonesian equivalent and, translated literally, read as awkward foreign copy rather than fluent Indonesian; better to write the Indonesian copy as an independent, benefit-led sentence (as BCA/OSS do) than to transliterate the English.

## What I could not verify

- **No Indonesian-specific text-expansion percentage exists in any of the four localization-industry sources checked.** The 25–35% figure in Section 3 is a reasoned estimate from adjacent-language data, not a cited measurement — this should be replaced with an actual side-by-side character count once real bilingual copy for the three surfaces exists.
- **The exact word-list translations in Section 3** (ajukan, unggah, perpanjangan, etc.) are general-vocabulary knowledge, not checked against Bali Zero's specific immigration/legal register — "verdict" in particular may need the more formal legal term "putusan" if Visa Oracle's copy is meant to echo actual Imigrasi document language, which I did not have a live source to confirm.
- **IKN Nusantara's logo/branding history** — I attempted to verify design-selection and public-reaction detail via Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusantara_(city), VERIFIED-LIVE fetched 2026-08-31) but the fetched excerpt confirmed only that a logo/emblem exists, with no design or reception detail — I chose not to cite any specific claim about it as a result, rather than fill the gap from unverified memory.
- **Indonesian date-format convention** ("31 Agustus 2026") and the **Indonesian name-order/single-name convention** are stated from general knowledge, not a source fetched this session — both are low-risk, well-established facts, but should get a quick spot-check against a live government form before being treated as settled.
- **A live screenshot/description of Tokopedia's and Shopee's own homepages** could not be obtained directly — both WebFetch attempts timed out or returned empty (JS-rendered SPA shells), so the density/clutter claims for those two specific sites rest on the cited academic comparison study and Medium case studies rather than a direct fetch of the live page — worth a manual screenshot pass by a human before treating the density description as current-state-exact.
- **DANA, BCA mobile app (native app, not the bca.co.id marketing site), QRIS visual conventions, and IKN/Garuda government rebrand specifics** could not be searched — the session's WebSearch budget was exhausted mid-research (shared across all parallel research lanes in this run), so these were dropped rather than filled from unverified memory. If a fuller pass is wanted, these four are the highest-value follow-ups.
