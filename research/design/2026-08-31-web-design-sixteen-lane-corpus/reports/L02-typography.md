## Executive summary

The industry converged on Inter for the same statistical reason your first three rounds converged on Cormorant Garamond: both are the "safe average" of every training set, which is exactly why a reader now decodes them as *nobody chose anything*. The fix is not a better pairing from a list — it's picking type for a stated reason (GDS Transport exists because road-sign legibility at speed; Public Sans exists because a government wanted zero licence friction) and then setting it with real numbers: a discrete step-table (not `clamp()`) for a page that must hold at exactly 360px and 1440px, `tabular-nums` plus an explicit `id-ID` formatter for every rupiah figure, and hard test data — Indonesian runs **+40% longer than English on a real button label**, verified against Canva's own SDK docs. Variable fonts are worth it only if you ship the fallback-metric-matched `@font-face` block that gets CLS to zero — skip that step and the "modern" choice becomes the thing that makes the price jump on screen. The 2026 fad most likely to embarrass this project in a year is scroll-triggered kinetic headline text: it is enabled by the same variable-font tooling you're about to adopt, it is already drawing its own backlash in trend coverage, and it is precisely wrong for an audience on slow Android connections who need to *trust* a price the instant it appears, not watch it assemble itself.

## 1. Pairings that are not Inter + a serif

**Finding A — one confident grotesque, no serif at all (Stripe's Söhne).**
- *Named example*: stripe.com uses **Söhne** (Klim Type Foundry, designed by Kris Sowersby), specifically the variable cut `söhne-var`, replacing the earlier Camphor in the 2020 redesign, with the `ss01` stylistic set enabled site-wide for a slightly more geometric letterform. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [typ.io/s/59wr](https://typ.io/s/59wr), [fontsinuse.com/typefaces/97994/soehne](https://fontsinuse.com/typefaces/97994/soehne).
- *Measurable rule*: no serif contrast at all — hierarchy comes entirely from weight (4 weights + italics available) and a type-scale jump, not a family change. This is the opposite move from "serif for gravitas, sans for UI."
- *What to steal*: for the **GARUDA VOA flow** — a transactional, 4-question-to-payment sequence — a single confident sans with real weight range removes the friction of a font-swap between "form" and "content" screens. Nothing in a payment flow should announce itself as "the editorial voice"; it should read as one continuous, calm system.
- *What to avoid*: the fad version is doing this with Inter itself, at default weight, because it's already installed. Söhne signals intent because it costs something and reads distinctly at 400/500/700; Inter at 400 everywhere reads as unstyled.

**Finding B — sans headline / serif body, chosen for a stated brand reason (Anthropic's Styrene + Tiempos).**
- *Named example*: anthropic.com sets **Styrene** (Berton Hasebe, Commercial Type) on headlines and subheads, **Tiempos** (Klim) on body copy. VERIFIED-LIVE (fetched 2026-08-31): [type.today/en/journal/anthropic](https://type.today/en/journal/anthropic).
- *Measurable rule*: the stated design rationale is to counter a "technological, intimidating" register with something that reads as considered and human — Styrene's unusually squared f/j/r/t give the sans headline character without going display-decorative; Tiempos is a text serif built for long reading, not a display face pulled in for "elegance."
- *What to steal*: the **Visa Oracle verdict** page is the one surface here that is genuinely long-form (a decision tree ending in an explanation of *why* this visa, editable answers, a named human) — it is the one place on the three surfaces where a text serif earns its keep, because there is real reading to do, not just a headline.
- *What to avoid*: Cormorant Garamond is a **display** serif (high-contrast, narrow, built for large sizes) pulled in to do **body-text** work — this is the same mismatch as pulling Playfair Display into a paragraph. If a serif is used, it should be a *text* serif (see Finding D) sized for reading, not a display serif sized down.

**Finding C — a commissioned variable family instead of a licence at all (Mercury's Arcadia).**
- *Named example*: Mercury (business banking) commissioned **Arcadia** / **ArcadiaDisplay**, a proprietary variable family, and ships it at a custom weight instance (480) calibrated to read "authoritative without being heavy." VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [blakecrosley.com/guides/design/mercury](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/mercury).
- *Measurable rule*: the variable weight axis is used for **precision**, not novelty — one exact custom instance, not "hover animates through the whole axis."
- *What to steal*: not the commission (out of scope for a service-agency budget) but the *principle* — pick a single named weight instance and standardise on it everywhere (e.g. `font-variation-settings: 'wght' 480`) rather than letting every component author reach for the nearest round number (400/500/600) inconsistently.
- *What to avoid*: variable-font demos that treat the weight axis as a toy — animating headline weight on scroll or hover has no reading benefit and is the mechanism behind Finding 6's fad.

**Finding D — the government-register option (Public Sans, or IBM Plex Sans + Serif).**
- *Named example*: **Public Sans**, the U.S. federal government's own typeface (adapted from Libre Franklin), free of licence friction — SIL OFL for the modifications, CC0 for the GSA's own work, on Google Fonts, adopted by roughly 200 U.S. government sites. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [github.com/uswds/public-sans](https://github.com/uswds/public-sans), [fonts.google.com/specimen/Public+Sans](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Public+Sans). Alternative in the same register: **IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Serif**, IBM's own corporate identity since 2017, both OFL, both on Google Fonts and Adobe Fonts. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Plex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Plex).
- *Measurable rule*: both exist because an institution wanted a typeface it could license to nobody's exception — no per-seat fee, no "which weights did we actually buy." For a licensed agency whose entire pitch is "the real thing, run by people who know the rules," a typeface with that same institutional, no-exceptions posture is not a coincidence of tone.
- *What to steal*: **home page and CRM-adjacent surfaces** — Public Sans (or Plex Sans) for UI/labels, paired with a genuine *text* serif reserved for long reading (Literata or Newsreader, see Finding under Q2) only where there is prose to read, not decoration to apply.
- *What to avoid*: reaching for Plex or Public Sans *because they are free*, then applying zero customisation (default tracking, default weight jumps) — this reproduces the exact "unmodified defaults" pattern flagged as an AI-slop tell (see §6). A licence-free typeface still needs a deliberate type scale and one authored move (tightened tracking on large sizes, a named weight instance) to read as chosen.

*Counter-example worth naming*: GOV.UK itself uses **no serif at all** — GDS Transport (the 1960s Kinneir/Calvert road-sign face, adapted 2012 for digital) exclusively, in two weights, and explicitly tells other government services *not* to use it off the `service.gov.uk` domain, recommending Arial/Helvetica instead. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/typeface](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/typeface). This contradicts the idea that "serious and document-like" requires a serif at all — GOV.UK's argument is that road-sign legibility at a glance beats literary gravitas for a service people are using under stress. Bali Zero's audience (scam-wary, on a slow connection, at night) has more in common with someone reading a GOV.UK benefits form than someone reading a magazine.

## 2. Variable fonts in production

- *Named example / measurable rule*: a documented before/after case reports a font payload dropping from **412KB to 108KB** and load time from **3.2s to 0.8s** by moving to a single variable file; separately, one **82KB** variable file is reported to replace four **53KB** static weights (212KB total) — a 62% size reduction and 75% fewer HTTP requests. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31, blog-level case study, not an audited benchmark): [robertcelt95.medium.com](https://robertcelt95.medium.com/variable-fonts-and-dynamic-typography-the-performance-win-nobodys-talking-about-320f4901d23f). The other side of that coin: a variable font's variation tables themselves add **50–150KB** of overhead versus a static cut, and stripping them back down to a single static instance (via `fonttools varLib.instancer`) can shrink a 200KB variable file to 60–80KB. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [inkbotdesign.com/variable-fonts](https://inkbotdesign.com/variable-fonts/).
- *font-variation-settings vs named instances*: a named instance is a designer-authored preset ("Extra Light") baked into the font's `fvar`/`STAT` tables, selectable even in apps that don't understand variable axes at all. Modern browsers now map the common axes to standard CSS (`font-weight`, `font-stretch`, `font-optical-sizing`) instead of requiring raw `font-variation-settings: 'wght' 480` — use the standard properties first, drop to `font-variation-settings` only for a non-standard axis (Fraunces' `SOFT`/`WONK`, see §1). VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [css-tricks.com/one-file-many-options-using-variable-fonts-web](https://css-tricks.com/one-file-many-options-using-variable-fonts-web/).
- *Android Chrome reality*: there is a live, unresolved report against Google's own font repo of visible variable-font rendering corruption specifically on Chrome for Android, and historically Chrome did not honour `format('woff2-variations')` inside `@font-face` or apply `font-weight`/`font-stretch` to a variable font reliably. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [github.com/google/fonts/issues/2815](https://github.com/google/fonts/issues/2815). Given the brief's stated audience — "mostly on 360–390px Android phones" — this is not a theoretical edge case; it is the primary device class.
- *Loading strategy that avoids both FOIT and CLS*: `font-display: swap` alone trades FOIT for CLS, because the fallback and web font have different metrics. The fix is a second `@font-face` block that redeclares the **system fallback** with `size-adjust`, `ascent-override`, `descent-override`, and `line-gap-override` tuned to match the real font's metrics. A documented worked example (Merriweather over a Noto Serif/Georgia fallback) used `size-adjust: 98.3%; ascent-override: 99%; descent-override: 27%` and measured **CLS at 0** a month after shipping. VERIFIED-LIVE (fetched 2026-08-31): [vincent.bernat.ch/en/blog/2024-cls-webfonts](https://vincent.bernat.ch/en/blog/2024-cls-webfonts). A separate production report on the same technique measured a **30% CLS improvement** from fallback-font metric matching alone. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [medium.com/duda/improving-cls-with-fallback-fonts](https://medium.com/duda/improving-cls-with-fallback-fonts-5ac1b0c81c29). web.dev's own guidance: `font-display: optional` delays text render by **no more than 100ms** and accepts the fallback if the web font is late — the safest choice where a layout must never shift, at the cost of sometimes not showing the custom font at all on a first, slow visit. VERIFIED-LIVE (fetched 2026-08-31): [web.dev/articles/font-best-practices](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices).
- *What to steal for Bali Zero*: for the **GARUDA VOA price screen** specifically — where a number must not visibly jump — ship `font-display: swap` + a metric-matched fallback `@font-face` (target CLS 0, per the Bernat case), and self-host a **single static instance** per weight actually used (not the full variable range) subset to Latin + the handful of Indonesian characters actually needed (Bahasa Indonesia uses base Latin, no extra diacritics beyond loanwords) — this sidesteps the Android Chrome variable-font rendering risk entirely on the one screen where a rendering glitch is a trust event.
- *What to avoid*: shipping the full variable file "for future flexibility" on a page that only ever uses two weights — that's the 150KB overhead bought for zero design benefit, on exactly the slow-connection device class this brief names.

## 3. The type scale

Every serious system I could verify live uses a **discrete step table**, not `clamp()`, for anything that is read rather than glanced at — fluid interpolation is reserved for hero/display sizes where an in-between value doesn't matter.

- **GOV.UK** — full numbers, verified live. Large screens (>640px): 19px/25px line-height is the *body* size; 24px/30px is `body-l`; 16px/20px is `body-s`. On small screens (<640px) the *same* named points shrink at discrete steps rather than interpolating — e.g. the 48-point heading drops from 48px/50px to 32px/35px, a hard jump, not a curve. VERIFIED-LIVE (fetched 2026-08-31): [design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/type-scale](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/type-scale/).
- **Material 3** — `display-large` is 57px font-size / 64px line-height; `body-medium`, the workhorse UI text size, is exactly `1rem`/`1.5rem` (16px/24px). VERIFIED-LIVE (fetched 2026-08-31 for body-medium): [material-web.dev/theming/typography](https://material-web.dev/theming/typography/); (searched 2026-08-31 for display-large): [medium.com/@vosarat1995/material-3-you-typography-cheatsheet](https://medium.com/@vosarat1995/material-3-you-typography-cheatsheet-ffc58c540181). The full 15-token scale (headline/title/label rows) is well-documented but I could not get the JS-rendered `m3.material.io` page to return its table to a fetch tool — **FROM-MEMORY (unverified)** for every row except the two above.
- **Shopify Polaris** — one scale for desktop and mobile, ratio **1.2** (the source calls this the "major third" scale, which is a naming inconsistency worth flagging: 1.2 is conventionally the *minor third* interval in musical-ratio type scales, 1.25 is major third — cited as-is, contradiction included per the sourcing rule), each step rounded to a multiple of 4px, tokens named by hundreds (`--p-font-size-100` as base, `-75` below, `-200` above). VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [polaris.shopify.com/design/typography/typography-tokens](https://polaris.shopify.com/design/typography/typography-tokens).
- **IBM Carbon** — fluid tokens exist, but are deliberately scoped to **large headline and display styles only** (`fluid-heading-03/05/06`, `fluid-display-*`); body text (`fluid-paragraph-01`) is the exception that IS fluid, but the base **Productive** type set used for product UI is fixed-step, with fluid reserved for the **Expressive** (marketing/editorial) type set. Typeface: IBM Plex. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [carbondesignsystem.com/elements/typography/code](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/typography/code/).
- **Gojek (Asphalt Aloha)** — deliberately raised its **base body size to 12pt**, explicitly overriding a legacy 9pt size "for legibility," using a 1.3× linear scale rounded to the nearest 4px, typeface Maison Neue. This is the one system in this set explicitly tuned for a Bahasa Indonesia / South-East Asian mobile audience. VERIFIED-LIVE (fetched 2026-08-31): [asphalt.gojek.io/pages/foundations_typography.html](https://asphalt.gojek.io/pages/foundations_typography.html).
- **Line-length numbers to design the scale against**: Baymard cites WCAG 1.4.8's 80-character ceiling for desktop, with 50–60 characters as the classically optimal range (Emil Ruder), and gives a behavioural number to back it: product copy set wider than 80 characters was **skipped 41% more often** than copy in the 60–70 character band. For mobile portrait, the practical range tightens to **30–50 characters**, minimum 14–15px, line-height ≥1.3. VERIFIED-LIVE (fetched 2026-08-31): [baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability).

*What to steal*: for a page that must hold at **exactly 360px and 1440px** — GARUDA VOA and Visa Oracle both fall in this category, since a misrendered price or verdict is a liability, not an aesthetic miss — copy the **GOV.UK/Gojek pattern**: a small explicit step table (3–4 named breakpoints, each with a fixed px value tested at that width), not `clamp()`. `clamp()` is right for hero/display type where the in-between values at, say, 768px or 1024px are never actually QA'd; it is the wrong tool for a price or a verdict that must be pixel-checked at the two widths the brief names.

*What to avoid*: a single global fluid scale applied to body/price/verdict text "for elegance" — this is the fad reading of `clamp()`, and it means nobody has ever actually looked at what "IDR 790.000" renders as at 1023px, because the formula, not a tested value, is doing the work.

## 4. Numerals and money

- *The property*: `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` forces every digit to the same advance width, so numbers in a column or a value that updates in place don't visually wobble. It has been broadly supported since 2020 with no fallback needed. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [developer.mozilla.org/.../font-variant-numeric](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/font-variant-numeric), [dev.to/alanwest/tabular-numbers-in-css](https://dev.to/alanwest/tabular-numbers-in-css-font-variant-numeric-vs-monospace-hacks-25cn).
- *What breaks with "IDR 790.000"*: Indonesian locale (`id-ID`) uses `.` as the thousands separator and `,` as the decimal separator — the reverse of `en-US`. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31, general locale references rather than an official Bank Indonesia formatting spec — flagged below): [vmancer.com/currency-regional-setting](https://vmancer.com/currency-regional-setting-open-office-microsoft-excel/). Two concrete failure modes follow from this: **(1)** any code that formats the number with a hardcoded or default `en-US` `Intl.NumberFormat` will render `790,000` or `790000`, not the brief's exact `790.000` — this is a locale bug, not a typography one, but it looks like one to a user; **(2)** any code that treats the price string as a number via naive parsing (`parseFloat("790.000")`) reads the period as a **decimal point** and silently returns `790` — a two-order-of-magnitude error that only a human proofreading the rendered page would catch, because nothing throws.
- *The correct setting*: `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining-nums` on any price or number sitting in a table/comparison context (the GARUDA VOA fee breakdown, a pricing comparison), combined with an explicit `new Intl.NumberFormat('id-ID')` (or the equivalent server-side formatter) — the CSS property fixes how digits *render*, it does nothing about how they're *grouped*; both are needed, and they are two different bugs with two different fixes.
- *What to steal*: on the **Visa Oracle** and **GARUDA VOA** price displays, set the currency code and figure with a non-breaking space (`Rp&nbsp;790.000` or `IDR&nbsp;790.000`) so a narrow 360px viewport can never wrap the currency marker onto its own line, separated from the number it prices — a small thing, but on a scam-wary audience an amount that visually splits reads as a UI bug at the exact moment trust matters most.
- *What to avoid*: the fad move here is an animated/incrementing counter for the price ("$0 → $790.000" counting up on load) — cute for a SaaS pricing page, actively wrong for a government-fee-inclusive all-in price, because it implies the number is being computed live rather than being a fixed, already-known fee. State the number once, correctly formatted, tabular, and static.

## 5. Bahasa Indonesia typography

- *The hard number*: Canva's own Apps SDK localization guidance gives a concrete, measured example directly on point — translating the button label **"Generate an image" into Indonesian increases the string's length by about 40%**, enough to overflow the container it was designed for. Canva's own fix recommendation is to design the **shorter** English label ("Generate") so the Indonesian expansion still fits, rather than assume English's length is the baseline. VERIFIED-LIVE (fetched 2026-08-31, quoted directly): [canva.dev/docs/apps/design-guidelines/localization](https://www.canva.dev/docs/apps/design-guidelines/localization/).
- *What this means for the brief's specific worry ("a button label designed in English")*: it overflows or wraps. A fixed-width button sized to the English string will either clip the Indonesian string or force an ugly two-line wrap with awkward padding. The industry-standard fix, sourced from two independent localization guides, is to **design containers for the longest expected language, not the shortest** — build the CTA in Indonesian first (the longer string), verify English sits comfortably inside it, not the reverse. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [simplelocalize.io/blog/posts/text-expansion-ui-localization](https://simplelocalize.io/blog/posts/text-expansion-ui-localization/), [translated.com/resources/text-expansion-ui-translation](https://translated.com/resources/text-expansion-ui-translation).
- *Line length*: apply Baymard's mobile figure from §3 (30–50 characters per line at 14–15px minimum) as the **English** ceiling, and expect Indonesian prose in the same container to run fewer *words* per line at the same character count, because Indonesian words themselves are frequently longer (agglutinative prefixes/suffixes: `di-`, `ke-`, `per-`, `-nya`, `-kan`) — I could not find a dedicated Indonesian-specific expansion-percentage study beyond the one Canva data point above; general localization industry guidance places most Latin-script languages at 20–35% expansion versus English, and Indonesian's grammar (particles and affixes rather than short function words) makes a figure in that range plausible but this specific claim is **FROM-MEMORY (unverified)** — treat the verified 40% button-label number as the one hard data point, not a general multiplier.
- *Hyphenation*: `hyphens: auto` has been supported across major browsers since September 2023, but it only fires if `lang` is set correctly **and** the browser ships a hyphenation dictionary for that language. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [developer.mozilla.org/.../hyphens](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/hyphens). No source I found confirms an Indonesian (`id`) hyphenation dictionary shipping in Chrome, Safari, or Firefox — Indonesian is a lower-resource language for this kind of tooling, and I could not verify one exists. **Recommendation, stated as a recommendation, not a verified fact**: do not rely on `hyphens: auto` for Indonesian body copy; instead give paragraphs room (the wider end of Baymard's range) and use `text-wrap: pretty` for orphan control.
- *`text-wrap: balance` / `pretty` support in 2026*: `balance` (best for short headings, ≤6 lines in Chromium) has full support in Chrome/Edge 130+, Firefox 121+, Safari 17.5+, at **91.63% global usage**. VERIFIED-LIVE (fetched 2026-08-31): [caniuse.com/css-text-wrap-balance](https://caniuse.com/css-text-wrap-balance). `pretty` (orphan control for paragraphs) has narrower support — Chrome/Edge 117+, Safari 26+, and Firefox does **not** support it as of early 2026. VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [savvy.co.il/en/blog/css/css-text-wrap-pretty-balance](https://savvy.co.il/en/blog/css/css-text-wrap-pretty-balance/). Both degrade harmlessly to normal wrapping where unsupported, so applying `balance` to every heading (both languages) and `pretty` to every paragraph is safe progressive enhancement today, with `balance` doing real work on ~92% of visits and `pretty` doing real work on a smaller but growing share.

*What to steal*: audit **every button, tab, and chip label** on all three surfaces against its Indonesian translation length *before* fixing pixel widths — the "4 segment doors" on the home page and the GARUDA VOA question flow are exactly the short-label, fixed-container UI pattern this breaks.

*What to avoid*: `text-overflow: ellipsis` as the silent fix for overflow — on a "4.9★ · 693 Google reviews" trust strip or a segment-door label, a truncated Indonesian string reads as broken, not tidy, to the exact audience the brief says is already primed to suspect a scam.

## 6. The fad

**Scroll-triggered / kinetic headline typography.** Variable-font tooling and better scroll-linked CSS/JS made animated, letter-by-letter, scroll-scrubbed headline text cheap to build in 2025–2026, and trend coverage documents it landing on hero sections broadly — and the same coverage is already pushing back, using language like "purposeful motion" and warning that "kinetic typography now competes directly with performance budgets and visual stability, especially on mobile," and that "a paragraph that moves while someone is trying to read it is the opposite of good UX." VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [digitalsilk.com/digital-trends/kinetic-typography](https://www.digitalsilk.com/digital-trends/kinetic-typography/), [raw.studio/blog/stop-scrolling-kinetic-typography-is-redefining-ux](https://raw.studio/blog/stop-scrolling-kinetic-typography-is-redefining-ux/).

*The measurable rule for why it fails here specifically*: the brief's own audience description — 360–390px Android, frequently slow connections, at night, already suspicious of being scammed — is the exact profile the trend pieces themselves cite as the risk case: motion that delays a headline's legibility on a low-power device, at the exact moment (the H1 "Most people moving to Bali pick the wrong visa in the first month," a verdict, a price) where the reader needs to *read the claim*, not watch it resolve.

*What to steal instead*: everything already verified above — a real weight/size jump from a step-table type scale (§3), one deliberate typeface choice with a stated reason (§1), tabular numerals that don't wobble (§4) — produces visual confidence through legibility and hierarchy, which is the same outcome kinetic type is chasing, without the failure mode. Motion, where used at all, belongs on **micro-feedback** (a checkmark confirming payment received, a progress step advancing) — never on the words that carry the claim, the price, or the verdict.

*What to avoid, and how to tell it apart from the legitimate move*: `text-wrap: balance` on a heading (§5) is *not* the same thing as kinetic type — it's a one-time layout computation that produces a static, better-balanced result; the fad is specifically **continuous, scroll-linked, or load-triggered animation of the words themselves**. If the text is still moving after the layout has settled, it's the fad.

**Named second candidate, tied directly to this project's own history**: the brief states the project already burned Cormorant Garamond + Inter because "everyone converged on it." Live-sourced industry critique in 2025–2026 names this exact pattern explicitly for Inter — "Inter set in Inter, at default weights, with no headline contrast, is now the single most recognisable signature" of AI-generated work, because an LLM "computes the weighted average of every interface it's seen," and Inter is that average. VERIFIED-LIVE (fetched 2026-08-31): [bruvora.com/blog/stop-ai-slop-typography](https://www.bruvora.com/blog/stop-ai-slop-typography/); VERIFIED-LIVE (searched 2026-08-31): [aiskill.market/blog/banning-inter-the-font-tell](https://aiskill.market/blog/banning-inter-the-font-tell/). I could not find a source naming Cormorant Garamond specifically as "the new default AI serif" the way Inter is named for sans — that specific extension is **FROM-MEMORY / my own inference**, reasoning from the same training-data-average mechanism the sources describe for Inter, applied to the fact (established directly by this project's own failure history in the contract) that multiple independent models converged on the same serif unprompted. Treat it as a plausible mechanism, not a cited fact. The antidote both sources give, independent of which specific fonts are involved, is the same one used throughout this report: **one deliberate, stated move** — a real type-scale jump, a chosen weight instance, a typeface picked for a traceable reason — rather than a name pulled off a "best pairings 2026" list.

## What I could not verify

- The full Material 3 type scale beyond `display-large` (57/64) and `body-medium` (16/24) — the headline/title/body-small/label rows are well-documented in general knowledge but the live `m3.material.io` page is JS-rendered and returned no static content to either WebFetch attempt. Worth a manual check against `m3.material.io/styles/typography/type-scale-tokens` or the Figma community file before implementation.
- Bank Indonesia's own formatting specification for Rupiah figures (period as thousands separator) — confirmed against general locale-formatting references, not against an official Bank Indonesia or QRIS technical document. The `id-ID` convention is extremely well-established practice, but the specific citation is weaker than the rest of this report.
