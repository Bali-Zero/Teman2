# Consent placement — say why, who sees it, and for how long, BEFORE the ask

**What this file is.** Second build-lane design deliverable of the R7 §4 backlog (item 2:
"the consent placement — A4 asked for 'why and who sees it' BEFORE the fields — has no R5
mockup; it rides the product lane with the M2b custody pattern as its template"). Executed
under the loop's doctrine: evidence classes, declarations through the round-grade process,
async veto open. Study only: no product code, `GARUDA_PUBLIC_ENABLED` untouched.

## §1 Ground — every consent and PII-ask surface, as it exists (class a; ground pass by a
dispatched reader, the load-bearing claims re-verified by the conductor against the tree)

**A4 (GARUDA dates + consent), `visa/voa/page.tsx`:** the wizard step renders entry date →
passport expiry → THEN a bare acknowledgement checkbox (`retention_notice_acknowledged`,
page.tsx:356-369): "I understand how my answers are stored and that I can delete this check
any time." It names no duration, no who-sees, links no policy — R1-A4's measured trigger
("storage consent after the fields", R1:105) confirmed byte-for-byte.

**A6 (passport upload), `upload/UploadFlow.tsx` + `messages.ts`:** ZERO custody language.
The four-bullet checklist is photo-quality only; grep for consent/privacy/retention across
the upload dir returns nothing. The funnel's most sensitive ask carries the least copy —
R1-A6's finding, still true. M2b (R5) designed the cure but its custody module is
placeholder tokens (`{CUSTODY_WHO_SEES}` · `{CUSTODY_RETENTION}`), never filled.

**Magic-link email, `[hash]/page.tsx:343-345`:** mechanism explainer only ("we'll send a
one-time link, no password") — no data-handling line.

**Checkout email+phone, `CheckoutFlow.tsx:105-135`:** bare labels, no consent surface; the
why lives in a code comment the user never sees. (The checkout LANE's design already cures
this surface — §3.1 of that report; this lane inherits it.)

**Visa Oracle:** the one REAL consent block in the ecosystem is `ConsentHandoff.tsx:39-58`
(scope, 2h TTL, no-CRM-record, guardian gate, schema-versioned expiring receipt) — but it
gates the WhatsApp handoff at the END; the 15-question interview, overstay and minors
included, has no upfront data-collection statement. `WhyWeAsk.tsx` explains PROVENANCE per
question (the closest live analogue to say-why-before-the-field), not custody. `visa/match`
has zero consent language.

**Backend reality (migrations 281/284/285/286):** the machinery that EXISTS is real —
`visa_decision_retention_policies` is a Zero-signed retention authority
(scopes VISA_DECISION / GARUDA_CHECK / GARUDA_ORDER / GARUDA_MAGIC_LINK), and
`garuda_voa_checks`/`_check_results`/`_magic_link_tokens` fail-closed at INSERT on both the
acknowledgement timestamp and an active policy. What does NOT exist: any consent RECORD
(no policy-text version, no per-client proof, no revocation event — grep `consent` across
migrations_v2 DDL: zero columns); and `garuda_orders` — the table holding
`applicant_full_name/email/phone/passport_number` (284:52-55) — has NO consent column and
NO retention-binding trigger (verified: zero `consent` occurrences in 284). The
PII-heaviest table has the least governance.

**The privacy-policy contradiction (verified verbatim by the conductor):**
`visa/privacy/page.tsx:60-71` states "We do not collect, and have no interest in
collecting:" — name, email, passport number, phone number, photographs. GARUDA VOA collects
ALL FIVE. Today GARUDA is dark, so the page misleads no live VOA customer — but it becomes
false THE MOMENT the flag arms. And neither GARUDA VOA nor Visa Match links ANY privacy
policy from any screen (grep zero matches); four separate privacy pages exist
(root, /v2, /visa, /visa-oracle), only the Oracle shell links its own.

## §2 The two tensions this design resolves

**T1 — the explanation comes after the ask, everywhere except the Oracle handoff.** A4 puts
the storage notice after the fields; A6 says nothing at all; magic-link and checkout say
nothing; the interview front-loads no custody. R1's fix hypothesis ("say why and who sees
it, before the fields", A4/A6 rows) + R4's question protocol ("Why we ask" on every
question) + M2b's custody template all point one way: the custody statement is a COMPONENT
that precedes or accompanies every PII ask — never trails it.

**T2 — the live copy promises what no machinery keeps, while real machinery goes unnamed.**
A4's checkbox promises "delete any time" with no user-facing delete path audited; the
2026-08-09 consent ruling itself warns "deciding to collect consent is not having it"
(SYMBIOSIS Law 2: no clause, no per-client proof, no revocation mechanism yet). Meanwhile
the ONE thing the backend actually guarantees — a Zero-approved retention interval, policy
row per scope — is exactly what the copy never states. The design inverts both: state the
REAL duration (the value exists in `visa_decision_retention_policies`), and stop promising
the delete path until its mechanism is audited (placeholder-constrained, R4 §5 "never
invented reassurance" — the upload custody line's own law, R4:118).

## §3 Design decisions (mockup: `mockups/consent-placement/a4-dates-consent.html` — the
A4 screen redesigned as the pattern's reference implementation)

1. **The custody module is ONE reusable component** — three lines + one link, rendered
   BEFORE the fields it governs: "Why we ask" (per R4 question protocol) · "Who sees it:
   {CUSTODY_WHO_SEES}" · "Kept for {RETENTION_WINDOW} — the retention interval is the
   POLICY TABLE's value, never authored in copy" · "How we handle your data →
   {PRIVACY_LINK}". Placeholder-constrained where no audited fact exists (who-sees needs
   the same audit M2b's law already demands, R4:118); real where the backend already
   guarantees it (the duration).
2. **A4 reference implementation**: identity header (R4:108) → counter ("Question {X} of
   {Y}") → custody module → date fields (unambiguous form, "27 Aug 2026") → the
   acknowledgement checkbox LAST but now pointing at copy the user has already read, with
   the real duration in its label: "I've read how my answers are stored (kept
   {RETENTION_WINDOW})." The delete-any-time promise is REMOVED from the label until the
   erasure path is a user-facing, audited mechanism (§5.3) — migration 286 designed
   erasure capability, no screen exposes it.
3. **A6 upload inherits M2b with values**: the module's `{CUSTODY_RETENTION}` is fillable
   TODAY from the policy table — the data exists; `{CUSTODY_WHO_SEES}` stays a placeholder
   until the who-sees audit lands (R4:118's own constraint). No new mockup — M2b is
   amended by reference, not redrawn (§6.2).
4. **Magic-link email gains one line** under the field: "Used once, to send this link.
   {PRIVACY_LINK}." — mechanism + custody in nine words.
5. **Oracle interview start gains the custody module** (before question 1, same component);
   `ConsentHandoff` is NOT touched — it is the ecosystem's best consent surface and stays
   the end-gate for the WhatsApp handoff. `WhyWeAsk` stays per-question provenance.
6. **Every funnel screen links the scoped privacy policy** in the trust strip — GATED
   (§6.3) on the policy itself being corrected first: linking today's `/visa/privacy` from
   GARUDA would wire a page that denies collecting what the funnel collects (§1). The
   arm-order is: fix policy → link policy → arm flag. Never the reverse.
7. **Checkout**: no changes — the checkout lane's §3.1 (why-we-ask before email/phone) and
   consent line ARE this pattern's instances; this lane only names them as such.

## §4 Behavioral acceptance (what a build must prove)

- On every screen that asks for PII (A4 dates, A6 upload, magic-link email, checkout,
  Oracle interview start), a custody module renders BEFORE or WITH the ask — a PII input
  whose screen lacks the module is a failed build.
- `{RETENTION_WINDOW}` renders the ACTIVE policy row's interval for the surface's scope;
  no active policy, no rendered duration (and the INSERT gate would refuse the write
  anyway — the copy and the gate read the same row).
- No rendered copy promises deletion/revocation unless a user-facing erasure path exists
  and is audited (would_fail_if: "delete" appears in consent copy while no route serves
  it).
- The acknowledgement checkbox cannot be pre-checked, and the step cannot advance without
  it (the DB gate at 281:275-277 stays the backstop).
- The privacy link renders on every funnel screen ONLY when it targets the corrected,
  surface-scoped policy (§6.3's gate).
- Text-only zoom, 44px targets, rem type scale — inherited from the checkout lane's
  acceptance, same instruments.

## §5 Findings handed to product lanes (not design's to fix)

1. **The `/visa/privacy` policy is an arm-time trap.** It affirmatively denies collecting
   the five things GARUDA VOA collects (verified verbatim, page.tsx:60-71). GARUDA's
   arm-order MUST sequence: correct/scope the policy → link it from the funnel → arm the
   flag. This belongs in the GARUDA mandate's pre-5% checklist as a BLOCKER. (class a)
2. **`garuda_orders` has no retention binding and no consent column** while holding the
   funnel's heaviest PII — contrast the three sibling tables that fail closed at INSERT.
   Bind it to the GARUDA_ORDER policy scope like its siblings. (class a)
3. **No user-facing erasure path exists** while A4's live label promises "delete any
   time". Either build+audit the path (286 designed the capability) or the copy overclaims
   today. (class a)
4. **No consent LEDGER exists** — `retention_notice_acknowledged_at` is a timestamp gate,
   not a record of what was shown (policy text version, per-client proof, revocation
   event). SYMBIOSIS Law 2's own 2026-08-09 ruling names this gap unarmed; a consent
   ledger is its cure and predates any "we recorded your consent" copy. (class a)
5. **Four privacy pages, no owner**: root, /v2, /visa, /visa-oracle — which is canonical
   per surface, who maintains them, and the /visa one is wrong (finding 1). Consolidation
   is a product/legal decision. (business decision)
6. **The who-sees audit** (R4:118's prerequisite for M2b's `{CUSTODY_WHO_SEES}`) still
   does not exist — until it lands, every custody module ships that field as a declared
   placeholder. (ops prerequisite)

## §6 Declarations (adopted under the standing pre-confirmation, async veto open)

1. **Custody-before-ask is funnel law for every PII ask**: a screen that asks for personal
   data renders the custody module (why · who sees · kept-for · policy link) before or
   with the ask — A4's after-the-fields checkbox pattern is superseded. Alternative: keep
   per-screen ad-hoc copy (rejected: five surfaces, four different behaviors today, three
   with nothing at all — the component is the cure for the inconsistency itself).
2. **M2b's custody module is amended by reference to carry the REAL retention value** from
   `visa_decision_retention_policies` ({CUSTODY_RETENTION} → {RETENTION_WINDOW}, same
   semantics), keeping {CUSTODY_WHO_SEES} placeholder-constrained until the audit (R4:118
   unchanged). Alternative: redraw M2b (rejected: the mockup is right; only its token
   needed grounding).
3. **Funnel screens link the scoped privacy policy — gated on the policy correction**
   (§5.1): the link ships in the design now, renders only when its target stops
   contradicting the funnel. Alternative: link immediately (rejected: wiring a page that
   denies the collection it describes would be the exact anti-pattern this lane exists to
   kill); no link (rejected: two funnels currently orphan every policy — that is the
   status quo being cured).
4. **Consent copy never outruns machinery**: the design states the duration the backend
   enforces, and strips the delete-any-time promise until an audited erasure path exists
   (§5.3). This is R4 §5's "never invented reassurance" applied to legal copy.
   Alternative: keep the friendlier promise (rejected: the doctrine's own words — deciding
   to collect consent is not having it; promising deletion is not serving it).

## Adversarial review (§7)

PENDING-PANEL — risk-tier: four §6 declarations spanning five funnel surfaces and legal
copy → round-grade panel (four cross-family seats, filesystem seat verifying §1 against
the live tree, dispositions row-by-row), same protocol as the checkout lane.

## §Meta

The ground pass found the ecosystem's consent story inverted twice: the best consent block
(Oracle handoff) sits where nothing is being collected anymore, and the heaviest
collection (upload, orders) sits where nothing is said. The design's one idea is symmetry:
one custody component, everywhere the funnel asks, stating exactly what the backend
already guarantees — and refusing to say one word more than that.
