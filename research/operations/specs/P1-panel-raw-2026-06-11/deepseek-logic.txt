1. ORPHAN STATUSES (§1.3, §3)  
The status state machine includes `'rejected'`, `'image_failed'`, `'fact_check_failed'`, `'render_failed'`, and `'rendered_shadow'` as outcomes produced by various stages. No consumer (reader, retry logic, alert handler) is defined for any of them in the target architecture. They are dead‑end statuses with no onward automatic processing, constituting orphans under the requirement that every named status have both a producer and a consumer.

2. METRIC M2 vs IMPLEMENTATION DESIGN (§2 Risks, §5 M2)  
M2 demands count equality between `rendered` drafts and `topic_type_log` rows (“every new `rendered` draft has a row”). The S1 implementation, however, plans a *best‑effort write* after the promote with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` and failure handled as a WARN. A failed write would break the equality without raising an error, so the metric is not guaranteed by the proposed implementation. This is a contradiction between the metric claim and the risk mitigation.

3. EVENT NOTIFICATION GAP (§1.4, §3, §4 R4)  
The target architecture shows the supervisor LISTENing to `wr2_status_change` NOTIFY from the PG trigger. But §1.4 documents that the channel is “not in `PG_CHANNEL_MAP`” (known scar) and there is no fix listed in the pre‑requisites or changes. After re‑enabling the supervisor in R4, real‑time notifications will still go “into the void”, removing the event‑driven path. The motor would then rely solely on periodic reconciliation, which is not the described behaviour and introduces a latent dependency on an unfixed known issue.

4. M6 FALSIFIABILITY UNDER‑SPECIFIED (§5 M6)  
The command for M6 is given as `“SELECT max(...) FROM wr2_supervisor_heartbeat”` plus `“launchd log”`. The latter is not a well‑defined command; determining “0 manual kickstarts needed for a full draft→rendered run” from launchd logs without a precise script or log‑stream specification makes the metric difficult to falsify in an automated, unambiguous way.

5. MINOR RISK: canva‑apply PLIST LEFT LOADED DURING R4 (§4 R2, §4 R4)  
The motor re‑enable (R4) occurs before Canva retirement (R2). Although the supervisor transition is patched to kick html‑apply and the kill‑switch is OFF, the canva‑apply plist remains loaded. A reconciliation map bug could theoretically start it; no direct contradiction, but a note for robustness.
