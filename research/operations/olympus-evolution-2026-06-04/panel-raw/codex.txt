**Verdict: thesis mostly right, but missing the first gate: containment.**

1. **Attack on Thesis**
The “half-blind and mute” framing is accurate, but too generous. Olympus is also **over-privileged, co-resident with the app, and writing to prod while measuring success weakly**. A health score of 98 is not meaningful if query load, lock pressure, retention growth, and action outcome deltas are absent.

The biggest smell is not pg_stat_statements. It is this: **an in-process app service with a superuser DB role is running autonomous maintenance on production**. That is a blast-radius problem before it is an observability problem.

Also, “zero degraded rules” after 20,504 actions is suspicious. Either failure detection is too narrow, success criteria are too weak, or the confidence model is ceremonial.

2. **Order**
“Observability + consume before more actions” is directionally right, but I would reorder:

1. **Contain first:** demote privileges, add hard kill-switches, action budgets, lock timeouts, statement timeouts, and explicit write-action allowlists.
2. **Make existing output useful:** retention, dedupe/supersede, weekly digest, owner-facing recommendations.
3. **Then improve visibility:** pg_stat_statements, pgstattuple, lock/txid metrics.
4. **Then qualify actions:** heuristics, replay windows, before/after measurement.
5. **Only then gated L3.**

Reason: pgss increases insight quality, but it does not reduce current operational risk. Containment does.

3. **Highest-Leverage 2-Week Move**
Ship an **Olympus Safety Envelope**:

- demote `backend_rag_v2` from superuser or split Olympus into a narrower maintenance role;
- add `statement_timeout`, `lock_timeout`, max actions per pulse, max runtime per pulse;
- add an operator kill-switch in DB/env;
- implement retention for `olympus_*`;
- publish one weekly digest from deduped insights.

That converts Olympus from “trusted autonomous actor” into “bounded operator with auditable output.” It also prepares the system for pgss and later L3 actions.

4. **Underweighted Risks**
- **In-process coupling:** a guardian bug can damage app latency, pool health, memory, and DB state simultaneously.
- **Pool corruption / asyncpg InterfaceError:** this deserves a circuit breaker, not just logging.
- **Superuser role:** this is the sharpest current risk.
- **False health:** score 98 may mask lock waits, slow queries, app starvation, table growth, and duplicated insight churn.
- **Insight table growth:** not just storage; it poisons decision quality.

5. **Overweighted Gaps**
- **Txid wraparound:** verified low risk now. Track it, but do not center the roadmap.
- **Matview failures:** historical only; do not overreact.
- **HypoPG absence:** annoying, not blocking. Good heuristics plus pgss history beat imaginary perfect planning.

**Architectural Concern**
Yes. Running inside the FastAPI app on a single 2GB Fly machine is a design liability. Olympus should eventually become a separate worker/process with its own pool, limits, role, logs, and restart behavior. Until then, no L3 automation.
