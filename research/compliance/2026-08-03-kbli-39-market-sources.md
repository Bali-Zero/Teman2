# The 39 codes — what the market says, and where the market disagrees with the law

**Audience:** agency owner and client-facing advisors. **Companion to:**
[`2026-08-03-kbli-39-closure-verdicts.md`](2026-08-03-kbli-39-closure-verdicts.md), which answered
*what the law says*. This one answers a different question: **what does the market say, where does
it disagree with the law, and which of those disagreements costs money.**

**This document has no legal authority and does not overturn a single verdict in the statutory
report.** Every source below is somebody's website. Most of them are selling something. Where two
sources contradict each other the contradiction is printed, not resolved — resolving it would
require a primary instrument or an actual OSS filing, and neither exists in this sweep.

### How to read the labels

Every claim carries **who**, **when**, and **what kind of source**:

| Label | What it means | How much weight |
|---|---|---|
| **government** | An Indonesian government body publishing on its own domain | Highest here, but note: even these are press releases, not instruments |
| **press** | A news outlet with no stake in the answer | High for *what happened and when*; low for code-level detail |
| **law firm** | An advocate/legal publisher | Medium — usually better sourced, still marketing |
| **agency marketing** | An incorporation, visa or property firm | Lowest. **They are selling the answer they give you** |
| **anecdote** | Unattributed, forum-level, or a claim I could not trace to a page | Lead only. Never quote to a client |

**Vintage flag.** Several pages below still number in **KBLI 2020**, or predate KBLI 2025 (Peraturan
BPS 7/2025) entirely. Those are marked `[pre-2025]` and are cited as *history*, never as current
law. A 2016 or 2019 page describing what foreigners could do then says nothing about today.

## 1. THE HEADLINE

**The market and our statutory report disagree in both directions, and the expensive direction is
not the one you would guess.**

Our report found 9 codes genuinely reserved and **19 with no restriction anywhere**. The market's
published lists do the near-opposite: **not one of our 39 codes appears on any market "closed to
PMA" list**, including the widely-reported Bali 18-code list — while the codes the market *does*
call closed (68111 real estate, 70209 consulting, 77100/77311 vehicle rental, 47711/47511/47249/47991
retail, 55110/55120 small hotels, 55900, 56303/56305, 93111/93116/93191 sport facilities, 14120,
70204) are almost entirely codes we publish nothing about.

Four divergences, in order of what they cost:

**1. The villa (55203) — the biggest ticket in Bali, and the market is split down the middle.**
Our report says *reserved* (Perpres 49/2021 Lampiran II, entry 48 sub-row *Vila*). A large cluster of
property agencies sells the opposite as the standard, compliant route: Bali Property Rules
(agency marketing, 11 Feb 2026, updated Mar 2026) calls 55193 *"the only accommodation KBLI available
to foreign PT PMA entities in Bali"*; Solar Property Bali (agency marketing, 2026, day undated) sells
*"up to 100% foreign ownership in accommodation businesses (KBLI codes 55193 or 55120)"*; Propertia
(agency marketing, 2026) names 55193 as correct and warns against 68111. Against them, Riga Global
Consulting (agency marketing, undated) says *"a foreign-owned PT PMA should not use KBLI 55203 to
operate a conventional Bali villa rental unless the OSS system and the competent authority expressly
confirm its availability"*, and TraceWorthy (agency marketing with a compliance posture, 9 Jan 2026)
cites the same Perpres schedule we do and calls PT PMA eligibility *"the critical, genuinely contested
question"*. **Nothing in the secondary literature resolves this.** Either we are turning away the most
common enquiry we get, or the market is selling foreigners a structure on an allocated-away code.
That is the money question, and it is unanswerable from the open web.

**2. The 19 false closures cost us exactly the clients worth having.** For a small client the badge
was accidentally harmless — a single barbershop, kiosk or camping ground cannot clear the IDR 10bn
PMA capital floor anyway (§4). It became expensive at the top end: a resort's golf club (93122), an
industrial laundry (96100's platform slice), a foreign **demolition contractor** — for whom
PP 28/2025 Lampiran I.H entry 39 publishes a *Besar* block written expressly for a *BUJK PMA* — a
head office (70100), a waste-collection operator (38110). We said no to business we could have taken,
and no published market list agrees that we should have.

**3. The gym conversation, where we are wrong twice in opposite directions.** We badge 11 sport
*club* codes closed; the market's own directory calls all 11 open, and no moratorium list names any of
them. Meanwhile every agency guide routes an actual gym client to **93116** (fitness centre) or
**85410** (sport education) — and **93116 is on the confirmed Bali 18-code blocked list** (ANTARA,
press, 23 Jul 2026). So we block the codes nobody uses and say nothing about the code the client
actually needs.

**4. The salon-inside-a-spa bundle is a live mis-selling risk, including on our own site.** Our
statutory report is explicit that 96220 (salon/beauty) is reserved but **96230 (SPA) is not**.
Agencies bundle them: CPT Corporate (agency marketing, 31 Mar 2026) lists "96112 Beauty salon"
alongside spa codes as one undifferentiated wellness cluster with no flag; and **our own older article**
(balizero.com, 21 Feb 2026) advises combining *"96230 (SPA) + 96220 (Beauty) + optional 96100
(Laundry B2B) in one wellness-focused PMA"* while describing 96220 as *"Open ✅"* — the direct
opposite of what balizero.com/kbli/96220 says today (dataset dated 2 Aug 2026). That February article
is publicly indexed and a client or competitor can find it.

## 2. PER CLUSTER

### 2a. Villa and homestay (55201, 55203, 55209)

**What agencies tell clients.** Two irreconcilable camps, and the camp correlates with what the firm
sells.

*Camp A — villa code is the PT PMA route:*

| Who | Date | Kind | Says |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bali Property Rules | 11 Feb 2026 (upd. Mar 2026) | agency marketing | *"the only accommodation KBLI available to foreign PT PMA entities in Bali"* (55193) |
| Solar Property Bali | 2026, day undated | agency marketing | *"Up to 100% foreign ownership in accommodation businesses (KBLI codes 55193 or 55120)"* |
| Propertia | 2026, day undated | agency marketing | 55193 is correct; *"Do NOT use KBLI 68111"* |
| Investland Bali | 2026 | agency marketing | leasehold + PT PMA holding HGB framed as the only enforceable structure |
| kbli.co.id (`/55203`) | undated | agency marketing (directory) | *"Villa Activities … Open to PMA"* |

*Camp B — it is reserved, or at best contested:*

| Who | Date | Kind | Says |
|---|---|---|---|
| Riga Global Consulting | undated, references KBLI 2025 | agency marketing | *"should not use KBLI 55203 … unless the OSS system and the competent authority expressly confirm its availability"* |
| TraceWorthy | 9 Jan 2026 | agency marketing | cites Perpres 10/2021 as amended by 49/2021, *Vila* in Schedule II *"allocated to Cooperatives and MSMEs"*; *"the critical, genuinely contested question"* |
| kbli.co.id (`/55193`) | 24 Apr 2026 | agency marketing (directory) | *"Villas · SME-reserved"* |
| Latest News Bali (byline: Fullers Properties) | 26 Jul 2026 | press (property-agency byline) | *"the villa accommodation code … cannot be used by a foreign-owned company, as its allocation is reserved for local business"* |

**Print the contradiction, do not resolve it.** The same directory (kbli.co.id) calls the **2020**
number 55193 *SME-reserved* and the **2025** number 55203 *Open to PMA* — the same real-world
activity, opposite verdicts, in the same April-2026 data refresh. And H-Studio (agency marketing)
published both verdicts **four days apart**: 26 May 2026 — *"KBLI 55193 (Villa): PT PMA / Indonesian
PT eligible … Correct code for high-end private villas"*; 30 May 2026 — *"55193 → 55203 Villa
Activity: Contested … it is restricted to Indonesian citizens (WNI). A foreigner cannot hold it."*

**Homestay (55201) — the market mostly agrees it is closed, but cites a different law than we do.**
Riga Global Consulting (agency marketing, undated): *"Foreigners cannot legally hold a Pondok Wisata
license — Permenpar 18/2016 restricts it to Indonesian citizens."* H-Studio (26 May 2026) says the
same. **That is a tourism-registration rule about who may register a business, not an investment
reservation** — a completely different legal rail from the Perpres annex our report relies on. Market
agreement here is not corroboration; it is a different argument arriving at the same place.
Contradicting all of it: kbli.co.id `/55130` says *"Pondok Wisata · Khusus UMKM"* while kbli.co.id
`/55201` says *"Homestay Rental Activities … Open to PMA"* — that page is dated **1 Sep 2024**
`[pre-2025]`, so staleness is as good an explanation as crosswalk-blindness. And Emerhub contradicts
itself across two of its own pages: one tells readers *"If you are interested in applying for a Pondok
Wisata license, you are required to register as a … PT PMA"*; another treats Pondok Wisata as held in
an Indonesian person's name alongside a separate PT PMA management vehicle.

**55209 — the market treats it as an escape valve, and warns about it.** kbli.co.id says *"Open to
PMA"* flat. H-Studio (30 May 2026) says the 55209 family is *"frequently used to sidestep the 55193
eligibility problem"* and that this triggers a *"reclassification-and-sanction trap"* on inspection.
Riga Global Consulting: *"not a general alternative for foreign-owned villas."* **No market source
anywhere discusses the sub-activity scoping our statutory report found (Pasal 5(5): only *Guest House*
is reserved, not bungalows or cottages).** That finding is ours alone and has no market echo.

**What agencies steer people to.** Four playbooks, all live in 2025–2026 material: (1) PT PMA holding
the villa code directly + 25–30yr lease with HGB; (2) the **management-fee model** — an Indonesian
entity or individual holds the Pondok Wisata/villa licence and the PT PMA is a separate hospitality-
management company earning a fee (Bali Property Rules calls this *"most popular for foreigners"*; Riga
routes it via KBLI 55901 accommodation-management services); (3) **nominee** — now described by every
2026 source as illegal and criminally dangerous; (4) **hotel-code substitution** (55101/55110/55120),
which Solar Property Bali recommends for *"larger operations"* and other sources warn against. Two
agencies give opposite advice on whether a hotel code is a legitimate villa substitute.

**Reported to actually happen.** No source in this sweep reports a single named, dated OSS rejection
for a villa or homestay KBLI. The rejection evidence that exists belongs to the separate 2026 risk-tier
block (§3). What *is* reported is physical enforcement: **48 illegal structures demolished at Bingin
Beach, Badung, July 2025** (press cluster, 2025–2026), fines from IDR 50 million, deportation and
blacklisting for operating without permits. A widely repeated *"90% of villas in Bali are in some form
of technical non-compliance"* figure appears only in aggregate search synthesis with no named report
behind it — **anecdote, do not quote to a client**.

### 2b. Personal services (95291 tailoring, 96100 laundry, 96210 barber, 96220 salon)

**What agencies tell clients: nothing at all.** This is the cluster's defining feature. No agency, law
firm or press source found in this sweep discusses opening a barbershop, salon or laundry as a PT PMA.
The most plausible reason is that foreign investors do not try — not that the reservation is absent.

**Where the market does speak, it is crosswalk-blind, and it is measurable.** kbli.co.id tags 95291
*"Clothing Alteration Activities · SME-reserved"* — the one code in this group whose **number did not
change** between KBLI 2020 and 2025. It tags all three **renumbered** siblings *"Open to PMA"*:
`96210` (2020 ancestor 96111), `96220` (2020 ancestor 96112), `96100` (2020 ancestor 96200). All four
pages sit in the same April-2026 refresh, so this is not simple staleness — the reservation is carried
only where the old code number is queried directly, and dropped across the BPS 2020→2025 conversion.
**This is the market suffering the exact structural weakness our own report names as its biggest
caveat, in the opposite direction: they produce false-OPEN where we produced false-CLOSED.**

Extra ambiguity on laundry: kbli.co.id lists **96100** *"Textile and Fur Washing … Open to PMA"* and,
separately, **96200** *"Laundry Activities · SME-reserved"* — treating industrial washing and consumer
laundry as two different things rather than one renumbered code.

**What agencies steer people to: the SPA code.** Cekindo/InCorp (agency marketing, undated) positions
**96230** as the PMA-viable wellness code. CPT Corporate (agency marketing, 31 Mar 2026) lists
*"96112 Beauty salon activities"* alongside spa codes 96121/96122/96129 as one undifferentiated
wellness cluster with **no flag that beauty-salon is separately reserved**. Emerhub's own code-checker
returns 96230 (large-scale risk: *high* — i.e. it **does** have a Besar row) as the best match for
"beauty salon, spa and wellness treatment services". **The risk this creates is concrete:** a PT PMA
formed to run a spa + salon combo may have a registered activity line that cannot stand, even though
the spa line is fine. No source reports an OSS rejection of that combo — it is a structural risk, not
an observed one.

**Our own site is the loudest contradiction in this cluster.** balizero.com/business/kbli-2025-fnb-
personal-services-bali-2026 (21 Feb 2026) says 96220 *"PMA Access: Open ✅ (large enterprise, premium
segment)"*, calls a single barbershop *"PMA only viable as a multi-location chain"*, and calls consumer
laundry *"UMKM territory. PMA viable only as an industrial B2B operation."* The corner pages
(balizero.com/kbli/96220, /96210, /96100, dataset 2 Aug 2026) say full closure. **Both are live.**
Note also that the older article's "scale up to a chain" and "go B2B industrial" workarounds would not
cure a Lampiran II reservation, which attaches to the KBLI code and not to revenue or footprint.

**Reported to actually happen.** One concrete enforcement story, and it is about the wrong thing:
Legal Indonesia (law firm, undated) reports a European-owned Bali salon raided after a **competitor
complaint**, for employing foreign specialists without work authorization — *"foreigners are not
allowed to work in the beauty industry — full stop"* — with fines to IDR 500 million, visa cancellation,
deportation and a possible business ban. **No KBLI code is named.** This is a labour/immigration risk
that runs in parallel to the ownership question and compounds it: a foreign owner who correctly used a
local structure to satisfy the reservation can still be raided for having foreign staff do the
treatments.

### 2c. Tour guides (79903, and the codes the market confuses it with)

**The market does not discuss 79903 at all.** kbli.co.id tags it *"Tourism Services … Open to PMA"* —
the only affirmative market position found, and it contradicts our RESERVED verdict. Every other source
(an OSS page whose data sits behind an interactive selector, startnow.co.id, Sah News, Badan Perizinan,
golaw.id) is silent on foreign-ownership status. golaw.id's own page for the neighbouring 79901 is
titled *"[BELUM LENGKAP]"* — **the publisher flags its own entry as unfinished**.

**The market is talking about the neighbour, not the code.** Every restriction conversation in tourism
services is about travel **agencies**: InvestinAsia (agency marketing, 5 May 2026) flags *"domestic
travel agency operations carry partnership requirements"*; H-Studio (30 May 2026) flags 79112 (tour
operator) as *"Conditional"*; and 79121 (travel agency) is in the Governor's original nine-code list
(§3). The individual guide code is not in that conversation.

**Code-vintage split is live and unflagged.** Multiple currently-published pages still put *Jasa
Pramuwisata* under the **KBLI 2020** number **79921**, not 79903: startnow.co.id, news.sah.co.id,
badanperizinan.co.id — none of them dated or version-flagged `[pre-2025 numbering]`. A client googling
"KBLI tour guide" today lands on 2020 numbering about as often as on current numbering.

**Every practitioner treats "foreigner as guide" as an immigration question, not an ownership one.**
Seven Stones Indonesia, The Bali Lawyer, LMI Consultancy (all undated) frame it as KITAS/RPTKA and the
Manpower Ministry's restricted-positions list — a different legal rail from the investment annex.

**What is reported to actually happen.** Enforcement attaches to the **person**, via the HPI guide card
(KTPP): three guides fined **IDR 25 million each** (or 3 months) *"for operating without official tour
guide identity cards"* (Coconuts Bali, press, **10 May 2019** `[pre-2025 — history only]`). Badung's
Satpol PP is explicitly nationality-blind: *"We do not look at their origin … Anyone who disturbs the
comfort of tourists, we will monitor them"* (The Bali Sun, press, 16 Nov 2023). The observed workaround
is **naturalization**, not a company structure: the guide in a 2023 viral case *"is actually an
Indonesian citizen who became naturalized in 2021"* and holds an HPI-endorsed licence (Coconuts Bali,
28 Aug 2023). Immigration sweeps name tour guiding among unauthorized activities alongside yoga
teaching, diving and photography (Jakarta Globe, 2024 and 2025).

**One claim that could matter and that I could not verify.** A DPR committee expert-staff blog
(harjasaputra.com, 28 Dec 2025 — **law-firm-adjacent personal blog, not an official DPR channel**)
states that UU 18/2025 (Third Amendment to the Tourism Law) inserted a **Pasal 11J**: *"Pramuwisata
asing wajib didampingi oleh pramuwisata WNI bersertifikat"* — foreign guides must be accompanied by a
certified Indonesian guide. Three other renderings of the same law do not mention it; the primary BPK
PDF, the DPR draft and a Hukumonline piece all returned 403 to fetch. **Treat as plausible and
unconfirmed. Do not put it in client copy.** It is also a rule about the *act of guiding*, not about
who owns the company — a separate layer from anything in the statutory report.

`[pre-2025, history only]` Coconuts Bali (15 Jul 2019) cites a 2016 Bali regional regulation under
which foreign nationals could guide after specialised training and certification. **That is 2016. It
predates KBLI 2025, PP 28/2025 and UU 18/2025 and must never be quoted as current law.**

### 2d. Hostel, camping and massage (55202, 55300, 86995)

Our report puts all three in the "no restriction" bucket. The market is largely silent or agrees — with
one structural exception that matters a great deal.

**Where the market agrees.** Seven Stones Indonesia (agency marketing / Terje Nilsen, 26 Feb 2026)
discusses 55202 by name and frames it purely as an **OSS integration lag**, not an ownership problem:
the new 2025 accommodation codes are *"legally valid — but their full integration into … OSS-RBA is
still ongoing"*, so operators get assigned to legacy clusters while licensing catches up. Bali Visa
(agency marketing, 11 Dec 2025) recommends *"Camping Ground"* as a normal PT PMA structuring choice for
a glamping business. Neither flags a closure. Emerhub's own accommodation open/closed table for Bali
(15 Apr 2026) lists Villa, Aparthotel, Hotel Melati and Pondok Wisata as closed — and **does not list
55202 or 55300 at all**. Sunrise Business (undated, self-dated "early 2026") gives an explicit list of
codes unavailable for new Bali PT PMA and neither is on it.

images are the authority.

**What the letter is:** `Nomor B.27.000/642/PM/DPMPTSP`, Bali **28 Januari 2026**, `Sifat: Segera`,

## 4. THE USAHA BESAR / IDR 10bn QUESTION

**The question:** for a small business, is the practical obstacle the annex at all — or the capital
floor?

**Answer: for almost every code in this set, it is the capital floor, and the annex never gets a
chance to matter.**

### What the market says the floor is

Near-universal agreement across agencies and law-adjacent publishers, wording almost identical:
a PT PMA needs an **investment plan above IDR 10 billion excluding land and buildings**, plus
**IDR 2.5 billion paid-up capital**, assessed **per KBLI per project location** (basis:
Permeninves/BKPM 5/2025). H-Studio (30 May 2026); Easybiz (Hukumonline's incorporation arm,
2 Jan 2026); TraceWorthy (9 Jun 2026, updated May 2026); Legalitas.org (23 Feb 2026).

**A nuance the marketing copy glosses over, and it is commercially significant:** the "per KBLI" unit
is **not uniformly 5-digit**. Agency sources report Wholesale Trade at **4-digit**, Food & Beverage
Services at **2-digit per location-point (regency/city)**, Construction at **4-digit**. For an F&B
client that is the difference between one floor and several.

**A real, described rejection mechanism — but not the one our badges assumed.** TraceWorthy:
*"The OSS system performs the calculation at the licensing stage and returns a rejection where the
declared investment plan does not satisfy the per-KBLI floor."* Note this is triggered by the
**declared capital figure**, not by which scale rows OSS happens to publish for a code. Kawan Legal
(agency, undated, references a 5 Jan 2026 maintenance note) describes the classifier the same way:
*"Skala usaha akan terkategorikan atas keseluruhan modal usaha yang diinputkan"* — **scale is an output
of the capital you enter, not a per-code gate.** That is precisely our statutory report's reading of
Permeninves/BKPM 5/2025 Pasal 26(1), arrived at independently by a commercial source.

ASEAN Briefing (Dezan Shira, 9 Apr 2026) frames the IDR 10bn as a **feasibility test** regulators
apply against the commercial logic of the declared activity — not a fixed annex gate.

### Why this answers the question

Take the two numbers together — and this is our own reading, derived from the statutory report, not a
market claim:

- Perpres 10/2021 **Pasal 5(2)(c)** caps a *reserved* (koperasi/UMKM) activity at **business capital of
  IDR 10 billion excluding land and buildings**.
- Permeninves/BKPM 5/2025 requires a PT PMA to invest **more than IDR 10 billion** per KBLI per
  location.

**WITHDRAWN — this argument does not hold.** It read: the two rules meet at the same number from
opposite sides and leave nothing in between, so for a genuinely small business the annex is academic
because the client cannot reach a PT PMA at all. Adversarial review (Codex, 2026-08-03) refuted the
step that carries it: **`modal usaha` (business capital) and `nilai investasi` (investment value) are
different legal quantities**, serving different tests. The same numeral and the same
land-and-buildings exclusion do not make them interchangeable, and no cited provision creates the
identity the argument needs. A business may therefore be able to satisfy the PMA investment threshold
while still falling inside the separate UMKM capital test. The empty interval is **not established**,
and every conclusion drawn from it below is downgraded to an untested hypothesis.

**So the badge was wrong on 19 codes and accidentally harmless on most small enquiries.** It became
expensive at exactly the point where the two rules stop overlapping: the client who **can** clear
IDR 10bn. A resort's golf club, an industrial or multi-site laundry, a foreign demolition contractor
(whom PP 28/2025 Lampiran I.H entry 39 contemplates by name), a regional head office, a multi-site
waste operator, a large hostel or campground group. Those are the clients the badge turned away, and
for them the annex — not the capital floor — is the only question that matters.

**And it cuts the other way too.** For the nine genuinely reserved codes, capital is *not* the reason.
A client who arrives with IDR 50 billion still cannot enter 96220 or 55203 through the front door, and
telling them "it's a capital problem" would be wrong and would invite them to solve it with money.

**One open proposal to watch:** DNT Lawyers (law firm; page dated 21 May, year context strongly implies
2026 but not confirmed on the page) reports a **proposed Bali-specific increase from IDR 10 billion to
IDR 100 billion**. **Proposal, not law.** If enacted it would widen the gap between "too small for a
PMA" and "reserved anyway" enormously, and would make the capital floor the dominant obstacle for
nearly every business in this set.

## 6. WHAT THE SECONDARY LITERATURE CANNOT SETTLE

Stated plainly, because a named gap is worth more than a confident paragraph.

1. **Nobody in the market has read Perpres 49/2021 Lampiran II.** The sources that agree with our
   reservations cite something else — Permenpar 18/2016 for homestay, an OSS scale table for personal
   services, nothing at all for tour guides. **Market agreement is therefore not corroboration.** It is
   a different argument reaching the same conclusion, and it will move if its own instrument moves.
2. **Nobody has published the Governor's letter.** Every claim about the Bali moratorium traces to one
   press statement plus agency paraphrase. The **government's own release names 18 categories by
   description and no numbers**; two agencies reverse-engineered numeric lists and disagree. The
   primary text is not on the open web.
3. **No source reports a single named, dated OSS rejection for any of our 39 codes.** The only quoted
   OSS rejection text belongs to the Bali risk-tier block. Every "in practice" claim in this document
   — Pasal 5(5) scoping, personal-capacity registrability, missing-Besar-row behaviour — is answerable
   only by an actual filing, and none exists in this sweep.
4. **The KBLI 2020→2025 crosswalk defeats the market exactly as it defeats us.** One directory gives
   opposite verdicts for the same activity under its two code numbers, in the same data refresh
   (55193/55203; 55130/55201; 96111/96210; 96112/96220; 96200/96100). **No published list is
   crosswalk-safe**, and several live pages still number in 2020 with no version flag.
5. **Nobody distinguishes "reserved by a Perpres annex" from "OSS has no Besar row."** TraceWorthy's
   own page concedes it treats them as *"functionally equivalent barriers"* without explaining which
   applies. Practitioners have no vocabulary for the distinction, so clients get "closed" for both.
6. **The commercially valuable sub-activity findings have zero market echo.** Nobody discusses Pasal
   5(5) scoping — that only *Guest House* is reserved inside 55209, that only *simple and intermediate
   technology* demolition is reserved inside 43110. If those readings are right, they are ours alone
   and no market source will confirm them.
7. **Land tenure, zoning and local Perda are unexamined for our codes.** No source in this sweep
   analysed whether a foreign-owned entity can hold the land under a villa, a golf course or a camping
   ground; the land literature is generic (HGB, HGU) and never touches our code numbers. Our statutory
   report names the same gap.
8. **The forum/anecdote layer was not surveyed.** The sweep's shared web-search budget was exhausted
   mid-run. Reddit, expat Facebook groups and YouTube agency explainers — where an actual rejection
   screenshot would most likely surface — were not queried. **That is a gap, not a null result.**
9. **Four claims are single-source and unverified.** UU 18/2025 **Pasal 11J** (foreign guides must be
   accompanied by a certified Indonesian guide); the *"90% of villas are non-compliant"* figure; the
   **31 Mar 2026** OTA licensing deadline (direct verification attempt failed); DNT's **IDR 100 billion**
   Bali proposal. None belongs in client copy.
10. **Almost every source is selling something, in both directions.** The firms warning that the
    restriction is broad sell restructuring; the firms saying the villa code is open sell company
    setup and property. **Alarmist and permissive framings each have a seller behind them**, which is
    why nothing in this document should outrank the statutory report.
11. **Pre-2025 pages are still ranking.** A 2016 Bali rule on foreign guides (via a 2019 press piece),
    a 2024-dated directory entry for 55201, a May-2025 page numbering in KBLI 2020. They are live,
    findable, and undated on the page. **A client will find them before they find us.**
